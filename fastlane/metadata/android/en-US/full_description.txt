Zanshin Calendar shows one day at a time in the Tibetan calendar or in the old Japanese calendar (旧暦), with the almanac readings that belong to that day, and sunrise, solar noon and sunset for your place. A meditation timer and a mindfulness bell come with it.

<ul>

<li>Monthly observances on the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th and 30th</li>
<li>Lunar mansion, yoga and karaṇa, the element pair of weekday and mansion, the lunar-day animal, trigram and number</li>
<li>What changes within the day, as the almanac of the White Beryl (བཻཌཱུར་དཀར་པོ, bai DUr dkar po) writes it: a second mansion, a skipped yoga and the span of Viṣṭi, each with its time</li>

<li>Lunar and solar eclipses as the almanac reckons them, by the karaṇa reckoning (བྱེད་རྩིས, byed rtsis) of the Kālacakra (དུས་འཁོར, dus 'khor), on their day, with the merit they multiply</li>

<b>Old Japanese calendar (旧暦, Tenpō rules)</b>
<ul>
<li>Month and day with the moon's age, rokuyō (六曜), the moon phase and the current solar term (二十四節気), with its reading and the ring of the 24</li>
<li>The twelve stations (十二直, the middle band), the lunar lodge (二十八宿) and the day's 九星</li>
<li>Lucky and unlucky days of the lower band (暦注下段) and the selected days (選日), such as 天赦日, 一粒万倍日, 不成就日 and 三隣亡</li>
<li>Seasonal markers (雑節), festivals, the day and year signs (干支) and the lucky direction of the year (恵方)</li>
<li>Choose a day: the days the annotations name good for a work, in date order, with the disputed days apart</li>
</ul>

<b>Meditation</b>
<ul>
<li>A meditation timer: a warm-up and periods of any length in one session, as a zazen of 20, 10 and 30 minutes, the bowl at the start and end and a wood block between periods</li>
<li>Sessions saved as presets, set again with one tap</li>
<li>Keeps time with the screen off; the countdown and a Stop button in the notification</li>
<li>Or, if you choose, keeps the screen on from Begin until you leave the timer</li>
<li>A mindfulness bell through the day, every 15 minutes to 2 hours, at fixed or random times, within the hours and on the days you choose, quiet while the phone is silenced, during a call or while music or a video plays; while it rings, a notice shows a message of your own</li>
<li>A large bowl, a small bowl, a bell and the wood block, sounded on the phone itself</li>
</ul>

Tibetan terms show their English with their Tibetan script and Wylie, and their pronunciation on tap; tap any kanji to see its English. Tap an almanac line to read what it means, what it favours, what to avoid and how to remedy it; every reading comes from a published source, listed in About & sources.

Glyphs show at a glance what the day's almanac favours and warns against; dials, rings and boards in the readings show where each term stands in its cycle.

In English and Russian: choose the language in the menu, or let the app follow the phone.

In six themes: Ink, dark, as it has always been; Paper, a light page with dark ink; Night, black for OLED screens; Maroon, the robe's maroon with saffron; Sepia, a parchment page for the evening; or the phone's own dark or light setting.

Large fonts and wide screens: the pages follow the phone's font size up to twice the default, and in landscape or on a tablet stand in two columns, the day at a glance beside its sections.

A morning notice, if you turn it on: once a day at the time you set, silently, the day in brief of the calendar you last read. On the Tibetan page, the day's tone and the factor that decides it, how many works it names good and to avoid, and the hours that run against it; on the 旧暦 page, the rokuyō and the day's mark. A tap opens the day.

Two home-screen widgets for the calendar you last read, in the app's theme and language. Day: the date, the day and its tone with the factor that decides it; on the 旧暦 page, how many works the day names good and to avoid. Day and hours: the same, with the twelve hours of the Tibetan day in the colours of their verdict, the present hour marked, and the hours that run against the day; on the 旧暦 page, the rokuyō and the day's mark. A tap opens the day.

<b>Computed on the phone</b>
Everything is calculated on the device for any date from 1900 to 2100: Tibetan dates with Svante Janson's published arithmetic, new moons and solar terms with Jean Meeus's algorithms and the VSOP87 solar theory. The results are checked against Edward Henning's Tibetan calendars and the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan.

<b>Private</b>
The app has no internet permission. Your location is taken once from GPS when you ask for it, picked from an offline list of cities, or typed in as coordinates, and it stays on the phone.
