Zanshin Calendar shows one day at a time in the Tibetan calendar or in the old Japanese calendar (旧暦), with the almanac readings that belong to that day, and sunrise, solar noon and sunset for your place. A meditation timer and a mindfulness bell come with it.

<b>Tibetan calendar, Phugpa (ཕུག་པ, phug pa)</b>
<ul>
<li>Lunar day, month and year, with skipped and doubled days and leap months</li>
<li>Festivals: Losar (ལོ་གསར, lo gsar), Chötrul Düchen (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་དུས་ཆེན, cho 'phrul dus chen), Saga Dawa Düchen (ས་ག་ཟླ་བའི་དུས་ཆེན, sa ga zla ba'i dus chen), Chökhor Düchen (ཆོས་འཁོར་དུས་ཆེན, chos 'khor dus chen), Lhabab Düchen (ལྷ་བབས་དུས་ཆེན, lha babs dus chen) and others</li>
<li>Monthly observances on the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th and 30th</li>
<li>Lunar mansion, yoga and karaṇa, the element pair of weekday and mansion, the lunar-day animal, trigram and number</li>
<li>What changes within the day, as the almanac of the White Beryl (བཻཌཱུར་དཀར་པོ, bai DUr dkar po) writes it: a second mansion, a skipped yoga, the span of Viṣṭi and the sun's terms, each with its time</li>
<li>The letters the almanac writes for each date: the vowels and consonants of the Kālacakra (དུས་འཁོར, dus 'khor) and of the rising of the vowels (དབྱངས་འཆར, dbyangs 'char) with their elements and sense objects, the date's stage, the twelve links (རྟེན་འབྲེལ, rten 'brel) counted from the month's middle term, and the moon's quarter of the hundred feet (རྐང་བརྒྱ, rkang brgya)</li>
<li>What the almanac writes at the head of each month: whether it is long or short, the planet that rises, the black months, the seven days of the sage (དྲང་སྲོང, drang srong) and of the pig, and the comet its count marks</li>
<li>The readings of the lunar mansion, date, weekday, yoga, karaṇa and trigram, the special days, Rāhu's course, the earth lords and the direction of the astrologer spirit (བླ་མཁྱེན, bla mkhyen)</li>
<li>A day in brief, weighed as the texts rank their factors: the hours above the day, what the activity lists name good or to avoid, the day's tone and the one factor that decides it</li>
<li>Choose a day: the best days for a work over one to twelve months, ranked as the texts weigh them, with its good hours in each month</li>
<li>Good and bad days for cutting hair, and personal days for your birth year</li>
<li>A round dial of the hours: the combination period of each hour, Jupiter's nectar periods, the earth lords of the hour, the black hours, what each hour is good for, the four rough times of the sun and the works an hour turns against the day</li>
<li>The four aspects of the year (vitality, body, destiny, luck) and, for your birth year, the personal mansions and the white and black pebbles of the year, month, day and each hour</li>
<li>Your year of age by the elemental divination of the White Beryl: the mewa (སྨེ་བ, sme ba), trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) and progressed sign (ལོག་མེན, log men) of the year, its twenty-four pebbles with their predictions, growth and decline, and the obstacle years, each with its reading</li>
</ul>

<b>Old Japanese calendar (旧暦, Tenpō rules)</b>
<ul>
<li>Month and day, rokuyō (六曜), the current solar term (二十四節気) and the moon phase</li>
<li>The twelve stations (十二直, the middle band), the lunar lodge (二十八宿) and the day's 九星</li>
<li>Lucky and unlucky days of the lower band (暦注下段) and the selected days (選日), such as 天赦日, 一粒万倍日, 不成就日 and 三隣亡</li>
<li>Seasonal markers (雑節), festivals, the day and year signs (干支) and the lucky direction of the year (恵方)</li>
<li>Choose a day: the days the annotations name good for a work, in date order, with the disputed days apart</li>
</ul>

<b>Meditation</b>
<ul>
<li>A meditation timer: a warm-up and periods of any length in one session, as a zazen of 20, 10 and 30 minutes, the bowl at the start and end and a wood block between periods</li>
<li>Sessions saved as presets, set again with one tap</li>
<li>Keeps time with the screen off; the countdown and a Stop button in the notification</li>
<li>A mindfulness bell through the day, every 15 minutes to 2 hours, at fixed or random times, within the hours and on the days you choose, quiet while the phone is silenced</li>
<li>A large bowl, a small bowl, a bell and the wood block, sounded on the phone itself</li>
</ul>

Tibetan terms show their English with their Tibetan script and Wylie, and their pronunciation on tap; tap any kanji to see its English. Tap an almanac line to read what it means, what it favours and what to avoid; every reading comes from a published source, listed in About & sources.

Glyphs show at a glance what the day's almanac favours and warns against; dials, rings and boards in the readings show where each term stands in its cycle.

In English and Russian: choose the language in the menu, or let the app follow the phone.

<b>Computed on the phone</b>
Everything is calculated on the device for any date from 1900 to 2100: Tibetan dates with Svante Janson's published arithmetic, new moons and solar terms with Jean Meeus's algorithms and the VSOP87 solar theory. The results are checked against Edward Henning's Tibetan calendars and the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan.

<b>Private</b>
The app has no internet permission. Your location is taken once from GPS when you ask for it, picked from an offline list of cities, or typed in as coordinates, and it stays on the phone.
