# The twenty-one children are named by their conditions together; no text counts them

WB (vol. 1, pp. 338–340) names each of the *Secret Innermost Essence*'s
twenty-one children by a list of conditions and says no more of how a
birth falls into one: no count of how many must hold, no "most of", no
weighing between two children a birth could be. Mipham's notes gloss the
conditions one by one (vol. 2, pp. 484–485) and add only that the child's
luck by the parents' years is the sum of what came before, nothing
reckoned apart (p. 489). The manuals that restate the reckoning give the
three groups' results and nothing on the count: Lha mo tshul khrims (LT,
printed pp. 158, 167; etext pp. 166, 175) lists the good, middling and bad
seven by name with their results and sends "the other small matters" to
other works ([corpus](../corpus/lt/ch4-birth.md)); Gyurme Dorje lists them as
types (GD, seen only in BDRC's search snippets of his English). BDRC's full-text search for six of the children's names
(2026-10-22) finds them only in WB's prints, LT and Dorje.

What the texts do give is the frame for the whole: WB p. 349, the good and
bad weighed by force and number throughout, good where the good are
greater and more, and no verdict from one or two results; and the
*Utpala Garland* (UT, printed p. 108, etext p. 130,
[corpus](../corpus/ut/ch1-results.md)), for any board of pebbles, many white
good, many black bad, even smooth, the speckled neutral.

So a child is a kind the conditions name together. Read literally, a
birth is that child where its conditions hold; a reckoner names the
child only where they all do, and no text allows a partial child. Where
a condition is an observation (dreams, signs at birth, the family living)
no reckoning settles it. Answers open question 185 as far as the sources
go.
