# The multipliers of the good days and the eclipses: Karma chags med against the White Beryl

RCZ and the White Beryl give multipliers for the same occasions and differ on two of three.

| Occasion | The White Beryl, ch. 9 (wb.1:p62; the lines whole in month-entries.md) | RCZ (rcz:p286-288) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the ordinary good days | a hundredfold («དུས་བཟང་དཀྱུས་རྣམས་བརྒྱ་འགྱུར») | the 8th and 23rd, the 14th and 29th, the full and the new moon a hundred thousandfold (འབུམ་འགྱུར, 'bum 'gyur) |
| a lunar eclipse | seven koṭi-fold («ཟླ་འཛིན་བྱེ་བ་བདུན་འགྱུར») | a hundred koṭi-fold («ཟླ་བ་བྱེ་བ་བརྒྱ་འགྱུར») |
| a solar eclipse | a hundred thousand koṭi-fold («ཉི་འཛིན་བྱེ་བ་འབུམ་འགྱུར») | a hundred thousand koṭi-fold («ཉི་མ་བྱེ་བ་འབུམ་འགྱུར») |

The White Beryl stands by rank (it names the *Sūtra of the Sun and Moon* (ཉི་ཟླའི་མདོ, nyi zla'i mdo) among its sources; RCZ says only "many sūtras and tantras"). RCZ adds what the White Beryl's lines do not: the multiplication at an eclipse is for the recitation of mantra in particular, and it lasts from the seizing until the sun or moon has recovered. Whether RCZ's "a hundred" for the moon is a variant of the White Beryl's "seven" or another tradition is not settled by these two passages.

Update 2026-11-16 (q1261): the White Beryl's seven koṭi for the lunar eclipse is the number a verse credits to Paṇ chen Śākyaśrī; RCZ's hundred koṭi was found in no other text read (claims shakyashri-verse-on-the-festivals-and-eclipses, three-traditions-of-the-multipliers).
