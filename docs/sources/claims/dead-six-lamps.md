# The six great lamps: deaths that repeat in a household

The six great lamps (སྒྲོན་ཆེན་དྲུག, sgron chen drug; WB vol. 2, p. 55) are six ways the earlier deaths in a household point at the living; Mipham's notes say how each is judged and that each is a pebble: "if the lamp strikes, a black pebble; if not, white" (p. 502).

| Lamp | Struck when (the notes, p. 502) | Cause (WB) | Remedy (WB) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| count of years (ལོ་གྲངས, lo grangs) | earlier deaths came at a fixed interval of years, two or three, and the interval falls again now | strife with the local *btsan* spirits (བཙན, btsan) | lay a blood clearing; a Bon priest of the five clans suppresses the lamp's harmful spirits of the dead (སྲི, sri) |
| number of people (མི་གྲངས, mi grangs) | deaths came in a number, three, four, and that number is reached again | as above | as above |
| age (ན་ཚོད, na tshod) | many died in their prime, or someone died at a critical age and someone is at that age now | "the line cut off": a white [one?] gone to the grave | clear with an ox and a dog [?] |
| month of illness (ན་ཟླ, na zla) | someone living now is of the count of years (the age) at which an earlier dead died | strife with the local Buddhist and Bon priests | turning back (བཟློག་པ, bzlog pa) |
| manner of death (ཤི་ཐབས, shi thabs) | an earlier dead died of a planet illness (གཟའ་ནད, gza' nad) or the like, and someone now is at risk of the same | strife with the *btsan*, an earth lord in the house | a white swastika clearing at the mouth of nine valleys; suppress the *sri* with nine skulls; a support of the gods, drawing out their poison |
| direction (ཕྱོགས, phyogs) | someone earlier went east to China and died, and someone now goes there | strife with that direction's *btsan* | divide off the *btsan* with the listed woods and a raven's flesh; cut the direction with the eight white |

WB's own definitions: dying at the same count of years; deaths matching a number; the same age, old or young; the same month [?] of the year; one and the same illness; one and the same direction. The general rite "when the mouth of death is open" follows: the rites turning back ill omens at length, the three-headed black *sri* suppressed under skulls by an old man, a son and a woman, a ransom of nine sticks, thirteen jewels as pledge; if the *sri* of sons still rises, suppression in an old sheep's skull (p. 55). Inputs: the household's earlier dead with their dates and ages at death, causes and places of death, and the living's ages and journeys; none of these is in the reckoning's own roots.
