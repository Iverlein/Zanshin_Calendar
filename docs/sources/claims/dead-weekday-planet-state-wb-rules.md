# How the death day's planet's state is found: WB's two rules, the element test, the two gaps on four prints

# How the death day's planet's state is found: WB's two rules, neither named by ch. 29; the element test fails; the two gaps stand on three prints

**Where the states come from.** The weekday entries of the third examination (wb.2:p63-65) give each planet's day a state, rising (དར, dar), declining (གུད, gud), waning (ཞུད, zhud) or contested (གྱོད, gyod), and do not say how the state of the day of death is found. WB gives two rules for it elsewhere, both the same in form: the planet that rises, and its son, are rising; its mother wanes, its enemy declines, its friend is contested.
- By the month: vol. 1, p. 176 (month-entries.md, "The weekday's rise"): roughly by the month's animal, «སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་སྤེན་དར་ཚེས། །བྱི་འབྲུག་སྤྲེལ་གསུམ་མིག་དར་ཚེས། །ཕག་ལུག་ཡོས་གསུམ་ཟླ་དར་ཚེས། །བྱ་གླང་སྦྲུལ་གསུམ་ཕུར་དར་ཚེས།» (Saturn on tiger, horse, dog; Mars on rat, dragon, monkey; the Moon on pig, sheep, hare; Jupiter on bird, ox, snake), finely from the element of the month's lord.
- By the date: vol. 2, p. 313 (wb.2:p313, the planets' dates): the same four triads of the day's animal for the weekday's planet, "in detail counted from the weekday's planet".
LT states it by the same triads, for the day (lt:p9-11; planet-state-by-rising). So the almanac's "rough rise and decline" of the month (the question's first guess) and the day's animal are WB's two ways; the dead's element enters ch. 29 only as the board's clan and no line makes the weekday's state depend on it. Which of the two the examination means is not said. Inferred, not stated: the day's, since the day's animal entries stand directly before the weekday entries and name the day's animal.

**The element test.** If only the four planets named as rising rise, the other three planets (Sun, Mercury, Venus) take their state from their element's relation to the riser's (lt-planet-rise-by-date-and-element: Sun and Mars fire, Moon and Mercury water, Jupiter wood, Venus iron, Saturn earth), and the relations are those of WB's own table (for wood: water mother, fire son, iron enemy, earth friend; dead-la-pebbles-layout). Our own count of which states each planet can ever take on that rule (not a source) does not give WB's table. With the enemy as what overcomes the riser, the Sun and Mars never decline, Jupiter is never contested and Saturn never wanes; with the friend and enemy turned round, the Sun and Mars are never contested, Jupiter never declines, Saturn never wanes; in both Mercury has all four. WB's table gives every planet all four states but two (below). So the rule by the riser's element does not explain the table's gaps; either WB's table is not built so, or the animal that rises is not the one counted.

**The gaps.** Mercury has no contested entry and Jupiter no declining entry, and the same on all four prints read: the 1996 print (Yigdzin-1 and by eye on img. 72–73), the Gser thang reprint (img. 070–071; RD31) and the Zhol print (img. 717, Yigdzin-1 over the whole leaf: Mercury «ལྷག་པ་དར་བར»; «ལྷག་པ་ཞད»; «ལྷག་པ་གུད»; then Jupiter «ཕུར་བུ་དར»; «ཕུར་བུ་ཞད»; «ཕུར་བུ་གྱོད»; the print spells waning «ཞད»). Lha mo Tshul khrims's verse (MW1KG21818, her p. 62) leaves the four states' results out: «དར་གུད་གྱོད་ཞུད་དག་གི་འབྲས། །རྒྱས་པར་གཞུང་ལས་ཤེས་པར་བྱ།», "the results of rising, declining, contested and waning: know them in full from the root text". The Taikhang reproduction (I5878 img. 099, Yigdzin-1 over the leaf) has the same: Mercury «ལྷག་པ་དར»; «ལྷག་པ་བཞུད»; «ལྷག་པ་གུད»; then Jupiter «ཕུར་བུ་དར»; «ཕུར་བུ་གནད» (the waning entry, the letter misread); «ཕུར་བུ་གྱོད»; no «ལྷག་པ་གྱོད» and no «ཕུར་བུ་གུད» stands anywhere on img. 098–101.
No reason for the two gaps is stated in any text read.
