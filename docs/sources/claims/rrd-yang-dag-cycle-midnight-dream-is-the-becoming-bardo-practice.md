# A Yang dag treasure cycle sets the dream of midnight as the becoming intermediate state, where the drop opens into the expanse; sleep, not sleeping and waking named (RD397, RD388)

The same Yang dag cycle's instruction on the four intermediate states sets the dream of midnight (nam gung gi rmi lam gyi dus su) as the time of the becoming intermediate state: the drop opens into the expanse, whatever appears is known as self-appearing, and the dream dawns as wisdom. The intermediate state of reality is set at the stirring of thoughts at dawn (tho rangs), cut by remembering the instruction. This is a practice of the midnight dream, not a reading of it as Māra's or as habit, and it differs from Marpa's song, where midnight is the unreliable watch. The cycle ends: if there is no obstacle to reality in falling asleep, not falling asleep and waking from sleep, one becomes a buddha in this life; the three are named but not described. A treasure text with no named author in the volume; its place in the ranking of sources is low.
