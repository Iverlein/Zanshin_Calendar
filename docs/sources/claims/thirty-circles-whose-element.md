# The thirty and the circles: whose element, in the manuals

Which element of the one reckoned picks the thirty's pairs and the circles' starting animals (question 259):

- The thirty, ch. 26. No text read says. The *Moonbeams* (dz:i029-034) names each pair as two years of the sixty, by the element the year is named by (the wood rat and ox, the fire tiger and hare), with WB's relation as a gloss seen from wood; two other prints write the first gloss out, "taking as the example one reckoned who is of the wood element". The element a year of the sixty is named by is its power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang; LT and UT, the power's key), and a pair's two years always share it. So the reading that the person's element is his power, the same element as the circles' (below), fits the *Moonbeams*' example; it is an inference, not a statement. The pair's two years share their body element too, so the example does not exclude the body.
- The circles, ch. 27. The notes say the power (wb.2:p533b). Zhechen Gyaltsab's worked example (zg:p303-304) starts a wood-monkey king's first circle from the iron bird, iron being the enemy of wood, the wood monkey's power and luck alike; his tenth circle names the power's element (དབང་ཁམས, dbang khams). LT (lt:p65-67) keeps WB's words, "the reckoned person's element".

The *Moonbeams*, LT and Zhechen Gyaltsab are manuals below WB; Mipham's notes, which name the power for the circles and the "year-horse" (ལོ་རྟ, lo rta) for the thirty, stand above them.
