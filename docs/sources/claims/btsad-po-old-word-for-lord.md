# «བཙད་པོ» is the old word for king or lord, and the nine-red's guard is not a slip

«བཙད་པོ» (btsad po) is a headword in Steinert's 64 dictionaries (locate.py dict, 2026-11-01): the old (རྙིང, rnying) form of «btsan po», glossed king, ruler, lord (རྗེ་བོ, rje bo), chief (དཔོན་པོ, dpon po) by the Great Tibetan Dictionary (no. 25), Dung dkar (no. 34), Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs (no. 37), Sgom sde (no. 58), Ives-Waldo (no. 8, nobleman, king, ruler), Rangjung Yeshe (no. 2) and the Brda dkrol gser gyi me long (no. 55, a name of btsan po or king; also dpon po). So the corpse not sent on the one-white or the nine-red is a ruler's or lord's; ch. 27 lists it with the king and the queen («རྗེ་རྒྱལ་བཙད་པོ་བཙུན་མོ», four ranks). The nine-red is the btsad po's elsewhere too: in ch. 24 the nine-red is the crux of the btsad po (the elders), in ch. 33 «དགུ་དམར་བཙད་པོ་ཕག་ཞོན» (the nine-red btsad po riding a pig); the eight-white's earth-lord text (year-month-earth-lords.md) also bars the btsad po's corpse. Narrowing for q292: the nine-red's peg guarding the btsad po and the patron agrees with the nine-red's own btsad po, so it is not evidently a slip for the eight-white; why the eight-white has no peg stays unexplained.
