# The Vairocanābhisaṃbodhi has the pupils tell their dreams at dawn and lists good and bad dreams

# The Vairocanābhisaṃbodhi itself (Toh 494) has the pupils tell their dreams to the teacher at dawn and lists good and bad dreams (q1012)

The Vairocana-specific text that names signs read in the pupils' dreams is the tantra itself, in the Derge Kanjur. In the rite of drawing the maṇḍala (vab:p329-330) the teacher gives the pupils tooth-sticks and a knotted thread, then lists the dreams that are good (a monastery, a pleasure grove, a sword, a wish-fulfilling jewel, a woman in white, Buddhas and bodhisattvas, crossing the sea) and says the reverse are bad, and orders: «བརྟུལ་ཞུགས་བཟང་པོ་ཁྱོད་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། ནང་པར་ཐམས་ཅད་ང་ལ་ཟློས།» (you of good vows, repeat all of it to me at dawn). Later (vab:p494-495) the pupil's good or bad dreams are told to the lama to examine his share; the good ones (a stūpa, books, a jewel, the sun and moon, a great elephant) show accomplishment, the bad (donkey, camel, dog, tiger, cat, jackal, a naked or oil-smeared man) are to be avoided, or the dreams are spoken of as essenceless. That the dawn question of the Sngags rim chen mo (snr:p367-371) and of the Vairocana initiation text rests on it is likely from the wording but not shown: neither is read against it here. The page gives no watch of the night and no day for the dream; the telling is the next morning. Etext only; the passage headings of the volume (the chapter's name) were not found, the etext having no chapter colophons near these pages.
