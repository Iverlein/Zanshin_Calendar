# The eight places that open death: three by implication, and the animals they would take

The eight places that open death (ཤི་ཁ་ཕྱེ་བའི་ས་བརྒྱད, shi kha phye ba'i sa brgyad, WB vol. 2, p. 90) are named in the root text for five trigrams only: rat or pig dying on *li*, tiger or hare on *khon* (worse than the rest), horse or snake on *dwa*, the four executioners (ox, dragon, sheep, dog) on *kham*, bird or monkey on *zin*. Mipham's note (p. 510) says: the five are those the root shows outright, with tiger and hare on *khon* shown as especially bad; on *khen*, *gin* and *zon* "death is also opened", which makes the eight, shown only by implication (ཤུགས་བསྟན, shugs bstan). The note names no animals for the three. The pattern of the five is that the dead's animal has an element that cuts the element of the trigram (water on fire, wood on earth, fire on iron, earth on water, iron on wood), and by that pattern the three left would be: *khen* (iron), horse or snake; *gin* (earth), tiger or hare; *zon* (wood), bird or monkey. This is read from the pattern and from the note's "by implication", not stated in any text found; LT and UT (searched) do not list the eight. Narrowed: answered in outline, the three pairs of animals are an inference.
