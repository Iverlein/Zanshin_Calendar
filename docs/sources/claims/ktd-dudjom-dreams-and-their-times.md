# Dudjom Rinpoche's dreams in his biography carry their hour (dawn, evening), day (tenth, fourteenth, fifteenth) and repeats (KTD)

In the biography of Dudjom Rinpoche (BDRC MW29259, images 181-187, in his own words as quoted there) his dreams are given with their times, as in Rtsa gsum gling pa's own life: the dawn of the night after a hundred dākinī feast offerings (a verse is kept on waking, and the day after he is freed from an illness); a dream at thirty-four in a practice cave (the phurba given by Mtsho rgyal, the whole phurba teaching clear on waking, left unwritten); a supplication prompted by a girl in his consort's dream on an evening in the waxing half of the month Smin zla, again on the night of the tenth, again on the night of the tenth of the next month; the fifteenth chosen as the day to begin, the fourteenth evening spent in supplication, and a dream of his own at dawn that confirmed it. Three things tie the dream to time: an hour of the night (dawn, evening), a day of the month (the tenth, the fourteenth, the fifteenth) and a repeat (three visits, a month apart, until he acted). The source of the dream is a named person (Mtsho rgyal, a girl in the consort's dreams, a white young man at dawn). The biography does not give a rule; it records what he said happened.
