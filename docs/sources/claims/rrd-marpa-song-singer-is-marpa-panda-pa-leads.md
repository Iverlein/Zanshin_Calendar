# In the song of the three watches the singer is Marpa; Paṇḍa pa (Pen ta pa) leads the assembly, asked what the dream meant and is pleased (RD397, q1115)

In the Life of Marpa by Dpa' bo Gtsug lag phreng ba the song is set at a feast gathering in Nepal in the temple of Rin chen tshul. Marpa has told the company of a dream of Maitrīpa; the teacher Paṇḍa pa (Pen ṇḍa pa), called the lama there, shows how the qualities arise and puts the question: it is fitting that you say what the meaning of that dream is. Marpa answers with the song, in his own voice («my fellow students», «I recall it thus»), and it is he who asks the company not to put it into song. Paṇḍa pa leads the assembly he addresses (དབུ་མཛད, dbu mdzad, the one who heads the row) and is pleased by the offering. The Mgur mtsho and the Mthong ba don yod say the same in the third person: Marpa offered the dream «as realisation» (རྟོགས་པ་ཕུལ་བ, rtogs pa phul ba) to the fellow students with Paṇḍa pa (spelt Pen ta pa, Pe dha ba, Piṇḍa pa in the prints) leading. So the singer is Marpa; Paṇḍa pa is the senior of the gathering and the one who asked, not the singer. Who Paṇḍa pa is beyond «the lama» of Marpa's Nepal stay the prints do not say.
