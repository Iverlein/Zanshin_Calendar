# The red-nosed monkey going east: the dawn of the next day, three days or the last night; the donkey going south: the fourth month or no span (RD374)

RD374. Two more texts give the monkey going east as a death sign. 'Jig rten mgon po (1143-1217), asked for the deepest of the death signs, gives Phag mo gru pa's saying: a white monkey with a red nose ridden to the east in a dream means death after three days, and explains it (the father's white drop and the mother's red drop meeting at the navel wheel) and a method of lengthening life, keeping the lama in the heart (dds.24:p23-24). The 'Bri gung pa's rules for the mantra retreat list the donkey going south and the monkey going east among the signs of one's own death, with no span (dds.23:p201-202). Tilopa's Tika in the Ras chung collection has the same monkey as «the early morning of the next day» (dds.15:p119-121). So the span of the monkey ranges from the dawn of the next day (Ras chung, Tilopa) through three days ('Jig rten mgon po, Phag mo gru pa) to «his last night» (Hevajra rite, dds.19:p53-55); the donkey going south gives the fourth month (Ras chung) or no span. None of the three ties the sign to the part of the night in which it is dreamed.
