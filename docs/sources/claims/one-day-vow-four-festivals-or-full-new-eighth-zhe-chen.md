# A householder keeps the five precepts for good, or the one-day vow on the four great festivals or the full moon, the new moon and the eighth (Zhe chen rgyal tshab)

Zhe chen rgyal tshab 'Gyur med pad+ma rnam rgyal, a Nyingma master of Zhe chen, says in his discourse on the three supports what a householder (ཁྱིམ་པ, khyim pa) who cannot guard a full discipline should do (zgt.1:p445-446, read on the scan): take the five precepts of those who live at home for good; or, if that cannot be done, keep the one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas) of eight branches for a single day and night on the four great festivals (དུས་ཆེན་བཞི, dus chen bzhi) or on any great day "such as the three of every month, the full moon, the new moon and the eighth", «ཟླ་བ་བྱུང་ངོ་ཅོག་གི་ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ».

- Days: the four great festivals of the year; in every month the full moon, the new moon and the eighth, counted as three.
- Practice: the one-day vow of eight branches, as the lesser alternative to the five lay precepts kept for life.
- For whom: householders.
- Benefit, from the sūtras he cites: more merit than serving a continent of arhats for life with the wealth of sixty countries; one pure keeping brings birth among the gods of the Four Great Kings, two among the Thirty-three, and so on up to the sixth heaven of the desire realm with six; no fall into the bad destinies, meeting the buddhas, pure morality and the fulfilment of aspirations.
- Tradition: Nyingma (Zhe chen); the wording agrees with Lo chen Dharma shrI's «ཉ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད» (dpn:i55) and adds the count "three" and the four festivals.

Rank 3. The same three days stand for the same vow, with the fasting practice beside it, in Zhabs dkar's advice to his patrons (zkt:p582, etext).
