# The Kālaprakāśikā's Sanskrit has a month for the fourth watch and twelve days at dawn: no half-month (RD291)

The Sanskrit of the *Kālaprakāśikā* ch. XLII v. 67 was read again on the page image by RD291 (2026-11-10), on the Lawley Press printing (Internet Archive item ZBAx_kalaprakasika-n-p-iyer-tamil-lawley-press, the Devanagari page printed १८९, leaf n476, the leaf as served at full size and viewed whole). The line reads "karoti varṣād varṣārdhāt trimāsān māsatas tathā" and the next, "aruṇodayaveḷāyāṃ dvādaśāhāt phalapradaḥ" (a year, half a year, three months, a month; at the hour of dawn twelve days), v. 68 opening "go-visarge yadi svapnaṃ sumahat tad-ahaḥ-phalam". There is no half-month in the verse: the fourth watch has a month, and the dawn has twelve days, as RD269 read it (kpd:p184-189). Iyer's English, which has "a year, six months, a month, twelve days", drops the third span, as RD269 found. The answer to the question whether the Sanskrit gives a half-month for the fourth watch (q854) is no.

What the Sanskrit of the Kālaprakāśikā therefore shares with the *Svapnohana* is the kind of series (four watches, a dawn span shrinking from a year) and no number except the year; its second span is six months, not eight, its fourth a month, not half a month, its dawn twelve days, not ten.
