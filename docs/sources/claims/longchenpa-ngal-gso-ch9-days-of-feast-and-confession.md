# Longchenpa's lines on the days stand in the root verses of Sems nyid ngal gso ch. 9 and his Shing rta chen po fixes the six days

Longchenpa's lines «ཉ་དང་གནམ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད་ཉི་ཤུ་དགུ། །ཚེས་བཅུ་ཡར་ངོ་མར་ངོའི་ཉིན་དང་མཚན། །བསྙེན་བསྒྲུབ་ཚོགས་མཆོད་བསྐང་བཤགས་འབད་པར་བྱ།» are the root verses of the ninth chapter, «the union of generation and completion», of his «Resting in the Nature of Mind» (Rdzogs pa chen po sems nyid ngal gso), pp. 57-58 of the Dpal brtsegs print's etext (nsg:p57-58), where they follow the twenty-five pledges of body, speech and mind; the print spells the third line «བསྙེད་སྒྲུབ». His own commentary on it, the Shing rta chen po, fixes them as «the special times of fulfilment» (nsc:p271-272): six days in the month, the fifteenth, the new moon («the day empty of the moon», gnam stong), the eighth, the twenty-fifth, the tenth and the twenty-ninth, on which the dākinīs move about outside and the channels and winds gather inside; above all the waxing tenth by day and the waning tenth (the twenty-fifth) by night, when accomplished dākinīs gather at the practitioner's place and every work is accomplished. What is done: the four kinds of approach and accomplishment, the feast offering, fulfilment (bskang) and confession; the gain is that all that has declined during the month is restored and the supreme and common accomplishments come quickly. Dpal sprul's quotation (dpk.1:p380-381) is these lines, from the second on, introduced as «the omniscient Dharma king». The earlier claim's «no work of Klong chen pa was found» is therefore answered.
