# WB's seven classes of the 28 mansions: the works each class is good for, its rain and the birth on it

At the end of the 28 verses WB sorts the mansions into seven classes (p. 328, last nine lines, to p. 329, line 23): the unstable and changing (Puṣya, Hasta, Revatī, Aśvinī), good for offering rites, teaching, vows, petitions to superiors and quick aims of one's own; the very stable (Rohiṇī, Uttaraphalgunī, Uttarāṣāḍhā, Śatabhiṣaj), good for new land, new chiefs, rites of great stability, gardens and enclosure walls; the quick and good (Mṛgaśiras, Citrā, Anurādhā, Uttarabhādrapadā), good for ornaments, games, field work and all quick work; of bad result (Ārdrā, Āśleṣā, Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla), good for destroying, robbing and fierce work; the quick and changing (Punarvasu, Svātī, Śravaṇa, Abhijit, Dhaniṣṭhā), good for far journeys, trade and what is not lasting; the stable and accomplishing (Maghā, Pūrvaphalgunī, Pūrvāṣāḍhā, Pūrvabhādrapadā), good for marriage alliances, buying livestock, empowerment, consecration, foundations and fire offerings; the neutral (Kṛttikā, Viśākhā), where a lawsuit comes out even. Each class also carries its rain and the life and nature of one born on it. The editors count the mansions from Aśvinī as 0, Citrā 13 and Abhijit sharing 21 with Śravaṇa; their round-bracketed note says that most copies bring Bharaṇī in as a fifth of the stable and accomplishing, but that the four named in the results are taken as right. The classes repeat what each mansion's own verse (wb.2:p313b to wb.2:p328) lists, in one place.
