# The thirty: each starting point's board read whole

Each starting point's eight pebbles read as one board (WB vol. 1, pp. 471–472, from Khyung's short commentary), after the eight readings one by one:

| Board | Reading | Remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper three white | good for the first part of life and the forefathers' place | — |
| Upper three black | a rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud) of oneself and the forebears in the first part of life has come or will come | — |
| Lower three white | good for the last part of life | — |
| Lower three black | bad | — |
| Middle two white | no harm by spirits; food, wealth and enjoyments grow | — |
| Middle two black | harm by demons (གདོན, gdon); food and wealth bad | — |
| Upper white, lower black | forebears good, oneself weak; first part of life good, last bad | — |
| Upper black, lower white | forebears in decline, oneself risen; happiness full | — |
| Upper and lower black, middle white | no spirits, no loss of wealth, wishes fulfilled | — |
| Upper and lower white, middle black | loss of food and wealth, harm by spirits, the family dwindles | press down the sri of the prime (དར་སྲི, dar sri), the rite of the loss dagger (གོད་ཕུར, god phur) |
| All white | an unexpected fault: enemies and envy from where none were expected | read the *'Bum*, the Hundred Thousand (འབུམ, 'bum), take empowerment, clear the narrow paths, strive at virtue |
| All black | sky torn, earth shattered, the la (བླ, bla) tree and lake dried, the la carried off, gossip gathers | the secret *Kun 'dus* (གསང་བ་ཀུན་འདུས, gsang ba kun 'dus) as remedy rite (གཏོ, gto), the king spirits' *gzhi mgyogs* (རྒྱལ་པོ་གཞི་མགྱོགས, rgyal po gzhi mgyogs; not identified) as thread-cross (མདོས, mdos) |
| All speckled | good | — |

Black in a given person's place calls for raising that person's aspect: a learned man or a *mi tshan* («མི་ཚན», perhaps a family's head) the luck (ཀླུང་རྟ, klung rta), a *khon* («ཁོན», not settled) a god-castle (གསས་མཁར, gsas mkhar), a minister the life force (སྲོག, srog) with a life-castle (སྲོག་མཁར, srog mkhar), women the power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang), boys the body (ལུས, lus), girls the glory (དཔལ, dpal) (p. 472; questions 203, 204). Above all: "for each thing reckoned, examine with intelligence and lay the verdict" (p. 471). The twenty-fifth point lays its own whole-board readings, speckled and all black (p. 468; question 215).
