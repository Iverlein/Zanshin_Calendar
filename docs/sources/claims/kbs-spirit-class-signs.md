# Klong rdol, a dream's figures by the class of spirit

The article (pp. 75–76) quotes Klong rdol bla ma's verses that sort the dream's frightening or pleasing figures by the class of spirit, the dream being read as the class's magic display: bound body, going under the earth, imprisonment, beheading: harm by the rbad 'dre spirit; riding a donkey naked towards the south, red flowers, a tiger's flesh: a forerunner of an obstacle to life; a white skeleton, a white bird flying, snow mountains: the gods; an old temple of lime or clay, owl and small birds: the demons that cause illness (འགོང་པོ, 'gong po); serpents, frogs, various earth lords, a blue-green girl, a weasel: the nāgas (ཀླུ, klu); rock, trees, riders, soldiers: the btsan; many ashen children, a termite hole: the the'u rang; monks, donkey, monkey, cat, horse, dog: the king spirit (རྒྱལ་པོ, rgyal po). If these speak kindly, give food, laugh and are glad to see, the sign is good; if they show a frightening shape, are wicked or angry, the sign is bad.
