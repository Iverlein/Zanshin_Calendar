# The dream among the signs of the unripe pestilence (ch. 23)

The dream among the signs of the unripe pestilence (མ་སྨིན་རིམས, ma smin rims), ch. 23 of the Instruction Tantra.

The chapter on the hot illness of the pestilence (རིམས, rims) sorts its signs into general and particular, and the general ones into three stages: unripe, grown and emptied (མ་སྨིན, རྒྱས, སྟོངས་པ). The unripe stage shows a long-lasting ཕྲུམ་སེར (phrum ser, sense not settled), aching of head, limbs and joints, a heavy body, a sour taste, many confused dreams (རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང, rmi lam za zi mang), much གཡལ (g.yal), dull ears, a confused mind, aversion to the sun, and pain especially in the evening. The dream marks the first, unripe stage only, as one of its signs, and the passage gives no remedy for it. The chapter is the twenty-third by its colophon, p. 230 of the print (img. 213); the passage is on p. 218 (img. 225). The etext has ལྡུམ་སེར and ཡ་སྨིན་རྒྱུས where the print has ཕྲུམ་སེར and མ་སྨིན་རྒྱས (st:p218).

What is done for the sick dreamer (RD229, 2026-11-09; read by machine with eye checks). For the unripe stage of the pestilence the treatment (`st:p222-223`) is the same ripening as for the unripe fever: the four manu decoction, or the five sle tres decoction, with food and conduct kept between cool and warm. The confusion (ཟ་ཟི, za zi) is not treated by itself; the later stages add sweat-bringing decoctions and burnt powders by the place of the illness, and the text names the junction between the unripe and the emptied stage as the time to be careful.
