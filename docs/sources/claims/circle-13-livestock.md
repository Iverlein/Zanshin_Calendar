# The circles: the thirteenth, the wild yak, for livestock

The thirteenth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the great wild yak, is read for horses, sheep and livestock. Its starting point: the ox of the mother element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points: whether the *gyi ling* horse's saddle and bridle are empty; whether the hand-rope of the *yo ma* is cut; whether the great *zhol* yak's tail-hair has fallen («བྱིད»); whether the divine female yak has rolled down a cliff; whether the tethering-rope of the fortune ewe is cut. In order the life force, body, power, luck and mewa, five, reckon rise and decline; these five are turned from the ox of the mother element of the one reckoned: the reckoning of horses, cattle and livestock, settled by five.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the *gyi ling*'s saddle and bridle not empty: good; horses and donkeys strong and useful; though weak in bone, good in stamina; good horses come in the valley below. | empty: great loss of horses; a horse like a lion now becomes worse than a village donkey. | horse fortune declined: the horse dhāraṇī and the rite of the eight *gnod sbyin*. |
| Body | the *yo ma*'s hand-rope not cut: horses and livestock increase greatly, an inexhaustible treasure; a sign of great help from the young. | cut: no increase; foals are not conceived, or conceived are lost. | press down the horse sri (རྟ་སྲི, rta sri), send a livestock ransom (གླུད, glud), strive at offering to the *Smug mo*. |
| Power | the *zhol* yak's tail-hair not shed: divine yaks come; no loss of large livestock; the son better than the father; dappled animals with marks on their hair, fortune gathered in them, great benefit. | shed: bad in the place of large livestock; the marked kinds scattered abroad, fortune declines; fault, benefit hard to come by. | recite the wealth dhāraṇī and press down the *god sri*. |
| Luck | the divine female yak not rolled down the cliff: good; large livestock increase greatly; no losses; females conceive well. | rolled down: among the female livestock no increase, or if any, no benefit; gossip pierces; a sign of loss where the females conceive. | take the *dri zan* of the livestock; the pit and the laying-down rite and the many ransom rites against gossip. |
| Mewa | the fortune ewe's tether not cut: good for small livestock, above all the fortune ewe: the fortune pen cannot hold the increase; small livestock bigger than others, or a fine one with marks on its hair bearing fortune: supremely good. | cut: bad for small livestock and above all the fortune ewe. | build the fortune pen and close the mouth of fortune; press down the *god sri* and send the small ransom of blood-bearing animals: good. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
