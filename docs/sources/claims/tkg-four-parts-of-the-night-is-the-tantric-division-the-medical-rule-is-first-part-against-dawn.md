# Tsong kha pa's four parts of the night (the fourth taken) are the tantric division; the medical dream rules read (Four Tantras, Gtsang sman, Siddhasāra) judge by the first part against the dawn (RD394, q964)

Tsong kha pa's mandala-rite text divides the night in four parts for the disciples' dreams: «the night made into four parts, the first three are not certain, so the fourth is taken» (རྨི་ལམ་ཡང་མཚན་ལ་ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད་པས། ཆ་བཞི་པ་ལ་བྱ), and gives under it the Mañjuśrī root tantra's verse of the four watches (phlegm, bile, wind, the fourth true); the first words of the line were read again on the scan (img. 386, line 5, enlarged four times) and agree with the machine reading, the rest of the verse is the machine's (tkg:i385-388). The Tibetan medical dream rules read in the pool do not use this division. The Four Tantras' ch. 7 rule has two parts only: a dream of the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod) is forgotten and has no fruit, one seen at dawn and clear gives fruit (gt:i42); its commentators read the first part as up to midnight, as dusk with midnight, or leave it unbounded, and none names a fourth watch or the humours of the verse (see gt-night-rule-commentaries-blue-beryl-zur-mkhar-ba). Gtsang sman's physician's dream is the one dream at dawn before the messenger comes, with no part of the night counted (gsm:p263-264); and the Siddhasāra's dream signs of the sick, as recorded in the pool, give no part of the night at all (sds:p75-77). The nearest three-part divisions are not medical and not the four-part one: Klong rdol bla ma's thirds of dusk, midnight and dawn (kbs-three-thirds-of-the-night). So the four-part, humour-by-watch division is the tantric one, in Tsong kha pa's rite and the Sakya and Drukpa manuals; the medical texts judge by the first part of the night against the dawn. Whether any other Tibetan medical text uses the four watches was not found in this reading.
