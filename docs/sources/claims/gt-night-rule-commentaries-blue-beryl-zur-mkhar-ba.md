# The Four Tantras' night rule in the Blue Beryl, Zur mkhar ba and Skyem pa Tshe dbang: still no part of the night between midnight and dawn; the source of the three-thirds line

Three more commentaries of the Four Tantras' tradition were opened as BDRC etexts (2026-11-10, RD299) and what each says of the night rule's «མཚན་སྟོད» (mtshan stod, the first part of the night) is told here in our words; the Tibetan wording is not copied. None of them gives the stretch from midnight to dawn a part of its own.

**The Blue Beryl (བཻ་ཌཱུརྱ་སྔོན་པོ, Vaidūrya sngon po), part 2, the commentary on the Explanatory Tantra** (BDRC etext I1PD411, p. 209 of the etext, printed page 181). On the dream passage the window read (p. 209) comments on the Moonbeams' (Zla ba'i 'od zer) verse «a dream of the first part of the night: its fruit is far, what is seen is small» and glosses it as Byang pa and the Moonbeams text do: a dream dreamt up to midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད, nam phyed yan chad) has its fruit far off and is small and weak. The dream seen at daybreak with sleep not taken again and no unfit talk between gives a great fruit that day. So the Blue Beryl marks the first part as the half of the night up to midnight, and the second half is only the time of the day-break dream.

**The Moonbeams on the Heart of Medicine** (Zla ba'i 'od zer, Kha che Zla ba mngon dga' on the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya, BDRC etexts MW23703_4312 in the Derge Tengyur and MW1PD95844_3549 in the Peking-type edition): the same sentence on «up to midnight» stands in the Moonbeams itself, so the line reaches Byang pa, the Blue Beryl and Zur mkhar ba from there. The Moonbeams comments on a verse of the Indian text, not on the Four Tantras' lines.

**Zur mkhar ba Blo gros rgyal po, The Words of the Forefather (Mes po'i zhal lung, rgyud bzhi'i 'grel ba)** (BDRC etext I8LS19191; etext pp. 352 and 356). At p. 352 he gives the Moonbeams' gloss as Byang pa does (the first part: up to midnight; the day-break dream seen with sleep unbroken: a great fruit that very day). At p. 356, on the Four Tantras' own two lines, he says otherwise: any of the six kinds of dream seen in the first part of the night, which he equates with dusk (སྲོད, srod), is cut by other sleep and becomes forgotten, so neither a good nor a bad fruit comes; midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) is of a kind with it; a dream at the close of the dawn (ཐོ་རངས་ཀྱི་མཇུག, tho rangs kyi mjug) with sleep not again breaking in and the dream clear in mind surely gives its fruit. The spelling he gives for «forgotten» is «བརྗེད་ངས» (brjed ngas). His reading of the Four Tantras' «མཚན་སྟོད» is therefore Khro ru's: dusk with midnight both are without fruit; his reading of the Moonbeams' is Byang pa's. He sets the two side by side without reconciling them.

**Skyem pa Tshe dbang, The Sun on the Words of the Explanatory Tantra (Bshad rgyud rnam bshad tshig don nyi ma)** (BDRC etext I4711, etext p. 190, printed page 168): on the two lines he says the dream of midnight and the first part of the night (ནམ་ཕྱེད་མཚན་སྟོད, nam phyed mtshan stod) is forgotten, with forgetfulness, not remembered, and so has no fruit; one seen at dawn, clear and unforgotten, gives the fruit. He joins midnight to the first part, as Khro ru and Zur mkhar ba do; the stretch from midnight to dawn is again not named, only the dawn.

**The source of «gsum cha phyis 'byung gsal byed yin» (q848).** The line stands in the verse of Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang (1719–1794) on how to judge dreams, in the ACIP etext «Rmi ltas sna tshogs brtag thabs» (BDRC MW1ALS06558E) and in the Chinese-published etext of his «Rmi lam gyi ltas la brtag pa byed tshul» (MW20242_55798C); Sha bo Tshe ring's «Mo dpe dang sa dpyad rmi lam brtag thabs» (MW21880) carries it too. The verse divides the night into three thirds by the watches (མཚན་མོའི་ཐུན་གསུམ, mtshan mo'i thun gsum): the dusk third (སྲོད, srod) is the habit-traces (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) of what went before, the midnight third (གུང, gung) the play of demons (བདུད་སྲིན, bdud srin), and the dawn third (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) is the one in which what is to come shows clear. Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal («Bod lugs gso rig gi sems khams rig pa», MW8LS18598, search snippet only; the etext is not open) quotes the dawn line from this verse and puts beside it the Four Tantras' two lines as a second authority, with «བརྗེད་ངེས» in the second. So the line is a Nyingma-side lama's verse of the 18th century, not a Four Tantras commentator's, and the three-thirds rule it states is that of LGD and RST (night-watches-st-rule-against-thirds-and-svapnohana), not of the Four Tantras.

**A further line of the same kind.** Karma Chags med's Ri chos mtshams kyi zhal gdams (MW3CN4735, MW23259, MW1AC387) has a rule of its own: the dusk dream (nam srod) is habit-traces and not certain, the midnight dream (nam phyed) the play of gods and demons and not certain, the dawn dream (tho rangs) with fruit (the Serthang etext reads «lod pa», the other two «yod pa»). This is a Kagyu retreat manual, not a commentary on the Four Tantras; it is noted as the nearest other three-part rule.

**What the commentaries together give.** Up to midnight (the Moonbeams, Byang pa, the Blue Beryl, Zur mkhar ba on the Moonbeams, the 'Bum nag gsal sgron); dusk with midnight, both without fruit (Khro ru, Zur mkhar ba on the Four Tantras' lines, Skyem pa Tshe dbang); no bound (Rin spungs pa, the Drikung). Nobody names the stretch from midnight to dawn; the only time after midnight that is named is the dawn itself.
