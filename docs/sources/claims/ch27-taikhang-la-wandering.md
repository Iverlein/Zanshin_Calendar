# Ch. 27: the la's wandering carved on the Taikhang as on the other prints

In the la's wandering by the year (WB vol. 2, p. 16), the Taikhang reproduction of a Lhasa print (I5878 img. 31, block folio 21, lines 4–5, crops enlarged about 1.1 times) carves the snake year's la going north and the horse year's la going north, the pig year's south, as the 1996 print, the Gser thang reprint, the Zhol print and the Sakya etext have it; Yigdzin-1 reads the Zhol print (img. 649) and the Taikhang line for line the same, down to a rare «ཕྱཽགས» in the horse verse. A fifth print therefore does not carry the east and west of the trine: the repeated north and south are not a slip of one print's typesetting or of the reprint.

The rat year's «བླ་ལྷོར་འཁྱམས» is the one word of the four verses that two readers split on: Yigdzin-1 reads «བླ་ལྟོར» on the Taikhang and «བླ་ལྷོར» on the Zhol print, and agy on one crop gave «ལྷོར» with «ལྟོར» as its alternative; the eye at 3 times could not tell the head of the stack. Only «ལྷོར» (south) has a meaning, and it matches the pig's «ལྷོ་ཡི».

So question 363 is narrowed once more: the pairs are carved so on the block as well; whether the tradition means north for both is still a question for a reader of the tradition.
