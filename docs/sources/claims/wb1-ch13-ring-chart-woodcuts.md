# Ch. 13 pp. 136-141: the figures of the twelve ring-chart woodcuts

The twelve ring charts of pp. 136-141 each hold a woodcut of a robed figure in a landscape, and the figure has an animal's head in at least five of them: a hare (p. 136 lower), a long-snouted head (p. 138 lower and p. 140 lower), a bird with a beak (p. 139 lower) and a horned ox head (p. 141 lower). The other figures show a human or shaggy face. Several woodcuts also show a second figure or an animal beside the main one (a spotted beast on p. 137 lower, lion-like beasts on p. 140 upper, a figure holding a round object on p. 140 lower, bowls of offerings on p. 141 upper). Each woodcut carries one to three lines of small captions inside its frame; they could not be settled at 4x. The assignment of the figures to the twelve animals of the cycle, and the order of the charts, is not established: only the animal-headed ones are certain, and the long-snouted head may be a horse or a sheep. The twelve woodcuts are the reprint's own; the Zhol print's leaves holding the ring charts were not found (RD199).
