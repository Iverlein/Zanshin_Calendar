# The trigram of death: the spirit that carried the dead off, the eating demon's object, the rite

Terms as named below: the trigrams *li*, *dwa*, *zin*, *kham* (ལི, དྭ, ཟིན, ཁམ), the fierce spirits (བཙན, *btsan*), the water spirits, the lu (ཀླུ, *klu*), the lu-*bsen* (ཀླུ་བསེན, *klu bsen*), the lu-*btsan* (ཀླུ་བཙན, *klu btsan*), the lake women (མཚོ་སྨན, *mtsho sman*), the ogres (སྲིན, *srin*), the *gnyan* spirits (གཉན, *gnyan*), the harmful spirit of the dead (སྲི, *sri*), the *ma mo* goddesses (མ་མོ, *ma mo*), the mother-demon king's thread-cross (མ་བདུད་རྒྱལ་མདོས, *ma bdud rgyal*), the dhāraṇī ablution (གཟུངས་ཁྲུས, *gzungs*), the *Hundred Prostrations of Spang skong* (སྤང་སྐོང་ཕྱག་བརྒྱ་པ, *Spang skong phyag brgya pa*), the *Five Great Hundred Thousands* (འབུམ་ཆེན་སྡེ་ལྔ, *'Bum chen sde lnga*).

The third examination of the dead (WB vol. 2, pp. 60–62) by the trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) of death: for each of the eight, the spirit that carried the dead off, where the demon cut the life, what is at risk, where the eating demon (ཟ་འདྲེ, za 'dre) hides, where the executioner (གཤེད, gshed) goes, and the rite. Mipham's note (p. 503) names the input as the trigram of the year of death.

| Trigram | Carried off by | Cut in | Hides in | Goes to | Rite, laid on the corpse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Li | a *btsan* demon and a king spirit (its owner has a large turquoise) | — | copper or an earthen pot, a churn, a sack, a head, a woman's things, a red pot, turquoise, a felt hat; south of the house | a great man of the Bon, one lame or lacking a sense, a red guard dog; north to west; or the maternal uncle | pig skull and water tips |
| Khon | earth lords; a quarrel, a black thing brought in | — | a black sack: carried to a crossroads | the old and lean; east to north; a household of five with a red guard dog; half a month | hare skull and kinds of wood |
| Dwa | a *btsan* king | east | armour, helmet, knife | a small child, one like its heart; south to east; a female-shaped frog | earth lords' heart-crushing: a *btsan* rite |
| Khen | a life-king | south-east (a demon) | — | the old; a red guard dog or cattle; north-west; the maternal uncle; the executioner from the south to east or south-east | if an old mother died: block the sky door, press the *sri*, recite rite and sūtra |
| Kham | *klu bsen* and *mtsho sman* | south-west (a demon) | copper or a water jar, or beer, about thirteen days | a small child; a woman with one son or one daughter | *gzungs* ablution for the grave threshold |
| Gin | the Pe har king | south-east | blue horses and cattle, ten days | where a black ox is; the paternal kin's male whose line is cut; east to north | — |
| Zin | planet and wolf; *klu*, *srin*, *btsan* | west (a demon) | a hide or a storeroom | paternal kin, a pregnant woman, brother and sister, a small daughter; west to south-west | heart's tip in the corpse's mouth, heart-crushing rite, *ma bdud rgyal* thread-cross, *Spang skong phyag brgya pa* |
| Zon | a male king spirit | north-east | clothes, silk, goat meat, sheepskin, leftover food, twenty-six days | an old paternal uncle, a pregnant woman, a priest or man of power, a woman with matted hair, one with a pack yak or speckled cattle; north-west to north-east | goat skull and a wooden peg; Uṣṇīṣa white and black twenty thousand times, *ma mo* blood thread-cross, earth lords' turbulence ritual |

Others hold (p. 62) a heart-crushing (སྙིང་འགྲམས, snying 'grams) on four trigrams, each with its Bon heart-joining: *dwa* of meaning (earth lords), *zin* of memory (*gnyan*), *li* of existence (*klu btsan*), *kham* of cutting off (the gods' torma body); for all, the *'Bum chen sde lnga*.
