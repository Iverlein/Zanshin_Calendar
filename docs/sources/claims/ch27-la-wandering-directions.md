# Ch. 27: the la's wandering by the year, three prints agree

Whose la wanders, read again on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 22, l. 23-26 of the page), on the Zhol print (WBZ I1KG1710 img. 649, lines cut and enlarged) and in the Sakya print's etext (VEIE0OPI0BA3A0E0_I1KG12740, p. 33, OCR): the three prints agree. The rat year's la wanders south (བླ་ལྷོར་འཁྱམས), the snake year's north (བླ་ནི་བྱང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འཁྱམས), the horse year's north (the same words), the pig year's south (བླ་ནི་ལྷོ་ཡི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འཁྱམས). The two pairs sharing a direction are therefore not a slip of the reprint or of one print.

What a count of the groups shows, as our own check and not a text's word: the rat and the horse stand at the north and south of the twelve, and the la of each goes to the opposite side; the snake (a bird-ox-snake year, west) and the pig (a pig-hare-sheep year, east) are the two whose printed direction is not the opposite of their group's side (that would be east for the snake, west for the pig). No text read so far states the direction of the snake's or the pig's la otherwise; the notes (pp. 533-538) are silent on the point; the Bhutan digest, the *G.ya' sel* and the Dpal bsdus manual have no parallel verse in their etexts. The reading stays as printed; the doubt stays a question for a reader of the tradition.
