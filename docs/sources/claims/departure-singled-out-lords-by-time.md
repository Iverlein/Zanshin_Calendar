# The departure's singled-out lords are the year's, month's, day's and hour's: seats and courses shunned, a remedy when met

Of the departure's singled-out lords (WB vol. 1, p. 375: the *the se*, the *the khyim*, the *bya ra (ba)*, the *se byi*, the *hal khyi*, the *bstan ma*, the gold and turquoise treasuries) only the Hal-dog is a lord of the date. By WB's own lists of the earth lords of time (vol. 2, p. 96, and the invocation of p. 370):

- the year's seventeen kings and ministers number the Thé-se 1, the Thé-khyim 2, the Se-byi 7, the watchman (བྱ་ར, bya ra) 10 and the Hal-dog 17;
- the month's eighteen number Tenma (བསྟན་མ, bstan ma) 10 and the Hal-dog 17;
- the day's god is the Golden Treasure the yellow and the hour's the Turquoise Treasure the blue; the invocation of p. 370 names the Thé-se first among the year's lords and the two treasuries as the day's and the hour's.

So the Thé-se, the Thé-khyim, the Se-byi and the watchman belong to the year's list; Tenma to the month's; the Hal-dog to the year's and the month's lists both and also to the date's own run (pp. 362–363), so it alone is a lord of the date as well; and the two treasuries are the day's and the hour's gods. None of these lines says which of the singled-out lords falls on which day; they are lords of seats and courses, not of days.

On bar or remedy, the verse itself divides the two: the seats and the directions of the course of the earth lords are shunned («ས་བདག་གནས་དང་རྒྱུ་ཕྱོགས་འཛེམ»), and when they are met the screening remedy is applied to each («ཕྲད་ན་སྒྲིབ་བཅོས་སོ་སོར་སྦྱར»). The commentaries add nothing that grades the six: the *Garland of Blue Utpalas* repeats the verse word for word (ut:p234) and, after it, calls the Hal-dog the earth lord's sky dog, whose face is to be shunned; the supplement to the *Moonbeams* has the verse in its list of what to avoid in sending a bride (kd.3:p644); Mipham's note (wb.2:p531) says that in the direction of the going the bad earth lords' seats and courses are not to be sent into with the people's sixty-year horses, and that for the bride the grudge-binding lords, the Thé-se, Thé-khyim, Hal-dog and the rest, are examined "as the text has them". The Bhutan collection's digest (etext only, I1KG11905 p. 228) lists, for the direction to be avoided, the Se-ba, Se-shar and others as certainly to be shunned and, from the month's round, the Hal-dog and Tenma among the lords whose places are avoided; it too grades none. Answer in short: a lord of the date, the year, the month or the hour is told by the lists above; every one is a bar on its seat and course, and a remedy only when it has been met; no commentary says that a day on which one stands is barred.

State: the lists of pp. 96 and 370 are read on the scan (see their passages); the commentaries are read from BDRC's etext only.
