# RD362: Mipham names no time or day for reading a dream sign; sources only for the rites and the sūtra's readings

RD362's answer for the pool. What Mipham's writings read for RD362 say of a time, a day or a source for a dream sign:

Time. None for reading a dream: in all that was read no watch of the night, no weekday and no lunar day is given for what a dream sign means or when it counts. Times occur only for the practice of asking for a dream: at bedtime (mph.128:i189-192, mph.25:i374-376), the first to the eighth of the month, the eighth or the fourteenth (mph.128:i189-192), and "by night" for the Sukhāvatī practice (mph.104:i236-237); the colophon of the Skul byed ma rite dates its writing (1874), not a dream (mph.25:i374-376). The notes on the White Beryl give none (wb.2:p474, wb.2:p535-537).

Source. For the rites, named sources: the Realization of the Good Jewel (Nor bu bzang po'i rtogs pa), the Tantra of Mañjuśrī the hero, the one accomplishment, the Nyang treasure Khros nag, the Tantra of the Great Black One, "treasures and others" (mph.128:i189-192, mph.25:i374-376). For dream images with readings, the Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka (Snying rje pad ma dkar po'i mdo), named in the compilation, whose dreams are read by the Buddha, "one skilled in dream signs" or a sign-man (mph.101:i42-43, mph.101:i44-45, mph.101:i46, mph.101:i220-221, mph.101:i233).

So Mipham gives no list of dream signs and no rule of day or hour: what he supplies is the omen reading of the sūtra's narrative dreams and the methods of asking for a dream.
