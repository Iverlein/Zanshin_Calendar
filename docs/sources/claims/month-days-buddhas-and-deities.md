# The buddhas and deities of the month's days, after Karma chags med

Karma chags med's retreat manual (RCZ, pp. 286–288, read on the scan) gives in verse who comes or roams on which day of every month, as the times when practice multiplies. It is the fullest written list found for what the printed calendars put beside the days:

| Day | Who | What the verse says of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8th | the Medicine Buddha (སྨན་བླ, sman bla) comes; White Tārā (སྒྲོལ་དཀར, sgrol dkar), Tshe ring ma (ཚེ་རིང་མ) and Kurukullā guard the field | with the 23rd, the servants of the Four Great Kings (རྒྱལ་ཆེན་སྡེ་བཞི, rgyal chen sde bzhi) roam and write down virtue and evil |
| 10th and 25th (the tenth of each half) | Padmasambhava (པད་འབྱུང, pad 'byung), the wrathful Sow (ཕག་ཁྲོས, phag khros), Cakrasaṃvara (བདེ་མཆོག, bde mchog), Maning (མ་ནིང, ma ning), all heroes and ḍākinīs | at sunrise the one of Oḍḍiyāna comes |
| 14th and 29th | the sons of the Four Great Kings roam | — |
| 15th | Amitābha (འོད་དཔག་མེད, 'od dpag med) | the Four Great Kings and Indra roam themselves |
| 9th, 19th, 29th ("the three nines") | all gods and demons roam | — |
| 9th and 11th, 24th and 26th | Palden Lhamo (དཔལ་ལྡན་ལྷ་མོ, dpal ldan lha mo) and her retinue roam | the note in the verse gives the four dates |
| 19th, 28th | anniversaries of masters of the author's own lineage | on the 28th all of Yama's kind roam |
| 29th and the new moon | all the Protector's (མགོན་པོ, mgon po) kind roam | one offering then pleases them more than a year's practice |
| new moon (གནམ་གང, gnam gang) | Śākyamuni with the thousand buddhas of the Good Aeon | the Four Great Kings and Indra roam themselves |

On the 8th and 23rd, the 14th and 29th, the full and the new moon whatever virtue or evil is done is a hundred thousandfold (འབུམ་འགྱུར, 'bum 'gyur), and the same number is given for the three days when "the three teachers come", the full moon, the new moon and the eighth. The verse names its authorities only by name: a sūtra it calls *Establishing the World* for the Four Great Kings, "the Lord and Srong btsan" for the three teachers, Mtsho skyes rdo rje and the one of Oḍḍiyāna for the protectors' days; none of these was traced further.

The list is for anyone who has "entered the Dharma's door", set against the householder's trade; it names no fast or vow. Its author is a Karma Kagyu master of the seventeenth century, so the list is neither a modern calendar's convention nor peculiar to the Nyingma; the 19th and 28th are his lineage's own days. A Nyingma text with the same list was not found in this reading. The 25th stands here only as "the tenth of the waning half", with the same deities as the 10th, and not as a day of the ḍākinīs alone; the 29th is the protectors' day, shared with the new moon.
