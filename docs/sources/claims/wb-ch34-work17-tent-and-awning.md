# Work 17 «སྦྲ་ཕྱར»: the dictionaries give the hair tent and the awning cloth; «ཕྱ་ཐེར» has no entry

The dictionaries (Steinert's 64, searched 2026-11-01) give both words of work 17 a sense that fits sewing (འཚེམ, 'tshem). The tent (སྦྲ, sbra) is the black tent of yak hair (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives-Waldo; the Mahavyutpatti's paṭakuṭī, a tent); Tshig mdzod chen mo has a felt or hair stuff and the nomad's tent (སྦྲ་གུར, sbra gur) under the same headword. The cloth (ཕྱར་བ, phyar ba) is, in Tshig mdzod chen mo and Ives-Waldo, a covering made of hair, felt, cloth or rushes (examples «རྩིད་ཕྱར», «བལ་ཕྱར», «རས་ཕྱར»), a ground sheet or awning spread over grain; the Mahavyutpatti has it for kutapa; the dag yig gsar bsgrigs says it may be written short as «ཕྱར». So «སྦྲ་ཕྱར» of WB's list reads best as the tent and the awning cloth, two things of the sewer's trade, and KP's «སྦྲ་དང་ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ་པ» puts «ཕྱ་ཐེར» where the list has «ཕྱར». «ཕྱ་ཐེར» itself has no headword in the 64 dictionaries (prefix search «phya th»); it is taken as a form of the same word only by its place, not by a gloss. On the subjoined ra the dictionaries say only that the tent's spelling is «སྦྲ»; «སྦ» has no sense here; the printer's stack on p. 378 stays as the earlier claim left it. Dictionaries only: the scan of p. 378 was not read again.
