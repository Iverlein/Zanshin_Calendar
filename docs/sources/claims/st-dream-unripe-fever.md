# The dream among the signs of the unripe fever (ch. 15)

The dream among the signs of the unripe fever (མ་སྨིན་ཚ་བ, ma smin tsha ba), ch. 15 of the Instruction Tantra.

The unripe fever is the fever not yet matured, in the chapter on treating it. Its general signs are a thin, moving, quick pulse, red-yellow, thin and turbid urine, a pale coat on the tongue with red wind-pimples, unequal body heat that is high in the evening, aching of head, calves and joints, chills and shivering, aversion to the fire and the sun, and many confused dreams (རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང, rmi lam za zi mang) with more of the mind's doing. These signs come with every unripe fever. The dream is one of the signs of the illness and is not read for anything else; the passage gives no remedy for it, the treatment is in the chapter's later part (not read here). The chapter is the fifteenth by its colophon, p. 187 of the print (img. 256); the passage is on p. 183 (img. 260). The word za zi is left as printed; it is a word of turmoil, sense taken from the context (st:p183).

What is done for the sick dreamer (RD229, 2026-11-09; the passage read by machine with eye checks). The chapter's treatment (`st:p185-186`) is of the whole unripe fever and none is aimed at the dream: the fever is ripened, not cooled or purged, with the warm, mild decoction of the four manu (མ་ནུ་བཞི, ma nu bzhi), or of the three fruits with sle tres (སླེ་ཏྲེས); light, warm, easily digested food and boiled water; warm clothes, a seat between sun and shade; sweating for three days and then washing, and cooling powders after. Avoided: hard exertion, the fire, sleep by day, meat and beer, cold and damp, sour milk, cold water, and at this stage purging, emetics, sweating and cooling decoctions. Treated too warm, the illness gains strength; treated too cool, it is not ripened; the text calls for the middle way. The dream is not named again in the treatment, so no remedy for the dream as such is found.
