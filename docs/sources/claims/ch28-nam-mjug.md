# The tail of the year (nam mjug): the end of autumn

WB vol. 2, pp. 28 and 30–31: «ནམ་མཇུག», "the tail of the year", is the end of autumn: Ives and Waldo's dictionary gives "end of autumn" and the *Bod yig tshig gter* sends it to «སྟོན་འཇུག». WB itself sets it 21 days after the autumn equinox (p. 28, "the tail of the year is cut"), gives it fifteen days when the winter hoarfrost has been dull five days and the autumn's two, and five days when the spring and autumn hoarfrosts are both dull (p. 30); it is "long" when the hoarfrost is dulled by cloud, snow-rain, warmth and no wind, "short" in the reverse, and snow-rain not falling on the 26th of the last autumn month shortens it (p. 31).
