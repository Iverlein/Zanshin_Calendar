# The Dbyangs 'char's own dream verses are the lines the Mchog dga' comments on, and they omit Saturn, Rahu and Ketu and the number six

The root text of the Dbyangs 'char (དབྱངས་འཆར), in the third chapter of the Derge Tengyur etext (pp. 121-122), is the text the Mchog dga' (མཆོག་དགའ, Mchog dga') explains in its twelfth result, word for word with the comment between: the instant or nine-part junction's lord and whether it is present; the Sun (king, fire, blood, red), the Moon (women, flowers, jewels, white), Mars (coral, blood, flying), Mercury (teaching and hearing the Dharma), Jupiter (lines, reading, learning, horses, children at play, books, gods, kin), Venus (crossing water, dancing, play and strife); a planet not the house's lord, mixed or unknown dreams; the lord's enemies, a bad dream; the sun and moon seven apart for a clear dream; the parts of thirty for the part of the night. The verses give no dream to Saturn, Rahu or Ketu, which the Mchog dga' says too (the break in the root text), and have ten, fifteen and thirty parts (a third, midnight, dawn) without the six and the early night, which are the commentator's. «Mdzes dga' ma» in the Mchog dga' is the Beautiful lady whom the verses address. The dream lines are therefore the tantra's, in the Tengyur translation by Paṇḍita Jayānanda and Dgos 'dod thams cad 'byung ba, not a Phug pa invention; what is Phug pa's is the commentary on the five places and the thirty parts and the choice between the tattāla and the nine-part. Etext only.
