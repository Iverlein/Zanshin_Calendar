# The board of the birth's hundred pebbles

The hundred pebbles of the birth (WB vol. 1, p. 261) are the five
unchanging lucks, top to bottom, fought by the four aspects, left to
right, each split into own, mother, son, friend and enemy: a board of five
rows and twenty columns. The rows: the father's year (for a daughter the
mother's year), the mother's progressed sign at the birth, the trigram
and the mewa fallen at the birth, and the birth month. The columns: the
life force, body, power and luck blocks, each own, mother, son, friend,
enemy, whose elements are the aspect's own element and its four kin.

A cell holds one pebble, by Mipham's rule (WB vol. 2, p. 475): white
where the row's element (the same aspect's element of that luck; a trigram
or mewa has one) is the mother or friend of the column's element, black
where it is the son or enemy, and for the same element black for fire,
iron and wood, white for earth and water.

Under each column the deciding pebble (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod), the greater
of its five; the twenty deciding pebbles take the hundred's readings of
WB pp. 329–331, each of the twenty white or black. A white enemy pebble is
the bad one: KD's worked sheet draws the enemy's deciding pebble by its
meaning, black where the count is white.

Kun dga' rig 'dzin's worked sheet (KD vol. 3, pp. 716–718,
[corpus](../corpus/kd/v3-sheets.md#kd-3-p718)) lays a wood-mouse birth this
way, and every one of its hundred cells follows the rule from p. 717's
five lucks: a test vector. WB's general verdict (p. 331) reads where white
and black lie on this board: the son columns, the rows above, in the
middle and below, the corners, the rim and the centre. Answers open
question 184.
