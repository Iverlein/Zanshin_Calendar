# The small grave by mewa, trigram and the child's age

Where a dead child is laid is read by the mewa and the trigram of the place (WB vol. 2, p. 84): on one-white, no rebirth; two-black, the line cut; three-blue, the next child with dropsy; four-green, *shu 'bras* (ཤུ་འབྲས); five-yellow, plague; seven-red, the next dies by the blade; nine-red, the next is a girl; on six-white and eight-white the child is hidden. By the trigram of the place: life-living gives long life to the next, glory-raising increases sons, "send it on those two"; body cut, no rebirth; demon-cutting, the next dies soon. A child's age sets the rule (p. 84): to three years an illness corpse hidden out of sight; four and five years, four or five fathoms away; six and seven, an arrow's shot; eight to ten, *lha sman* (ལྷ་སྨན) (the notes, p. 505, read on the scan 2026-10-23: from eight to ten *mtshun lha sman* (མཚུན་ལྷ་སྨན), not a small grave). Six kinds of dead infant by age and their places are in the corpus passage (open question 115). The rites of pressing down and cutting are given in full there.
