# Wangkhyi's day-time lines on the Zhol and Taikhang prints are the 1996 print's, and say nothing of day against hour

The Zhol print (I1KG1710 img. 925) and the Taikhang reproduction (I5878 img. 307) carve Wangkhyi's paragraph of p. 210 as the 1996 print and the Gser thang reprint do. The ox's line keeps the full «གླང་གི་ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» ("on the ox's day, at its time", «ལྷོར་བལྟ་དེ་རུ་ཁ་མི་བསྟན», one does not face the south) and the three others have the short «X་གི་ཉི་དུས» (sheep north, dragon west, dog east). No print has a word that separates the day from the hour or says that the two must fall together: the same four animals, the same words. So the prints do not decide whether the ox day and the ox hour must coincide (q445). What they show is that «ཉི་དུས» is the short form of the long one in the same paragraph, and that WB's wording «གནས་པའི་ཉི་དུས་འཛིན» treats it as one thing, the place where the lord stays, held as a whole. The machine's «བྱི་ཡི» for the dog's luck-base and day-time is the dog's («ཁྱི»), read on both crops.
