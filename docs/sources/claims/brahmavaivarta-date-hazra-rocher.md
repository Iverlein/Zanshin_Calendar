# Published dates of the Brahmavaivarta: first about the 8th century, present form 10th to 16th; the dream chapter not dated by itself (RD291)

Published dates of the Brahmavaivarta and of its dream chapter, RD291 (2026-11-10), read in the text layers of Hazra, *Studies in the Puranic Records on Hindu Rites and Customs* (1940; the section on the Brahmavaivarta, pp. 166–167) and Rocher, *The Purāṇas* (1986, pp. 161–164), both Internet Archive items (facts only, in our words).

- **Hazra (1940)**: the Purāṇa is one of the latest of the extant Purāṇas. Following Jogesh Chandra Roy (Bharatavarsa, Asadha 1337 of the Bengali era, pp. 94–104), it was first composed most probably in the 8th century, altered from about the 10th by Bengal writers and recast into its present form in the 16th. Of about 1500 lines that the digests (Smṛticandrikā, Caturvargacintāmaṇi, Kālanirṇaya, Smṛtitattva and others) quote from "a Brahmavaivarta", only about 30 are found in the printed text, in the *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa* chs. 8 and 26 (the Janmāṣṭamī and Ekādaśī vows), so that the present contents were not known to the writers of the 16th century; a Brahmavaivarta of before 700 probably existed and is lost. The dream chapter (IV 73 in Jivananda's numbering) is not among the chapters Hazra names.
- **Rocher (1986)**: the Purāṇa has four parts, the *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa* the largest (131 or 133 chapters), and is held a late Purāṇa; the 15th and the 16th century have both been proposed for its present form, from an older version of the 10th century; Rawal (1972) places it between 1400 and 1600; it is a work of Bengal. A South Indian *Brahmakaivarta* is a separate or related text.
- **What this gives for the dream chapter**: no source open to RD291 dates the chapter on its own; it stands in the *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa*, whose present form scholars put between the 10th and the 16th century (Hazra: the *chapters* other than 8 and 26 are not traceable before then). The *Svapnohana* is datable to about 1200 at the latest (RD272). So the Purāṇic chapter in the form we have may be later than the Tibetan translation, and then the Tibetan series cannot come from this text as printed but from an older Sanskrit original of the same series, not named in any Indian source read (RD272); or the chapter may be older than its printed frame. Not decided by the sources.
- Scheuermann's chapter on the *Svapnohana* (Brill 2020, *Glimpses of Tibetan Divination*, pp. 161–180) could not be read: the publisher's site (brill.com) answered the automated browser with a "confirm you are human" page (HTTP 405), and no open copy was found in OAPEN, DOAB or the Internet Archive; whether it names a Sanskrit parallel is not known (q869).

Doubt: both books were read in text layers only, not on page images; the page numbers of Hazra are from the running heads of the layer.
