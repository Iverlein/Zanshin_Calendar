# The remains are sent after four days, checked by ten roads

The remains of an ordinary person are sent **after four days have passed** (ཞག་བཞི་འདས་ནས་གཏོང, WB vol. 2, p. 96): the consciousness's stay in the body is not certain, but its longest limit is three and a half days. A practitioner remaining in meditation is left until released; the eminent and those with qualities are best sent after seven days.

The direction and day of the sending are then checked by ten roads (ལམ, lam), numbered by the 1996 editors (p. 96):

1. the sky's mouse road (གནམ་གྱི་བྱི་ལམ, gnam gyi byi lam; p. 50 «བྱ་ལམ», bird road): a good planet and mansion; the rising and setting of planets and mansions;
2. the year's earth lords, 3. the month's, 4. the day's and 5. the hour's: send where none of them is (the earth's mouse road, ས་ཡི་བྱི་ལམ);
6. the mewa's and 7. the trigram's directions;
8. the living's door direction, 9. the dead's door and slayer directions;
10. the corpse-bearer (རོ་ཁུར, ro khur).

Then the places for each kind of corpse, the twenty-six places not allowed even if clean, and the repair of the place (pp. 100–104). The sizes of the chapter (p. 50) count the twelfth examination in 3,186 pebbles, among them Rāhu's course in twelve result pebbles and 133 identifying; the twelfth's own text (pp. 94–104) does not name Rāhu (searched on both prints' machine readings and by eye on p. 97).
