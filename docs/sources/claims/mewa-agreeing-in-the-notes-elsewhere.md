# The notes use «སྨེ་ཕྲེང་མཐུན་པའི་སྨེ་བ» in two other places: the compared year's mewa in the person's sequence

Mipham's own notes use the circles' phrase for the mewa (སྨེ་བ, sme ba) twice elsewhere (etext, printed pp. 495 and 500): in the sixteenth row the sky year and the mewa that agrees in sequence (སྨེ་ཕྲེང་མཐུན་པ) stand for the sky year, and for the one reckoned "the body mewa is fixed and, resting on it, the other three"; for the dead, the mewas of the sky year, month, day, hour and progressed year are each taken as "the mewa that agrees in sequence with the one reckoned" and fought. So the agreeing mewa is a compared year's mewa taken in the mewa sequence of the reckoned person's body mewa; the body mewa of the person is the base, the compared point's mewa is read in its sequence. Applied to the circles (pp. 533), the person's body mewa fights the mewa of the point reached by counting upward, read in the person's own sequence: one mewa pebble, not two combined. This agrees with mewa-sequence-agreeing and is a reading of a machine text, not a worked example; the notes give none for the circles.
