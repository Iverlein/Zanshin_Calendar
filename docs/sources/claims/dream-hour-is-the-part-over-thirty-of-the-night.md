# The dream's hour is the part of thirty over thirty of the night: 6, 10, 15 and 30 are one scale, a fifth, a third, a half and the whole (q1018)

The root text of the Dbyangs 'char (དབྱངས་འཆར, Dbyangs 'char) says: the instant's parts are of the nature of thirty, so look at that; when ten parts rise, at a third (སུམ་ཆ, sum cha); when fifteen rise, at midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed); if there are thirty, at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs); the particulars of this and the rest the wise are to examine. The Mchog dga' (མཆོག་དགའ, Mchog dga') adds, as the first of the «and so on», that six parts is the early part of the night. Read as one scale, the part number n of thirty is the fraction n/30 of the night: six is a fifth, ten a third, fifteen a half (midnight), thirty the whole night, dawn. So the numbers 6, 10, 15 and 30 are not four scales, and «ten parts, a third of the night» is not a misprint: ten of thirty is a third, and a half at fifteen shows that the third is counted from the start of the night, since ten comes before midnight. The number is the one in the parts place of the five places at the hour of the question (see mchog-dga-five-places-and-the-thirty-parts), and the Mchog dga' says that a fine reckoning also needs the water-hours, the places below the parts. The text does not say from what the night is counted (dusk is the only start that fits «dawn» for thirty), nor why a part of the rising sign is read as a part of the night, nor which night a question about a dream means; these stay open. The Kun 'dus etext's clause for ten is garbled («rmi bar bar bya ste 'gyur zhing»); the Zlos gar etext's snippet, the Derge root and the dpe bsdur ma etext give the clean reading, and the dbu can print of the National Library of Mongolia has, on one crop checked by eye, «thirty» for the ten (img. 279), so thirty twice, probably a copyist's slip: the root decides ten.
