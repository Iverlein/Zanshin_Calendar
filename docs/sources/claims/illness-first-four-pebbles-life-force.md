# The illness's first four pebbles are the four times' life-force elements; the regent's reply is about the fifth

For the illness identified by five pebbles (WB vol. 1, p. 435), the first four pebbles are the life-force elements (སྲོག་ཁམས, srog khams) of the year, month, day and hour of falling ill, and the fifth is the one element most common among the trigrams (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) of those four times.

- **WB's words**: «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་སྲོག་ཁམས་བཞི» stands in every print checked: the 1996 edition and the Gser thang reprint (wb.1:p435, read on the scan), the Zhol blocks and the Taikhang reproduction (read on the scan 2026-10-25; the note under wb.1:p435), and the line as the head of the regent's reply 133 quotes it (ys:i491).
- **Mipham's notes** (wb.2:p497, scan) read it so: the four life-force elements of the year, month, day and hour of falling ill, and "among those four, the element of whichever trigram's element is most", one pebble: five.
- **The regent's reply 133** (ys:i491, scan) answers a question that quotes WB's line and sets beside it an "inner reckoning" with "the trigram of the year, month, day and hour". The doubt he answers is whether that reckoning's "illness of the four times" means medicine's seasons of wind and phlegm; he says it does not, quoting the *Speckled Elements* (ཁམས་ཁྲ, khams khra) plank-tree, and closes "the trigram elements of the four, and one element pebble of whichever is most among them added, five; no doubt". He finds no conflict with WB's «སྲོག་ཁམས་བཞི», which his question had quoted; his summary is of the inner reckoning's line and does not say WB's four life-force pebbles are to be replaced.

Which stands: WB's own words, with Mipham's reading of them, by the rule of rank; the regent's summary, read with the doubt it answers, is about where the fifth pebble comes from (the four times' trigrams), on which all three agree. The claim [illness-five-by-time-of-falling-ill](illness-five-by-time-of-falling-ill.md), written from reply 133 alone, is to be read with this one.
