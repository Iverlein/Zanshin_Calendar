# Ch. 27, p. 17, l. 9: «ལྕོང་མོའི་རྔ་མས», the tadpole's tail, two syllables on both scans

On p. 17, l. 9, the last verse of the trigram piled up reads «ལྕོང་མོའི་རྔ་མས་རྒྱ་མཚོ་དཀྲུགས», "the tadpole's (ལྕོང་མོ, lcong mo) tail (རྔ་མ, rnga ma) stirs the ocean", the image for a fire person in his prime when many fire people are piled on the trigram *li* (ལི, li).

Read on the scan, 2026-10-28 (RD90): on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 23, l. 9, at 5×) and on the 1996 print's own scan (I1KG12907 img. 25, at 6×) the doubtful syllable after «ལྕོང་མོའི» stands alone, with a syllable dot (ཚེག, tsheg) after it and then «མས»: two syllables, so the one-syllable «རྔམས» (splendour) that Yigdzin-1 gives on both prints, and on the Taikhang and the Zhol print (RD57), is not what this setting prints. The syllable itself, which RD38, RD51 and RD62 saw as a ཛ-like letter, is this typeface's «རྔ»: the fourth circle's «རྔ་གཤང» on p. 6, l. 20 (reprint img. 12, 1996 img. 14), read so since RD38, has the same glyph, a single letter's height with the hooked top of the ར pressed onto the ང, on both scans side by side. «རྔ་མ» is the tail, and the dictionaries define the tadpole as the frog's young with a tail ([ch27-words-in-steinert-dictionaries](ch27-words-in-steinert-dictionaries.md)), so the reading carries a meaning: a small creature's tail stirring the great ocean. BDRC's etext of the 1996 print (VEIE0OPI51524892_I1KG12907, searched 2026-10-28) has the line as «ལྕོང་མོའི་རྔ་མས་རྒྱུ་མཚོ་དཀྲུགས», two syllables as on the scan (its «རྒྱུ» for the scan's clear «རྒྱ» a slip of that etext); RD67's search in the etexts was for «ལྕོང་མོའི་རྔམས», which this etext does not have.

The Taikhang's line (wb.2:p17b) stays as RD57 and RD77 left it: its fused ink was not looked at again here.
