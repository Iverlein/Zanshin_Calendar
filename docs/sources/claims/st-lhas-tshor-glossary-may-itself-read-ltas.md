# The glossary's own sentence may read ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས: RD356's reading of it as keeping ལྷས་ is doubtful

RD356 read the glossary's «ལྟས་ཚོར་» as what "some say" and took the glossarist to keep ལྷས་. BDRC's exact search finds the phrase «ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» (and not ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས) in both copies of the glossary (I5609, I00KG07616) and in the catalogue's two etexts of it (MW29689, MW00KG07597), in the sentence «here the time of ltas tshor and the time the patient himself feels are the two taught, so there is no fault of letters». Read plainly, the glossarist's own answer then uses ལྟས་ for the clause, and he defends it as no error against those who find the letters odd; RD356's eye pass could not tell the stack under ལ in line 2 (ྷ or ྟ) and the first word of line 1 «ལྷས་ཚོར་ཅེས་པ» is the quoted commentary's wording. Both readings of the glossarist stay open until the stack is read at a sharper scan; what is firm is that a glossary of the Supplement knew the clause with ལྟས་ and called it faultless. The Blue Beryl's etext and the glossary then agree on ལྟས་, which makes a link possible (the glossary names no source) but does not show one.
