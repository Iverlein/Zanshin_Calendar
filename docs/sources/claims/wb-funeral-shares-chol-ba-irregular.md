# The six «འཆོལ་བ» funeral shares are the irregular ones

The funeral shares are eighteen: six great, six small and six more. The count line (p. 68) prints the last six «འཚོལ་བ» ("sought") and their heading (p. 69) «འཆོལ་བ» ("disordered, irregular": «འཆོལ་བ» is "confused, topsy-turvy, mixed up" in Hopkins, Rangjung, Valby), both read so on the Gser thang reprint. What the six are settles it: each is an irregular case outside the regular order of great and small shares (a great man whom the living cannot afford, a childless man given a royal rite, a youth killed by the blade, the old and the infant, those who may not be buried even with merit or with power). So «འཆོལ་བ», irregular, is the sense, and «འཚོལ་བ» in the count line a spelling slip, though «འཚོལ་བ» "sought" in the sense of shares sought for those not entitled cannot be excluded. The Zhol and Taikhang prints were not read at these two places in this reading.
