# Ch. 91's two bracketed remedies are notes in the Derge and Chag-po-ri prints too and in the commentaries; ཕྱྭ and བ་ར stay undefined, དྲི་ཆེན is dung

RD304 (2026-11-10). The Derge (I00JW501163 img. 605-606) and Chag-po-ri (I1KG13487 img. 602-603) prints carry both remedies that the Serthang brackets, the milk with dung and the thread, in the verse's line without brackets. By eye the Derge sets them in smaller type than the verse (to the margin of img. 605 and over the start of img. 606 until the verse returns at «དགོས་པ»); the Chag-po-ri's first line of img. 603 looks smaller than the lines below, the extent not settled. So, with the Zhol print (RD294), the Serthang's brackets are no addition of one print: four prints (Serthang, Zhol, Derge, Chag-po-ri) carry the notes, and only the Drathang machine text of the root verse lacks them (q824). Whether they are the author's or an early editor's is not shown by a print; the commentaries have the same matter in their glosses (Blue Beryl, Drikung, Khro ru), Skyem pa Tshe dbang's has the thread only, so the notes read as the commentary tradition's, set in small type in the prints.

The letters. ཕྱྭ (phywa) is read by Yigdzin-1 on the Derge and the Chag-po-ri as on the Serthang; the Blue Beryl and the Drikung commentary spell ཕྱྭ, Khro ru ཕྱ, Skyem pa has མོ་ལྟ (mo lta, divination) in its place. No commentary glosses ཕྱྭ beyond its place beside མོ as the means of finding which spirit it is (the Blue Beryl: do the divination with its omen, then identify the spirit; q896 stays as the dictionaries answered it). དྲི་ཆེན (dri chen) is plain dung in the commentaries (one's own dung and urine in the Blue Beryl; the dung the patient himself has voided in the Drikung commentary), not a drug name. བ་ར (ba ra) occurs as «བ་རའི་འོ་མ» among the causes of cold kapha beside curd, cold water and tea and is defined nowhere; no commentary says what the milk is for.
