# Two Guhyasamāja commentaries list dreams as signs of accomplishment, all favourable, in the time of sleep with no hour, and say the dream is examined to give up clinging to the sign (RD386)

Two Guhyasamāja commentaries of the Derge Tengyur volume VE23703_1355, on the tantra's fifteenth chapter, treat the dream as a sign of accomplishment and as something to be examined so as not to cling to it. Ci lu pa's «Rin po che'i ljon shing» (pp. 202–204) lists signs seen "in a dream": the dharma body won by oneself and equal to all buddhas, the enjoyment body worshipped, the five families of buddhas and their bodhisattvas, Vajrasattva and the wrathful forms, a lovely adorned goddess or a boy or girl (a common accomplishment), the buddhas of the ten directions, the wheel of Dharma turned, consecration as king of the Dharma; and, for the yogin who sees himself joined to a low-caste girl, a dog or a wild mare, the dharma body will be accomplished. Every image listed is favourable; none is a bad dream. The dream comes "in the time of sleep" blessed by the Tathāgatas and "mostly always" to one who practises by day and night; no part of the night, no day of the month. Rgyal bas byin's «Dka' 'grel» (pp. 532–536, chapter 15) says the dream-sign arises in two states, of having slept and not slept, and that "to give up fixing on signs (མཚན་མའི་མངོན་པར་ཞེན་པ) the examination of the dream is told"; the dream is "like a dream", because it does its work though not true in the ultimate. Neither commentary gives a remedy. The question of RD381 on p. 533 is answered: the passage is about clinging to the sign and fixes no time.
