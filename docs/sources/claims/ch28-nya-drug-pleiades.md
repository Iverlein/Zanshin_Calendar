# The six full moons are the Pleiades (nya drug)

WB vol. 2, pp. 27–28: «ཉ་དྲུག» is the Pleiades itself: Ives and Waldo and the Rangjung Yeshe dictionary give "Pleiades, the six fish" for *nya drug*, as WB's own verse equates it with the six stars (སྨིན་དྲུག, smin drug). The examination is one: on the evening of the 15th of the tenth month the Pleiades are watched for where they stand to the moon (before, behind, to the south, to the north, close together), not six moons. Modern handbooks that give the same examination (the *Bod kyi rtsis rig kun 'dus chen mo*, MW28845, the festival book MW1AC283 and a school question-book, MW3CN4999, in BDRC's full-text search) describe it as the moon's and the Pleiades' meeting, "before and behind the moon, right and left, close together"; they date it to different Tibetan months (the tenth, the twelfth, the second), so the month is not a fixed tradition's and WB's tenth is its own. The name: «ཉ་དྲུག་བརྟག་པ», "the examination of the six fish".
