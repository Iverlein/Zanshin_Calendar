# What is a source for the tenth in the lineage of Rtsa gsum gling pa and Mipham: the Guru's pledge, the feast, the waxing half; no twenty-fifth

Settled from the passages read in the pool to 2026-11-21 (this claim gathers; each point has its own claim).

**The tenth is a source in the lineage, firmly.** (1) The Guru's own pledge to come on the tenth: in Mipham's quotations of Ratna gling pa's tenth-day text and the Key Instruction (rank 2: tenth-day-padmasambhava-promise-to-come), in the Padma bka' thang's «every tenth I come to Tibet» (padma-bka-thang-every-tenth-i-come-and-the-feast) and in the Seven Chapters' «on the waxing tenth, in person» (tenth-day-guru-comes-on-the-waxing-tenth-in-the-seven-chapters); the Seven Chapters is the line recited every day across the schools. (2) The way he is met: the feast (tshogs), «circulate what you have» (obk:p337); Mipham, by his biographer, kept it for both halves (mipham-keeps-the-tenth-feast-of-both-halves). (3) For Rtsa gsum gling pa's own lineage, the waxing tenth (tsl-lineage-keeps-the-waxing-tenth-and-names-no-twenty-fifth).

**Waxing only, or both halves.** The Seven Chapters name the waxing half; the Padma bka' thang and Mipham's quoted texts say «every tenth»; Zhe chen rgyal tshab and Mipham's life keep both. A rule «the waxing tenth is the Guru's, the waning tenth is kept too» is what the pool's claims support (tenth-day-waxing-half-is-the-gurus-both-halves-kept).

**The twenty-fifth is not a source in the lineage's Guru texts.** Not in the Padma bka' thang, the Seven Chapters, Rtsa gsum gling pa's vols. 27-30, Mipham's collected writings or his biography (padma-bka-thang-and-seven-chapters-name-no-twenty-fifth). It stands with the tenth in Zhe chen rgyal tshab's retreat rule (feast-without-fail-on-the-tenth-and-twenty-fifth-in-retreat) and in a modern anthology's pure-vision life (tenth-and-twenty-fifth-in-a-pure-vision-life): a later pairing. The owner's choice to show the 10th and 25th stays his; the sources here make the 10th certain and the 25th a custom of later texts. The 11th and the 26th are named by none of them.
