# WB ch. 12: title, plan and what the author leaves out of the Dbyangs 'char

Chapter twelve (pp. 91–103) is, by its title and colophon, "a drop of the needed water taken from the sea of the *Tantra of the Victor in Battle* (*G.yul rgyal rgyud*) to slay the hunger of those who pose as reckoners for understanding the rising of the vowels (*dbyangs 'char*)". It opens (p. 91) with a homage to Avalokiteśvara in the form of Īśvara (*dbang phyug*, an eighth-ground bodhisattva said to have risen from the forehead of the great god "Knower of all"), and states the plan in three verses: his qualities, the action of the nine planets with the goddess Dga' ma and the twenty-eight mansions within the twelve houses, and the names of the things that rise from good and evil. At the close (p. 103) the author says what he has left out: the 123 wheels of the *Dpal dbyangs 'char* and the wheel kinds of the *Rin chen rgya mtsho* and the *Yid bzhin nor bu*, and the red and black wheels of the *Gyul rgyal* group, which he holds to be of little use to spread and to rest on a secret oral line; he sets down only what is unavoidable, and a separate text for the Rāhu eights and the life reckoning. The chapter's rules are for judging the good and ill of regions, kings and persons by the planets in the mansions.
