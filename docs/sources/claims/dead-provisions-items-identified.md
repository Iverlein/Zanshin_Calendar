# The provisions of the trigrams: what the recitation and the dictionaries identify, what is left

The provisions of the trigrams (WB vol. 2, pp. 75–76) are given again as a recitation on pp. 129–131 (etext only), and the two lists with the dictionaries identify most of what the print left open: «བྱ་ཐི་བ» is the bird *thi ba*, the pigeon or wood-pigeon (བྱ་ཐི་བ, phug ron), the grave bird of *khon* in the recitation; «སྦྱར་པ» is the poplar, *sbyar pa* (a tree also called *shol po*); «ཕུ་ཐུང» is *phu thung* (ཕུ་ཐུང), a short-sleeved garment (the recitation gives *khon* "this old woman's left *phu dung*" as her clothes); «བོང་གོས» is a donkey's cloth; «སོ་བ» is *so ba*, a coarse thick-shelled barley (the recitation: "donkey meat, the right rib, the grain *so ba*", *zin*); «སྐ་རགས» is a belt, and the recitation gives the belt of *zin* as tiger, leopard or donkey-foal hide, matching «སྟག་ལྤགས་བོང» of the list (the word «སོར་ལ» before it stays unidentified); «ཕུག་དར» of *zon* is silk (the recitation gives five kinds of silk and brocade for *zon*, so «ཕུག» is a modifier or a slip); «སྐུར་རྩིབ» (*dwa*), «སྐྱུར་རྩིབ» (*zin*) and «སྒྱུར་རྩིབ» in the etext are the same word for a rib, as the recitation gives the right rib of the donkey to *zin*; and «རྟ་རྨ་པུ་སྟི» (p. 76, the good old one's right-hand side) is read by the 1996 etext «དྷ་རྨ་པུ་སྟེ», the *dharma pusti*, a Dharma book, which another verse of the same chapter lays as the old one's pillow («སྔས་སུ་དྷརྨ་པུསྟི་བཞག», p. 142 etext): the printing «རྟ་རྨ» is a misreading of «དྷ་རྨ». Still not identified: «ཚར་ཤིང» (*dwa*; the recitation sets a tree «ཚར་བ» beside the crane of *khen*, where the list gives *khen* the poplar, so either the list's *dwa* is shifted or *tshar ba* is a second name of the poplar), «ཇུ་ཚེར», «སོག་པ་སྲང» (the recitation gives «ཕུག་སྲང་སོག་པ» as the old woman's wealth, and *sog pa srang* may be a "Mongol ounce", a money measure, but this is a guess) and «ཚ་རགས» of the old (p. 77; the Gser thang etext has «ཚ་རགས་པ»).
