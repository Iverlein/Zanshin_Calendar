# The Blue Beryl's rbad mgo is the forehead fringe and drwa phad the cord net-bag or waistband; the clause ends «gos dkar lcang lo'i sde rnams dang» (q988, q989)


The clause on those of a like kind to the ya mtshan can messenger (the Blue Beryl, ch. 7, etext pp. 198-199; gt:i41c) lists, as marks of the Drukpa school's realised ones (རྟོགས་ལྡན, rtogs ldan), what they bear (ཐོགས་པ, thogs pa): «རྦད་མགོ» (rbad mgo), «དྲྭ་ཕད» (drwa phad), the thighbone trumpet and bone ornaments. The two unglossed words are ordinary words of the dictionaries (tmz:box7; state etext, the dictionaries' scans not opened).

**rbad mgo**: "the fringe of hair that covers the forehead" (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho, Tshig mdzod chen mo), a head with its hair cut in a fringe; Ives Waldo, "forehead covering bangs". So the first mark is a hairstyle: the yogi's hair is not shaved like the monk's. BDRC's full-text search (2026-11-14, «རྦད་མགོ» 39 hits, Wylie snippets of search-only etexts, nothing built on them) shows the word used so elsewhere: "an old man with a rbad mgo on his head" in the Karma dbang 'dzin 'das log biography; the hair-styles of the Tibetan grammar verses (rbad mgo skra dbyibs); and, in a text of Dbra ston Ngag dbang skal bzang, «gri mtshon dang rbad mgo byed pa», wearing a knife and making a fringe, named among the signs unfit for an ordained man.

**drwa phad**: the dictionaries key the word as «dra phad» (no headword «drwa phad» in the 64 sets), "a vessel with a net of cord", "a bag with a mesh of cord in which things are gathered", and also a name of the waistband (ske rags: Brda dkrol gser gyi me long, citing the Brda yig blo gsal mgrin rgyan); Ives Waldo "cord net bag". The spelling «drwa phad» is found in the same sense: the verse «mkho dgu tshang ba'i drwa phad rgyal srid min» of the Kāvyādarśa glosses, which the Gold Mirror quotes with «dra phad»; a Sdom gsum commentary lists «da dril drwa phad phreng ma» (damaru and bells, net-bag, rosary) among the ornaments with which the ordained dress themselves like householders; the Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po collection has «drwa phad» as a net-like shape. A copy of the Blue Beryl's clause in the commentary of Tshul khrims rgya mtsho (MW30522, 2001, search-only snippet) has «dwa phan». So the second mark is the yogi's net-bag or net-belt, carried with the trumpet and the bone ornaments; whether bag or belt the clause does not say.

**The clause's end.** The etext's broken «གས་[གོས]དཀར་ལྕང་ལའི་[ལོའི]» is read «གོས་དཀར་ལྕང་ལོའི་སྡེ་རྣམས་དང་» by a second copy of the clause, the Gso ba rig pa vol. 3 (MW1PD95727, a modern compilation, search-only Wylie snippet): «bod 'dir mnga' bdag khri srong lde btsan gyi dus mchod gnas sde gnyis kyi gos dkar lcang lo'i sde rnams dang / gal te de gnyis min yang de dang rigs mthun pa cha lugs btsun par bcos pa'i rang nyid dad pa 'dren pa…». "In Tibet, the communities of the white-robed locked ones (gos dkar lcang lo'i sde) of the two seats of offering of the lord Khri srong lde btsan's periodic offering; and if neither of these two, one of like kind who has made his dress venerable and draws faith to himself". The two seats are the saffron-robed ordained and the white-robed mantra-holders (a snippet of MW2PD19446, "the dge 'dun sde gnyis, the head's offering-recipients", agrees with the claim gt-drikung-skyem-pa-ya-mtshan-can-blue-beryl-clause).

**The Drikung dka' mchan** glosses this verse (etext p. 79) with the Moonbeams' outsider and a Tantra's list of sects; it names no Drukpa yogi and no king's communities (inv-e, 2026-11-13). The clause is the Blue Beryl's own addition.
