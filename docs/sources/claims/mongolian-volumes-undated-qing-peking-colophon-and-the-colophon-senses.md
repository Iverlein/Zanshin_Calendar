# No volume of the dream text's Mongolian copies carries a date; one text of GDR's volume is Qing; the quoting books are modern; the senses of Klong rdol's colophon

# No volume of the dream text's Mongolian copies carries a date

**The volumes.** BDRC's records of the three Mongolian National Library volumes (W1NLM3822, W1NLM9044, W1NLM9419) give no date; no part's record carries one, only notes of missing leaves. The volumes are collections of small rites and liturgies by many hands, so only their texts' colophons can date them (BDRC's catalogue and the scans; passages gdm:i82, gdm:i135, gdr:i37, gdn:i175):

- W1NLM9419 (GDR), first text: completed in the Tibetan Dharma temple of Peking in the reign of the «འཇམ་དབྱངས» emperor, the title of the Qing emperors, so that text and the volume that holds it are of the Qing period (after 1644). The same monk, Dkon mchog bstan pa'i sgron me, signs an 8th text in this volume and an 8th text in the Dga' ldan Byang rtse volume (GDM's), which ties the two volumes to one circle of copyists, undated.
- W1NLM9044 (GDN): its 10th text, by the mantra-madman Padma 'od zer, is dated to the fifteenth day of the month Sa ga of an ox year, the ninth of an era called «སྲིད་གསལ». The era is not identified (BDRC's exact-phrase search for «སྲིད་གསལ་གྱི་ཁྲི་ལོ» has no hit). The dream text is the 30th text, so the dated text shows only that the volume holds datable writing, not when the dream text was copied.
- W1NLM3822 (GDM): the colophons of the neighbouring texts name only teachers and writers (Blo bzang 'jigs med rgyal mtshan with the scribe Blo bzang karma bkra shis; Ngag dbang bkra shis; Dkon mchog bstan pa'i sgron me) and no year.

So no volume is shown older than Klong rdol bla ma (1719-1794, BDRC P22): the one datable thing is a Qing-period text in GDR's volume, and q954's "older than Klong rdol" is not decided by the volumes.

**The quoting books** whose snippets BDRC's search gives for the verses are modern prints: KBS, the 1993 article in China Tibetology no. 4 (the earliest, and it names Klong rdol bla ma's way of examining dreams); MDP (MW21880) 1997; Padma rdo rje's work on sleep and dreams (MW25126) 2002; the volume on crafts, reckoning and dance (MW1PD95727) 2003/2006; the book of Tibet's old rites (MW1AC12) 2012; MW8LS18598 2012. None dates the text. The Mongolian bundle SKP carries the same verse under the names «པདྨ་དཀར» and the third of Vairocana, also undated.

**What Klong rdol bla ma's colophon in GDN can mean.** The sentence is «ཅེས་པ་འདི་ … ཀློང་རྡོལ་བླ་མ་ངག་དབང་ … བྲིས་པའོ» (gdn:i262). In the colophons of these volumes the verb «བྲིས» (written) covers the composer who writes from others' words (W1NLM9044 part 33: «རང་གི་བརྗེད་བྱང་དུ་བྲིས་པའོ», written as his own memorandum), the writer who sets another's words down as notes (GDM's 5th text: «ཟིན་བྲིས་སུ་བགྱིས», made as notes), and the copyist, who is named separately as «ཡི་གེ་པ» (scribe) where the colophons separate the two. So the sentence can mean that Klong rdol bla ma composed or compiled the text, that he wrote this copy by hand, or that his colophon was copied with the text from an earlier copy. No word of owning or of commissioning stands in it, so "owner of the copy" is not a sense of these words. The name stands after the women's marks, at the end of the collection and not after the dream part, as in RST: it closes the whole.
