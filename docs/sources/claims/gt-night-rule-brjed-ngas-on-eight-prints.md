# The ch. 7 night rule on eight prints: brjed ngas on every one, and what the commentators say of mtshan stod

The Four Tantras' ch. 7 two lines of the night rule, «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད་ངས་འབྲས་བུ་མེད། ཐོ་རངས་མཐོང་དང་གསལ་ན་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན།» (a dream of the first part of the night, མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod, is forgotten, བརྗེད་ངས, brjed ngas, and has no fruit; seen at dawn, ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs, and clear it gives fruit), were looked up on every print of the pool that holds the chapter: Yigdzin-1 over the page or pages of eight prints, and by eye on two.

Count by print (the reader's text of the second line's first half, བརྗེད་ངས): ངས on the Drathang I4CZ327131 img. 42; the Zhol I5910 img. 57; the Chag-po-ri I1KG13487 img. 48; the reprint I5907 img. 66; the Derge I00JW501163 img. 41; the Chag-po-ri I3CN1711 img. 41 (the lines run over from img. 40, where the verse begins), I3CN1696 img. 44 and I3CN1702 img. 36. Eight prints, eight ངས; no print gives ངེས. By eye: on the Derge (a crisp bilevel print, 3-4x) the line begins མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད and the next syllable is a ང with a ས, no vowel stroke over it (moderate: the ས is clipped by the blot of the ink); on I3CN1711 (3x) the same, ངས without a vowel stroke (moderate to good). So «བརྗེད་ངེས» of the other etexts and the modern quotations is not a reading of any print the pool can see; the Yu thog, Grwa thang and Peking etexts and Khro ru already have ངས. The Serthang leaf (printed pp. 31-32) is still not in the pool's scan. The Drathang's ངས therefore agrees with every open print; whether the Serthang print has it is unknown, and the question is narrowed to that print alone.

Other reader faults noted, not readings: I5910 and I3CN1711 «འབྲས་བུ་མད» for མེད (I3CN1711's scan shows ེ by eye); I1KG13487 «བསལ་ན» for གསལ་ན; I3CN1702 «ཚེ་རངས» for ཐོ་རངས. Each of the eight prints has ཐོ་རངས་མཐོང་དང་གསལ་ན་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན in the second line.

Line 17: the readers' texts of the pages read here are as RD275 counted (I3CN1711 མི་བཟད་པ, the Derge མི་བཟང་བ); no new eye reading, the count stays four prints each way.

Commentators on «མཚན་སྟོད»: nothing new beyond st-dream-night-first-part-dawn. Byang pa (up to the half of the first part: མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད, in the quoted Moonbeams line) and the 'Bum nag gsal sgron (from midnight up, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ལ) mark the first half of the night; Khro ru gives dusk with midnight alike; Rin spungs pa and the Drikung give no bound. Nobody in these four names the stretch from midnight to dawn as a part with its own rule; it is the dawn's own time (ཐོ་རངས) that is named, and the Four Tantras' text itself names only the two terms. Not looked for beyond these commentaries: Gso bya lus kyi rnam bshad (etext restricted).
