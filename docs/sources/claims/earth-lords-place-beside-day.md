# The earth lords decide the place, the day is chosen apart

In the rites of the ground and in the almanacs read for D4, an earth lord's (ས་བདག, sa bdag) rule is a rule of place: it says where a work is not done (the direction of the empty vase (བུམ་སྟོང, bum stong), the month's hal dog (ཧལ་ཁྱི, hal khyi), the parts of the house where the dragon lord (འབྲུག་རྗེ, 'brug rje) stays) or where the ground is opened (at the belly or armpit of the serpent (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ, lto 'phye), never its head or tail). The day is chosen apart, by date, mansion, planet and part of the day, and the work is done on the chosen day at the allowed place or facing the clean direction. Both conditions are met together; none of these texts weighs the one against the other, ranks an earth lord among the day's factors or lets a good day lift a bad place. An inference, not a statement of any text: they treat place and time as two separate tests, both to be passed.

Only MTA's passage was read on the scan; the others rest on BDRC's etexts (the serpent's lines stand alike in several prints; weighing.md, D4 C).
