# The earliest text with the Svapnohana's series: the dates as far as published (RD272)

The Svapnohana's series (a year, eight months, three months, half a month, ten days at dawn) is found in no Indian text that the pool has read earlier than a Purāṇa whose date is itself unsettled; what is published, as far as RD272 could read, is this.

- The series stands whole in the Brahmavaivarta's chapter of the good dream (bvp:p662-665; skd:p470-471; Negelein's b). The dates given for the Purāṇa in the summaries RD272 read (Banglapedia, Wikipedia, both as web summaries, Hazra and Rocher not read): an original perhaps before A.D. 700, a present form attributed by some to the 8th century with changes from the 10th, by others mainly to the 15th–16th, with *smṛti* chapters held to be inserted later. The *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa*'s dream chapters are not dated apart from the whole.
- Texts with a series of the same shape and earlier or datable: the Atharva *Pariśiṣṭa* 68 (a year, eight months, half of that; undated in the pages read; apv:p445); the *Bṛhadyātrā* (6th century by the registry; a year, half a year, a quarter, a month; bya:p41-46); the *Matsya* and *Agni* Purāṇas (six months for the second watch; mtp:p453-455, agp:p280-281; undated); the *Adbhutasāgara* of about 1168, which quotes Parāśara, Varāha and the *Matsya*/*Viṣṇudharmottara* but not the Brahmavaivarta's eight months (adb:p513-515); the *Svapnacintāmaṇi* (about the 12th century, the registry). Achmet's *Oneirocriticon* (under the caliph al-Ma'mun, 813–833 A.D., in Greek, by his own account from Indians, Persians and Egyptians; Latin by Leo Tuscus about 1160) is not shown to have a series: the stepped series Negelein quotes (20 years down to a year, then a half, a quarter, a twelfth of a year and ten days) is that of the German *Traumbuch Apomasaris*, derived from the Latin of 1577 (neg:p14-16, Introduction p. XXI): the year–half–quarter–month shape, not the Svapnohana's numbers.
- The Tibetan *Svapnohana* is by Vibhūticandra, whose life the Tsadra bibliography (a search summary) puts at 1170–1230; the translation is therefore datable to about 1200 at the latest, and with that the series with half a month and ten days is witnessed in a datable text no later than that. The Brahmavaivarta's chapter may be earlier or later: the pool cannot say.
- The *Atharva* appendix and the *Agni*/*Matsya* have the verbal shape of the series in different numbers; the Svapnohana's phrase "seen without doubt" echoes the *Matsya*; its numbers are the Brahmavaivarta's: its source was a text of that family (indian-dream-spans-by-watch-nine-texts).

Not found: a dated manuscript or print of the Brahmavaivarta's dream chapter older than the 1888 and the *Śabdakalpadruma* printings; no Indian text read gives the series with a date.
