# The dream of being led away by women marks a spirit, and its remedies (ch. 91)

The dream of being led away by women as the sign of a spirit (གདོན, gdon), and its remedies, ch. 91 of the Instruction Tantra.

In the closing part of the chapter on making the flavour (རོ་ཙ, ro tsa), the Four Tantras give the failing of the matter the chapter treats by cause. For cold wind of the kidneys there are medicines; for heat, pills with salt given with beer; for poison, the signs outer, inner and secret are identified, it is killed by its antidote and the body is guarded; for a spirit the sign is a dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) in which women lead the dreamer away. For the spirit the remedies are divination (མོ, mo), ཕྱུ (phyu), the rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) and the dependent link (རྟེན་འབྲེལ, rten 'brel), and the healing by mantra: a thread twisted by a clean boy from the wool of a sheep untouched by hands, seven vajra knots made in it, a mantra spoken a thousand times over it and the thread bound on; with it, milk of cattle and goats (བ་ར, ba ra) mixed with dung (དྲི་ཆེན, dri chen) rubbed on the soles; both are bracketed in the Serthang print and are not in the Drathang print's verse (machine reading), so they are notes put into the text. The dream is thus a sign of the spirit as the cause among several, within a chapter on a particular matter, not a sign of a spirit in general. The mantra is read letter by letter on the scan (RD227): ཨོཾ་ཧ་ར་ཧ་ར་བྷན་དྷ་ནི་ཤུ་ཏ་དྷ་རི ··· ནི་ཐ་ར་ཏེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ, with three dots of the print's own in the middle; its meaning is not asked. The chapter is the ninety-first by its colophon, p. 493 of the print (img. 507) (st:p493).

What the commentaries add (RD255, 2026-11-09; etext only). The two bracketed remedies of the Serthang text (the thread and the milk and dung) are in the commentaries as part of the gloss on the line, not in the verse: the 'Bri gung *Dka' mchan* (Chos kyi grags pa, 1595–1659; `bdk:p789-790`) has both, and Khro ru Mkhan po Tshe rnam (a modern author; `ktr:p684-685`) has them almost word for word as the Serthang brackets do. So the brackets are the notes of the commentary tradition worked into the Serthang text, which answers question q824 for the thread and the milk; the Drathang print's verse without them agrees. The commentaries also say how the dream marks the spirit: in the dream one is attached to women, kisses and embraces them, and seed is drawn out. Both take the first two items of the remedy, divination (མོ, mo) and the word the print gives as ཕྱུ, as the means of finding which spirit it is (Khro ru: *having done the divination and «khyu», one identifies which spirit it is; then, in accord with it, the rite of service and the dependent link*); the rite and the link are what is done once the spirit is known. The spelling differs among witnesses: ཕྱུ (phyu) in the Serthang print, ཕྱྭ (phywa) in the Lhasa 1982 print, another Instruction Tantra print and the 'Bri gung quotation, ཕྱ (phya) in Khro ru's verse. The next chapter, on seeking a child, uses the same pair for the same purpose: the spirit as cause is examined by divination and the reckoning «ཕྱུ» (`ktr:p688`; the 'Bri gung verse there has «མོ་རྩིས་ཕྱུ»). Neither commentary defines ཕྱུ itself; what they settle is its place, a means of examining which spirit it is, beside མོ, and it is not the name of a medicine or a rite. The mantra's gap in the Serthang print is not filled: Khro ru writes it without a gap and the 'Bri gung gives it twice in two different forms.
