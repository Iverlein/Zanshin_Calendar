# The Vimalaprabhā gives the long vowels midnight to midday and calls the long vowel the dream's nature, on two pages, and never joins them

Two sentences of the Vimalaprabhā (Dri med 'od, དྲི་མེད་འོད) set the vowels against the day. On the world chapter (p. 409) the short vowels run from the middle of the day to the middle of the night and the thirty long ones from the middle of the night to the middle of the day; the same page makes the five Rāhus (སྒྲ་གཅན, sgra gcan) the nature of waking, dream and deep sleep. On the individual chapter (p. 539) the long vowel is called the dream's nature (རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་རང་བཞིན, rmi lam gyi rang bzhin), the 360 letters with the twelve vowels waking's, and the five empty vowels with the sun, moon, Rāhu and time deep sleep's. Read together, the long vowels would fall in the half from midnight to midday and so make the dream's half the later night and the morning. The text never joins the two pages in one sentence, the first speaks of the vowels' hours and the second of the three states' natures, and the etext of p. 409 was not read on the scan; the joining is the reader's and is held as a question, not as a rule.
