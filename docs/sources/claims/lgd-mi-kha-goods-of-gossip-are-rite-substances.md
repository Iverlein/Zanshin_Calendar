# མི་ཁའི་རྫས in the Lama Gongdü's bad dreams: the substances of a rite against gossip

The Lama Gongdü's «མི་ཁའི་རྫས» (mi kha'i rdzas), the "goods of gossip" among the bad dreams (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, BDRC full-text search and etext windows, no scan opened): not a name but a ritual class: the things of a rite against gossip, set out as a list.

**The words.** མི་ཁ (mi kha): gossip, slander, bad talk, "the mouth of the people" (Rangjung Yeshe, Jim Valby, Ives Waldo, Dan Martin: gossip, and, as a kind of pollution, Epstein's dissertation; Tshig mdzod chen mo: bad speech, a rumour that falls on a wrong-doer, "slander that cannot be sustained"). རྫས (rdzas) is substance, a thing used; མི་ཁའི་རྫས (mi kha'i rdzas) has no headword.

**In use.** BDRC's exact search for «མི་ཁའི་རྫས» has 143 hits, nearly all rites. The things are named in lists: in Kongtrul's treasury, «རམ་བུ་སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ་རུས་པ་སོགས་མི་ཁའི་རྫས་བསགས» (ram bu, the beer's mash, g.yer ma, bone and the like, goods of gossip, heaped up in a vessel) and, for a ransom, «གླུད་གཟུགས་མར་མེ་ཆངས་བུ་མི་ཁའི་རྫས་ལྟེ་ཕྱེ། ཉུང་མ། སྦང་མ། ཐལ་བ། ཤིང་བུ་སོགས» (a ransom's offering in the Sgrub thabs kun btus: the figure, a butter lamp, beer, the goods of gossip, the mash, ash, kindling and the like); in the Bka' brgyad bde gshegs 'dus pa's manual, the "goods of gossip" are «བཙོ་ཚུར། ཞུ་མཁན། ཤ་རུ། དགོ་རུ། བྱ་མ…» (the OCR's reading; the words are not identified here); in another, «ཚྭ་སྒོག་རམ་པ་གཡེར་མ་བྲ་བོ་ནག་པོ» (salt, garlic, ram pa, g.yer ma, black buckwheat). They are put into a woollen bag, a cloth, or a vessel and sent off with the figure of the ransom, as the second text of the Lama Gongdü has it (`lgd.42:p969b`). So the dream, "using the goods of gossip" (སྤྱོད་པ, spyod pa), is to handle the stuff of such a rite; why that is a bad sign no text found says, and the item stands among the bad ones.

**For the passage.** The item of the bad list names the goods, and «རམ་རུག་སྦང་གཡེར་ལ་སོགས་» is a short list of three or four ("ram rug, sbang, g.yer, and the like"), not a three-word name: see the claims lgd-ram-rug-is-the-ram-bu-spelling and lgd-sbang-g-yer-beer-mash-and-pepper. The first words of q816 and the two items of q826 and q840 are thus answered as to structure; what ram rug itself is, and whether the beer mash is meant, stay the narrowed points.
