# The seven kinds of conduct of the sage's text: three told, four named

The chapter names seven kinds of conduct (སྤྱོད་པ་བདུན, *spyod pa bdun*) of the sage's text, of which it states only the first three with their mansions and results: the ordinary conduct (Svātī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, Rohiṇī): no disease, heavy rain, a good year; the leading conduct (Ārdrā, Maghā, Āśleṣā); the summarised conduct (Punarvasu, Pūrvaphalgunī, Uttaraphalgunī), and, when the leading and the summarised meet, disease and a middling year. The other four (the sharp, the one from the end of the combination, the fierce sinful one, and the rest) are named without their lists. A further division by numbers (one to six, seven to twelve, thirteen to twenty-four, twenty-five to thirty) gives many diseases, quarrelling, famine, and at last comfort for all beings by the straight course; the text does not say what is counted.
