# KD's sheets lay the verdict with a count: the white-minus-black sum of the five cells

On Kun dga' rig 'dzin's sheets of the meadow's ornament the verdict pebble of each column is laid with a count taken from the five cells above it: the whites and blacks of the five cells are counted pebble by pebble (a cluster of three is three whites, a pair two, a white with a black one each), the black taken from the white, and the verdict is laid on the colour left over, its count read from the size of what is left.

Read on the lent scan, 2026-11-01, over 40 columns of two sheets (kd.3:p723 and kd.3:p724, the second round; 8 groups of five columns, 20 columns on each page), with the cells counted as white minus black:

- left over 1 to 3 white: one white pebble (15 columns: 1, 2 or 3);
- 4 or 5 white: two white (6 columns);
- 6, 8 or 9 white: three white, the cluster (4 columns);
- exactly even, 0: one white with one black (6 columns, all on p. 724);
- 1 black over: one black (2 columns, both on p. 723);
- 2, 3 or 5 black over: two black (6 columns).

39 of the 40 columns fit; the one that does not is the second column of p. 724's first group, whose cells leave 3 black over and which carries one black, where the same count on p. 723 (three columns) carries two. Either a printer's slip or the bands overlap there; not settled.

So the count is the reckoning's own pebbles summed, not a number taken from elsewhere, and a column that leaves only a little (one to three, or one black) carries the fewest pebbles. What the band edges are (5 and 6, 3 and 4 for the white side, 1 and 2 for the black) is read off the sheets only; no text read states them. The cells themselves are the meetings of p. 718's board (kd.3:p718), the same pebbles as in the year of age, so the sheet shows the verdict in practice as a sum of the pebbles with a size. WB's verdict rule (vol. 1, p. 381: count the white and the black, not the threes and twos, and lay it on the more) gives the colour only; the sheets add the size.
