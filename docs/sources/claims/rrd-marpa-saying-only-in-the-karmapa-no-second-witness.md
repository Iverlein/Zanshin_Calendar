# (Corrected by RD390: see the end.) The saying of dusk habit, midnight the power of spirits and dawn true is found only in the third Karmapa's verses: no work or song of Marpa, Mi la ras pa, Ras chung pa or Sgam po pa read has it (RD387)

BDRC's exact-phrase search (2026-11-11, RD387) finds the Karmapa's «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་གདོན་དབང་ཡིན» and «སྲོད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བག་ཆགས» in the third Karmapa's vol. 5 only. «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ» stands in the death-sign teaching of the Lho brag pa (six prints of one text, `dds.9:p173-174`), in two biographies and a defence of the treasure-finder Padma gling pa; nothing in the songs, lives or counsels of Marpa, Mi la ras pa, Ras chung pa or Sgam po pa read by words (Marpa's vol. 4 and Ras chung pa's whispered lineage on the etext, the rest through the search). The saying, with «gdon dbang», is therefore known from the Karmapa alone (`rrd.5:p57-59`); where he took it from is not found.

**Corrected by RD390 (2026-11-11):** the three-watch saying itself is in Marpa's own works, a song of the translator on his dream of Maitrīpa in the Lives of Marpa (rrd.23:p102-104, rrd.23:p296-298) and the great songs (vol. 5, p. 31): «ནམ་གུང་ལ་བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན། དེ་ལ་ཡིད་བརྟན་བྱར་མི་རུང་།», at midnight the emanations of Māra are shown, and it is not to be trusted. The search above missed it because the song says «ནམ་གུང» and «བདུད», not «ནམ་ཕྱེད» and «གདོན་དབང». Only the wording «gdon dbang» is the Karmapa's, a paraphrase. See [[rrd-marpa-song-has-the-three-watch-saying-midnight-mara]].
