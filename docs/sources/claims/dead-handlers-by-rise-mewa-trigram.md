# Who may not handle the corpse, by rise and decline, mewa and trigram

Who among the living should not handle the corpse is read three ways (WB vol. 2, pp. 92–93; the notes, pp. 509–510, read on the scan). By rise and decline: twelve rounds counted down the dead's age from fixed animals (life force from the hare and from the monkey; body from tiger, bird, pig, rat; power from dragon, sheep, ox, dog; luck from snake and horse); the stage reached is read with the aspect's own element: washing, wearing clothes, working, rising white; breath, womb, body, birth speckled; decline, death, grave black. A black round is bad for its office: life force, the rite-maker and the abbot-monk; body, the grave Bon priest with the paper, the sharer of the corpse's dough, the woman with the ladle, the corpse carrier; power, the companion, the *phywa* (ཕྱྭ) priest, the dough-maid with the basin, the paternal aunt lifting the dough; luck, the maternal uncle and the spear-holder. The notes' worked case: a water-pig dead dying at three gives a speckled, a white and two blacks on the rounds it names. By the mewa where the dead fell, the living with the same mewa avoid an office: one-white, setting up the support; two-black, carrying the corpse; four-green, cooking; seven-red, the meat; eight-white, guiding and counsel; nine-red, lifting the dough. The dead's pebble is black when the death mewa cuts the birth mewa. By the trigram: the birth trigram cutting the death trigram is good, the reverse bad; living ones of the same trigram seat are at risk; died on *li* with a black pebble, those on *li* avoid cooking; *khon* the dough lamp; *dwa* carrying fire and the copper ladle; *khen* gathering stones and wood; *kham* beer work; *gin* monks' work; *zin* the great hall; *zon* grinding.
