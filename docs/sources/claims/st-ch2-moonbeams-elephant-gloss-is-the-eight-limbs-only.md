# The Moonbeams' «a man and an elephant» glosses the Eight Limbs' verse, not ch. 2's line; no commentary on the Four Tantras has it

The Moonbeams (Zla zer, Kha che Zla ba mngon dga' on the Eight Limbs, Body ch. 2) glosses the Eight Limbs' verse «དགའ་ཞིང་རྨི་ལམ་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་» with «རྨི་ལམ་དུ་སྐྱེས་པ་དང་གླང་པོ་ཆེ་ལ་སོགས་པ་མཐོང་བ», "in the dream sees a man and an elephant and the like" (Sde dge etext VE23703_1517, p. 37). That gloss belongs to the Eight Limbs' verse only, and to no commentary on the Four Tantras' line «ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་» (st:p21).

Evidence. (1) The Moonbeams is a commentary on the Eight Limbs, whose verse differs from the Four Tantras' in these lines: «དགའ་ཞིང་རྨི་ལམ་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་» for «ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་». (2) BDRC's exact search for the elephant sentence «སྐྱེས་པ་དང་གླང་པོ་ཆེ་ལ་སོགས་པ་མཐོང་བ» finds it only in three copies of the Moonbeams (Derge MW23703_4312, Peking-type MW1PD95844_3549, a Tengyur chapter copy MW00PD1011353); the etexts of Byang pa, Rin spungs pa, the Drikung dka' mchan, Skyem pa, Zur mkhar ba and the Blue Beryl were searched whole for «གླང་པོ་ཆེ་ལ་སོགས་པ་མཐོང་བ»: no other hit. (3) The Blue Beryl quotes the Eight Limbs' verse in the Moonbeams' translation beside the ch. 2 line (pp. 138–139) and glosses it in its own words: men seen many times, desire not born; no elephant and no Moonbeams named there. Byang pa and Rin spungs pa gloss the Four Tantras' own line with "a man seen again and again" (st:p21b).

So the three readings of the dream stay three: one man (the Moonbeams, "a man and an elephant", on the Eight Limbs), men many times (Byang pa, Rin spungs pa, the Blue Beryl, and the great commentary I5618 and others of RD300), and no commentary that reads དག as "clear". Nothing from the Moonbeams' gloss is read into ch. 2's line. (The Eight Limbs' own verse is a Sanskrit-derived text; the Tibetan of the Moonbeams is the only witness read here.)
