# The line «མར་ངོའི་རྨི་ལམ་མང་དུས་སུ» stands in a pregnancy rite with a powder; its «རྨི་ལམ» stays unexplained (RD395, q1034)

The line «མར་ངོའི་རྨི་ལམ་མང་དུས་སུ། ཞག་དགུའི་བར་དུ་གཏང་བར་བྱ» stands, in the print read (BDRC I1PD440, etext p. 439), in a rite on the care of a pregnancy: a gait test of the woman, a powder of three hots, three fruits, three sweets and three animals' parts ground and mixed with milk and curd, a mantra said over the mustard that is thrown on it, and then the line, and then the advice to avoid cold and fatigue for nine days and a binding at the waist, "so a boy is born without fail". What is given for nine days is most likely the powder; «རྨི་ལམ» in the line is not explained by the page, and nothing in the page speaks of dreams. The sense stays unsettled, and the line gives no tie of dreams to the waning fortnight. The other print, known only from BDRC's snippet («མ་དུས་»), was not fetched (403).
