# The small element and the do gcig in reply 152

Two terms of the regent's reply 152 (ys:i513-514), on the tenth examination's strikes and the trigram repetition (WB vol. 2, pp. 86–90).

The small element (འབྱུང་ཆུང, 'byung chung). The reply says that when a woman's trigram has fallen on li or kham, "the small element does not go beyond rat, dragon and monkey", and that therefore the three Tuesdays gather. WB's chapter on the letters uses the same term of the five classes of letters, the "small elements" as against the great ones of the vowels (wb.2:p39-41). In the reply it is the element carried by the year animal; rat, dragon and monkey are one triad (the water one), so the clause says that the woman's own animal stands within that triad whatever trigram she has fallen to. This is a reading of the context, not a gloss; no text found defines the term for the animals.

The *do gcig* (དོ་གཅིག). The reply restates WB's line («when it has fallen on the *do gcig*, and the trigram of the *ma klungs*, and year, month, day and hour, four, are stacked») with an example: the *do gcig* "fallen on dwa" (དོ་གཅིག་དྭར་བབས་པ), the *ma klungs* born on dwa, and the year, month, day and hour of death bird or monkey, of the trigram's element. The *do gcig* is what falls (བབས) onto a trigram, and in the example it falls on the very trigram of the birth. That agrees with the notes' reading of the line as the descended trigram of the year of death ([[dead-trigram-fallen-to]]) and shows what the line asks: that the descended trigram and the birth trigram stand on one trigram, with the four times of death in its element. Whether «དོ་གཅིག» names the descended trigram or the coincidence the reply does not say, and the word is glossed nowhere.

The hand's «ཡི་བམ» (img. 514, l. 3): the sentence repeats «ལི་ཁམ» of the question just before it, and Yigdzin-1 reads «ལི་ཁམ» here as in the first; the hand is taken as li and kham, there being no other word that makes the sentence.
