# WB sets the eight planets round the directions from the east for war, a road and aims; the Sun in the east is good

Before the mansion verses, WB sets down a rule of direction for war, for setting out on a road and for seeking one's aims (p. 313, lines 1–10 of the 1996 print): the eight planets are named in the order Sun, Venus, Mars, Rāhu, Saturn, Moon, Mercury, Jupiter, "from the east, then east-south, in order", so each takes the next of the eight directions round from the east. The text then speaks of the "one who has power" at the final laying down: the Sun in the east is good, "the lord of water" bad, and "by this it is to be known for all". It closes: one's own direction is good, and the intermediate directions are examined well, the bad one directly facing; "let all works be accomplished". The line before the list, «མ་རྒྱབ་གྲོགས་མདུན་གཏད་པ་གཅེས», prizes the friend set in front and not behind, but which direction is the friend's is not said in these lines, and which of the eight planets stands on which of the eight points (Sun in the east, Venus east-south, and so on) is read from the order of the words, not stated. The passage carries no number of days and no work beyond war, a road and seeking aims.
