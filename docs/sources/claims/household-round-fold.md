# The household round: the fold, the refold and the places, for any number of members

How the household's plain row is folded into its places.

The procedure (the notes, vol. 2, pp. 490–491; WB vol. 1, p. 386; the *Moonbeams*, DZ f. 23a):

1. **The fold** (ཁུག་པ, khug pa): the row folded in half from its tail, the last pebble under the first, while the rows are equal; a single pebble left over is the fold's leftover (རྡེལ་རོ, rdel ro), set apart at the outer side.
2. **The refold** (ཡང་ཁུག, yang khug): the double row folded in half again, the last column under the first, into four rows; two pebbles left over (one column) are the refold's leftover, set apart.
3. **The places.** In each row only the first three pebbles and the last are read; whatever lies between is cast off (so the box "this row … cast off" over the fourth and fifth columns of the fold of five; the reading of «རྡེལ་ཕྲེང་གསུམ» as the first three pebbles of each row is ours). The top row: the god's place (ལྷ་ས, lha sa), best, middle and last, and its last pebble, the tail; below it the people's place (མི་ས, mi sa); below that the house's place (ཁྱིམ་ས, khyim sa) with the wealth's place (ནོར་ས, nor sa); the lowest row the enemy's place (དགྲ་ས, dgra sa). WB places the wealth's place one each in the god's and people's places and two each in the house's places, not saying which pebbles. The fold's leftover belongs to the god's and people's places, the refold's to the house's place (the notes, p. 491).

The notes' fold of five members (p. 491): 25 pebbles, rows of six, the 13th the fold's leftover, no refold leftover; the four rows read 1–6, 25–20, 14–19, 12–7.

**Any count, worked out here from that procedure** (not stated in the text): with N members the row holds 5N pebbles. The fold leaves one over when 5N is odd; the refold leaves one column of two over when the folded length ⌊5N/2⌋ is odd; the four rows are ⌊5N/4⌋ long. So the leftovers are the remainder of 5N divided by four: 0 none, 1 the fold's, 2 the refold's, 3 both. Four members give rows of five (one column cast off), five rows of six, six rows of seven with the refold's leftover, seven rows of eight with both leftovers. Three members would give rows of three, too short for three places and a tail, which agrees with the notes' "not fewer than four". The notes' own shortcut (p. 491) reads «གསུམ་གྱིས་བགོས», divided by three, yet names a remainder of three and grounds the rule on the rows "doubled and quadrupled"; only a division by four gives what it describes (a question for a reader of the tradition).

The *Moonbeams* names three places only, the god's, people's and house's, each by its first three pebbles, and folds "in half" from the tail, then takes from the tails of both rows into the rows below the people's place.
