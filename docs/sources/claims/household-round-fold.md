# The household round: the fold, the refold and the places, for any number of members

How the household's plain row is folded into its places.

The procedure (the notes, vol. 2, pp. 490–491; WB vol. 1, p. 386; the *Moonbeams*, DZ f. 23a):

1. **The fold** (ཁུག་པ, khug pa): the row folded in half from its tail, the last pebble under the first, while the rows are equal; a single pebble left over is the fold's leftover (རྡེལ་རོ, rdel ro), set apart at the outer side.
2. **The refold** (ཡང་ཁུག, yang khug): the double row folded in half again, the last column under the first, into four rows; two pebbles left over (one column) are the refold's leftover, set apart.
3. **The places.** In each row only the first three pebbles and the last are read; whatever lies between is cast off (so the box "these two rows cast off", «ཕྲེང་བ་འདི་གཉིས་དོར» in the Chengdu edition, over the fourth and fifth columns of the fold of five; the reading of «རྡེལ་ཕྲེང་གསུམ» as the first three pebbles of each row is ours). The top row: the god's place (ལྷ་ས, lha sa), best, middle and last, and its last pebble, the tail; below it the people's place (མི་ས, mi sa); below that the house's place (ཁྱིམ་ས, khyim sa) with the wealth's place (ནོར་ས, nor sa); the lowest row the enemy's place (དགྲ་ས, dgra sa). WB places the wealth's place one each in the god's and people's places and two each in the house's places, not saying which pebbles. The fold's leftover belongs to the god's and people's places, the refold's to the house's place (the notes, p. 491).

The notes' fold of five members (p. 491): 25 pebbles, rows of six, the 13th the fold's leftover, no refold leftover; the four rows read 1–6, 25–20, 14–19, 12–7.

**Any count, worked out here from that procedure** (not stated in the text): with N members the row holds 5N pebbles. The fold leaves one over when 5N is odd; the refold leaves one column of two over when the folded length ⌊5N/2⌋ is odd; the four rows are ⌊5N/4⌋ long. So the leftovers are the remainder of 5N divided by four: 0 none, 1 the fold's, 2 the refold's, 3 both. Four members give rows of five (one column cast off), five rows of six, six rows of seven with the refold's leftover, seven rows of eight with both leftovers. Three members would give rows of three, too short for three places and a tail, which agrees with the notes' "not fewer than four". The notes' own shortcut (p. 491) reads «གསུམ་གྱིས་བགོས», divided by three, yet names a remainder of three and grounds the rule on the rows "doubled and quadrupled"; only a division by four gives what it describes (a question for a reader of the tradition).

**The division by four in the other witnesses** (2026-10-25). Every print of the notes reads «གསུམ», Mipham's own included (wb.2:p490-491). But the rule the notes correct in that very passage, the *Good Vase*'s, is a division by four: its verse, carried also by the Bon *Mirror* (a witness only), reads "the five divided by four, with three left, two are the fold's leftover and one the refold's; with two or one left there is no refold" (sn:p70-71), and the notes object only to which leftover is the fold's and which the refold's, and to "no refold". LT cuts the count into four sets and says that more than three cannot be left (lt:p20-25). LT also says that with five pebbles a member the round needs four members and with six (a trigram pebble added, as an unidentified "son text" has it) three: 15 pebbles would give rows of three and 18 rows of four, so its limit is the same as the rows of a quarter worked out above. Mipham's pupil Zhechen Gyaltsab, restating the notes in his *Lotus Pond* with the notes' own chart of five, gives the leftovers by the folds alone, one the fold's, two the refold's, three one and two, with no division (zg:p285-286). A division by three cannot leave three, and with the convention that an exact division leaves the divisor it gives a leftover of two for four members (20 pebbles) and of three for six (30), where the fold leaves none and two: so no reading of «གསུམ» agrees with the fold the notes describe. The notes' own worked example, 25 pebbles, leaves one either way and does not tell them apart.

**Two ways of laying the four rows.** The notes fold the row in half and in half again: the fold's leftover is the middle pebble of the plain row, the refold's the two pebbles of the middle column (with 25: rows 1–6, 25–20, 14–19, 12–7, the 13th over). LT and the *Good Vase* (as the Bon *Mirror*'s chart of 23 pebbles shows it) lay four runs of a quarter of the count one after another: the first forward in the god's row, the second reversed in the enemy's, then the fold's leftovers, the third forward in the house's row, then the refold's, the fourth reversed in the people's. With one pebble left the two ways coincide pebble for pebble (with 25 the runs also give 1–6, 12–7, the 13th over, 14–19, 25–20). They part when two or three are left: with 35 (seven members) the notes' folds leave the 18th as the fold's leftover and the 9th and 27th as the refold's, with the enemy's row 17–10, while the runs leave the 17th and 18th as the fold's and the 27th as the refold's, with the enemy's row 16–9; and with two left the runs call the pair the fold's, where the notes' refold leaves it: the very difference the notes name. By the rank of the sources the notes' way stands. Kun dga' rig 'dzin's worked sheet of seven members from the *Spor thang* (kd.3:p726) would show which way and which division it follows, but has only been seen in the etext.

The *Moonbeams* names three places only, the god's, people's and house's, each by its first three pebbles, and folds "in half" from the tail, then takes from the tails of both rows into the rows below the people's place.
