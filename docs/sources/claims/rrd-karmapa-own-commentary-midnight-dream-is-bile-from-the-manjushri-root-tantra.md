# The third Karmapa's own commentary on the Zab mo nang gi don: evening phlegm, midnight bile, dawn wind, from the Mañjuśrī root tantra; no Māra and no spirits (RD405)

The third Karmapa's own commentary on his Zab mo nang gi don explains the appearances of dream by the channel-places the mind passes through with the life-wind and by the constituents, and quotes the verse: in the evening (སྲོད, srod) phlegm (བད་ཀན, bad kan) predominates, at midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) bile (མཁྲིས་པ, mkhris pa) is said, dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) is the time of the wind (རླུང, rlung); the dream then appears as an object by the nature of the constituent. He says it is a summary of what the Blessed One taught at length in the root tantra of Mañjuśrī and that it is said many times in the tantras. The images that follow are the wind's (blue, black, flying, riding, unsteady), bile's (fire, red and yellow, gold, yellow robes, fright) and phlegm's (water, snow, white, women, elephants, silver, pearls, the bliss of touch, steadiness).

In this commentary the midnight dream is the dream of bile; no word of Māra, of spirits or of habit stands in it, and no remedy. The same author's verses on his own dream (rrd.5:p57-59) give Marpa's saying that the midnight dream is the power of spirits. His own commentary and his verses on dreams therefore characterise the midnight dream in two ways, and neither text reads the one as the other. The source named here, the root tantra of Mañjuśrī, is the tantra whose Derge text has not the verse (see the claim on the humour verse); the commentary of the Karmapa is a second witness, with Zhwa dmar IV, that the tradition of the Zab mo nang gi don takes it from that tantra. The commentary does not name which of its parts the verse is in.
