# The Tibetan Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya renders the cows' line as «nang par» (the morning): an early witness for gosarge

The Tibetan translation of the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* in the Derge Tengyur (Yan lag brgyad pa'i snying po, etext VE23703_1514, page marker [p257]) renders the cows' line of Śārīrasthāna 6.63, Sanskrit «gosarge tadaharmahat», with the words «nang par mthong dang gnyid ma log … de yi nyin par 'bras chen 'gyur»: a dream seen at the morning (nang par, ནང་པར) without the sleep being broken, and the fruit great on that very day. The Tibetan has no word for cows or their path; it translates the time. So the translators, working from the Indian text of about the eleventh century, read the line as a time of day, the one «gosarge» (the cows' release, early morning) gives and «gomārge» (on the cows' path) does not. This is the oldest witness to the line reached so far, older than the manuscripts behind the Nirṇayasāgara apparatus, and it agrees with «gosarge».

Kunte's 1880 print (Bombay, with Aruṇadatta's Sarvāṅgasundarā; Internet Archive text layer, OCR) reads «gosarge tadaharmahat» in its text and the lemma of the commentary «gosarge gavāṃ mokṣakāle» ("at the time of the cows' release"); the OCR shows no foot-note with another word at the line. That is a seventh print agreeing; the page was read in the text layer only, not on the image.

The Tibetan etext spells the first part of the night «mtshan stong» (the Four Tantras' word is «mtshan stod»); the etext is not read on the scan.
