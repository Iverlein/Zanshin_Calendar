# The witnesses of Klong rdol bla ma's dream verses on BDRC: eight etexts and the manuscript, what each is and how it reads

BDRC's exact-phrase search (2026-11-10) finds the thirds-of-the-night verses «གསུམ་ཆ་ཕྱིས་འབྱུང་གསལ་བྱེད་ཡིན» in eight etexts, all search-only (the etext API answers 403) except RST. The snippets say what each is:

| Witness (BDRC work) | What it is | What the snippet shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RST (MW1ALS06558E) | the ACIP etext of the whole text, Klong rdol bla ma's | read in full (rst:p1-8); reads «ཐོ་རངས་ཁ་ཡིན» |
| KBS (MW20242_55798C) | the 1993 article's print, open scans | the verses with the article's title and Klong rdol bla ma's name (kbs:p68-70); «སུམ་ཆར», «དུས་ཀྱི» |
| KBS second print (MW1CZ690_FBE63E, in a collection of studies, pp. 662-663) | the same article | «ཐོ་རངས་དུས་ཀྱི་ནམ་གྱི་གསུམ་ཆ་ལ» after the words "klong rdol bla mas" (Wylie as in the snippet) |
| MDP (MW21880, Sha bo Tshe ring and 'Brug rgyal mkhar) | the folk collection; the text from p. 138 (mdp-p138-is-the-rst-text) | the opening verses with «མ་གཅིག་ཟབ་མོ་གཅོད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་འབུམ» and the dream verses to the death omens, RST's wording ("tshe rabs sngon ma", "zhon nas gyen rgyug mthon po'i" (Wylie)) |
| Padma rdo rje (MW25126), the work on sleep and dreams | quotes the whole text | headed "klong rdol bla ma ngag dbang blo bzang" (Wylie as in the snippet), then the opening verses; the verses carry small interlinear marks between the images in the snippet; reads "tshe rabs sngon ma", "zhon nas gyen rgyug mthon po'i" (Wylie) |

The manuscript (gdm) is the ninth witness and the only one with scans of a manuscript hand; it is not found by the etext search because it has no etext. The good-sign verse «རི་བོ་མཐོན་པོའི་རྩེ་ལ་འཛེགས་པ་རྨི» has four hits (RST and three search-only prints), the death-omen line «གཤིན་དང་མཉམ་པོར་ཆང་འཐུང་གར་བྱེད» five (RST, MDP, Padma, the book of old rites, the volume of crafts); no hit in a Gcod collection, a medical or an astrological text outside the list. The three books and the volume of crafts quote the text beside other dream material and give no source of their own: they are copies, not further witnesses of an earlier text.
