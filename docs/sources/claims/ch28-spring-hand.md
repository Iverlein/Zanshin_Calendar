# The year read on three spring dates by the moon on the hand

WB vol. 2, pp. 26–27: on the 27th, 25th and 23rd of the three spring months (first, middle, last; the verse does not say whether one date belongs to each month in turn or all three to each), six things are read for the year: where the moon falls on the hand of the stars (the ogress's severed hand, p. 26), the wind and its direction, water, clouds and a substance. The first date rules the early sowing, the second the middle, the third the late.

Where it falls, in the text's order: at the wrist below the thumb, good; the thumb's root, good for livestock, much rain; its middle, great drought; its tip, men die by the knife but the year is fair; in front of the thumb, good crops; between thumb and index, quarrels; the index, rough for lords and ministers, bad for sheep; between index and middle, rain even, crops middling to good; the middle finger, bad for emperors and teachers, frost, hail and blight; between middle and ring, much snow; the ring finger, women's star, great drought, a bad year; between ring and little, great drought, good for livestock; the little finger's tip, bad for children and the old; its middle, frost; its root, a good year; the lower palm [?], a good year; hanging below, floods; at the wrist, people and wealth go round [?]; the outer corner, showers; the bracelet, good fortune; below it, good for all; the centre, famine; hovering above, neutral.

The dates are calculable from the calendar; where the moon stands is observed against a figure (p. 28) whose labels were not read.
