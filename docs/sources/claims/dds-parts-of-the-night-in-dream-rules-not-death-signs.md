# The texts that count the parts of the night for dreams give dream rules, not death signs; only the Gdams ngag mdzod names the midnight dream as a death sign (RD374)

RD374. Texts that count the parts of the night for a dream give them as dream rules, not death signs, and none of them ties a death sign to a part of the night except the Gdams ngag mdzod's «midnight dream»: (a) the Four Tantras: the dream of the early night (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod) is certainly forgotten and gives no fruit, the dawn dream (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) seen and clear gives fruit (dds.21:p234-235); Byang pa's commentary adds that a dream up to the half of the early night has its fruit far and weak, and a dream in the morning (ནང་པར, nang par, when the cattle are let out) seen without sleeping again gives its fruit the same day (dds.20:p213-220); (b) the Vajrāvalī rite: the first watch, evening, is mixed with the day's imprints; the second and third, midnight, are thick sleep and give no dream; the last, dawn, is clear and is the one examined (dds.17:p392-393); (c) the third Karmapa's verses and Marpa's saying: evening habit, midnight spirits, dawn true (dds.18:p57; claim lgd-three-parts-of-night-named-in-the-karmapa-and-the-rin-chen-gter-mdzod). The sources disagree on midnight: for the Vajrāvalī rite it is no dream at all, for the Karmapa the power of spirits, for the Gdams ngag mdzod the dream to be examined for death signs. No text counted the three or four watches by the hour; the Four Tantras and Byang pa count two parts (early night, dawn) and the Rgyud sde kun btus three watches.
