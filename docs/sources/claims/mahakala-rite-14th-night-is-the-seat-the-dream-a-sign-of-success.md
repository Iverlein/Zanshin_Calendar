# In the Mahākāla sādhana the night of the fourteenth of the dark half is the sitting night, not a dream's; the dream is a sign of success, its lowest rank armed, leading the army, killing and eating flesh (RD386)

In the Mahākāla sādhana of the Derge Tengyur (vol. 28 pp. 496–502, «Dpal nag po chen po yum can gyi sgrub thabs», whose colophon says it arose from Nāgārjuna and names Śrīvanaratna and 'Jam dpal ye shes as translators) the night of the fourteenth day of the dark half (ནག་ཕྱོགས་ཚེས་བཅུ་བཞིའི་མཚན་མོ) is the night on which the mantrin sits in the charnel ground in black clothes to begin the accomplishing; it is not the night of any dream. The dream comes in the text as a sign (མཚན་མ) in two places: in the approach (བསྙེན་པ), "in reality or in a dream" a great black woman, black birds and dogs and jackals or wolves become glad; and in the accomplishing, three ranks of sign: the best is the body of the Great Black One seen directly, in a dream or in an experience of meditation; the middle is light-rays seen in the mandala; the lowest is a dream in which one is armed, bears weapons, leads the army, kills the wicked and eats human flesh. The text gives these dreams no part of the night and no remedy; for exhaustion it gives a black cloth laid over the mandala. The same night of the fourteenth is named once more on p. 502 for the fierce fire-offering against the one to be accomplished. RD381 left open whether the Mahākāla dream is bound to the night of the fourteenth: it is not, the night belongs to the rite's seat. A dream of being armed, in a Mahākāla rite, is a good sign for the one who does the rite; it is not a list of dream images for the ordinary dreamer.
