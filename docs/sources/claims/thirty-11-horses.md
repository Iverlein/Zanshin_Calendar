# The thirty: the eleventh point, the precious supreme horse, for horses: the eight horse reckonings

The eleventh of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the precious supreme horse (རྟ་མཆོག་རིན་ཆེན, rta mchog rin chen), is read for horses: the eight horse reckonings. Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the golden bit is lost from the mouth | Not lost from the stallion's mouth; the horse herds grow in the forefathers' place | The golden bit is lost from the mouth: broken or lost, bad | — |
| 2 | Whether the leaping peg (མཆོང་ཕུར, mchong phur) has gone up into the sky | Not gone up; the stallion's life is firm | It has gone up into the sky, bad | — |
| 3 | Whether the golden saddle has fallen down the precipice (རྦབ་གཡང, rbab g.yang) | Not fallen | It falls down the precipice and breaks: the king spirits (རྒྱལ་པོ, rgyal po) harm the horses continually | Throw the horse ransom (གླུད, glud) and the king thread-cross (མདོས, mdos) to the east |
| 4 | Whether the hair of the nape has fallen out | Not fallen out | It has fallen out: after the blue horse-day («རྟ་ཞག་སྔོན་པོ», not understood) the prosperity (གཡང, g.yang) is said to decline | Recite the horses' calling back prosperity (གཡང་ལོན, g.yang lon) and protecting prosperity (གཡང་སྐྱབས, g.yang skyabs) |
| 5 | Whether the halter rings (ཨ་ལུང, a lung) of the turquoise bridle are worn | Not worn, good | Worn: good for white and yellow horses, bad for red and black | — |
| 6 | Whether the horse cloth has gone | Not gone, good | The horse cushion of the pasture is stripped: when there is a horse there is no saddle cloth | The horses' dhāraṇī (རྟ་གཟུངས, rta gzungs) and the horse ransom |
| 7 | Whether the tail tip of the great horse leaks | It does not leak, good | It leaks: horse and owner, if old, bear it; or horse disease comes, take care | — |
| 8 | Whether a thief (དོམ་དོམ, dom dom) has stolen it | The horses' prosperity has not declined | A thief carries it off; the horses' prosperity has declined | Calling back prosperity |

Whose year is reckoned: The horse owner's year (notes, p. 532).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
