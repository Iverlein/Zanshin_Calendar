# Gnam chos: the Great Compassionate One's dream rite, mantra water and the wheel at the pillow

The Great Compassionate One's dream examination in the Gnam chos begins on printed p. 558 with a rite of mantra water and a mantra over the ashes of a dead woman, smeared on five places of the body; a second, "new prophecy" text opens with the instruction to draw a wheel at one's pillow (a lotus from the middle of a lake at its centre, a mantra in its ten spokes). The two pages read (machine reading only, unchecked on the scan) state no lunar day, hour of the night or count of nights; the prophecies are dated only by the night they were spoken (the 21st of a Fire-Dragon month, the 14th of a Fire-Monkey month). The rest (pp. 559-561, machine reading, no MITRA, scan not checked syllable by syllable) is the same prophecy: the wheel is finished with rings of lotus petals and mountain-fire and a vulture at its centre, drawn with vulture flesh; paper rolls with a bird's beak are set right and left of the sleeper; the five limbs are washed and rubbed with vulture-flesh powder, mantra water is drunk, and the mantra is recited until sleep comes, "through this the dream becomes clear". The rite does not read the dream (no sign is interpreted) and gives no lunar day, hour of the night or count of nights: the text goes straight from the rite to its colophon, where the dakini Ye shes mtsho rgyal is said to have prophesied it on the fourteenth night of the Pleiades month (a date of revelation, as in the two colophons before it). The Mtsho rgyal text is therefore a clarifying rite, not a dream-reading.
