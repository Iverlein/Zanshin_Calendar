# The circles: the eleventh, the divine sheep, for land and treasury

The eleventh of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the yellow divine sheep, is read for land, country and treasury. Its starting point: the pig of the enemy element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points, turned from the pig of the enemy element of the one reckoned: whether one has moved from the land; whether the peak god has gone up; whether the cook has turned away («གློ་འདོགས»); whether the golden pillar and turquoise beam are broken; whether others have planted boundary marks in the father's field.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |

| Life force | the paternal estate and home not moved: this good land, kept by the son, is held: good. | the home and land moved, the land split; since looking east from the land, earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag), *klu* and *gnyan* harm; moving from the land: bad. | the large and small *dong tse* of the earth lords; put in the treasure of the eight great *klu*; send the earth lords' thread-cross and the like. |
| Body | the castle's peak god not gone up: supremely good; the male god's banner set on the castle; strife among men in the water («མི་དར་ཆུ་རུ་འཁྲུག་པ་འོང», not understood); castle and owner not parted: good. | the castle's god gone up from the peak: the castle's site and line cut off, or pollution strikes; loss to the castle. | clear the gods («ལྷ་སེལ»), the war god's praise. |
| Power | the cook (glossed: the name of the one who cooks the food) has not turned away: good; though the kitchen is miserly, the guests are well satisfied. | turned away: the nourishment of food small. | hide the wheel of the mixed mewa under the hearth and fasten the power-raising on the cook. |
| Luck | golden pillar and turquoise beam neither broken nor burnt: good; the father's land firm, no illness or gossip in the men's and women's places. | the golden pillar broken, the turquoise beam burnt: hostile talk; the house feared to collapse. | gather the heart of the earth lords' abode, the pit against gossip, set up the restoring of the luck. |
| Mewa | no one has planted a mark in the field: no dispute over the field, workers without illness, long life, good harvests: good. | a mark planted in the middle of the rhinoceros-boundary: the field like a belly («གྲོད་པ»), or like a scorpion cut at the waist; by its force fortune declines. | offer to the land's local god for the field's support and strive at summoning the earth's fortune. If this meets the nine-eye it is rough. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
