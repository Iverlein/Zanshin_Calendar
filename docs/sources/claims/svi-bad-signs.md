# The Svapna-īkṣā's bad signs

Advayavajra's bad signs, which "come as torment of the mind" and are taken as signs of virtue and non-virtue (p. 262): an enemy stripping one of ornaments; a young woman grieving; being put inside a dungeon; goods seized and put in irons; being driven by evil beings of the lower worlds; wild beasts hanging from the body; being bound with thread and covered with filth; eating and drinking filth; fleeing at the sight of the guru, the master, friends or parents; a deserted place without people, or a wilderness, wretched with hunger and thirst, crying "alas" and a yak's sorrowful call; armour stripped and struck with weapons; being set on by tigers, jackals and animals in the middle of a forest. The text gives no verdict for any of them but "bad sign".
