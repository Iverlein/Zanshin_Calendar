# The Karmapa's dream verses record his own dawn dream; the saying of habit, spirits and true is Marpa's, and no hour is named (RD384)

In the third Karmapa's verses on dreams (Rang byung rdo rje's collected works vol. 5, etext p. 57-59) the three-part rule is a saying he quotes of Marpa, and what the verses record is his own dream at dawn (RD384, 2026-11-10, etext only).

The verses open as a personal record: he prayed to the Lord at dusk (སྲོད, srod) and "at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) a sign of the night came thus". Then, "in general", the dream of dusk is habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags), the dream of midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) the power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang), "whatever comes at dawn is true", as Marpa's mouth said, and he sets down "a few dreams" because of it. What follows is the dream "of sleep at dawn": a mountain like a jewel, a god-palace, Avalokiteśvara, and a yogin beside him who names Tilopa, Nāropa, Marpa and Mi[la] as those who realised mahāmudrā by serving the lama and by austerity; he wakes at that moment. So the dream he trusts enough to write is the one the saying calls true, and it carries counsel, not a sign of the day to come. The saying names three parts of the night and no hour, watch or span, and no text of the volume says where dusk ends or midnight begins.

His other dream records in the same volume (read in the etext, not made passages except p. 64): three place the dream at dawn (pp. 9, 57-58 and 64, the last a dream of Orgyenpa, «ཐོ་རངས་དུས་སུ»), and the others (pp. 10, 33, 125-126) say only "on a night" (ནུབ་གཅིག, ནུབ་མོ). None names dusk or midnight as the time of a dream.
