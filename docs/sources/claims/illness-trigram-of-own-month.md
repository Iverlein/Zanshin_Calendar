# The trigram of the date in its own month: the four times' trigrams by WB's almanac rule

WB's «སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་ལིར་བརྩིས་པའི། །ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་བཞི་ཡི་ཁམས། … །རང་ཟླར་ཚེས་པའི་སྤར་ཁ་ཡིན།» (wb.1:p435), "the elements of the four, year, month, day and hour, counted with the tiger, horse and dog on li … it is the trigram that begins in its own month", is a pointer to the rule by which a date's trigram is found, not a new rule:

- WB's almanac (wb.1:p174-178, p. 178) gives it: the trigram and mewa "rise on the dates" (ཚེས་ལ་འཆར, tshes la 'char); in tiger, horse and dog months the first date begins (ཚེས, tshes) with li (ལི, li), in pig, sheep and hare months with zin, in mouse, dragon and monkey months with kham, in bird, ox and snake months with dwa.
- Mipham's notes (wb.2:p535-537, scan) put the noun in: «སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་ཟླ་བ་ལི་ཡིས་ཚེས», then from that date the trigram turns one place a date, and within the date through the eight by eighths of the day and of the night. His gloss on p. 435 itself (wb.2:p497) repeats WB's words with «སོགས», "and so on": the four times' trigrams found by this rule.
- LT (lt:p9-11, scan) and the *Moonbeams* (DZ, in KD vol. 3, etext p. 514) give the same four triads and "driven on in order" for the dates, and the hour's trigram from the day's at dawn.

So «རང་ཟླར་ཚེས་པའི་སྤར་ཁ» reads: the day's trigram is the one that falls to that date counted on from the trigram with which its own month begins (li for a tiger, horse or dog month); and "counted with the tiger, horse and dog on li" is the head of that rule standing for all four triads. For the hour, the trigram runs on from the day's. For the year and the month the rule of p. 178 says nothing; the *Speckled Elements*' chain «ལོ་ལ་སྤར་ཁ་ལི … ཟླ་བ་ཡང་ལི … ཚེས་ཀྱང་གཅིག་ལ་ལི … དུས་ཚོད་ཀྱང་ནམ་ལངས་ལི» (ys.2:i016-017) would carry it from the year down, a reading the pool does not settle (q390). LT's English in lt:p9-11 renders the triads as days; WB p. 178 and the notes make them months.
