# Scheuermann's chapter on the Svapnohana: what its abstract says, and that the chapter is unread

Scheuermann's chapter on the *Svapnohana* (SCH, pp. 161–180) was not read: the Brill page answers a human check, no open or repository copy exists (RD257, PLAN.md Dead ends), and what follows rests on the chapter's published abstract and the editors' summary of the volume, both facts only and in our words.

The chapter takes the *Svapnohana* (rmi lam brtag pa, Tohoku 1749) as one of two short stand-alone dream-divination texts in the Tengyur (བསྟན་འགྱུར, bstan 'gyur), the other being Advayavajra's (the pool's SVI). It gives the work's authorship, together with its Tibetan translation, to the Indian master Vibhūticandra of the twelfth to thirteenth century. It presents the text's content and its main subjects, sets out the associations between signs and meanings, and relates the examination of dreams in the text to the dream images used in Tibetan and Indian works on meditation, which places the work in Buddhist literature at large. The editors' summary adds the chapter's conclusion: from the signs it examines, the text stands firmly in an Indian tradition of dream-divination texts which, though kept in the Tengyur, need not count as specifically Buddhist.

What the abstract and the summary do not say, and so is not known from them: whether a Sanskrit original survives, what its parallels are, and how Tibetan readers used it. The editors' volume index lists the work on pp. 161–177 and a separate entry for the *Svapnekṣā* (the pool's SVI).

Beside RD207: nothing here contradicts the reading of the text. The remedy of RD207 (a fire offering with the Gāyatrī, svo:p271) is a rite with no Buddhist deity named in it, which fits the chapter's conclusion; that fit is our inference from the pool, not a statement of the chapter. The attribution of authorship, not only of translation, differs from the Derge colophon: see svo-authorship-witnesses.

State: the abstract of the chapter (Crossref and OpenAlex record, DOI 10.1163/9789004410688_009) and a search-engine summary of Brill's volume front matter and index (the Brill pages themselves answer the human check and were not opened); the chapter is not read.
