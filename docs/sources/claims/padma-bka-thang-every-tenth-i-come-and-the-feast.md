# Padma bka' thang ch. 102: every time the tenth comes I come to Tibet, and what one has is circulated as the feast

In ch. 102 (the Guru's prophecy for the prince Lha rje, with the vows to keep) the Guru says «ཚེས་བཅུ་བྱུང་རེས་ང་ཉིད་བོད་དུ་འོང་», every time the tenth comes round I myself come to Tibet (obk:p337). The next line, «རླུང་ཁྱེར་རྫས་བཞིན་ཅི་ཡོད་ཚོགས་སུ་བསྐོར», tells him to circulate whatever he has, which the wind would carry off like goods, as the feast (tshogs). So this treasure gives both halves of the pair that later texts name: the Guru comes on the tenth, and the feast is the way he is met. The line does not say waxing or waning (compare the Seven Chapters, which says waxing: tenth-day-guru-comes-on-the-waxing-tenth-in-the-seven-chapters), names no twenty-fifth and no ḍākinī.

It is the same promise that Mipham quotes from Ratna gling pa and the Key Instruction (tenth-day-padmasambhava-promise-to-come); the wording «byung res nga nyid bod du 'ong» is the Padma bka' thang's own, and two further etexts quote it, both lives of 'Jigs med chos kyi dbang po (BDRC phrase search «ཚེས་བཅུ་བྱུང་རེས་ང་ཉིད་བོད་དུ་འོང» 3 hits, 2026-11-21). For whom: it is said to a prince and the lay and clerical keepers of the treasure; no restriction to vows. State: etext, the line seen on the scan (img. 351).
