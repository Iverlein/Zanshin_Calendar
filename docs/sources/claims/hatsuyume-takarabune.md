# The treasure ship under the pillow and the remedies for a bad first dream

The treasure ship (宝船) custom of the first dream: a paper picture of the Seven Gods of Fortune in their ship, bearing the palindromic verse «ながきよのとおのねぶりのみなめざめなみのりぶねのおとのよきかな», was put under the pillow on the night of 2 January; the custom is attested by the Muromachi period and in the Edo period the pictures were hawked as woodblock prints. If the dream was bad, the ship was floated down a river next morning, with the bad dream; if good, it was offered to gods and buddhas. Further remedies for a bad dream in the entries: the picture of the baku (獏), the beast that eats dreams, or saying "baku, baku", or telling the dream first to a nandina tree and shaking it. A good first dream is the Edo saying "first Fuji, second hawk, third eggplant" (the entry for 夢占 names it as the sign of good dreams in general, not of the first dream only). The entries name no Edo book; Japanese Wikipedia lists nine Edo texts for the saying (the 『甲子夜話』, the 『嬉遊笑覧』 and others), not read.
