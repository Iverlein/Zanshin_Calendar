# The date's earth lords keep the date's span: they change when the date changes

Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan, explaining why the day-entry's six sets of figures matter, says that the date lasts as long as its water-hours (ཆུ་ཚོད) last, counted in order from the day's rising; when they are used up the date is ended and the next date has come, even if the weekday has not changed. "For this reason the earth lords and the like that belong to the date (ཚེས་ལ་དབང་བའི་ས་བདག་སོགས) keep company with that date's course and are to be reckoned the same way" (kd.1:p496-498). So the date's earth lords change where the date changes, at the moment the date's time is complete, not at the day's rising or at midnight. The same passage gives the mansion its own span: the mansion's remainder at the day's rising is what it holds over the ground that day, and when it ends the planet-and-mansion concord changes though the planet has not moved.

This is a statement of the author about the almanac, a modern work; it agrees with how the almanac itself lists the earth lords among the day's entries (kd.1:p495), and it sets no weight on them (kd.1:p498-499, which names none among the factors).
