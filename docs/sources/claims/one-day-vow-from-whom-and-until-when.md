# The one-day vow: taken from another before sunrise, kept until the next sunrise

From whom and until when the one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas) is taken, as Lo chen Dharma shrI sums up the *Treasury* (dpn:i58, scan) and as the Derge commentary on the *Treasury* has it (akb:i362-363, akb:i364-365, etext):

- **Until when**: one day and night. It is taken before sunrise and kept until the sun has risen on the next day; the two limits of a vow are the end of life and the end of a day and night, and a longer keeping is the day and night repeated.
- **From whom**: from another person, not by oneself, sitting lower than the giver, repeating the words after him, without new ornaments. For the Vaibhāṣikas the giver is a fully ordained monk only; for the Sautrāntikas anyone from a lay devotee keeping the fast upward.
- **Who**: a man or a woman; one who is not a lay devotee may take it if he goes for refuge that day (akb:i365-366, etext).
- **The standing pledge**: one who has pledged beforehand to keep it always on the eighth (the commentary on the *Treasury*), or on the full moon, the new moon and the eighth of every month (Lo chen), still takes it each time; the earlier pledge allows him to take it after the meal (Vaibhāṣika) or by himself before an image or other support (Sautrāntika).

For everyone who takes it, lay people first; common to the schools (the Abhidharma), restated by a Nyingma author.
