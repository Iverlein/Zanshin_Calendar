# The Sidha-sa-ra's dream signs of the sick and the dream of each nature

The medical work whose Tibetan translation prints its Sanskrit title as Sidha-sa-ra (SDS) puts the dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) of a sick person into its chapter on the signs of death (འཆི་ལྟས, 'chi ltas), in the order bad dreams, good dreams, the messenger (ཕྲིན་པ, phrin pa), the physician's own setting out. A bad dream makes a healthy person ill and a sick one die: oil on the body, going south, riding a pig, buffalo, beast of prey, donkey or she-camel, a woman in black led south, falling from a height, red garlands and red clothes, being carried off by water, eating boiled meat, finding iron. A good dream gives a healthy person wealth and a sick one a quick recovery: gods, brahmins, banner, parasol, cattle, lotus, king, white flowers and cloth, clear water, blazing fire, a living friend, riding a horse, climbing a mountain, a fruit tree, a mirror, crossing a great water.

The same work, in its first chapters, links the dream to a person's nature: the wind (རླུང, rlung) nature flies in the sky, the bile (མཁྲིས་པ, mkhris pa) nature sees blazing fire, the phlegm (བད་ཀན, bad kan) nature sees water and white things. Read in the etext only.
