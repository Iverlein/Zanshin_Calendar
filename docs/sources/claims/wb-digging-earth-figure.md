# Breaking the ground: the earth figure, the measures and the signs

Breaking the ground (WB vol. 2, p. 387): a figure of the earth (the girl's house) as tall as oneself is laid out for the digging, its body in colours: the tail very blue, the secret place green, the navel like a lotus root, the arms golden, the heart green, the throat red, the ears white, the crown blue. It is fixed by the season (head to the upper south at the start of winter, one house a day, turning clockwise). Digging is done with milk and herbs, a vajra drawn on the hoe, one-pointed meditation, the right hand above. The measuring stake is half a height; the face one height; the navel a height and six finger-breadths; the centre of the ground the secret place; nine parts beside the body, with the square held above; five clods dug from the part not touching the body increase the dharma and wealth. Results by the place dug (back: the digger dies or moves; the nine parts: kin die; the tail: livestock), by the soil found (oily, black, jewels, five colours: good; charcoal, stone, bone, rotten wood: bad), by water in the trace and its fish, by the colour of the soil (yellow for the merchant, white for the royal and brahmin classes, blue for the common, red [as printed]), then the filling with grain and jewels, a torma and truth-words.
