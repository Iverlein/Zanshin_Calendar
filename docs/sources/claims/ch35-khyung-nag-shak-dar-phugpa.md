# WB ch. 35 pp. 450–451: Khyung nag Shākya dar the Phugpa named for the practice of black reckoning

Pages 450–451 name the master Khyung nag Shākya dar (ཁྱུང་ནག་ཤཱཀ་དར, khyung nag shAk dar) twice in the account of black reckoning. On p. 450 the way a student begins is given in his words («མཁས་དབང་ཁྱུང་ནག་ཤཱཀ་དར་བས།»): seek a learned teacher, learn the outer, inner and secret orders and their practice, and at last speak true words for each case. On p. 451, after the list of the rite-works, the texts the practice rests on are «ཁྱུང་ནག་ཤཱཀ་དར་ཕུག་པ་བ། །ཁུ་དབོན་བརྒྱུད་པར་བཅས་པ་དང་།»: Khyung nag Shākya dar the Phugpa (ཕུག་པ་བ, phug pa ba), with his uncle-and-nephew line, then the documents of authoritative scholars and the White Beryl itself. The word «ཕུག» stands at the end of the print line and is cut off on the Gser thang scan; it was read on the 1996 print (I1KG12907 img. 459). Read on the scan (RD84, 2026-10-31). The page names him; it gives no rule of his.
