# The Blue Beryl and the Moonbeams on the mother's dream of a man in ch. 2

Ch. 2's line «ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང» (st:p21) in the two works RD300 did not reach, both read as BDRC etexts (2026-11-10; etext only, not read on a scan).

The Blue Beryl (བཻ་ཌཱུརྱ་སྔོན་པོ, bai ḍūrya sngon po; Sde srid Sangs rgyas rgya mtsho; etext VEIE0OPICF5AF372_I1PD411, the Dpe tshogs print, its part on the Explanatory Tantra; etext pp. 138–139 are printed pp. 110–111). The exact-phrase search did not find the Four Tantras' line in it, because it does not quote it. It quotes the Eight Limbs' verses on the signs of a boy (the mother glad, a man seen in her dream, the right breast's milk first, the right of the womb high, the womb round), then says in its own words: in the ninth month the right side of the womb is a little higher by her leaning on the right, the body is light by nature, in her dreams she sees men many times (མང་དུ) and desire is not born, and the right breast's milk comes first and drips; these are the signs of a boy. The girl's signs are mostly the reverse: she desires to meet a man and delights in song, dance and ornament. So the Blue Beryl takes the dream as of men seen many times, ཡང as the body's lightness, and adds that desire does not rise: the same three points as the great commentary I5618 in st-dream-ch2-commentaries. དག is not glossed as a word and is not read as clear.

The Moonbeams (ཟླ་ཟེར, zla zer; the Tengyur's Yan lag brgyad pa'i snying po'i rnam par 'grel pa tshig gi don gyi zla zer, Sde dge etext VE23703_1517, whose colophon names the teacher Zla ba la dga' ba). It is a commentary on the Eight Limbs, not on the Four Tantras, so it has the Eight Limbs' own verse on the boy, whose line «དགའ་ཞིང་རྨི་ལམ་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང» matches ch. 2's (etext p. 37). Its gloss: the woman delights in speaking of things with men's names, and in her dream sees a man and an elephant and the like. So the Moonbeams takes the dream as of a man or an elephant, one sign among the verse's, neither as many men nor as a clear dream; the Eight Limbs' verse has no ཡང and no དག, so there is no gloss of them. Byang pa quotes this work as «Zla zer».

Count: with RD300, four commentaries and the Blue Beryl take the dream as of many men; the Moonbeams, on the Eight Limbs' parallel verse, as of a man and an elephant; none reads དག as clear. Facts only; the wording is the works', not ours.
