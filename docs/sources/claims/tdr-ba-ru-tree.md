# The ba ru tree of the Śramaṇā rite is the myrobalan tree

RD393 (2026-11-11). In the Śramaṇā rite of the be'u bum one of the places for the recitation is "at the foot of a «བ་རུའི་ཤིང» (ba ru tree) or of a banyan tree (ནྱ་གྲོ་དྷ, nyagrodha)", a hundred thousand recitations in silence ([p339]). The dictionaries (tmz:box1) give ba ru as a fruit tree, one of the three fruits of Tibetan medicine with a ru ra and skyu ru (the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho), and as "beleric myrobalan" (Ives Waldo). So the rite sits under a myrobalan tree or a banyan, two trees of the Indian countryside; the dictionaries do not say why these two.
