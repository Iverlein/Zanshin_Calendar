# Death on each lunar mansion, and by element and year

WB vol. 2, pp. 119–122 (read on the Gser thang reprint 2026-10-23; the full table is in the passage). For a death on each of the mansions (རྒྱུ་སྐར, rgyu skar) WB says what it portends, what is done, and the skull and place for the wheel. The portents a reading would name:

- bad, or bad for a count: *smin drug*, *snar ma*, *nabs so* and *rgyal*, *skag* (printed «སྐྲག»), *me bzhi* (bad for seven), *sa ga* (the ruin-maker, not allowed; bad for three), *lha mtshams* (bad for five), *chu stod* (bad for small children; luck spoiled, it wants human corpses), *chu smad* (bad for people and livestock), *mon dre*, *nam gru* (bad for luck and light), *bra nye* (the dead goes to a bad rebirth); *mgo* "six heads"; *lag* "the hand used up"; *sa ri* the planet-king angered; *gro bzhin* and *byi bzhin* "grazing on the wealth"; *snron* "a mishap";
- no repetition: *nag pa*; good for the dead: *dbyu gu* ("the heart of all");
- the four first and four last mansions take readings in the four directions; a death on a bad planet and mansion is best kept secret for the land's sake.

By element (p. 122): earth element with an eastern mansion, an axe, saw, adze and sickle on the corpse; iron with a southern, a black animal's horn of water; water with an intermediate, a beam; wood with a western, a board burned with nine eyes in the hand; fire with a northern, a five-earth lotus on the corpse's cloth.

By the dead's year (p. 122), when the attachment to the living is great, a wheel in a named skull pressed by a man of a named year at that animal's hour: rat on the six northern mansions, a sheep's skull; ox on *skag* or *gre*, a weasel's; tiger on *smin drug*, a monkey's; hare on *snar ma* or an eastern mansion, a bird's; dragon on *spo* (?), an ox's; snake on a mansion printed «སྦ་སྨད» (not identified), a pig's; horse on *chu* or *gre*, a rat's; sheep on *byi bzhin*, an ox's; monkey on *lag* or *sor* (?), a horse's or snake's; bird on *lag sor* (?), under a horse's hoof; dog on *bra nye*, a goat's; pig on *mon dre*, a dog's. Mipham counts the mansions' remedies twenty-seven, the first mansions' seventeen, and the five mansion graves with the year's mansions twenty-four (p. 517).
