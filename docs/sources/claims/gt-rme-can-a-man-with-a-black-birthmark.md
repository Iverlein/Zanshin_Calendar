# རྨེ་ཅན in ch. 7's verse: a man with a black birthmark on the face; rje can is a variant

The word «རྨེ་ཅན» (rme can) that rings the dreamer in ch. 7's verse (RD271, 2026-11-09; the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho in Steinert's collection and the Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo on BDRC): a person with a black birthmark on the face; Byang pa's and the Drikung's «རྗེ་ཅན» has no headword and is the misspelling or variant.

**The dictionaries.** རྨེ་ཅན (rme can) is a headword in the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho (Steinert no. 62): the name of a person with a black mole or birthmark on the face; it cites, for this, the *Rgyud bzhi'i bka' 'grel* of Dpal spungs Dbon Karma bstan 'dzin 'phrin las rab rgyas, and the glossary *Gso rig skor gyi ming tshig nyer mkho'i don gsal* of De'u dmar Dge bshes Bstan 'dzin phun tshogs, "rme sha can and rme can are names for one with a mark of flesh on the face". The Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo (BDRC I00EGS1017132) has «རྨེ་ཅན། གདོང་ལ་ཤ་མཚན་ནག་པོ་ཡོད་པ་ཅན་གྱི་མི» and, with it, «རྨེ་ཤ་ཅན» (rme sha can), a name of a base-born butcher caste. The Brda dkrol gser gyi me long, Dan Martin, Ives Waldo, the Tshig mdzod chen mo and the Sgom sde have རྨེ་ཤ་ཅན (rme sha can) only: a man of low birth, a butcher or an eater of corpses of the charnel ground, by the *Lam rim chen mo*'s commentary; the word རྨེ (rme) alone is "a mole" (Ives Waldo) and, in the Brda dkrol, an old word for "to scold".

**The commentaries.** Byang pa's *Bdud rtsi'i chu rgyun* (`bjp`, etext pp. 213–214) quotes the verse with «རྗེ་ཅན» (rje can) and in its own words that follow writes «རྨེ་ཅན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་སྐོར་བ» with the plural, "ringed round by crows, hungry ghosts and the rme can"; so the rje can of his verse and of the Drikung is a variant spelling of the same word, as he himself shows. A second etext of the *Bdud rtsi'i chu rgyun* and the *Grub pa lus bcos sgrig ma* (a further compilation that quotes it) read rme can in the same sentence.

**The sense.** The dream of being ringed by crows, hungry ghosts and men with a black mark on the face (or, in the dictionaries' second sense, base-born men), the company of the dead's world. The first sense is the Four Tantras' own commentators'; the second is the word's common one outside medicine; the plural «རྨེ་ཅན་རྣམས» is a group of people. The question q859 is answered as to the word; which of the two senses the verse means is open, the first being the medical one.
