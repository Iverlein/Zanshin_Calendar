# The Brahmavaivarta's 'Kāṇva branch, book of merit': tathaiva against tatraiva, and the Nepali gloss

# The Brahmavaivarta's "Kāṇva branch, book of merit": two readings, "tathaiva" and "tatraiva"; no book named by the Nepali gloss either (RD357)

Verse 2 of the chapter on dreams in the Brahmavaivarta's *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa* (the Lord's answer to Nanda), as three prints give it:

- **Jivananda 1888** (bvp:p660, read on the image): "vedeṣu sāmavedaś ca praśastaḥ sarvakarmasu | **tathaiva** kāṇvaśākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare", "likewise" the Kāṇva branch, in the lovely book of merit; the Kāṇva branch stands beside the Sāmaveda as a second authority, and the usual tradition (the Kāṇva as the White Yajurveda's) is not contradicted.
- **The Śabdakalpadruma** (skd:p470-471) and the **1935 print with the Nepali commentary** (bvg:p1-2, read on the image, spelling *kaṇva*): "**tatraiva** kāṇva/kaṇva-śākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare", "in that same one (the Sāmaveda), in the Kāṇva branch", which makes the Kāṇva branch a part of the Sāmaveda: the reading RD272 noted as odd.
- **The 1935 commentator** (our English of the Nepali): the Sāmaveda is best of the Vedas for the actions of the rite; "in it too, in the section (khaṇḍa) called Kaṇvaśākhā, there is something special; the dream chapter told there I tell you". He takes *kaṇvaśākhā* as the name of a section of the Sāmaveda and names no book, and no title *puṇyakāṇḍa*.

So the oddity of the Kāṇva branch under the Sāmaveda (q866) is the product of one of two readings, "tatraiva" (the Śabdakalpadruma and the 1935 print) against "tathaiva" (Jivananda); with "tathaiva" no Sāmavedic Kāṇva is needed. The question which book the "puṇyakāṇḍa" of the Kāṇva branch is stays open: no print or commentary read names it; the 1935 commentator does not try. A manuscript of the chapter would decide between the readings; none was found (Berlin and Leipzig records and the NCEB Bengal manuscript of 1852, NCEB_MSS_02703, give no contents).

Doubt: v. 3 differs in the two traditions too (Jivananda "tat sarvaṃ nikhilaṃ tāta kathayāmi niśāmaya", 1935 "tat sarvaṃ likhitaṃ tāta kathayāmi sunirṇayam"); the first word of Jivananda's v. 3a is not told apart in the type (su-/duḥ-).
