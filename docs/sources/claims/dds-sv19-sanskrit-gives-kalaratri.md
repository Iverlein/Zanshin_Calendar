# The Sanskrit of the Saṃvarodaya's chapter 19 reads «kālarātri» for «dus mtshan»; its dream verses are 21-25, give Yama and no span for the donkey, monkey and black woman, and the Tibetan «lto bar» against «yamadarśana» (RD382)

RD382, q1065. The Sanskrit of chapter 19 of the Śrī Mahāsaṃvarodaya Tantrarāja is in Shinichi Tsuda's edition (*The Saṃvarodaya-tantra, selected chapters*, Tokyo 1974; read in the form of his 1970 ANU doctoral thesis, open on the ANU repository, chapter XIX *Mṛtyunimittadarśanotkrāntiyogapaṭala*, the verses on the thesis's printed p. 134, read on the page image). Verse 23 there reads *kṛṣṇavastrā tu yā nārī kālī kāmayate naram / kālarātris tu sā jñeyā gacchate yamadarśanam*: so «Time-Night» (དུས་མཚན, dus mtshan) is Kālarātrī, the black woman in black clothes who desires a man, and the verse ends «he goes to the sight of Yama». In his apparatus manuscript A reads *kalātris* and L *kālarātrin*; C, D, E, P and I read *kālarātri*. This answers the question whether «dus mtshan» is *kālarātri*: it is.

The Tibetan of the verse is the Derge etext's (dds.25:p580-583), with one point where Tsuda parts from it. He collated five Tibetan editions (Derge, Narthang, Peking, Cone, Lhasa), says that Sanskrit and Tibetan agree in this tantra except in one place of chapter 23, and lists among his emendations of misprinted words the change of «གཤིན་རྗེའི་ལྟོ་བར» (gshin rje'i lto bar, «to Yama's belly») into «ལྟ་བར» (lta bar, «to the sight of») after *yamadarśanam* (his note on 19-23); the list names the editions for some of its examples only, so that all five print «lto bar» is not said. The Derge Kanjur prints «lto bar» (dds.25:p580-583) and so does the fourth Zhwa dmar's treatise (dds.26:p129); Tsong kha pa's quotation of the verse has another wording again, «གཤིན་རྗེ་རུ་ནི་འགྲོ་བར་བསྟན» («it is shown that she goes to Yama's», dds.16:p11-13). The choice between «belly» and «sight» is the Sanskrit's against the Tibetan prints read.

The other correspondences. Verse 21: *bālukābhasmarāśiṃ vā vihārayaṣṭim eva ca … maraṇaṃ tatra pūrvavat*, a heap of sand or ashes or the pole (*yaṣṭi*) of a monastery (*vihāra*): the Tibetan has «lha khang» (temple) and «mchod sdong» as two words; «as before» is *pūrvavat*, and the verse before it (20) ends «within six months» (*ṣaṇmāsamadhyataḥ*). Verse 22: *gardabhaṃ vānararūḍho valmīkaṃ pāṃsurāśikam yo 'bhirohati svapnānte dakṣiṇādiśi niyatam*: the donkey and the monkey, the ant-hill (*valmīka*) and «a heap of dust» (*pāṃsurāśi*) for the Tibetan «phyag dar phung po»; so «phyag dar», which the readings of this pool left not understood, is dust or rubbish. The direction is the south, and the span of life of verses 22 and 23 is none: only Yama. Verse 24: *śvakākagṛdhragomāyuṛkṣaiḥ pretapiśācakaiḥ bhakṣyante svapnam paśyed ekavarṣād viniścitam*: dogs, crows, vultures, jackals, bears, ghosts and flesh-eaters, one year. Verse 25: red clothes, a red garland, oil, six months (*ṣaṇmāsāt … na jīvati*). Verse 26 is the turn to *tattva* and *dharma*, as in the Tibetan.

Not in the Sanskrit of this chapter, and so not the tantra's: the bound black girl, the seventh day, the karavīra garland, darkness, pit and prison, trees on the body, the monkey rider going east and his «last night» (མཚན་མོ་ཐ་མ), the man with an iron staff in three months, the red scent in eight months, and the opening verses on the kovidāra and karavīra flowers. All are in 'Deceiving Death' (cbs:p247-249) and so in Marpa's Hevajra rite (dds.19:p53-55); see the claim on that rite.

The first verses show a second thing. Verse 2 of the Sanskrit says *trayadivasāt parād ūrdhvaṃ pañcatvaṃ gacchate tadā* and Tsuda renders the clause «he will then die after three days»; the Tibetan of the Kanjur has «ཉིན་ཞག་གསུམ་ཕ་རོལ། ལྔ་ཡི་གོང་དུ་འགྲོ་བར་འགྱུར» («after three days, before five, he goes»; Tsong kha pa's quotation the same with «ཉི་མ་གསུམ», dds.16:p11-13), with the number five where the Sanskrit has the word for dying; 'Deceiving Death', which reworks the same verse, says «ཉིན་ཞག་གསུམ་གྱི་འོག་ཏུ་ནི། འཆི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་འགྱུར་བ་ཡིན» («after three days he will be dying»; Derge etext VE23703_1344, [p241]). No commentary was read on the point.

The Sanskrit was read in the printed edition's thesis form only; the manuscripts, Tsuda's 1974 book and the commentary of Ratnarakṣita (the *Padminī*, Peking no. 2137) were not opened. Tsuda's English translation of the thesis was read beside the Tibetan to settle doubtful words; the thesis's own notice asks that its passages not be copied or closely paraphrased, so only the Sanskrit words are quoted and the facts put in the pool's own words.
