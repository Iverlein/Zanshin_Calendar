# Gtsang sman: the physician's dawn dream before the messenger comes

Gtsang sman Ye shes bzang po, a modern physician (GSM), lists thirteen ways of examining a sick person and puts first the physician's own dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) at dawn on the night the sick person's messenger (ཕོ་ཉ, pho nya) is to arrive. If he dreams of new clothes, a rising sun, a good horse ridden to a mountain top or to the north-east, known and well-dressed people, music, a feast, flowers and leafy trees, and wakes glad, the sick person will not die whatever the illness; if he dreams of nakedness, mire, weariness, death or killing of animals, weeping, ragged hatless men, quarrels, hail or a setting sun, the sick person will not live and the physician should not go. The same arrangement, the physician's dream and then the messenger, is the order of the older work in sds-dream-signs-of-the-sick; Read in the etext only; modern, facts only.
