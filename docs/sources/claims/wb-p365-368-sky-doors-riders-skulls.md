# WB pp. 365-368: the sky doors are headed by numbered pairs (three-three to ten-three), not weekdays; riders and skulls of the eight trigrams and nine mewa as the etext has them, unchecked on the scan

Read 2026-11-01 on the 1996 etext (IE0OPI51524892, markers [p373]-[p376] = img. 373-376 = printed pp. 365-368); the scan of img. 374-376 was cropped but not read line by line (the Yigdzin-1 run was still waiting for a card slot at the end of the session), so every name and number below is the etext's and unchecked on the scan. The sky doors (གནམ་སྒོ, gnam sgo) are headed by numbered pairs, not by weekdays: on p. 368 the etext has, one after another, the guest's door, the trade door, then "three-three: the child's door" (གསུམ་གསུམ་བུ་ཆུང་གནམ་སྒོ), "four-three: the war door" (བཞི་གསུམ་དམག་སྒོ), "five-three: the kin door" (ལྔ་གསུམ་གཉེན་སྒོ), "six-three: the fortress door" (དྲུག་གསུམ་མཁར་སྒོ), "seven-three: the bride door" (བདུན་གསུམ་བག་མའི་གནམ་སྒོ), "eight-three: the charnel door", "nine-three: the feast door" and "ten-three: the general door": the first number rising by one and the second always three, so the pair is not a weekday. BDRC's full-text search (2026-11-01) finds the same list of doors in other published texts, with the rule: a physician's reckoning (Namkha Senge's medical reckoning, etext MW2DB13644_565451, open scan) gives for the war door «the 4th of the first decade, the 14th of the middle, the 24th of the last», and a modern medical collection (MW1KG10631, search only) lists the doors as «one-three: the guest's door, two-three: trade, three-three: child, four-three: war, five-three: kin, six-three: fortress, seven-three: bride, eight-three: charnel, nine-three: feast». So «n-three» is the n-th date of each of the three decades (n, n+10, n+20): the guest's door falls on the 1st, 11th and 21st, trade on the 2nd, 12th, 22nd, child on the 3rd, 13th, 23rd, war on the 4th, 14th, 24th, kin on the 5th, 15th, 25th, fortress on the 6th, 16th, 26th, bride on the 7th, 17th, 27th, charnel on the 8th, 18th, 28th, feast on the 9th, 19th, 29th, and the general door on the 10th, 20th, 30th. The reading of «ten-three» as the general door (spyi, 10th, 20th, 30th) rests on WB's own heading; the two outside texts were seen as search snippets only, not opened on the page. Each door has its bad works, the remedy "if it is covered" (སྒྲིབ་པ་ན) and a mantra. The riders and skulls come on pp. 367-368: for each of the eight trigrams a skull (Li a butcher's, Khon a woodcutter's, Dwa a pauper's, Khen a leper's, Kham that of one whose line has ended, Gin a skull with a spike stuck in, Zin a smith's, Zon one of a woman who prayed) and a rider for the remedy (a makara, a sin-bearer, a snake, a pig, an elephant, the spike, a rider, the rtsangs-pa); then for the nine mewa, from "one white: a red man on a red horse", the colour with a man and an animal each. The names of the skulls and riders as printed here are the etext's gist and were not weighed against the scan: the question about the "rtsangs pa" and Gin's skull stays open.

- RD183b (2026-11-06), read on the scan (I3CN5029 img. 373): Dwa's skull is the kin-slayer's («དམེ་ཐོད»), not a pauper's; the claim's other skulls stand.
