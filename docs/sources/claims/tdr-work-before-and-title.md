# The work that ends on TDR p. 343 is the Śramaṇā text; White Tārā's begins at the doubled title in line 6 (RD286)

BDRC's outline of the Delhi volume (MW25101) cuts the work that ends on printed p. 343 as «Lha mo shra ma Na'i sgo nas rmi lam brtag pa» (the examination of dreams through the goddess Śramaṇā), part 64, pp. 337–343; White Tārā's text is part 65, pp. 343–344, with a second title «sgrol ma dkr mo'i sgo nas rmi lam brtag pa rten 'brel mngon shes su 'char ba'i man ngag»; the lion-faced dakini's rite is part 66, pp. 344–347. So the work that ends on p. 343 is the Śramaṇā text; the Śākyamuni rite is not a work of its own but the second of three rites inside it. The etext of the other print (tdr:p337-345e) gives the reason: its section opens, under the number «gsum», with the heading «lha mo shra ma Na, shAk+ya thub pa, sgrol ma rnams la brten pa'i rmi lam brtag pa gsum», three examinations of dreams relying on the goddess Śramaṇā, Śākyamuni and Tārā, and the Śākyamuni rite ends in the words «rje btsun shAk+ya thub pa'i sgo nas brtag pa'o» just before the White Tārā heading. The registry's TDR (the White Tārā text) therefore begins where the scan shows it, at the title written twice at the end of line 5 and in line 6 of p. 343 (tdr:p343h, read on the scan), and what stands before it on that page is the end of the Śramaṇā text's Śākyamuni rite, not a separate work. The lion-faced dakini's rite is called "fourth" in the etext, so the section holds four rites; no author and no colophon name stands at the end of White Tārā's text in either print (the history of the instruction that follows is the next rite's).
