# The other solstice from the first: six days and the desired part, seven house-days at the place

From one solstice the other follows: of whichever solstice the date and desired part are known, add six to the date and take the desired part from it for the following solstice; the summer solstice of the author's place is also had from the winter's by adding seven days of date to the days, two to the desired part and four to the fifth part (the place lies seven house-days from the Brahmā-line of the east and twenty-three from the middle one, after the *Lamp that Makes the Solstice Clear*). Adding or subtracting the house-days gives the solstice of the following year. The text says the earlier rule of the summer solstice was unclear and mostly in error, and gives this one in its place.
