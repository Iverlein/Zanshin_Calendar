# The print names no writer or lama for the three dream examinations; the colophon near them closes the mercury set

RD393 (2026-11-11). The print's own words about the three dream examinations (Śramaṇā, Śākyamuni, White Tārā) name no writer and no source text. The section opens on p. 309 ([p337]) with a verse of homage to an unnamed lama ("the captain who holds sway over the ocean-island of the instructions"), the Śramaṇā rite speaks twice in the voice of a transmitter ("so it is said", gsung ngo, [p338]; "the lama says, and it is established by experience", bla mas gsung zhing myong bas grub bo, [p339]), and no colophon stands before the fourth section begins on p. 315 ([p343]). The one colophon near by, on [p331]-[p332] (printed pp. 303-304), closes the first section of the lower part, the mercury instructions of the adept Orgyan pa, and names Lha btsun Rin chen rgya mtsho Blo gros dpal bzang po as the writer of that set at the hermitage Thar pa gling in Khab 'brong dkar, with a scribe, Nam mkha' dpal bzang, and a lineage that ends in Lha btsun himself. It cannot be carried over to the dream set: it is the end of another section, the dream set follows after the second section, and the editors' contents (tdr:p21e) count the dream examinations as the third of ten sections of the lower part, each with its own heading.

So the compiler of the three examinations is not told by the print. What the editors say of the book (tdr:p15-23e) is that its owner and compiler is Lha btsun Rin chen rgya mtsho of 'Brong rtse and that it collects "many special oral instructions of other holy forebears"; that is a statement about the book, and the dream set may be one of the borrowed instructions (that Lha btsun composed it himself is possible; nothing in the print supports it over the other reading). The lama of the Śramaṇā rite is not named anywhere in the print.
