# The key to the notation of the sūtra Tiger-Ear, and what skya rgyal is

The key printed on p. 210 for the sūtra *Tiger-Ear* names the marks of its notation: buckwheat and peas "damp" means heavy rain, "dry" little rain, "harmed" a bad year; "the white [crop] wins" is a good year, "the white [crop] damp" is *skya rgyal*, "the white [crop] wolf" is the sign of famine; wheat, the white [crop], buckwheat and peas are the grains of Tibet, and *khre*, *zar ma*, *so ba*, *ju tse*, *nyung ma* the grains of China; "harmed", "wolf", "locked up" and "damp" are the marks of the faults of the grains. The eastern sky-army means drought and the western rain; the north's herdsman rising is *sad*. When the king contends against the minister the *skya* are defeated; when the minister contends against the king it is *skya rgyal*. The key explains the sūtra's code only in part: several of its clauses on women, children and the fields are given in words that are not settled here.
