# The nine classes name the difference of white and black among the fifteen

The nine classes of WB p. 425 («སུམ་ཆས», «བཞི་ཆས», «ལྔ་ཆས», «དྲུག་ཆས» with «ལྷག་དཀར» or «ལྷག་ནག», and «དཀར་ནག་ཕྱེད་མ») are named by the surplus of one colour over the other among the fifteen starting points, the pebble door not counted. The class number is the size of the difference, not a share: Mipham's notes (wb.2:p495-496, read on the Gser thang reprint, img. 502, line by line) give the examples and the rule.

- Of the fifteen without the pebble door (རྡེལ་སྒོ, rdel sgo), six white and nine black is "a third more black" (སུམ་ཆས་ལྷག་ནག, sum chas lhag nag): nine less six is three.
- The reverse, nine white and six black, is "a third more white".
- Five white and the rest (ten) black is "a fifth more black" (ལྔ་ཆས་ལྷག་ནག): ten less five is five. "And so on" for the others.
- Where white and black stand even (the speckled pebbles making an even split possible), four more white is "a fourth more white" (བཞི་ཆས་ལྷག་དཀར), and so on.
- Some say the "more" classes must be joined on top of an even split, because otherwise five black and ten white would have to be called "a third more white" (five being a third of the fifteen), which is not how it is counted. The notes' own verdict: in fact three more white is like "a third", four like "a fourth", and so on (དོན་ལ་དཀར་པོ་གསུམ་མང་ན་སུམ་ཆ་བཞི་བཞི་ཆ་སོགས་དང་འདྲ་བ). So "a third", "a fourth" are names for a difference of three, four, five, six.

This settles what the names count, and why a fraction of fifteen does not fit them. It leaves open, because no text says it: whether a speckled pebble counts to the difference (a difference of fifteen pebbles of two colours is always odd, so "a fourth" and "a sixth" need speckled ones or an even-split base); whether WB's order on p. 425 (third, fourth, fifth, sixth more white, half, sixth to third more black) is the order of rising whiteness (it is not: the difference grows from a third to a sixth and then falls to nothing at the half); and which colour the pebble door is read as. Read by RD53 (2026-10-25) on the scan (the reprint img. 502, I3CN5029): the two sentences «སུམ་ཆས་ལྷག་དཀར་སོགས་ནི» and «དོན་ལ་དཀར་པོ་གསུམ་མང་ན» are as the 1996 print's Yigdzin-1 reads them, with the syllables checked on the reprint.
