# 'Deceiving Death' names the works it rests on, not a source for the dream verses

The root work's last chapter says what the treatise was drawn from: "the Vajraḍāka (རྡོ་རྗེ་མཁའ་འགྲོ, rdo rje mkha' 'gro), the Four Seats (གདན་བཞི, gdan bzhi), the Buddha's «བླ་གབ» (bla gab) and others, the «དུས་ཀྱི་ཕྱི་མ» (dus kyi phyi ma) and the «དུས་ཕྲེང་བ» (dus phreng ba), the Fifty of Time (དུས་ཀྱི་ལྔ་བཅུ་པ, dus kyi lnga bcu pa), the Fifty on Life (འཚོ་བ་ལྔ་བཅུ་པ, 'tsho ba lnga bcu pa) and so on" (cbs:p267-268; the verse is the same in the Dpe bsdur ma etext, vol. 14 p. 1358). The explanation (cbl:p278-279) glosses the list as the tantras the work was gathered from, calls the Fifty on Life "Nāgārjuna's" and adds the books on medical examination and the garland of formulae (སྨན་དཔྱད་སྦྱོར་ཕྲེང, sman dpyad sbyor phreng) and the meditation established from the treatises of the Buddhists and of the other sects. No verse and no gloss ties the dream list to one of these works; so the Indian dream verses of 'Deceiving Death' have a stated general ground (tantra, the two Fifties, medicine) and no named source of their own. The size given is three hundred and sixty verses, with a note in CBL "with eighty added" (cbl:p278-279). State: etext only; the identification of the titles beyond the names is not made here.
