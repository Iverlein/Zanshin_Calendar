# Tsong kha pa's mandala rite: the disciples' dreams asked at dawn, the four parts of the night, the reversal of bad signs

In the mandala-consecration rite of Tsong kha pa's vol. 3 (the section BDRC titles on good and bad dream signs), the teacher has the disciples sleep, in the lion's posture on kusha grass, after the winding mantra, and at dawn asks their dreams (img. 385-386). He names two images and no more: seeing the Three Jewels, friends and the like is good (he gladdens the disciple); falling from a mountain and the like is bad (he protects him). The night is in four parts: the first three are not certain, the fourth is taken, and, from the Mañjuśrī root tantra as the text names it, the first part's dream comes of phlegm, the second of bile ("what is blamed in the world"), the third of wind, the fourth arises truly. This is a different division from Klong rdol bla ma's thirds of the night (evening, midnight, dawn; kbs:p68-70, where the dawn third gives the fruit) and the two should not be merged. The reversal of a bad sign is the seven ways of the Cakrasamvara practice (cake offering, protection wheel, recitation, armour deities, supplication, divine pride in six ornaments, fire offering), with other masters' remedies named (Nāgabodhi, Dārika-pa, the Garland, Lamp-maker the Good, Vighnāntaka). WB ch. 30's list has no parallel: of its items only the shape "falling from a height" (B5, the fall) is near the mountain, and the text judges the fall for a disciple at consecration, not for a reckoned person.
