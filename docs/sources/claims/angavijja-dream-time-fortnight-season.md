# The Aṅgavijjā's chapter on dreams asks when the dream was seen: night or day, the part of it, the fortnight, the season (RD263)

The *Aṅgavijjā* (Jaina, Prakrit; ch. 42, *Suviṇajjhāya*, end; agv:p120-121 in Puṇyavijaya's edition of 1957) is a method of divination by the contacts of the enquirer with his body and with things at hand, and its dream chapter asks, among the facts of a dream, at what time it was seen. It answers that dark things signify a dream seen at night, bright things one seen by day; dark things at the beginning and the root, the evening (*padosa*); the thick ones, midnight (*addharatta*); bright things at the ends, the afternoon (*avaraṇha*); the lines for the morning and midday are garbled in both text layers read. It then says that the bright and the dark fortnight (*sukka-pakkha*, *kaṇha-pakkha*) are found by the indications of the fortnight "as said before", and the six seasons (*udu*) by the indications of the seasons.

So the *Aṅgavijjā* ties the time of the dream to the part of the day or night, to the fortnight and to the season, as Wayman reports of it, but only as facts of the dream to be found out by the diviner's signs. It gives no span from the dream to its fruit, no lunar day (tithi), no weekday and no month. The "part of the phase" Wayman names is not found in this chapter's last page; the lines before it, garbled in both layers, may hold it. Other chapters of the work (the chapter on travel, the time chapters) were not read.

Doubt: the Prakrit is read from two OCR layers of the same print, which agree on the last sentence and differ in the lines before it, so the middle of the day-and-night list is given by its pattern, not word by word.
