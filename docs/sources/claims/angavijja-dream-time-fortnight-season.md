# The Aṅgavijjā's chapter on dreams asks when the dream was seen: night or day in six parts, the fortnight, the season (RD263, RD265)

The *Aṅgavijjā* (Jaina, Prakrit; ch. 42, *Suviṇajjhāya*, pp. 186–191 of Puṇyavijaya's edition of 1957; agv:p190-191, the same page that RD263 cited as agv:p120-121, whose number the OCR misread) is a method of divination by the contacts of the enquirer with his body and with things at hand, and its dream chapter asks, among the facts of a dream, at what time it was seen. RD265 recovered the whole answer from three further text layers and from the parallel list of ch. 41 (agv:p186): the diviner finds the part of the day or night by what is touched, dark things meaning night, bright things day, and within each the beginning or root, the deep middle and the ends: dark, evening, midnight, the break of day; bright, forenoon, midday, afternoon. RD263's doubt that the lines for morning and midday were lost is answered: they are the break of day (dark ends) and the forenoon and midday (bright beginning and middle).

The chapter then says that the bright and the dark fortnight (*sukka-pakkha*, *kaṇha-pakkha*) are found by the indications of the fortnight "as said before", and the six seasons (*udu*) by the indications of the seasons. The rules "said before" are in ch. 59 (agv:p259-260; on pp. 260–261 of the print by the page images of RD269): bright things are the bright fortnight or a day, dark things the dark fortnight or a night, dusky things the twilight or the joint of the fortnights; oily or moist things are the rains, bright and clear ones autumn, cold ones winter, the dusky and the glad spring, dry and hot ones the hot season (the line for the cool season, *sisira*, is a lacuna of the print); and the lunar month (Kattika, Maggasira, Posa from the east; Māha, Phagguṇa, Cetta, Vesāha from the south; Jeṭṭhāmūla, Āsāḍha, Sāvaṇa from the west, …) from the direction of the contact. So the *Aṅgavijjā* ties the time of the dream to the part of the day, the fortnight, the season and the month, as Wayman reports of it, but only as facts of the dream to be found out by the diviner's signs. Chs. 43–44 (agv:p191-193) are on travel and its length (counted in fortnights, days, months and years by the direction of the contact), not on dreams.

It gives no span from the dream to its fruit, no lunar day (*tithi*) by number, no weekday, no nakṣatra. The "part of the phase" Wayman names is the "bright or dark fortnight" and the parts of the day and night of the list above.

Doubt: RD269 read the time list of ch. 42 on the page images of the print (pp. 190–191): it is whole there, with "kaṇhesu" lost in one place only (printed "ka[ṇhesu]", the editor's own bracket); ch. 41's list stands on p. 185 (not p. 186, where the id agv:p186 keeps its old number) and has "afternoon" for the bright beginning where ch. 42's print has "forenoon"; the editor's notes give no variant for the word in either chapter, so which reading is the original is not settled.

RD295 (2026-11-10): ch. 53 (p. 210, line 10) has the forenoon for the bright beginning and the afternoon for the bright end, as ch. 42 does; ch. 41 alone has the afternoon for both (claim angavijja-ch53-bright-beginning-forenoon).
