# The Aṅgavijjā's chapter on dreams asks when the dream was seen: night or day in six parts, the fortnight, the season (RD263, RD265)

The *Aṅgavijjā* (Jaina, Prakrit; ch. 42, *Suviṇajjhāya*, pp. 186–191 of Puṇyavijaya's edition of 1957; agv:p190-191, the same page that RD263 cited as agv:p120-121, whose number the OCR misread) is a method of divination by the contacts of the enquirer with his body and with things at hand, and its dream chapter asks, among the facts of a dream, at what time it was seen. RD265 recovered the whole answer from three further text layers and from the parallel list of ch. 41 (agv:p186): the diviner finds the part of the day or night by what is touched, dark things meaning night, bright things day, and within each the beginning or root, the deep middle and the ends: dark, evening, midnight, the break of day; bright, forenoon, midday, afternoon. RD263's doubt that the lines for morning and midday were lost is answered: they are the break of day (dark ends) and the forenoon and midday (bright beginning and middle).

The chapter then says that the bright and the dark fortnight (*sukka-pakkha*, *kaṇha-pakkha*) are found by the indications of the fortnight "as said before", and the six seasons (*udu*) by the indications of the seasons. The rules "said before" are in ch. 59 (agv:p259-260): bright things are the bright fortnight or a day, dark things the dark fortnight or a night, dusky things the twilight or the joint of the fortnights; oily or moist things are the rains, bright and clear ones autumn, cold ones winter, the dusky and the glad spring, dry ones the hot season; and the lunar month (Kattika, Maggasira, Posa from the east; Māha, Phagguṇa, Cetta, Vesāha from the south; Jeṭṭhāmūla, Āsāḍha, Sāvaṇa from the west, …) from the direction of the contact. So the *Aṅgavijjā* ties the time of the dream to the part of the day, the fortnight, the season and the month, as Wayman reports of it, but only as facts of the dream to be found out by the diviner's signs. Chs. 43–44 (agv:p191-193) are on travel and its length (counted in fortnights, days, months and years by the direction of the contact), not on dreams.

It gives no span from the dream to its fruit, no lunar day (*tithi*) by number, no weekday, no nakṣatra. The "part of the phase" Wayman names is the "bright or dark fortnight" and the parts of the day and night of the list above.

Doubt: the Prakrit is read from three OCR layers of the same print which agree on the whole list and differ in slips; the dark/bright word is lost in two places and restored from the pattern and from ch. 41, whose list has "afternoon" where ch. 42 has "forenoon" for the bright beginning.
