# Vibhūticandra and the date of the Svapnohana: Stearns 1996 and the early catalogues

Stearns's study of Vibhūticandra (JIABS 19.1, Summer 1996, pp. 127–171, open access on the journal's site) fixes the man: an eastern Indian master born in the later twelfth century, first in Tibet in 1204 (the Kashmiri master Śākyaśrībhadra's pupil and companion), three times in Tibet and also in Nepal; in Pu rang in 1213 with Śākyaśrī; the builder of a Cakrasaṃvara temple at Drang srong Srin po ri in Dbus. He is the author of at least eight works in the Peking Tengyur and translator of about thirty more, almost all tantric; his most lasting legacy in Tibet is the six-limbed yoga (sbyor ba yan lag drug) of the Kālacakra, handed on in the Jo nang school. The Svapnohana is only one line in his appendix (no. 23) and the study discusses it nowhere.

Stearns note 83 cites Bcom ldan Rig pa'i ral gri (about 1235–1315) in a catalogue of translated works, written probably before 1283, in a list of works by or translated by Vibhūticandra which names the dream examination («རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ», rmi lam brtag pa; spelled «rmi lam rtag pa» in the printed note, a misprint). That makes the work known to Tibetan cataloguers within about eighty years of the translator, and agrees with Bu ston's listing (svo-authorship-witnesses). Neither source says anything of how the text was used.

So the date of the Svapnohana is that of its translator, from 1204 on (he came to Tibet that year); the text's own date, if it was composed earlier in India, is not given by any source found.
