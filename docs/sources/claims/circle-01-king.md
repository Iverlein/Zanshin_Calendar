# The circles: the first, the white garuḍa, for the king

The first of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the white garuḍa, is read for the lord of men, the king. Its starting point: the bird of the enemy element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: whether the dynasty's seat is empty; whether the tiger's and leopard's stripes and smile have faded; whether the silk knot of the rule is loosened; whether the palace is lost to others; whether the hat and felt have gone under the dust.

| Life force | the dynasty of the earth lord-king, its seat not empty, supremely good; no obstacle to his rule, his life firm, like a rock of meteoric-iron vajra. | the lord and his line somewhat waste away, blaze up and die out; from of old the forefathers' grave sri (དུར་སྲི, dur sri) has been there, and now the sri of the prime (དར་སྲི, dar sri) has risen. | five or three mantrins or Bon priests (སྔགས་བོན, sngags bon) of another clan press down the sri; ransom the life of animals and ransom death; press down the *dar sri* in a lion's skull; the ransom rite (གཏོ, gto) of the king's seat. |
| Body | the king's tiger-and-leopard pride grows, good for the queens; a prince is born who overawes appearance and existence; all the realm is set in happiness; the king's own body without illness, supremely good. | the smile fades; discord between the king and the queens, or father and son split and the realm is ruled in two; each harms the other, the rule declines; illness comes to his body. | wide giving without partiality, changing the body (ལུས་བསྒྱུར, lus bsgyur) and keeping vows. |
| Power | the silk knot of the rule not loosened, the realm's affairs not lost to outsiders, enjoyments grow from every side, the dominion of both systems grows: good. | the palace's affairs slip away, wealth and livestock are scattered by loss, the white silk knot of the rule is loosened, sons and horses decline, enjoyments grow poor. | summoning fortune (གཡང་ལོན, g.yang lon), reading the *Bka' 'dus*, giving and feast offerings. |
| Luck | the palace not lost to others, people speak kindly, ministers and subjects do what they are told. | the realm is blown about by gossip like the wind; times troubled by strife, disputes over the rule. | set up a [...] restoring rite (the first syllable broken), turn back and press down the enemy, give the best food offerings; if it meets the nine-eye (དགུ་མིག, dgu mig) it is very rough: raise the rites (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro). |
| Mewa | the hat and felt not under the dust, the emperor's line flourishes: good. | war in the realm and bad years; an age of illness, weapons and famine. | diligent recitation of fierce mantras and the general thread-cross of the Mamo (མ་མོའི་སྤྱི་མདོས, ma mo'i spyi mdos). |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
