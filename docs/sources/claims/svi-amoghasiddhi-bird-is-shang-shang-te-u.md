# The Amoghasiddhi bird of the three spellings is probably the shang shang te'u, jīvajīvaka; no commentary on the Svapnekṣā says so (RD309)

The three spellings of the Amoghasiddhi sign (svi-amoghasiddhi-bird-spellings) are, taken together, the forms of one bird's name: «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ» (shang shang te'u), a bird of Tibetan lists of singing birds. Evidence, all from BDRC full-text search snippets of etexts (not read on scans, so the wording below is our own summary, not quoted):

- The phrase «ཤང་ཏེའུ» gives 397 hits, the commonest as «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ» (369 hits); the Derge Tengyur's own etext (MW23703) lists it among the birds of a forest with the peacock (རྨ་བྱ), the mynah (ནེ་ཙོ), the cuckoo (ཁུ་བྱུག): «རྨ་བྱ་ནེ་ཙོ་ཁུ་བྱུག་ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ». A Śambhala guide (MW28922) lists «ཤང་ཤིང་ཏེའུ» (shang shing te'u) with the cuckoo and the kālantaka, one hit for that spelling: the same form as the Peking's «ཤིང་ཏེའུ».
- What the bird is: a Gelugpa Vinaya commentary (Akya Yongzin, MW1KG24061, and a copy of the passage in MW1KG9561) says the Sanskrit is jīvajīvaka (the search page's Wylie reads "jA ba jA bi ka"), rendered "one who lives and makes live" (འཚོ་བ་འཚོ་བྱེད), a bird about the size of a peacock; Guru Padmasambhava's collected works (MW1AC305) say it sits as a bird above and a person below; a Tibetan dictionary-commentary (MW3CN2232) calls it a bird of the sea-shore with a sweet voice. These agree only on "a bird with a fine voice".
- The same verse occurs in a medical collection (MW1KG14614, a modern print of Tibetan medical treatises whose BDRC record names Nāgārjuna and Candragomin as authors; its title in Wylie is "sman dpyad gces pa grub pa") as «བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ་ཕོ་ཉར་གཏོང»: the Derge form.

So the Narthang's «ཤང་ཏེའུ» (by the Dpe bsdur ma note) is the end of «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ» and the Peking's «བྱ་ཤིང་ཏེའུ» the same with the second «ཤང» written «ཤིང»; the Derge's «བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ» would be a misreading of it by a scribe who did not know the bird. This is an inference from the spellings and the lists, not stated by any commentary on the Svapnekṣā: none was found. As a dream sign, the line says the bird "sends a messenger" (ཕོ་ཉར་གཏོང), among the signs of the Amoghasiddhi family.
