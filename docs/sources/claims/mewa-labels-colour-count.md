# The number after a mewa's colour on p. 70 and in the notes is best read as a count of pebbles

The nine mewa's labels carry a number after the colour in two ways, and the best fit for the notes' «ལྗང་དང་གསུམ» and WB p. 70's «ལྗང་གསུམ» is neither a count of mewas to a class nor the mewa's own number but the count of pebbles laid for that colour's mewa.

- The number before the colour is the mewa's own: «བཞི་ལྗང» the four green, «གསུམ་མཐིང» the three blue, «ལྔ་སེར» the five yellow (vol. 1, p. 335; LT p. 172 and UT, «བཞི་ལྗང་གསུམ་མཐིང»).
- A count of the mewas of a colour is possible only for the two colours with more than one: three whites (1, 6, 8) and two reds (7, 9). There is one green, one black, one blue and one yellow mewa, so «ལྗང་གསུམ», «ནག་གཉིས» and «སེར་གསུམ» of p. 70 cannot count mewas.
- The Moonbeams' table of the pebbles laid for the descended mewa gives the number after the colour as a count of pebbles: «བཞི་ལྗང་གསུམ» four green, three; «བརྒྱད་དཀར་དཀར་གསུམ» eight white, three white; «དགུ་ལ་དམར་གཉིས» nine, two red; «བདུན་དམར་དམར་གཅིག» seven red, one red (dz:i055-057). This agrees with p. 70's «ལྗང་གསུམ», «དཀར་གསུམ», «དམར་གཉིས» exactly, and is the reading that unifies them; the Moonbeams' yellow is half and half, WB's «སེར་གསུམ» three, and its black and blue differ in the same way (DZ blue two, black one; p. 70 «ནག་གཉིས»), so the match is not complete.

Read so, «ལྗང་དང་གསུམ» in the notes (p. 501) is "the green and the three" in a run of colour-with-count pairs («དཀར་གསུམ», «དམར་གཉིས», «སེར་གསུམ», «ནག་གཉིས»): the green mewa's three pebbles, joined by «དང» to the last pair, not three mewas of one class. This is an inference from the Moonbeams' table, not stated by the notes.
