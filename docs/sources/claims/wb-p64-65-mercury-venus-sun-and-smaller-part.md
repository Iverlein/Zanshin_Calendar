# WB's Mercury and Venus clause: the true sun by its context, a smaller part by the Seven Special Qualities alone

# WB's Mercury and Venus clause: the true sun by its own context, a smaller part by the *Seven Special Qualities* alone

For questions 117 and 118. The clause (WB vol. 1, p. 64, l. 26–28: either Mercury or Venus together with the sun in one mansion within 135 sixtieths, «བཟུང་མི་སྲིད», "cannot be seized") is the last of the solar eclipse's exceptions, and the lines that follow it on p. 65 name the two suns that WB sets against each other, the true reckoning's and the abridged reckoning's (wb.1:p64-65).

- **Which sun.** The clause itself sends «དང་པོ་འཛིན་ནམ་མི་འཛིན་ལ། འདི་ལ་གྲུབ་རྩིས་ལེ་ཚན་དང་། ཅུང་ཟད་བསྟུན་ཙམ་དགོས», "for whether the first is seized or not, the chapter on the true reckoning and some agreement are needed": the clause is to be judged by the true reckoning (གྲུབ་རྩིས). The next lines give the true reckoning's own test in the same words as the clause's "one mansion": the true sun (ཉི་མ་དག་པ) and the nearer of Rāhu's head and tail «སྐར་མ་གཅིག་ལ་བབས་པ», fall on one mansion. The abridged reckoning's sun comes in only where p. 65 rules which of the two reckonings decides when they disagree: «བྱེད་རྩིས་འཛིན་ལ་གྲུབ་རྩིས་ནས། སྐར་མ་སྟོངས་ཙམ་མི་འཛིན་ཀྱང་། འཁྲུལ་མེད་བསྡུས་རྒྱུད་དགོངས་དོན་གཙོ», "where the abridged reckoning seizes and the true one leaves the mansion void, the intent of the Abridged Tantra is principal", and for the seven eastern mansions the abridged reckoning is principal outright. That rule governs a seizing the two reckonings give differently (the void mansion), and says nothing of Mercury or Venus. So the clause's own idiom and its send to the true reckoning point to the true sun, as the *Seven Special Qualities* has it (kd.2:p441: «ཉི་དག»); the principal rule of p. 65 is not about this clause. The wording does not state it: this is the reading the context bears.
- **Which place of the planet.** Neither WB nor the *Seven Special Qualities* says more than the planet's name. Blo gros dpal gives the mean place of both planets as the sun's, so a comparison of mean places would always hold; the clause therefore compares a place moved by the planet's own equation, which is our step and no text's (month-entries.md, "Mercury and Venus at an eclipse"). Not found in any text read.
- **Smaller or none.** WB's own wording on this page has both kinds of result: «བཟུང་མི་སྲིད» for this clause, and for other exceptions a smaller part («ཆ་རེས་ཆུང་», smaller by a part; «ཆ་ཕྱེད་ཙམ་གྱིས་ཆུང་བར་སྣང་», appears smaller by about half a part, for the sun in the bow house and in the crab and the sea-monster; wb.1:p64-65 holds the verse only in the machine text). So WB knows both and chose "cannot be seized" here; the *Seven Special Qualities* (written in 1937 and revised in 1987, kd.2:p441) says «འཛིན་ཆ་གོང་ལས་ཆུང་བར་འགྱུར», "the part seized becomes smaller than above", and does not name WB. Whether it reads WB's «བཟུང་མི་སྲིད» softly or takes the rule from another source is not told by either text; no third text has the clause (searched, see ch. 27's section for RD179).
