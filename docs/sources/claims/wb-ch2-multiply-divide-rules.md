# WB ch. 2 states the rules of the written multiplying and dividing: zero rules, direction, and the carry

As p. 21 states them (machine reading): adding and counting (the first word unsettled) begin from the right, multiplication and division from the left. The dots (zeros, ཐིག་ལེ) of the multiplier are set at the end of the row of the multiplicand. If the multiplicand is empty there is nothing to multiply; if the multiplier is empty the base is erased. Multiplying by one changes nothing; by two and more it increases upward. In division, a dot in the middle or at the end of the divisor cannot divide because it holds its own place; where nothing is obtained, a dot is given as the share. Division is single (take whatever is obtained) or joined (the lower figure governs). One added above becomes ten; when emptied, the ten and the rest are brought down in order and the remainder set in its own place. A dot cannot come at the edge of a row. For the mixed counts of the astronomical wheels (the seven of the planets and the 'maker', the twenty-seven of asterism and yoga, chu tshod and chu srang at sixty, six breaths), whatever fills a wheel, the excess is added to the higher one, and when the higher place has what it obtained, the leftover share is for the most part dropped.
