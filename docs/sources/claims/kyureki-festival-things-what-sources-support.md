# What the sources support of the nine 旧暦 festivals' things for their frames

Read on 2026-11-24 ([festivals.md](../festivals.md), RD409 and its follow-up): NAOJ 暦Wiki through a Japanese exit, Japanese Wikipedia (facts only), and prints seen in the Tokyo National Museum's (ColBase) and the Metropolitan Museum's online collections.

- 1/1 旧正月: pine, bamboo and plum (松竹梅, an emblem of celebration) and the gate pine (門松, pine and bamboo) confirmed; the first sunrise (初日の出) confirmed as a New Year custom.
- 1/7 人日: the seven herbs of spring (春の七草) confirmed; no source read gives a basket or snow (q1359).
- 3/3 上巳: peach blossom (the "peach festival", kept when the peach blooms) and paper dolls sent down the river (流し雛) confirmed.
- 5/5 端午: the plant is the sweet flag (ショウブ, Acorus), a waterside plant with sword-shaped leaves and an inconspicuous yellow-green spike, hung from the eaves and put in the bath; it is not the flowering garden iris (ハナショウブ). The carp streamers of Edo are black carp (真鯉) on a pole with a five-coloured streamer; Hiroshige's Suidōbashi Surugadai shows three black carp and no red one.
- 7/7 七夕: leafy bamboo with paper strips confirmed; the strips' five colours are those of the Five Phases, green, red, yellow, white and black; in Edo the bamboo stood high over the roofs hung with strips and coloured paper cut as ground cherries, watermelons and account books (Hiroshige's Tanabata print shows a gourd, a watermelon slice and long streamers); the sky is a half moon and the Milky Way.
- 8/15 十五夜: moon, pampas grass (ススキ) and dumplings (月見団子, round in Edo) confirmed.
- 9/9 重陽: chrysanthemums confirmed ("the chrysanthemum festival"); Eisui's *Beauties of the Five Festivals* sets chrysanthemums in its fan-shaped title cartouche.
- 9/13 十三夜: a moon that is never full, chestnuts and soybeans (栗名月, 豆名月) confirmed.
- お盆: the welcoming fire (迎え火), the Bon lantern (盆提灯) and the cucumber horse and aubergine ox (精霊馬) confirmed; Shibata Zeshin's print shows a woman lighting a hanging lantern for Obon.
