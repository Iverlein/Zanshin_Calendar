# What a dream sign depends on in LGD: the hour, the lunar day, the practice

What LGD makes a dream's reading depend on. Time of night: yes, in the dream's kind, not in its content: dusk dreams are mostly of habit, midnight dreams of the afflictions, and only the dawn dream is of omen (lgd:p966-967); a good followed by a bad, or the reverse, turns toward the second; a good or bad of the dusk-and-dawn mixture is uncertain (lgd:p969). No individual sign in the lists of good, bad, resembling-good and resembling-bad is given a different verdict by the hour. Lunar day: only for the rite, not for the reading: the examination is made in the waxing half, with planet and mansion good, "the eighth and the like" (གཟའ་སྐར་བཟང་ཞིང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཡར་ངོ་ལ, lgd:p961-966), and the rite is repeated in the waxing half again; no sign's verdict changes with the lunar day. The practice in hand: yes, the whole text assumes it: the dream is of a yogin who has done the approach and the application, asks the dream and catches it at waking; and the verdict of a dream is weighed by the rank of the dreamer: the best yogin knows the dream as a dream and has nothing to examine, the least loses the sign in dullness, the middling examine for themselves and others (lgd:p971). The text also says dreams arise from the channels and winds, habit, the god and the demon born with the person and outer spirits' tricks, so none is certain (lgd:p970).
