# «སྲུ་མོ», «སྨྱུག་མོ», «ཚོས»: the bad trigram strikes by the shape of names, not by kin

In Mipham's note on the bad trigram that touches father and mother (WB vol. 2, p. 508, l. 17, Gser thang img. 514; wb.2:p504-512) the words «སྲུ་མོ», «སྨྱུག་མོ», «ཚ་མོ» and «རྒྱལ་པོ», «སྐུད་པོ», «གཡོག་པོ», «མི་དང་ཚོས་སོགས» are not kin terms. The note says that the "bad for father and mother" of the other tradition is by name and not by relation, and that it is the persons whose names are of the form of the trigram that suffer: those named like «ཨ་མ» (*a ma*), «ཆུ་མ» (*chu ma*) and the other names ending in «མ» for the woman's side, names ending in «མོ» such as *sru mo* (སྲུ་མོ), *smyug mo* (སྨྱུག་མོ, a name, "bamboo"), *tsha mo* (ཚ་མོ), and for the men's side names ending in «པོ» such as *rgyal po* (རྒྱལ་པོ), *skud po* (སྐུད་པོ, "thread") and *g.yog po* (གཡོག་པོ, "servant"), and likewise names carrying *mi* (མི) and *chos* (ཆོས). «ཚོས» is the scan's printing of the last word (the 1996 etext reads «ཆོས»); *tshos* alone is "dye" or "paint", which fits nothing here, so it is read as *chos* ("the Dharma" in a name such as *Chos skyabs*), by the sense. Answered: they are name-endings, not relations.
