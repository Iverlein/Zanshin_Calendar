# Dated dreams in Tibetan biographies and treasure texts are log entries; the tenth and fifteenth are the usual nights

# Dated dreams in Tibetan biographies and treasure texts are log entries; the tenth and the fifteenth are the usual nights (RD367)

Biographies, songs and treasure texts date a dream by lunar day, month and year, as an event: «on the night of the thirteenth» (dds.1:p523, the Nyingtik Yabzhi's supplication, of a lama's dream of the Sow, Vajravārāhī), «a dream on the fifteenth of the third Mongol month in my thirty-third year» (dds.2:p280, the apparition of Mi la ras pa), «the night of the fifteenth of the eighth Mongol month, in the year of the earth male horse» (dds.3:p8, the treasure-finder Bdud 'joms gling pa's title page), «the night of the fifteenth of the twelfth month of the water male dog year» (dds.4:p524, Lokeśvara), «the tenth of the waxing of the Tiger month, a clear pure dream» (dds.7:p23, a colophon), «the Pleiades month, the night of the tenth» (dds.5:p297, Gesar in the sky), «the night of the tenth, a dream, the dakini says to follow whatever auspicious connection arises the next day» (dds.5:p346), «on the night of the tenth Orgyan, red, dancing» (dds.6:p44), and, with the numerals missing from the etext, «the earth-dog (month) and day» (dds.14:p332, dds.13:p209) and «the earth-dog month» (dds.11:p340, Klong rdol's biography). Of the 13 hits of «ཚེས་བཅུའི་ནུབ་མོ་རྨི་ལམ» (9) and «ཚེས་བཅོ་ལྔའི་ནུབ་མོ་རྨི་ལམ» (4), several of the tenth-night ones (the Bdud 'joms gling pa texts, which are three prints of one story, a Dzogchen manual, a Bde mchog initiation colophon, dds.6:p44) show Orgyan (ཨོ་རྒྱན, o rgyan, Padmasambhava) or a dākinī from his land; the fifteenth-night ones show a plain of flowers (dds.3:p8), Mi la ras pa (dds.2:p280) and Lokeśvara (dds.4:p524).

None of these sentences says that the date makes the dream true, false, good or bad. The date is told because the vision is told, as one tells the year and place of a journey; the fifteenth (the full-moon night) and the tenth are the nights on which a practitioner would be at a rite, which would explain their frequency without any rule about dreams, but no text says so and this is only the reader's inference.

Whether a tradition holds a dream of the tenth day to be better than another is a question for a reader of the tradition, not for this search: no sentence in the hits states it.
