# WB's «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» singles out the case that counts most in its line, no weights

WB uses «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» ("in particular", "above all") in its verses to single out one case inside what the line has just named, and the singled case is the one that counts most there, without a number or a rank over the rest. In ch. 34 itself, p. 387 (the digging rite; etext p. 395): «བྱུང་ན་བཟང་ལ་ཁྱད་པར་དུ། སླར་བླུགས་ལུད་ན་མཆོག་ཏུ་ཤིས» (if oily soil, jewels and the five colours appear it is good; above all, if the soil poured back is in excess, it is the best of fortune): the particular case is a stronger degree of the same good. P. 402 (etext p. 410): «ཀུན་ལ་གཅེས་ཀྱང་ཁྱད་པར་དུ། སྨན་དཔྱད་ལ་ནི་གཙོ་བ་སྟེ» (dear to all, above all it is chief for medicine). Likewise «ལམ་ཞུགས་སྤྱིར་ངན་ཁྱད་པར་དུ» (pp. 308, 309, 312: for a journey bad in general, above all the south and north corners) and the pair «X སྤྱི་དང་ཁྱད་པར་དུ» (pp. 193, 217, 338: the general and the particular). Printed pages are the etext's minus 8. The word is found by searching the 1996 etext (IE0OPI51524892, vols. 1–2) and read in its context there, not on the scan for each page. So in the closing verse (p. 428) it fits the same use: the person's own rise and decline and what harms him are the case examined above all, and the verse gives no weights to the other members. It does not say that this member overrules the rest.
