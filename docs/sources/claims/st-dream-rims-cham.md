# The dream among the signs of the cham of the pestilence (ch. 27)

The dream among the signs of the cham of the pestilence (རིམས་ཆམ, rims cham), ch. 27 of the Instruction Tantra.

The chapter on treating the cham fevers (ཆམ་རིམས, cham rims) names four kinds: of the throat, the lung, the pestilence and the nose. The cham of the pestilence shows aching of head and joints, an uneasy upper body, hips and waist felt as beaten, shivering with cold, flesh hot in the evening and many confused dreams (རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང, rmi lam za zi mang). If violent exertion and a loss of the essence are added and it grows, it is the cham gone sour (ཆམ་པ་སྐྱུར་ཤོར, cham pa skyur shor), of which many die. The dream is one sign of the illness; the passage gives no remedy for it, the cham's treatments are in the chapter's later lines (not read here), and the text says nothing of the dream's content. The chapter is the twenty-seventh by its colophon, p. 242 of the print (img. 201); the passage is on p. 241 (img. 202) (st:p241).

What is done for the sick dreamer (RD229, 2026-11-09; read by machine with eye checks). The chapter's treatment of the pestilence cham (`st:p241-242b`) is fasting and boiled water, then a decoction of the three fruits, sle tres, tig ta, ma nu and hong len taken as powder, which ripens and kills the fever at once; the evening dreams are not treated apart from it. Violent exertion, sleep by day and heating, rich food are avoided, and a cham that falls into pestilence or confusion is treated by the texts of those.
