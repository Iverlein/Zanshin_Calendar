# The thirty: the fourteenth point, the Chinese peacock with crossed necks, for the bride's going

The fourteenth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the Chinese peacock with crossed necks (རྒྱ་ཡི་རྨ་བྱ་མཇིང་བསྣོལ, rgya yi rma bya mjing bsnol), is read for the bride's going. Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the bride's power-mountain is lost to the fire of existence | The fire of existence has not taken it, good | The mountain of existence burns: a great risk of sorrow in the father's line's place; the bride meets the face of the household's queen («ཐེ་ཁྱིམ་བཙུན་མོ», not understood) and dies | Turn the torma (གཏོར་བསྒྱུར, gtor bsgyur) |
| 2 | Whether the bride's directions are reversed | Her direction does not strike the nose of the *bstan ma* (བསྟན་མ; not identified), good | It strikes it: bad for the bride | Strive at service rites (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) and remedy rite (གཏོ, gto) |
| 3 | Whether the lustre of gold and turquoise has faded | Not faded | Faded: facing the bride herself is bad | Protecting prosperity (གཡང་སྐྱབས, g.yang skyabs) and the like |
| 4 | Whether the silk skirt is frayed | Not frayed at its edge, good | Frayed: bad for both the bride and the household | — |
| 5 | Whether the back-mountain of the father's line is good | It has not fallen, very good | The back-mountain is bad: the father's line declines or helps little | Send the life ransom (གླུད, glud) and set up the life-castle (སྲོག་མཁར, srog mkhar) |
| 6 | Whether the house of glory is pitched where she dwells | It is pitched; good in the household's place | Not pitched: bad in the household's place | Strive at expelling misfortune (བྱུར, byur) and raising glory (དཔལ, dpal) |
| 7 | Whether the food of glory is lost to the enemy | Not lost; she obtains it herself, good | Lost to the enemy: bad, above all for wealth in the later part of life | Tie on the protection that raises the eating fortune (ཟ་ཐང, za thang) |
| 8 | Whether the many mouths have talked of emptiness | No fear of such talk, good | They have, bad | Press down, turn back and turn the gossip (མི་ཁ, mi kha) |

Whose year is reckoned: The son's and the bride's years (notes, p. 532: «བུ་བག», between the horse owner and the house owner, so presumably this point).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
