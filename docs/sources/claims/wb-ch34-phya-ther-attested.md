# «ཕྱ་ཐེར» (phya ther) is a sewn tent-like shelter named beside the yak-hair tent and the manuals' pair with «སྦྲ» varies between it and «ཕྱར»

«ཕྱ་ཐེར» (phya ther) is a real word, a sewn, tent-like shelter named together with the yak-hair tent «སྦྲ» (sbra), and the manuals write the pair as «སྦྲ་ཕྱ་ཐེར» in some and «སྦྲ་ཕྱར» in others.

BDRC's full-text search (exact phrase, 2026-11-08; the hits are etext snippets read as search results, not seen on a scan) finds «ཕྱ་ཐེར» in 19 etexts, among them: (1) the Longchenpa biography, in which the great knowledge-holders and their pupils made some seventy of «སྦྲ་ཕྱ་ཐེར» (sbra phya ther), tents, and lived in them; (2) the Sakya Lamdre collection, where a black frog-demon appeared "inside a phya ther", so a thing one can be inside; (3) a Vinaya manual's gloss that a fly-cover is shaped like a small «ཕྱ་ཐེར» (the gloss is read from a snippet only); (4) the Tai Situ Byang chub rgyal mtshan's testament, where a phya ther is spread over the upper part of an enclosure for the lama's seat, and the Tagtshang history, which lists a phya ther among goods carried. Four astrological manuals name the work as a pair: the Bhutan anthology «དཀར་རྩིས་དང་ནག་རྩིས་ཀྱི་གཞུང» (dkar rtsis dang nag rtsis kyi gzhung) in two places, the Karma Kagyu manual «རྩིས་ཀྱི་ལག་ལེན་ཉེར་མཁོའི་བུམ་བཟང» (rtsis kyi lag len nyer mkho'i bum bzang), and Blo yangs rgyal's Study of Tibetan astrology; all read as work 17's «ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ» or «སྦྲ་དང་ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ» (to sew the tent and the phya ther). In Blo yangs rgyal the pair appears once more as «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ» (sbra phyar 'tshem), so the manuals give phya ther and phyar in the same place of the pair; whether they are two words or one spelled two ways is not settled.

So work 17's second object is a sewn shelter or cover of the tent kind; the sense "small cover" rests on the Vinaya snippet alone, "tent-like shelter" on all of them. The 64 dictionaries have no headword, as RD113 found; that stays true.
