# What the dream texts say of their own date: none names one; the dated copy is of 1824; Scheuermann still unread

# What the sources say of their own date: none names one; the dated copy is of 1824; Scheuermann still unread (RD357)

- **The Brahmavaivarta's chapter** (bvp:p660, bvg:p1-2): names no date and no author beyond the Purāṇa's; the Jivananda print of 1888 and the Gorkha Pustakalaya print of 1935 are the oldest witnesses of it read; the 1935 print is of the chapter alone with a Nepali paraphrase that glosses the fourth span of half a month as fifteen days (bvg:p3). The Purāṇa's published dates (claim brahmavaivarta-date-hazra-rocher: first form perhaps 8th century, present form 10th–16th) remain the only dating; no study read dates the dream chapter alone, so q869's first part (is the chapter older than the Svapnohana's translation of about 1200?) is not decided by anything RD357 read.
- **Jagaddeva's Svapnacintāmaṇi** (neg:i20, Introduction p. XV): the colophons call the author the son of Durlabharāja, who wrote on palmistry (Sāmudratilaka); Negelein knows nothing else of him and places him later than the physician Vāgbhaṭa; no date is given in the text or in the Introduction page.
- **The Atharva-pariśiṣṭa 68** (apv:p445, p456): the editors' apparatus carries no date of its manuscripts on the page read.
- **The Penn Svapnādhyāya copies**: Item 673 is dated saṃvat 1881 (1824) by its colophon (pn673:i6, read on the image); Items 1871, 2730 and 687 are undated and dated by the catalogue to circa 1700–1850; no copy of the Bṛhaspati text is older.
- **Scheuermann, "Vibhūticandra's Svapnohana and the Examination of Dreams"** (Brill 2020, pp. 161–180): the publisher's page still answers with a "Human Verification" page (HTTP 405) to the headed browser; the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität's repository (epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/75271) has a record of the volume that says the full text is not available there; the Friedrich-Alexander-Universität's record (cris.fau.de/publications/240544364) has the DOI 10.1163/9789004410688_009 and no text. Whether the chapter names a Sanskrit parallel is not known.

Doubt: "no date named" is true of the pages read; Hazra's lines quoted from a "Brahmavaivarta" by the digests (about 1500 lines, only about 30 in the printed text, claim brahmavaivarta-date-hazra-rocher) may include the dream chapter's verses under another name; that was not searched in the digests.
