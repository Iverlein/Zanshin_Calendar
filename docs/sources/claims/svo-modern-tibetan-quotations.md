# Modern Tibetan books quote the Svapnohana's rule of the watches; no earlier quotation found

Tibetan readers of today quote the *Svapnohana*'s opening rule of the watches. A BDRC full-text search (2026-11-09, exact strings from the Derge text of RD207, in Tibetan script) finds the verse of the watches («ཐུན་གསུམ་ཟླ་བ་གསུམ་གྱི་བར… ཞག་བཅུའི་ནང་དུ་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན») outside the Tengyur in two modern books, both published in China and both held on BDRC as search-only etexts:

- Padma rdo rje, a work on the connection of sleep, dream and the between state (བར་དོ, bar do), titled in Wylie *gnyid dang rmi lam bar gyi 'brel ba rnam par dpyad pa Dākī'i glu dbyangs* (Kan su'u mi rigs dpe skrun khang, Lanzhou 2002, 404 pp.; BDRC MW25126, a note calling it a dream analysis and its relation with sleep): the verse from the third and fourth watches to «de rjes rab tu bshad bya ba» stands in it, with small differences of spelling in the machine text.
- Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal, a book on psychology in Tibetan medicine (*bod lugs gso rig gi sems khams rig pa dus rabs khye'u gsar bu'i rang mdangs*, Mi rigs dpe skrun khang, Beijing 2012, 235 pp.; BDRC MW8LS18598): the verse is quoted and glossed that a dream arising at the dawn (tho rengs, here tied to the tiger's time, stag gi dus) surely gives its fruit.

Both were read only as search snippets in Wylie: whether they name the *Svapnohana* as their source, and what else of it they use, is not known. The second gloss ties dawn to the tiger's double hour of the Tibetan twelve; that is a modern reader's clock reading, not the text's, and RD215 found no source fixing the watches by the clock (night-watches).

The search for Tibetan works quoting the text before the twentieth century found nothing beyond catalogues: no commentary, no quotation in a medical or astrological work (the strings «བི་བྷཱུ་ཏི་ཙནྡྲའི་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ», «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་བི་བྷཱུ་ཏི་ཙནྡྲ» and three variants gave no hit). The Derge, Narthang (BDRC MW2KG5015_1416) and Dpe bsdur ma (MW1PD95844_0652) etexts of the text itself carry the fourth-watch line of the opening verse as snippets (the Peking print lacks it, RD233: svo-peking-fourth-watch-line).

RD262 (2026-11-09), by BDRC search snippets chained into passages (both etexts are search-only; svo:p271f; nothing read on a scan): Padma rdo rje quotes the whole *Svapnohana* on about pp. 17-26 (the etext's page markers), naming no source in the parts reached and glossing nothing there; his p. 16 is a modern account of the dream's length, not of the watches. Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal quotes the verse twice (about the page break of pp. 110/111 in chapter 6 and at a later place), the first time as taught "in the Conqueror's Tengyur" (rgyal ba'i bstan 'gyur), and glosses it: a dream arising at dawn, in the tiger's time (tho rengs stag gi dus), surely bears fruit. He adds two lines, one unsourced, one "from the tantra itself" (rgyud rang las), the latter found in the Four Tantras' tradition by search: a dream in the first part of the night, surely forgotten, has no fruit; seen at dawn and clear, it bears fruit. As a modern reader's reading, lower in rank than the texts: the dawn is tied to the tiger's time, no hours are given for the watches, and neither book in the parts reached names a lunar day, a weekday or a season for a dream. The first, introducing sentence of Padma's quotation and anything after p. 26 were not reached.
