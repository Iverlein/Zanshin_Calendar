# «རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང» is a root line only in the fevers; in ch. 81 the dream is the commentators' clause, the spirit's sign

Question q936 asked whether the Four Tantras' line «རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང» (rmi lam za zi mang) means one thing in the fever chapters and in ch. 81, where Khro ru adds a rite. Read on the commentaries (RD321, 2026-11-10; etext, the Serthang verse on the scan) the answer is that the root has the line only in the fevers, and that in ch. 81 it is the commentators' own sentence.

In the root. The fever line «རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི་མང་ལ་སེམས་ལས་ཆེ» stands among the general signs of the unripe fever, the unripe pestilence and the cham fevers (st:p183, st:p218, st:p241). In ch. 81 the Serthang verse on how the spirit enters (st:p352, img. 364, read on the scan) names the five ways, the game, the wish for enjoyment, the unclean and the defiled, the mind that kills an enemy and the ripening of deeds, and says no more of them: no dream stands in those lines. The Drikung's worked verse and the Blue Beryl's, Khro ru's and Skyem pa's glosses (st:p352b) all give the second way, the wish for enjoyment, a sudden onset with many confused dreams, and so the dream clause is theirs. Khro ru, when he quotes the verse itself, quotes it as the Serthang has it, without the dream. This is on one print of the Tantra; the Derge, Chag-po-ri and Drathang prints were not opened at this place, so that the verse of another print holding the clause is not excluded.

In the commentaries. At the fever (st:p183b) the Blue Beryl, Khro ru and the Drikung place the dream among the sick one's own signs, with chills, aching limbs, a heavy body and a clouded mind, and Skyem pa only outlines the range up to it: no rite, no spirit. At ch. 81 (st:p352b) the same dream is set under the wish-for-enjoyment way, and the Blue Beryl and Khro ru tie it to the rite against the spirit and the gathering of merit (གདོན་བཅོས་དང་ཚོགས་བསག); the Drikung gives the merit; Skyem pa glosses the dream as «dreaming of the spirit» (གདོན་རྨི་བ). So the word is the same and its place differs: a sign of the sick body at the fever, a sign of the spirit's entering at ch. 81. No commentary says that the Tantra means one by both, and none defines ཟ་ཟི (the claim za-zi-confused-not-many holds its sense from the lexica: confused, troubled).

What this settles for the app's use: the dream of the spirit's entering is not a line of the Four Tantras on the Serthang print but a sentence of the commentary that the Blue Beryl and Khro ru share (claim st-blue-beryl-khro-ru-one-gloss), so it ranks as a commentator's word. What stays open: the same place on the other prints.
