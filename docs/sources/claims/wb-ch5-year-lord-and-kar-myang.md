# WB ch. 5: the making-year's lord, its king and minister, the Kar myang reckoning and its rain signs

The making-year's lord and its king and minister, and the Kar myang reckoning, WB's chapter 5, pp. 35–36. The lord of the making-year (བྱེད་པ་ལོ་བདག) is found from the passed years in five places (1, 15, 31, 1 ...) with the factor 121 and the constants 5, 32, 16, 1, 211 and 317, and 7 thrown away; it passes to the next lord by the same rule. The king and the minister of the year: the passed years ×3, in two places, the upper less 4 and the lower less 1, divided by 7: the remainders are the king (upper) and the minister (lower); less 4 or plus 3 gives the later pair. The Kar myang reckoning, "that the Nepalese scholars hold" (ནཻ་པ་ལ་ཡི་མཁས་བཞེད་པའི་ཀར་མྱང་རྩིས): to 1609 (9, 0, 16 as the number words stand; the machines read ༡༦༩) add the years passed from the first of the rab byung to the year counted: this is "the Shaka year" (ཤཱ་ཀའི་ལོ). Multiplied by 2 and divided by 7, the result is set aside; the remainder ×2 plus 5 is "the rain-fruit"; the earlier result is multiplied by 3, 7 is taken away, and so on eight times for grain, grass, coolness, heat, wind. The least is 5, the most 17, the middle 12. Again: the Shaka year less 11, thrown away by 4: if 1 rises in the east, "entering the pond", great drought; 2, "entering the limb", rain in some lands and not in others; 3, "all-entering", a fine rain everywhere; 4, "flood", the rivers swell (p. 36).
