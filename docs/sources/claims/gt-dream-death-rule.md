# After the bad dreams: the cause, who dies, the six kinds, the dawn dream and the good dreams, in the Drathang's verse

What follows the list of bad dreams in the Explanatory Tantra (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud), ch. 7, in the Drathang print (gt:i42-43), by the lines.

The cause: the bad dreams are seen because the passages of the mind's entry (ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག, yid 'jug bu ga) are blocked by the illness. The rule: the sick who dream them constantly die; for one without illness it is not certain, and the rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) brings release. The six kinds of dream: what was seen, what was heard, what was felt, a dream after a prayer to a deity, one that came true as dreamt (the commentaries: from the accomplishment of a chosen deity), and one that arose from illness. A dream of the early night is forgotten and has no fruit; one seen at dawn and clear gives its fruit. The four commentaries quote these lines (bjp:p177-181, rsp:p208-211, ktr:p303-307, bdk:p53-54); the Drathang differs from them by no more than a syllable, and the differences stand in gt-dream-verse-variants.

The same verse names the good dreams and the lines before the change of bearing: a god, the bull of the herd, a man of renown; a blazing fire, a lake, the body smeared with blood or filth; white clothes worn, a pennant, a parasol, a tree, grain found; climbing a mountain, a fine house or a tree with fruit; riding a lion, an elephant, a horse or a cow; crossing a great river or lake, going north-east; escaping distress, defeating an enemy; offerings made to oneself by the gods and by father and mother: whoever dreams any of these has long life, no illness and wealth. The Drikung commentary has the same list with small words worked in (bdk:p53-54). The verse does not say these dreams are for the sick only; it gives them after the rule on the bad ones. Before RD268 the pool held the good dreams of this verse only as the Drikung commentary quotes them (the Serthang scan lacks the page).
