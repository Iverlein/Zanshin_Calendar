# Translator only or author as well: the catalogues against the Derge colophon

Witnesses disagree on whether Vibhūticandra only translated the *Svapnohana* or also composed it. RD207 read the Derge colophon, which names him once, as maker of his own translation (rang gyur) and names no author (svo-author-and-translator). The other witnesses found by RD257 split.

Name him translator only: 84000's Scholar's Room (the page of Vibhūticandra, Toh 1749 "Analyzing Dreams", Sanskrit title Svapnohana: role Translator, where Toh 1832 is marked Author); the Tohoku catalogue (Tohoku University, Sendai 1934, No. 1749, Derge Ša ff. 133b–135a) gives only "T. Vibhūticandra" and no author (the author field of No. 1749 is empty on the page image, Tohoku p. 277; a footnote to the Sanskrit title reads "-uhāna-"; the page was read on the Internet Archive's scan), where the neighbouring entry for Advayavajra's examination (No. 2100) gives an author; Bu ston Rin chen grub, in the list of Tengyur works in his history of the doctrine, names the work with the words "Vibhūticandra's own translation" and no author, while listing a second «rmi lam brtag pa», among the works of Advayavajra, as translated by Smṛti. Bu ston's line was found in two etexts (the ACIP etext of his history, BDRC MW1ALS12060E, and a search-only etext of the Bu ston history volume MW3CN22342), and the same words, «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་བི་བྷུ་ཏི་ཙནྡྲའི་རང་འགྱུར» (the dream examination, Vibhūticandra's own translation), stand in the Tengyur catalogue of the Jo nang abbot Phyogs las rnam rgyal (BDRC MW1KG9028, «བསྟན་བཅོས་འགྱུར་རོ་འཚལ་གྱི་དཀར་ཆག», Jo nang dpe tshogs 23, Beijing 2010), which places it just before the Mahākāla works of the mother tantras. All three were read as BDRC search snippets in Wylie, by an exact search of this Tibetan string (3 hits, 2026-11-09), not on a page image: state etext.

Name him author and translator: the Peking catalogue (Otani No. 2621, Peking vol. 59, 110.3.8–111.2.5, as cited by Stearns 1996, JIABS 19.1, appendix no. 23: "composed and translated by Vibhūticandra"); Cordier's catalogue of the Paris Tengyur (II p. 126) as cited by Bose (1923), who writes that Vibhūticandra composed it in Sanskrit and translated it himself; the New Catalogus Catalogorum vol. 29, which lists the Svapnohana both among his works (Cordier II p. 126, Otani 2621) and among his translations (Sendai 1749); and Scheuermann's abstract (svo author and translator, see sch-svapnohana-abstract). These later witnesses are catalogues and surveys, not independent readings of the colophon, and none names a Sanskrit manuscript.

Which stands: the colophon of the print, as read by RD207, outranks a catalogue's summary of it; the question of a Sanskrit original is not decided by any of these. The text stays "Vibhūticandra's own translation, author unnamed", with the later attribution of authorship noted. Stearns puts the work, with the texts he made or translated at Drang srong Srin po ri in Dbus, among the works of his later residence there (appendix heading); this is his grouping, not a colophon.
