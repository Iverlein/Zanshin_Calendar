# Mipham on the Kālacakra: householders with wealth offer to the yoginīs on the 8th and 14th; six complete dates for the maṇḍala's works

Commenting on the empowerment chapter of the Kālacakra (mgz.27:p19-20, read on the scan), Mipham (rank 2) states a rule for "householders who have wealth": to pacify outer obstacles they always make offering to the yoginīs on the eighth and the fourteenth as far as their means allow, give tormas to the elemental spirits day by day, and make offering to the Buddha, the maṇḍala and the like. A disciple who has renounced everything owes no such fixed offering, because buddhas and vajra yoginīs hold the intention, not the things offered, as chief. The teaching of the tantra itself and the offering to the outer yoginīs belong to the twelve full moons, the full moon of Caitra first.

- Days: the 8th and the 14th (of which half, or both, is not said); the twelve full moons; every day for the torma.
- Practice: offering of food and drink to the yoginīs; torma to the spirits; offering to the Buddha.
- For whom: lay householders with means who are disciples of the Kālacakra; expressly not required of renunciants.
- Benefit: outer obstacles are pacified.

The same commentary names six "complete" days of a month, the 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th, 25th and 30th, for beginning the works of a maṇḍala rite, the full moon praised above the rest (mgz.27:p45). That is the only place found where Mipham names the twenty-fifth, and there it is a date for the master's rite, not a day of feast.
