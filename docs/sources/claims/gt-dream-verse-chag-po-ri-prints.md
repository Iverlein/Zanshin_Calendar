# The dream verse of ch. 7 in three Chag-po-ri prints: the unsettled places against the Drathang

Three other open prints of the Four Tantras carry the verse of the bad dreams (the Explanatory Tantra, ch. 7), all Lokesh Chandra Collection scans of the Chag-po-ri (lcags po ri) type on BDRC, each folio side an image of 3500 px (about 55 px a syllable, against about 35 on the Drathang): MW3CN1709 (scans I3CN1711, the Zungchu monastery coloured print; the verse on img. 40), MW3CN1694 (I3CN1696; img. 44) and MW3CN1700 (I3CN1702; img. 36, faint and blurred). None of them is the Serthang print; they are witnesses of the Chag-po-ri tradition and of the machine reader, not proof of the Drathang's own letters.

On the Chag-po-ri copy read best (I3CN1711) the five places stand as ལེན་པ, འདེད, བྲོ་བའི, རྟ་ཕག and རྔ་མོ. That agrees with the commentators who have ལེན་པ (Byang pa, Khro ru, the Drikung) and with Khro ru's བྲོ་བའི, and goes against Byang pa's འདོད and བྲོ་པའི. Against the Drathang's machine reading ལན་པ it differs in the vowel. Line 17 may split the prints: བཟད་པ in the eye reading of I3CN1696 and in the reader's text of I3CN1711, བཟང་བ in the Drathang and in the reader's text of two others; unsettled. No change of sense in any of these.
