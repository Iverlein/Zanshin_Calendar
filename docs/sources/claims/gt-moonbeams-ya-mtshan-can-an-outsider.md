# The Moonbeams reads the good messenger «ya mtshan can» as an outsider ascetic; Rin spungs pa and Khro ru read a monk or Brahmin, the Blue Beryl both

The Moonbeams (the Tengyur's commentary on the Eight Limbs, etext VE23703_1517, p. 145) glosses ya mtshan can (ཡ་མཚན་ཅན) as an outsider (ཕྱི་རོལ་པ, phyi rol pa) living in a kind of vow-observance (བརྟུལ་ཞུགས, brtul zhugs), the monk (དགེ་སྦྱོང, dge sbyong) as one gone forth who keeps celibacy, and the one of like kind (རིགས་མཐུན་པ, rigs mthun pa) as one of a similar kind. It comments on the Eight Limbs' verses, not the Four Tantras' ch. 7, whose line is the same verse turned. Byang pa (etext p. 205) copies the Moonbeams' gloss almost word for word and names it. Rin spungs pa (p. 227) reads ya mtshan can as a monk, a gone-forth or a pure Brahmin of perfected qualities, no outsider, and still cites the Moonbeams for the bad messenger (p. 229). The Blue Beryl (pp. 198-199, 201) says the word is the name of an outsider's vow-observance, cites the Moonbeams' gloss, and names as the good ones of a like kind Buddhist monks, a school of yogis, the communities of the Tibetan king's offering and clean householders. Khro ru (VEIE0OPI4CEE4109_I5618, pp. 296-297) reads one of exceptional qualities, gone forth with a virtuous mind, and does not cite the Moonbeams there. So for the first half of q973: the Moonbeams reads an outsider ascetic; the later commentaries split, the earliest of them (Byang pa) following the Moonbeams. Etext only.
