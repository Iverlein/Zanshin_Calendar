# Death on a trigram by the dead's year: the four eyes and the four horns

# Death on a trigram by the dead's year: the four eyes and the four horns

WB vol. 2, pp. 109–111 (Yigdzin-1 read through on the Gser thang reprint) gives a further rite where the dead's year animal and the trigram of death fall together. The four cardinal trigrams are "the eyes that look", the four corners "the striking horns".

| Dead's year | Trigram of death | Name | The rite in short | Not done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bird, monkey | *li* | the brain and eye-fat taken unnoticed | names in a sheep's skull under the corpse; a cubit-high clay man with a pig's skull head and the enemy's name, pressed with own-spun yarn; the *Uṣṇīṣa*, market reversal | rough on the head-gatherer |
| tiger, hare | *dwa* | the heart torn out unnoticed; the yellow earth lord's heart-strike | three hearts hidden beside the corpse (not burned); fish or frog skull on the head; iron and copper powder; ox and sheep skulls with *li*'s seal; *kham*'s ransom and nine beams in a hole at the foot; *Avataṃsaka*, *Vajra Beak* | the run cuts the vein unnoticed |
| horse, snake | *kham* | the trigram's bad slip lost | names in a weasel's and a turtle's skull under the corpse, a goat's and a dog's skull above; a yellow stupa with the five jewels on the corpse, enthroned by a sheep-year man; Yama's heart-mantra; the dharma read in the market | bad for thirteen girls |
| the four executioners | *zin* | "the rotten nest of existence" | names in a donkey's skull with a peg; the donkey's limbs pressed in the directions; an eight-spoked wheel with the *Eight Appearances*, a bird's skull above; the *Twenty Thousand*, nine rites | bad for seven |
| dog, pig | *khon* | "the door separated" | gold, turquoise, copper and iron in a woman's left sleeve; an old woman's clay figure; names in a sheep's skull; a red stupa a cubit high, enthroned by a sheep-year man; tiger and hare bones in a blind carpenter's skull on the corpse | an old woman leads, bad for seven |
| any | *khen* | the sky iron makes a grave-door | the king spirit's name in an old speckled horse's skull, the man's in a dog's skull above, an old man's clay figure above that, a yellow stupa on top; the *Ratnakūṭa* | bad for nine |
| rat, pig | *gin* | a strike | ox and sheep skulls tied with blue and red thread, names inside, under the corpse, covered with stones from three peaks; a meditator on an elephant's figure; a mantra in a defective man's skull at a north-east rock; the *Prophecy of Ox Horn* | three die |
| any | *zon* | "the robe's hem torn" | names in a madwoman's hybrid-skull; a robe's hem put in; the death-*sri* pressed under a pass cairn; the *Gzungs bsdus* and the earth-lord rite | bad for three, for women and siblings |

The funeral share is given by exchange for *gin*, *zon*, *kham* and *khon*, by a Bonpo for *li*, *khen*, *dwa* and *zin* (p. 111). Mipham's notes count these as the year's trigram in pairs, ten remedies (p. 517).
