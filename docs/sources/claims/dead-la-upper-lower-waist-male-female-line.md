# The la pebbles' upper, lower and waist: WB states them as the male line, the female line and a split of the kin

# The la pebbles' upper, lower and waist: WB states them as the male line, the female line and a split in the kin

WB's general verdict of the twelve la pebbles (བླ་རྡེལ, bla rdel; vol. 2, p. 52, 1996 scan img. 60 ll. 22–23, Gser thang reprint img. 058 ll. 22–23, Zhol print img. 699, the end of line 5) says what an empty upper, a lower and a middle mean, in so many words:

> རྡེལ་སྟོད་སྟོངས་ན་ཕོ་བརྒྱུད་ཀྱི། །ས་ན་དར་གུད་གང་སྟོངས་ཆེ། །རྡེལ་སྨད་སྟོངས་ན་མོ་བརྒྱུད་རྨང་། །རྡེའུ་རྐེད་པར་ཆད་པ་ན། །ཕ་ཚན་དུར་སར་བཅས་པ་ནི། །གཉིས་སམ་གསུམ་དུ་གྱེས་ཤིང་ཆད།

If the upper pebbles (རྡེལ་སྟོད, rdel stod) are empty, it is the male line's (ཕོ་བརྒྱུད, pho brgyud): the more of whichever place's rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud) is empty, the greater [the harm]. If the lower pebbles (རྡེལ་སྨད, rdel smad) are empty, it is the female line's (མོ་བརྒྱུད, mo brgyud) «རྨང». If the pebbles are cut at the waist (རྐེད་པ, rked pa), the paternal kin (ཕ་ཚན, pha tshan) with its grave splits into two or three and is cut off.

So the pairing of upper with the male line and lower with the female line is WB's own statement, not an inference (the claims dead-la-general-verdict and dz-dead-board-rows called it unstated). All three prints read the same words. The last word of the lower clause is «རྨང» on the scan of the 1996 print (Yigdzin-1, MITRA reads «མང»), the reprint and the Zhol print: in its ordinary sense a foundation, so "the female line's foundation [is empty]"; the earlier gloss "cut off [?]" was a guess (the ordinary sense is from general knowledge, not looked up in a dictionary).

What WB does not say is which of the twelve places (the cut-offs, the mother, power, son, enemy and friend years, each great and small; dead-la-pebbles-layout) lie in the upper, the lower and the middle. The verse names the places only by their own names, and the notes (p. 502, wb.2:p502) say that what each image refers to is "not seen in the commentary" and is judged from the general shape; they add that the white and black pebbles are laid "from the eldest down" (རྒན་རིམ་ནས་བཞག, rgan rim nas bzhag), which suggests the row runs from the old to the young, upper being the elders'. The same vocabulary stands in ch. 26's general board of eight (wb.1:p471-472): the upper three (ཡར་གསུམ, yar gsum) are the place of the forebears and the first part of life, the lower three (མར་གསུམ, mar gsum) that of oneself and the last part, the middle two (བར་གཉིས, bar gnyis) that of harm by demons and wealth; and «རྡེལ་སྟོད་ནག་ལ་རྡེལ་སྨད་དཀར», upper black and lower white, is there "the forebears decline though oneself rises". That is another board (eight places, not twelve), so it shows the words' habit, not the twelve la places' division.

No division of the twelve places is in the manuals read for the question (the *Moonbeams*' board, dz-dead-board-rows and la-pebbles-colours-manuals); BDRC's full-text search for «རྡེལ་སྟོད་སྟོངས་ན» and «རྡེལ་སྨད་སྟོངས་ན» as exact phrases found no hit (2026-11-03).
