# Ch. 32's and ch. 34's counts of the digging figure sum to four mtho at the navel if one mtho is twelve finger-breadths

Ch. 32 (wb.2:p281) and ch. 34 (wb.2:p387) give the digging figure's parts in the same *mtho* (མཐོ, *mtho*), and the two counts agree to the end if one *mtho* is twelve finger-breadths (སོར་མོ, *sor mo*). Ch. 32 (read on the reprint, the lines «ལུས་ཀྱི་དཀྱུས་སུ་མཐོ་བཅུ་པ» to «དེ་བཞིན་སྦྲུལ་མཇུག་མཐོ་ལྔར་འདོད», 2026-10-28): the body ten *mtho* long; the stake, face, heart, navel and secret place one *mtho* each, five, and the tail five. So the stake, face, heart and navel together are four *mtho*, and the secret place, the fifth, brings the figure to its middle. Ch. 34 (read on the reprint, the lines «གདེངས་ཀ་མཐོ་ཕྱེད་ཡིན། གདོང་ལ་མཐོ་གང་སྟོད་ལ་དོ། ལྟེ་བར་མཐོ་གང་སོར་དྲུག་གནས། གསང་གནས་ས་ཡི་དབུས་ཡིན་ལ»): the stake half a *mtho*, the face one, the chest the same, the navel one *mtho* and six finger-breadths, the secret place the middle of the ground. If the six finger-breadths are half a *mtho* (twelve finger-breadths the *mtho*), the example's navel is one and a half, and the sum to the navel is 0.5 + 1 + 1 + 1.5 = 4, as in ch. 32, with the secret place at the fifth, the middle. With any other length of the *mtho* in finger-breadths the sum does not come to four. So the example's own numbers fit the dictionaries' twelve; and since ch. 32 makes the figure ten *mtho* long on a plot ten great parts a side, one *mtho* is also one great part (nine small parts, «ཆ་ཕྲན་དགུ») of the plot. The two are not in conflict if the figure is drawn at the digger's own span, which the phrase «རང་མཐོ» ("own *mtho*") may mean. Not settled: whether «རང་མཐོ» is the digger's own span or the figure's own unit; no text read says which a digger measures with, and the build need not give a length.
