# The omens' «middle watch» stands between midnight and the end of midnight, not in the second watch; no fourth watch is named in the omens (RD394, q1058)

In Derge Kanjur ch. 18 of the Mañjuśrī root tantra the words «བར་མའི་ཐུན» (bar ma'i thun, the middle watch) occur once in the whole volume, in the earthquake omens at [p436], and the order of the lines fixes what they stand for. The night's omens run: the first watch (མཚན་མོའི་ཐུན་ནི་དང་པོ), with its decline and its run out; the beginning of the second watch (ཐུན་ནི་གཉིས་པ་དང་པོ); the middle of the second watch (གཉིས་པའི་ཐུན་དཀྱིལ་དུ); then «at midnight» (མཚན་ཕྱེད་དུས་དག་ཏུ), a shaking that kills the eastern lord; then «the middle watch having lessened, near its end it shakes» (བར་མའི་ཐུན་ནི་བྲི་གྱུར་ནས། ཐ་མར་ཉེ་ལ་འགུལ་བ་བྱུང་), for all lands peace and no disease; then «at the end of midnight» (མཚན་ཕྱེད་མཐར་གྱུར་ལ), a shaking in the middle of the wet land; then «going into the third watch» (ཐུན་ནི་གསུམ་པར་ཕྱིན་ནས); then dawn and sunrise (mmk.1:p435-436, mmk.1:p436-437). The second watch has its own ordinal and its own beginning and middle, so the «middle watch» is not it. It stands between «midnight» and «the end of midnight», in the pattern the first watch has («beginning, lessening, run out»), so it is the stretch that midnight opens and the end of midnight closes: the middle of the night as a watch of its own, after the second watch's middle and before the third. This is the order of the lines, not a definition: the text never says what a «middle watch» is, and the division into three numbered watches plus a middle one does not match four equal watches of the night (the measure of mmk.1:p438-439), where midnight is a boundary and not a watch. The corrected reading refines the earlier note that midnight falls at the close of the second watch (mmk-ch18-night-omens-name-three-watches), which this does not contradict.

The second half of q1058: no fourth watch is named in the omens. The sequence goes from the third watch straight to dawn. In all of the volume's etext the ordinal «fourth watch» (ཐུན་ནི་བཞི, ཐུན་བཞི་པ, བཞི་པའི་ཐུན) does not occur; the last watch of the night is called «the last» (ཐུན་ཐ་མ, ཐུན་གྱི་ཐ་མ) at [p408] and [p430], and the dream verse's «fourth» is a numeral in the verse (mmk.1:p396-397, p397). So the fourth watch is named only as «the fourth» in the dream verse and as «the last» elsewhere. KD vol. 2's etext (VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4716, pp. 410–411) quotes the same omens in a metrical form with the same «beginning of the second», «middle of the second watch» and «middle watch» in the same order and no gloss, so it is a second copy of the lines, not a witness to their meaning.
