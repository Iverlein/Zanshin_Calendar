# «ལོ་རྟ» and «སྣང་པར་བཤད»: the rough years and the rough seven as they appear

In WB «ལོ་རྟ» (lo rta) is the year as a counted, ridden thing: «ལོ་རྟ་ཞོན་ནས» (vol. 1, img. 241 of the 1996 etext, 'riding the year-horse'), «བཙས་པའི་ལོ་རྟ་མང་བ་ན», «ཁྲུས་བྱེད་ལོ་རྟ་མང་བ་ན» (img. 315, ch. 22: when the years of birth, of washing, are many, that is the years that meet a stage), «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ལོ་རྟ་དག་དགྲ་དུར་ཡི» (vol. 2, img. 107, with the grave and the enemy). So p. 391's «དུར་གཤེད་རྩུབ་པའི་ལོ་རྟ» is the rough years of the grave and the slayer, years and not a horse; no dictionary has the compound. «རྩུབ་བདུན་འདི་ནི་སྣང་པར་བཤད» stands once in WB and nowhere else in BDRC's full text (its other hits for «སྣང་པར་བཤད» are other works' own uses, 'told to appear'): the plain reading, 'these seven rough ones are told as they appear', that is as they come on the year, month, day and hour, is the one the lines after it continue («ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ལ་འཆར་བའི་ཚེ»), and the line cites no work called *Snang*.
