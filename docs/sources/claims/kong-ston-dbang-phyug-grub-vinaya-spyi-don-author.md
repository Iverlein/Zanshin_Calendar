# Kong ston Dbang phyug grub pa wrote the Vinaya overview with the dawn and the nine parts (RD322, q938)

The Sakya Vinaya treatise that sets the dawn against the night's three watches and nine parts ("some say ... the dawn arises in the middle of the last watch; dividing into nine, from the middle of the seventh"; his own view: from the middle of the eighth part) is the «འདུལ་བ་མདོ་རྩ་བའི་སྤྱི་དོན་ལེགས་བཤད་འོད་ཀྱི་སྣང་བ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ» ('Dul ba mdo rtsa ba'i spyi don legs bshad 'od kyi snang ba tshig don rab gsal), by Kong ston Dbang phyug grub pa (BDRC P987, work MW23205), a pupil of Go rams pa Bsod nams seng ge, who wrote it at the request of Ngag gi dbang phyug Grags pa rgyal mtshan dpal bzang po in a wood-male-dog year at Thub bstan rnam rgyal. The Beijing edition of Go rams pa's collected works prints it (and BDRC's record of that print lists Go rams pa as creator), but its own colophon and the Sakya editions' editors name the pupil. The clause on the ninth part is the same in all three etexts.
