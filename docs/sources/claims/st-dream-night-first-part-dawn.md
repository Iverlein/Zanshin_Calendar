# The Explanatory Tantra's rule of the dream by night: the first part of the night forgotten and without fruit, the clear dawn dream gives it

The Explanatory Tantra (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud), ch. 7 (the signs of the body's destruction, ལུས་ཀྱི་འཇིག་ལྟས, lus kyi 'jig ltas), in its run of dream verses, gives its one rule of time for a dream: «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད་ངེས་འབྲས་བུ་མེད། ཐོ་རངས་མཐོང་དང་གསལ་ན་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན།» A dream of the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod) is surely forgotten and has no fruit; a dream at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) that is seen and is clear gives its fruit. It stands after the six kinds of dream (seen, heard, felt, a prayer, a sign of accomplishment, born of illness) and before the list of good dreams; the order of the verses in the commentaries is: the bad dreams of the sick, their cause, who dies of them, the six kinds, this rule, the good dreams.

Where it is read. On the Drathang woodblock print (GT, I4CZ327131, img. 42, machine reading with the first half of the lowest line read by eye) the lines stand in ch. 7 after the six kinds, with «བརྗེད་ངས». They are also in the etexts of the Explanatory Tantra of the Yu thog edition (BDRC I1KG25029, etext pp. 44–45), the Grwa thang edition (I1PD464, pp. 68–69) and a Peking edition of the Four Tantras (I4839, pp. 49–50), and are quoted in the commentaries below. The Serthang print (BDRC I3CN5038) is taken to have them on printed pp. 31–32, which its scan does not hold (see st-serthang-scan-pp31-32-missing); they are not on the scan, so this reading did not see them there. The Bon medical corpus carries the same two lines (a Bon Kangyur volume, a Bon *Gso rig 'bum bzhi*); a Bon text is a witness to the Buddhist text only and nothing is taken from it. The spelling differs: the Yu thog, Grwa thang and Peking etexts, Khro ru's commentary and one Byang pa etext read «བརྗེད་ངས» (brjed ngas, forgetfulness, the word the Serthang print has in the heart-illness signs, «བརྗེད་ངས་ཆེ»); other etexts and the modern quotations read «བརྗེད་ངེས» (surely forgotten). The sense is the same.

What the commentaries say of «མཚན་སྟོད».
- Byang pa (bjp:p183, quoting the *Moonbeams*, Zla zer): the first part of the night is a time when dreams are not clear and what arose is forgotten; and the *Moonbeams* line «a dream of the first part of the night: its fruit is far, what is seen is made small» is glossed: a dream dreamt up to the half of the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད, mtshan stod phyed pa yan chad) has its fruit far off and is for the most part small and weak. The same *Moonbeams* quote has the dream seen at daybreak, sleep not taken again and not cut by unfitting talk, giving a great fruit that very day.
- The *Dpe tshogs* text of 'Bum nag gsal sgron (BDRC etext I1PD409, p. 85; the work is not yet registered as a source here): a dream dreamt from midnight upward (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ལ, nam phyed yan la, that is, in the earlier half) is surely forgotten and has no fruit; one dreamt from midnight on (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཚུན་ལ, nam phyed tshun la) and not clearly forgotten gives its fruit.
- Rin spungs pa (rsp:p213): the first part's dream is unclear, heavy sleep presses after it and the earlier dreams are forgotten; dawn is when the wind-mind of most beings enters the central channel, the body is clear and sleep is thin, so a dream then is as if seen before one.
- Khro ru (modern author, ktr:p306-307, etext only; facts only): the first part, that is dusk (སྲོད, srod), is cut by other sleep and forgotten, so no fruit, and midnight is of a kind with it; a dream at the close of dawn, with no sleep after and kept clear in mind, surely gives its fruit.
- The 'Bri gung (bdk:p53-54): the first part's dream forgotten is a delusion (འཁྲུལ་པ, 'khrul pa) with no fruit; a clear dawn dream surely gives its fruit.
- The *Gso bya lus kyi rnam bshad* (BDRC MW8LS18598, etext restricted, search snippet only) brings the line as «from the Explanatory Tantra» when it says that a dream's fruit is tied to the time it was seen; no more of it was reached.

So the commentaries agree that «མཚན་སྟོད» is the part of the night before the dawn and that its dreams are lost to the sleeper; they differ on how far it reaches: up to midnight (Byang pa, 'Bum nag gsal sgron) or dusk, with midnight alike (Khro ru). None gives a clock hour or a count of days for the fruit.
