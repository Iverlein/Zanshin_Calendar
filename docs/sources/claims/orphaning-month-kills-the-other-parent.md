# The father's orphaning month kills the mother, the child's both

Reading of 2026-11-01 (question 236), not a stated gloss. The notes' «ཕ་ཡི་དྭ་ཟླས་མ་འཆི། བུ་ཡིས་གཉིས་ཀ་འཆི་བའོ» (vol. 2, p. 488; the line breaks after «དྭ་», read on the Gser thang reprint, I3CN5029 img. 494, 2026-11-01) glosses WB's three orphaning months of the year-of-life chapter (the tomb month the father's, the womb month the mother's, the months of illness and death the child's own; see year-of-life.md, *Open questions 161–165*). «མ» is the noun, the mother: by the father's orphaning month the mother dies, and by the child's own both parents. Two things support it: the pair on p. 489 (wb.2:p489, read by Yigdzin-1 and by the reprint's etext), «དྭ་ཟླ་ནི་སྐབས་འདིར་ཕར་ངན་པ་མའི་དྭ་ཟླ་གོང་བཤད་ལྟར་དང་», the orphaning month "here is the mother's orphaning month as told above, bad for the father" — the mother's month harms the father, so the father's harms the mother; and the third clause, «བུ་ཡིས་གཉིས་ཀ་འཆི», which needs the first two to name one parent each. As a negative («མ་འཆི», "does not die") the clause would have no subject and would stand against the pair. Read again on 2026-11-03 (RD132) on the 1996 print itself (I1KG12907 img. 496, lines 18–19, by eye at 4×): the same break, «ཕ་ཡི་དྭ་» ending one line and «ཟླས་མ་འཆི། བུ་ཡིས་གཉིས་ཀ་འཆི་བའོ།» opening the next, so the reading does not rest on the reprint alone. A grammatical point against the negative: before a present or future stem such as འཆི the negative is «མི», not «མ» (which negates past and imperative stems), so «མ་འཆི» is not "does not die"; and the clause stands in a run of «ཕ་ཡི», «བུ་ཡིས» that names the family. What «གཉིས་ཀ» (both) holds is still an inference from the pair: both parents. BDRC's search for «ཕ་ཡི་དྭ་ཟླས» finds this line only (the 1996 notes).
