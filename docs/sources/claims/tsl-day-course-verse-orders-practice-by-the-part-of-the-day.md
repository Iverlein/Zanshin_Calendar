# The treasure's verse on the day's course orders practice by ten parts of day and night; its only day of the month is the tenth

The verse of the treasure of Rtsa gsum gling pa (rank 2) quoted in vol. 27 of the edition (images 221-222, printed pp. 214-215, read on the scan) is an instruction for every day, given, as the editor's frame says, to the prince, the lord, Ye shes mtsho rgyal, Gnubs chen and the other subjects, from the Heart Practice Bsam pa lhun grub ma. It names ten parts of the day and night, dawn, daybreak, sunrise, noon, past noon, afternoon, sunset, dusk, after dusk and the last part of the night, and gives each a reflection or practice (prayer to the field of great bliss, impermanence, mixing conduct with the after-attainment, the decline of the times, the parting of the elements, the bardo, karma, birth) and a prayer to the speaker.

It contains no schedule by the day of the month. The one lunar day in it is the tenth (ཚེས་བཅུ, tshes bcu), in the noon line: the prophecy in person, on the tenth meet in pure vision (དག་སྣང, dag snang). The editor's prose after the verse (tsl.27:i225, etext) says that for the fortunate, at times of special quality, the tenth and the like, a pure vision in person, in experience or in dream comes. For whom: the verse closes "prince, lord and subjects, in the future act so", so practitioners of the treasure in general, lay or ordained; no benefit by date is named beyond the meeting on the tenth.

In vol. 27's etext the 8th, the 15th, the 25th, the 29th, the 30th, the full and new moon, the one-day vow and the restoring rite do not occur as days of practice (the hits of those words that were opened are colophon dates, a date of death and counts of days in a retreat schedule; the one-day vow, the restoring rite, the full and new moon and the great occasions have no hit at all).
