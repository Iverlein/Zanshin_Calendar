# The tenth day: what is done and what is promised

What is to be done on the tenth day (ཚེས་བཅུ, tshes bcu) and what is promised for it, in the passages read.

The practice. The Key Instruction on Accomplishing the Guru (བླ་མ་སྒྲུབ་པའི་གནད་ཡིག, bla ma sgrub pa'i gnad yig), as Mipham quotes it (mph.127:i305), tells those who entrust themselves to the Guru to set up the torma (གཏོར་མ, gtor ma) called Blazing Jewel, to sound the skull drum (ཐོད་པ་ལྟག་སྤྲོད, thod pa ltag sprod) and to call him with the Seven-Line Prayer (ཚིག་བདུན་གསོལ་འདེབས, tshig bdun gsol 'debs) in a melody of longing; the Guru's own promise names the same prayer, melody and drum (mph.127:i302). The verses quoted after Ratna gling pa's Means of Urging on the Tenth Day (mph.127:i303-304) name "striving and praying on my tenth day" and prayer with the Seven-Line Prayer. 'Jigs med gling pa's prayer names "keeping the tenth day's offering" (ཚེས་བཅུའི་དུས་མཆོད, tshes bcu'i dus mchod) and praying, with the remembrance of the Guru's life (jgl.8:p739-742).

The benefit. The Guru comes and blesses; he gives the supreme and common accomplishments; one who spent his life accomplishing the guru dissolves at death into his heart (mph.127:i302); blessing descends like a river, and the blazing of experience in all present is the sign that he has come; one who strives and prays on the tenth may entrust himself wholly to him as to falling into saṃsāra (mph.127:i303-304). In 'Jigs med gling pa: the tenth days are the great occasions of the Guru's deeds and the time when the mothers and ḍākinīs gather; mastery of the four activities; rain in season, sickness of people and cattle stilled, frost and hail, poverty, war and loss stilled.

For whom. No passage read restricts the day to monastics or to holders of tantric vows: the addressees are "those devoted to me" and the subjects of Tibet at large. The torma and the skull drum are a ritual practitioner's implements; the prayer and the offering are asked of all.

Not named in these passages: the feast gathering (ཚོགས་འཁོར, tshogs 'khor) by that word, the Vajra Guru mantra, lamps, a count of recitations, a time of day other than sunrise for the sixth month's tenth. The app's reading of the tenth after Edition Rabten (the tsok rites, guru pūjās) is not contradicted; these texts add the Seven-Line Prayer and the Guru's coming as the reason.
