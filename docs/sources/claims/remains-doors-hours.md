# The living's and the dead's directions, the hours and the corpse-bearer

The living's and the dead's directions, the hours, and the corpse-bearer at the sending of the remains (WB vol. 2, pp. 99–100; read on the scan, the Gser thang reprint, 2026-10-23):

- **The living's outer door direction** (གསོན་ཕྱི་སྒོ་ཕྱོགས, gson phyi sgo phyogs): each living person's year blocks its own direction (a tiger-year person: the tiger's); when the living are many, only each one's own power is avoided; many of the tiger: the monkey avoided; many of tiger and hare: the bird and monkey; the seventh and the enemy not sent onto; for the day and hour, one's own and the enemy directions shunned.
- **The dead's slayer direction**: horse and snake, north; bird and monkey, south; tiger and hare, west; mouse and pig, the corner(s) (as printed); the corpse is not sent toward the four slayers (the print's «ཤར་དུ», "to the east" or "where they rise", is not plain). The four-slayer on the five-yellow is bad for the friends: a mouse- or pig-year corpse sent there, bad for the horse- and snake-year living.
- **Kinds of corpse not drawn onto an animal's direction**: *bkra* not onto the monkey, *sri'u* not onto the snake, *lhog* not onto the tiger, *'bras*, *gnyan* and plague not onto the bird, *dmu chu* and *rdel* illness not onto the mouse, the *byad kha* and the monk not onto the ox, the mad not onto zon; and by the dead's white year (pp. 99): knife-death onto the tiger, the *nal ma* onto the hare, the choked onto the dragon, the mad, *ma mo* and *hreng* onto the snake, the plague, mute and *lhog* onto the horse, the *thibs gri* and leper onto the sheep, *bkra* onto the monkey, *sre'u* onto the pig, *dmu chu* and belly illness onto the mouse, the *phur kha brje* onto the ox; the bird, dog and *yangs* not drawn, some say. A corpse drawn onto a male year is bad for males, onto a female year for females.
- **Avoided in general**: the year's, month's, day's and hour's wind-horse years; the clan's enemy and grave; the dead's own, seventh, power and enemy; the general slayers, ox, sheep, dog and dragon; the particular slayer, the grave year; planets and mansions with repetition; the dead's mouse, dragon and monkey years; one's own la-planet and *mig dmar*; where harmful earth lords move. The opposite is drawn as good.
- **The hours of the funeral**: said to be bad at dawn for the reckoner, at sunrise for the maternal uncle, in the morning warmth for the priest, at noon for the nephews, in the evening for sons-in-law and nephews, at midnight for the spiritual friend; WB finds no pure source for this, notes that Sprug and others hold day good and night bad, and puts the particular hours first.
- **The corpse's facing**: died on li, it faces khen; on khen, li (its own fighting enemy); or it faces the way it is led, away from bad earth lords.
- **The corpse-bearer's bad times** (p. 100): ox year, the evening, north; horse year, sunset, east and south; sheep year, sunrise, south-west; bird year, the great warmth; dog year, dawn to the great warmth, east; every day the two noons and the two "rays" are demon times; above all the east at sunrise, the south at noon, the west at sunset and the north at midnight. Not to carry: one whose great obstacle shares the dead's seat, or whose seventh, enemy or slayer meets the dead; a horse- or snake-year man for one who died on the mouse-pig kham; where the dead and the bearer are bad enemies, or their trigrams' fighting ones (འཐབ་དག, 'thab dag) meet; one on whom the three whites fall. The bearer's month's, day's and hour's seventh, obstacle and slayer are avoided.
