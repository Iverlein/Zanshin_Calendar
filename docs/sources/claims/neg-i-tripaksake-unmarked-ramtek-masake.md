# Negelein marks nothing at 'tripakṣake' of his manuscript i; the Ramtek copy has a locative in -māsake for the third watch (RD312)

Negelein prints the verse of his manuscript i (Leipzig 1178, "Bṛhaspati said") on p. 15 of the 1912 print (leaf n44), without a note or a mark on the third watch: "dvitīye cā'ṣṭame māse triyāme ca tripakṣake". He prints the two lines before it as a parallel, "like s 2–3", the Matsya's text, and does not say that the manuscript itself reads "tripakṣake" or that he emends it. The other copies of the Bṛhaspati *Svapnādhyāya* read here give the third watch: Srinagar "triyāmake" after "tribhir māsais", Penn 1871 "tribhir māsais triyāmake" (RD277), Ramtek M 856 a locative "-māsake" after "tṛtīye" (the conjunct before it looks like "tri", moderate): none has a fortnight. The Ramtek copy also shares with i the form of the fourth watch's line, "caturthe yāme tu yaḥ svapno māsena phala-" (i: "tūrye yāme tu yaḥ svapno māsena phalado bhavet"), and a month for it; so the fourth watch of i and of every copy read is a month (the Odia print has a month and a half), never half a month, and the half-month stands only in the Brahmavaivarta and the Svapnohana.

What follows: "tripakṣake" of i is the one reading of the three fortnights, found in no open copy; the Ramtek "…māsake" makes a corruption of "trimāsake" (three months, "tri-māsake") a possible origin of i's word, since "trimāsake" and "tripakṣake" differ in the second member only and the verse's metre allows both; the Leipzig manuscript is the only thing that would decide it.
