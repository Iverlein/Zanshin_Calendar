# WB ch. 29's counts recomputed: the parts close, the whole is 57 over

Recomputed from the count lines of WB vol. 2, pp. 44–51 (the 1996 etext, lines of p. 50 and p. 47 checked by eye on the Gser thang reprint). The sixteen parts as each states itself sum to 993 result and 9,321 identifying pebbles, 10,314 (checked again, digit by digit); the opening's 1,008 + 9,363 = 10,371 is itself consistent (1,000 + 8; 9,300 + 63). The gap is 15 result and 42 identifying pebbles, 57 in all.

Where a part's own pieces can be added, they close for several parts: the seventh's 135 identifying are 57 + 6 + 13 + 4 (the blades "gathered into four") + 26 + 21 + 8; the eighth's 180 are 8 + 9 + 40 + 29 + 32 + 33 + 29; the ninth's 177 are 8 + 11 + 10 + 25 + 15 + 13 + 83 + 12; the twelfth's 3,035 identifying are exactly 1,874 + 287 + 220 + 86 + 74 + 133 + 177 + 31 + 121 + 26 + 6 (with the year's 287 holding the 26 kings and ministers inside the 49); the eleventh's 94 are 13 + 5 + 5 + 23 + 8 + 4 + 6 + 20 + 9 + 1, so the leftover pebble's 1 + 9 is already inside the 94 and the earlier worry that it was uncounted falls away. They do not close for the fourteenth (components 143 result and 2,768 identifying against 136 and 2,784) nor for the twelfth's result pebbles (components exceed 151), nor, by one reading of its strikes, the tenth's 1,952; there the print's own sub-totals disagree with its totals.

Two arithmetic coincidences, not stated by the text: the identifying gap 42 is the six lamps' 59 minus the preliminary's 17; the result gap 15 is the twelve la pebbles plus the three verdicts. Neither is a reading of the passage; the preliminary's 39 and 17 cannot be rebuilt from the verses (they vary with the household). So the 57 stay a gap in the work's own figures: three prints carry the same totals (question 65).
