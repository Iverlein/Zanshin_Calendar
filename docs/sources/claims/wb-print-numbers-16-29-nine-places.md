# The print numbers the hal khyi's group 16–25 and Dkar-sham 26; Langging is 28 in the print

The numbers the print carves in the chapter run on the page as follows. The hal khyi is «༡༦», and the bracket after «མ་ལྔ་བུ་ལྔ་བཅས་པ་སྟེ» holds nine numbers «༡༧ ༡༨ ༡༩ ༢༠ ༢༡ ༢༢ ༢༣ ༢༤ ༢༥» (six on the first line and three on the second, each under an overline, read on the Gser thang reprint at 2.8x; the glyph of «༢༤» is the least clear). The next four headings on p. 186 are Dkar-sham «༢༦», Sbal-te «༢༧», Langging «༢༨» and Tsang-kun the bird «༢༩». So the print's number runs on from 16 to 26 across the nine bracketed places, and the numbers after them are nine above the order in which this pool counts the lords (Dkar-sham no. 17, Langging no. 19, Tsang-kun no. 20 in year-month-earth-lords.md). The «no. 19» given for Langging in the passages p186b and p186d and in the claims on his line is that pool order, not a number in the print: the print's number is 28. That the offset is exactly nine, and the bracket holds nine numbers although five mothers and five sons are ten persons, is a fact of the print; the verse does not say which numbers belong to which mother or son, nor whether the bracket counts the hal khyi's own place among the nine. No line of p. 185, the verse, the note or the work and remedy lines to the end of the page, names a seat for any mother or son (read by eye for «གནས», «གདན» and a direction; the page's directions «ཕྱོགས་དེར» refer to the lord's own seat).
