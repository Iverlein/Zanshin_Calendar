# The thirty: the thirtieth point, the eight-petalled lotus, for great men, the learned and the craftsmen, at the place of the luck

The thirtieth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the eight-petalled lotus (པདྨ་འདབ་བརྒྱད, padma 'dab brgyad), is read for great men, the learned and the craftsmen, at the place of the luck (ཀླུང་རྟ, klung rta). Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the luck's flagpole is broken | The flagpole grows: forefathers like kings, daughters («བུ་མོ», as printed) like ministers | Broken: the near rise as enemies unexpectedly, enemies and thieves rob, the forefathers' country is lost and one wanders elsewhere | Strive at the rite of raising the luck |
| 2 | Whether the gossip of the country's people strikes | No hostile gossip, good | Though there is no fault, gossip hates; giving pleases no one; acts are fruitless | Strive at white acts; offer clothes to stupas, sweep the narrow paths, strive at whatever roots of virtue can be done |
| 3 | Whether the pleasing fame has faded | The luck's banner stands: in decline now but good in the long run | The luck's banner fades: mocking talk from the country's people; obstacles to life | Fulfilment, restoring, approach and accomplishment, many |
| 4 | Whether the luck is caught in the demon's net | Not caught: all aims achieved, good | Caught: blame comes | The rakṣa's (རཀྴ, rakSha) enemy thread-cross (མདོས, mdos) and the gossip skyas help |
| 5 | Whether the luck's *dmu yab* (དམུ་ཡབ, dmu yab; not identified) has dried | Not dried, good | Dried, bad: no prosperity of wealth, no essence of food; acts fruitless, wealth does not stay | Offering to the gods (ལྷ་གསོལ, lha gsol), summoning prosperity (གཡང་འགུགས, g.yang 'gugs), long-life practice; bind the livestock pledge and the like |
| 6 | Whether the luck's food is carried off by spirits | Not carried off | Food and wealth («སྲིས་སྤྱད[?]», doubtful) carried off by spirits, bad | — |
| 7 | Whether the cord between gods and men is cut | The gods do not give the person away, good | The gods sell the person; the protectors fall to the demons: bad in the forefathers' place | Offer to the gods and protectors |
| 8 | Whether «high love, low hate» comes | The banner of fame flies; men and women happy in old age, «warm when the sun is old» | High love, low hate | The luck-raising remedy rite (གཏོ, gto); through it very auspicious |

Whose year is reckoned: the person's own (the notes, vol. 2, p. 533: «གཞན་རྣམས་སོ་སོའི་ལོ་རྟ»).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
