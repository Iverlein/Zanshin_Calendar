# The Svapnohana's རྟ་བླ་མོ: the centipede; the line names creatures

The Svapnohana's «རྟ་བླ་མོ» (rta bla mo) in «རྟ་བླ་མོ་དང་ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་དང་། ཆུ་གནས་སྲོག་ཆགས་བཟུང་བ་ནི» (RD271, 2026-11-09; BDRC's exact full-text search, etext snippets, no scan): the centipede, with its feminine ending, and ལྟོ་འཕྱེ (lto 'phye, the belly-walker) the snake; so the line names creatures, not "a horse and a bla mo".

**The word.** རྟ་བླ་མོ (rta bla mo) is a stock word for a many-limbed creature in Indian tantras in Tibetan: BDRC has 20 hits, among them the *Guhyagarbha* commentary's «ལུས་གཅིག་ལ་ཡན་ལག་མང་པོ་དང་ལྡན་པ་སྟེ་དཔེར་ན་རྟ་བླ་མོ་ལྟ་བུའོ» (a body with many limbs, like the rta bla mo), the Saṃvara tantra's commentary «སྡིག་པ་དང་རྟ་བླ་མོ་ལ་སོགས་པ» (scorpions, rta bla mo and the like, among the harmful), and the Vajraḍāka's rite of grinding the rta bla mo fine. Beside it the dictionaries give རྟ་བླ (rta bla), the centipede (claim st-rta-bla-is-the-centipede). ལྟོ་འཕྱེ (lto 'phye) is the snake in its common poetic name (many hits, 3084). The line's three hits in BDRC (the *Rmi lam brtag pa* etexts, and the Derge's own) all read «རྟ་བླ་མོ་དང་ལྟོ་འཕྱེ»; the Peking and Narthang «རྟ་ལ་མོ» is the same word without the subjoined ལ (a scribal shortening).

**For the passage.** "A centipede and a snake, a creature of the water seized: victory or goods, or a son". The question q825 (which animal is the ལ་མོ) is narrowed by this: the word is the centipede; q814's «བཅུ་ཡི་བར» is not touched here. The passage `svo:p270` is a machine reading and the claim says only what the word is; the dream image dream-snake-frog-water-creature is not rewritten here.
