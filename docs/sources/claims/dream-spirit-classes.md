# A dream's figures sorted by the class of spirit (RST, KBS)

RST (etext) and KBS (the article quoting Klong rdol, machine) sort a dream's figures by the spirit whose display they are, and agree: a lone white man, a white bird soaring, a white skeleton, snow mountains: the gods; an old ruined temple, a fox, a small child, the owl: the 'gong po spirits; snakes, frogs, a blue-green girl, a weasel, meadows: the nāgas; rocks, trees, horsemen and soldiers: the btsan; an ash-coloured child, cliffs and ravines, a termite hole: the the'u rang; monks, a donkey, a monkey, a cat, a horse, a dog: the rgyal po spirits. If the figures speak kindly, give food, laugh and look kindly, the sign is good; if they show a fearful form, scowl or rage, bad. KBS adds bound body, going under the earth, imprisonment and beheading for the rbad 'dre spirit, and riding naked south, red flowers and a tiger's flesh as the forerunner of an obstacle to life. These are not verdicts on images but a sort by class; the picker takes them as notes, not as rows.
