# The turtle's wood verse in UT: the wood sheep is the cow's year, the remaining are nine

UT (the *Garland of Blue Utpalas*, etext only, printed p. 170) carries the golden turtle's root years in verse, close to WB's but shorter, and its wood verse reads «ཤིང་གྱུར་བཅུ་གཉིས་ཤིང་བྱི། ཤིང་ལུག་བ་ལ་ཤིང་གླང་མི། ལྷག་མ་དགུ་པོ་སྲིན་པོའི་ལོ།»: of the twelve wood years, the wood rat (class not named in the verse; WB's god); the wood sheep **for the cow** (བ་ལ, with བ, ba); the wood ox, man; the remaining **nine**, the years of *srin po*. WB (wb.2:p20-22) has «ཤིང་ལུག་པ» with པ on every print read and «ལྷག་མ་བཅུ་པོ», ten, on all of them.

What this settles of question 364. First, the wood sheep's class is the cow: UT's «བ» is the word, so WB's «པ» is a variant spelling or slip of the same syllable, and the identifying pebbles of WB (the cow two white) have a year to be read for. Second, the *srin po* are nine, as the sum asks: counted from the two texts' own class lists, the sixty years divide god 2, man 1, asura 2, animal 1, hungry ghost 1, hell 2, planet 8, *re 'jam* 9, garuda 4, *klu* 8, turtle 4, earth lord 8, cow 1, *srin po* 9 = 60; with ten, 61 and the wood has thirteen years. UT's fire, iron, water and earth verses agree with WB's in every class and count (fire monkey animal, snake hungry ghost, bird hell, dragon asura, eight planet; iron tiger god, monkey asura, bird hell, nine *re 'jam*; water rat, horse, ox, sheep and the garuda, eight *klu*; four earth slayers and the turtle, eight earth lords), so the wood verse is the only place the two part. The printed «བཅུ་པོ» of WB (five prints carved alike) is thus WB's own slip, or a slip of its exemplar, and not a different reckoning; no print read gives nine.

How a year "becomes" an element: by the element of its name in the sixty-year cycle, twelve years for each element (computed, 2026-11-14: a throwaway cycle of five elements by twos against the twelve animals gives twelve years to each element, and every year WB and UT name falls in the element they put it under: fire monkey, snake, bird, dragon; iron tiger, monkey, bird; water rat, horse, ox, sheep; wood rat, ox, sheep). The texts do not say it in words; it is the only count that fits all five groups.

State: UT is a modern edition read by etext only; the claim rests on it for the variant and on the counts for the rest.
