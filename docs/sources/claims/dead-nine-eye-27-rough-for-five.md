# The nine-eye of 27: «ཇ་ལ་རྩུབ» is probably «ལྔ་ལ་རྩུབ», rough for five

At the nine-eye of 27 (WB vol. 2, p. 87, l. 14) the Gser thang reprint prints, read twice by eye, «སྙིང་དང་འདྲ་བ་ཇ་ལ་རྩུབ» ("like the heart, ... rough"). The word before «ལ་རྩུབ» is read «ཇ» on the scan, but the three OCR readings of BDRC (Yigdzin-1, the 1996 etext, the Gser thang etext) all give «རྔ་ལ་རྩུབ», and the OCR of the Zhol print gives «ལྔ་ལ་རྩུབ» ("rough for five"). The line stands among rows of the same form, "rough for seven" at 18 («བདུན་ལ་རྩུབ») and "three, or seven, rough" at 63, in which a number and «ལ་རྩུབ» tell how many are struck. A stacked «ལྔ» (la with the subjoined nga) is a form that a small print shows as «ཇ» or «རྔ». «ཇ་ལ» and «རྔ་ལ» are in none of the dictionaries (Steinert's 64, searched 2026-11-01) and «ཇ་ལ་རྩུབ» and «རྔ་ལ་རྩུབ» have no hit in BDRC's full-text search beyond WB's own prints. Narrowed: the best reading is «ལྔ་ལ་རྩུབ», "rough for five" (the dead of 27 on *dwa*, like the heart strike, is rough on five); it rests on the Zhol print's OCR, the pattern of the rows and the letter's shape, and is to be confirmed on the Zhol scan.
