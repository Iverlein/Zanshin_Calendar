# སྦང་གཡེར in the goods of gossip: the beer's mash and the Sichuan pepper, written short

«སྦང་གཡེར» (sbang g.yer) and «སྤང་གཡེང»/«སྤང་གཡེར» in the Lama Gongdü's list of goods of gossip (RD271, 2026-11-09; Steinert's collection, BDRC full-text search, etext windows, no scan opened): the beer's mash (སྦང་མ, sbang ma) and the Sichuan pepper (གཡེར་མ, g.yer ma), each with its ending ma dropped.

**The dictionaries.** སྦང་མ (sbang ma): the mash of brewed beer, the dregs after the beer is pressed (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, the Tshig mdzod chen mo, the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs: the husk dregs left after brewing; Dan Martin: possibly beer yeast; the claim wb-sbang-ma-is-the-beer-mash). སྦང (sbang) alone: to wet, to soak, or dung (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo: separate senses). གཡེར་མ (g.yer ma): the pepper tree and its seed, Zanthoxylum or Guinea pepper (Jim Valby, Ives Waldo, Hopkins's pepper entries, the Tshig mdzod chen mo: a tree medicine, an aid to digestion that kills worms and eases hangovers, with its synonyms "the mouth-opener" and "the hot pepper"; the Dag tshig: a thorny tree with a black seed in a red husk, a strong spice). གཡེར (g.yer) alone is "to become wakeful, surprise" and an old word "equal". གཡེང (g.yeng) is "distracted, to float". སྤང (spang) is a meadow or the verb "to abandon". Not one of these makes a pair; the pairs སྦང་གཡེར and སྤང་གཡེང have no headword.

**In use.** The exact search has «སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ» in four etexts: Kongtrul's treasury's list «རམ་བུ་སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ་རུས་པ་སོགས་མི་ཁའི་རྫས», a medical collection («སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ་ཚྭ་སྔོན» among the additions to a meal) and two craft handbooks (the Bzo rig phyogs bsgrigs and the Bzo rtsis zlos gar), where «སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ་སྤྲུ་མ་གསུམ» are three substances for treating materials; and the Bon Kangyur and the Bon *'Bum nag* (Bon texts, witnesses to the usage only) have «སྦང་གཡེར་ཚ» together. The phrase «སྦང་གཡེར» (4 hits) is the short form of the two items. So «སྦང་གཡེར» is "the mash and the pepper", written short, as the lists write them.

**The variants.** Palri's «སྤང་གཡེང», Kazi's «སྤང་གཡེར» and the other prints' «སྦང་གཡེར» (`lgd.42:p969b`) are one item in four spellings by ear: spang for sbang (the same sound), g.yeng and g.yer near each other; the Ngodrup and Sherab Drimay print's «སྦར་ཁྱེར» is sbang g.yer in a spelling by ear (the ra for ng, kh for g.y). The reading best supported is the eastern prints' «སྦང་གཡེར», with the Zhe chen and the Kongtrul lists; Palri's «སྤང་གཡེང» (spang g.yeng) has no support in the dictionaries or the lists.

**For q804, q816, q826 and q840.** The "three words" are three items of a list; ram rug, sbang (ma) and g.yer (ma), none a place or a person, all things of the rite, the one unsettled (ram rug, see lgd-ram-rug-is-the-ram-bu-spelling).
