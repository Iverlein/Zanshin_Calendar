# KD pp. 632-634 is WB ch. 20's verse on the fifteen circuits in prose; the counts agree (1,624, 84,000, 120+180+180)

Nor brang O rgyan's account of the circuits (kd.1:p632-634) is not an independent source: it follows the White Beryl's verse, vol. 1 ch. 20, pp. 241-243 (wb.1:p242-243), line by line. WB's verse lists the fifteen Chinese circuits (the fifteenth: «ས་བདག་མགོ་གསུམ་ཕན་གཏོ་སྟེ། བཅོ་ལྔ་ཕན་རྒྱུའི་སྐོར་འགོའོ»), the fifteen Tibetan ones, the cherished things (vajra, wheel, turquoise swastika, lotus), 84,000 gates, the 360 tantras and the five, the 1,624 circuits of the elements in six groups, and the hidden points 120, 180 and 180 or 360 each in eight kinds; KD's counts are WB's, with the two syllables «ཆིག་སྟོང» (one thousand) giving 1,624, not 624. Ch. 26 works the thirty starting points of the same two fifteens, and there the three-headed earth lord stands as the twenty-sixth (wb.1:p468-469), not the fifteenth: the numbering of the ch. 26 list is its own. KD adds nothing a rule could rest on; the source of both is WB's verse, found in the Beijing print IE0OPI51524892 ([p252]-[p253]) and the Gser thang reprint IE0OPI3864355D ([p237]-[p238]), 2026-11-21.
