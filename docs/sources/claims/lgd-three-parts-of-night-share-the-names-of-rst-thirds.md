# LGD's dusk, midnight and dawn are the names of RST's three thirds of the night, with no hours

LGD's dusk, midnight and early dawn (སྲོད, srod; གུང, gung; ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs; lgd:p966-967) are the same three names as the three parts of the night in RST's verses (mdp:p138; rst:p1-8): the night is cut into three thirds (ཐུན་གྱི་གསུམ་ཆ, "the three-part division of the watch"), dusk the habit of the earlier, midnight the tricks of demon and srin, the dawn the one that comes clear. Neither text gives hours; RST says "thirds" and LGD gives no span. The Svapnohana's four watches (svo:p268) are a different division, and nothing in LGD uses the word for watch (ཐུན) of the night. So the answer to the question whether LGD fixes its parts by hours or by the Svapnohana's watches is that it names three parts by the three-part scheme of RST, the Buddha's night in WB (vol. 1, p. 22) and the Svapna-īkṣā, with no hours at all; LGD's midnight dream is of the afflictions where RST's is of demons' tricks, so the names are shared and the content is not.
