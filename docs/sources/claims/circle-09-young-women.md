# The circles: the ninth, the blue peacock, for young women

The ninth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the blue peacock, is read for young women. Its starting point: the dog of the mother element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points drawn from the dog of the mother element of the one reckoned. The text names eight questions, settled into five: whether the wool-thread's store is empty; whether the blue spring is dried; whether the *ha lo* flower is carried off by frost; whether the swastika fence is torn; whether the white silver mirror is cracked; whether the silk brocade's lustre has faded; whether the golden meadow's turquoise leaves are lost; whether the humble store is empty.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the blue spring not dried: long life, existence grows, no illness or demon. | dried: harm to life. | recite *Amitāyus* and receive the long-life empowerment; the nine springs of existence; three Bon priests of different clans ransom the life and make the long-life horse (ཚེ་རྟ, tshe rta). |
| Body | the *ha lo* flower not carried off by frost: no illness, mother and children help one another, existence grows: good. | carried off: bad; existence does not grow, illness strikes; rise and decline for brothers, sisters and mother; existence comes with something missing. | fasten the wheel that changes and raises the body, the ransom rite of expelling the *byur*. |
| Power | the swastika fence fine and ripe below: the lovely girl flourishes, ornaments, clothes, food and wealth grow: good. | torn: bad for the girl, the villagers and the neighbours. | recite the dhāraṇī that increases jewels, press down the *god sri* and summon fortune, many kinds of turning back. |
| Luck | the white silver mirror not cracked at its base: a good home and castle found; though no help, all love her. | cracked: gossip blows about like wool; bad for mother, grandmother and sisters; many households but few held. | fasten the luck-raising wheel on the body, offer a hundred lamps and the like. |
| Mewa | the golden store and turquoise leaves not lost from the hand, the humble store not empty: la and life firm, existence and line, the vase of life full of wishes. | lost: grief among retinue and servants: bad; the vase of la and life empty; a long illness feared. | receive the deepest la-ransom and long-life empowerment; recite Tārā in the Dharma or the Bon. If it meets the nine-eye it is very bad. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
