# The thirteen gates end with the ruin-horse; the sixty-year signs and the doors are their small reckonings

The thirteen gates of reckoning of the *General Tantra of Brahmā* (WB vol. 2, pp. 170–171): by the regent's reply 160 (ys.2:i018), the thirteen proper end with the ruin-horse riding the drum («འཕུང་རྟ་རྔ་ཞོན», so named twice in the reply, as in WB's heading), and the reckonings WB gives after them, among them the sky and earth doors, are the small reckonings (རྩིས་ཕྲན, rtsis phran) of the thirteen gates, from the same chapter, "the fourteenth, showing the thirteen gates of reckoning".

So the fixed sixty-year signs that follow the gates (pp. 171–172), which end in the sky and earth doors, belong to the gates' small reckonings, counted as the gates are, male down and female up from a fixed sign and fought with the moment; whose years are counted from them the reply does not say (question 344). The thirteenth's name with "horse" stands in WB's heading and twice in the regent's reply; its result line's "bird" stands alone (question 343).
