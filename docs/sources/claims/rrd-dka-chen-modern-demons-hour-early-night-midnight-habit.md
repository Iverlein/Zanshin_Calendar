# A modern teacher puts the demons' hours in the early night, midnight is the day's habit, dawn prophecy: the reverse of Marpa's song on evening and midnight (RD397)

A modern Tibetan answer on dreams (Dka' chen Ngag sgros, entry of 2014-3-25) reads the saying in the sense of the demons' hours, and puts the hours differently: in the night (མཚན་མོ་སྲོད, mtshan mo srod), when the 'dre srin walk, the dream is mostly their magic and is not to be trusted; the dream of midnight (nam gung) comes from the habit of the day just gone and is not taken as a measure; the dream of dawn can be taken as a prophecy. So here the untrusted dream is the early-night one and midnight is habit, the reverse of Marpa's song, which gives habit to the evening and Māra to midnight; the author cites Mi la's saying that a dream is false and Marpa's giving of Mi la's dawn dream of the four pillars as a prophecy. A reading of Māra's emanation as a work of demons (འདྲེ་སྲིན) in the dark part of the night is therefore found in a living teacher, with the hour moved earlier. The source is a modern author and has no rank in the ranking of the sources.
