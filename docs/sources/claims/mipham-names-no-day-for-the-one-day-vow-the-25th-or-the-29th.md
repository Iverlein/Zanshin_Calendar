# What Mipham does not say: no day of the month for the one-day vow, no twenty-fifth of the dākinīs, no twenty-ninth of the protectors

What the search of Mipham's works found and did not find. The 33 volumes of the Gzhung 'grel skor print of his collected works were searched in BDRC's etext (downloaded whole on 2026-11-16) for the eighth, the tenth, the fourteenth, the fifteenth, the dates in the twenties, the full and new moon, the good and great occasions, the offering of the times, the one-day vow, the fast, the restoring rite, the feast gathering, the six times and the eclipse; an etext search can miss a word the OCR broke, so "not found" is not "proven absent". The Chengdu Collected Works (MCW) could not be searched: its etext is search-only.

Found, and read on the scan: the three nya as the 8th, 14th and 15th (mgz.33:p180); offerings in particular on the 8th, 14th and 15th and in the miracle fortnight (mgz.23:p7-8); the householder's offerings to the yoginīs on the 8th and 14th (mgz.27:p19-20); the six complete dates (mgz.27:p45); the Four Great Kings on the 8th, 14th and 15th of the half-month (mgz.11:p146); the eclipse as the special time (mgz.33:p51); the great occasions' multiplying explained (mgz.21:p135); a mantra of Sarasvatī for Tuesdays and the new moon (mgz.33:p166); the tenth day of every month in his commentary on the Seven-Line Prayer (mph.127:i302, mph.127:i303-304, mph.127:i305, read before).

Not found in his own words: a day of the month for the one-day vow (his Treasury commentary gives the hour, the giver and the eight branches and no date, mgz.11:p179-180); the twenty-fifth as a day of the dākinīs or of a feast; the twenty-ninth as a day of the protectors or of a fulfilment offering; a day for the feast gathering; a list of the good occasions or a figure of multiplied merit; a gloss in his notes on the White Beryl (vol. 2, pp. 474–541) of the "ordinary good occasions", of which the notes say nothing. The other hits for these words in the 33 volumes are dates of colophons, chronology of the Buddha's life, Kālacakra astronomy, narratives retold from the sūtras (a nāga who kept the restoring rite on the eighth and the fourteenth) and single rites of the mantra collections bound to a date (the 8th, the 14th, the 23rd, the full-moon night); they were seen in the etext and not recorded as passages.
