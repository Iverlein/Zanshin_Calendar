# ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ in ch. 81: the clay statues of the temple move; lder so is a spelling of lder bzo

The three words «ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ» in the dream list of ch. 81 (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, BDRC etexts, no scan opened): the clay statues (ལྡེར་བཟོ, lder bzo) of the temple (ལྷ་ཁང, lha khang) move (འགུལ, 'gul).

**The dictionaries.** ལྡེར་བཟོ (lder bzo) is a headword in the Tshig mdzod chen mo (Steinert no. 25, a figure of the Buddha or a god made of clay), the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho (no. 62, the same), Rangjung Yeshe (no. 2), Jim Valby (no. 7), Ives Waldo (no. 8: clay statues), Dan Martin (no. 9: a name for images, made of clay and the like, with a page of the Dpe chos) and the Sgom sde tshig mdzod chen mo (no. 58: a clay image, and a picture drawn on a wall); the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs (no. 37) has ལྡེར་སྐུ (lder sku) for a likeness made of clay and ལྡེར་བཟོ for the craftsman in clay. ལྡེར (lder) alone is only "clay; on the side of a wall" (nos. 2, 7, 8). ལྷ་ཁང (lha khang) is the temple, the house of images of body, speech and mind (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Sgom sde). So the question of q821 about what ལྡེར is has its answer: the first half of a stock word, the clay figure.

**The phrase elsewhere (BDRC full-text search, etexts).** «ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་བཟོ» or «ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་སོ» (44 hits for the first two words) stands as an image of stillness: the body held "like the clay statue (ལྡེར་སོ) of a temple" in a meditation instruction of Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams; the thoughtless yoga (བསམ་མེད་རྣལ་འབྱོར, bsam med rnal 'byor) in Tāranātha's collected works and in a text on Niguma's six teachings, "like the clay statue (ལྡེར་བཟོ) in a temple, it keeps its posture and smiles and thinks nothing"; and, among the signs that arise from reciting a wrathful rite in Kongtrul's treasury, «ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་སོ་ཁྱི་བོང་བསད» (the temple's clay statue, a dog and a donkey killed). So in the Four Tantras' line the dream is of the temple's clay images moving, which fits the sign of a male spirit that Khro ru and the Drikung give it (`ktr:p329-332`, `bdk:p580-581`), and the sign of a god or image moved against its nature.

**The spelling.** BDRC's search for «ལྡེར་སོ» gives 242 hits against the headword's བཟོ; the Kongtrul and Karma Chags med and Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams texts above spell it སོ. So the Bhutan print's and the Drathang print's ལྡེར་སོ (the clay "so" for "bzo") is a known variant spelling of the same word, not a different word; the two readings (RD264) are one phrase in two spellings. Which an editor chose is not known.

What it does not settle: Khro ru's «a temple shaking» (ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ་བ) takes the three words as one; the dictionaries make it two things, the temple and its clay images, and say "move" for the verb (འགུལ, 'gul: to stir, to shake).
