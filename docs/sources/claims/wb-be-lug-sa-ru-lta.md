# «བེ་ལུག་ས་རུ་བལྟ»: a place to be looked at; the word itself is not glossed

Narrowed 2026-11-01, not settled. P. 334's body-friend line «གྲོགས་ཀྱིས་བེ་ལུག་ས་རུ་བལྟ» (wb.1:p334) ends in WB's own formula for "look to the place of such a one": «ས་རུ་བལྟ» stands in ch. 25 for «ལྷ་ཡི་ས་རུ་བལྟ་བར་བྱེད», «བློན་པོ་ཕོ་མོའི་ས་རུ་བལྟ», «ཁྱོ་ཡིན་བུད་མེད་ས་རུ་བལྟ», «སྐར་ཁྱིམ་བཟའ་མིའི་ས་རུ་བལྟ» and «ལས་སྲིན་ཟོད་གཏོང་ས་རུ་བལྟ» (1996 etext pp. 427–429 = printed pp. 417–419, the reckoning for the sick, etext only here), each time "look at the place (of the god, the minister, the husband, the wife, the household member)". So «བེ་ལུག» is a thing that has a place, and the body's friend pebble is said to show where it is. The Zhol print's etext reads «བུ་ལུག» (a lamb; the scan was not read), and «བེའུ་ལུག» (calves and lambs) is a common phrase for the young of the herd (BDRC's search, 236 hits elsewhere), which suits the neighbouring lines (the life force's friend: «རྨིག་ཟླུམ», the hooves, the livestock; the power's friend: the doors of what suits). As a reading only: the body's friend shows the young of the herd, and where to look for them. No dictionary of Steinert's 64 has «བེ་ལུག», no gloss in Mipham's notes (vol. 2, pp. 483–485) or in the other etexts read; BDRC's full-text search for «གྲོགས་ཀྱིས་བེ་ལུག» finds WB's 1996 print and the Gser thang reprint only.
