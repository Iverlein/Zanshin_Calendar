# The circles: the tenth, the golden rat, for servants

The tenth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the golden rat, is read for servants and workers. Its starting point: the bird of the mother element, or finely by the four nails (see the passage) of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points. Though it is held to be drawn upward from the bird of the mother element of the one reckoned, the fine starting point is by the four nails of the elements between: the tiger, horse and dog risen, count from the tiger, the unchanging nail of the east: count from the tiger of the friend element of the one reckoned; the rat, dragon and monkey, on the turquoise dragon, the unchanging nail of the south: count from the dragon of the mother element; the pig, sheep and hare, on the black bone («རུས་ནག»), the unchanging nail of the north: draw from the pig of the same power as the one reckoned; the bird, ox and snake, on the red bird, the unchanging nail of the west: count from the bird of the son element of the one reckoned. Where it falls fights: whether the five arrows and one bow are lost; whether the punishment of lord and ministers has come; whether the man's house is covered by mist; whether the yoke of the *byur* is carried; whether the mountain valley's grazing is blocked; whether the subjects' land affairs go wrong.

| Luck | the *byur*'s yoke not carried: the lord does not fall to demons, the servant is kindly spoken of, sons, wealth, enjoyments and glory. | the yoke carried on the back: a great risk of an unbearable burden, or punishment from above, or gossip without fault. | dig out gossip and enemies, repair the narrow paths. |
| Mewa | the land's affairs not gone wrong, friends fill the rows; servants grow within the paternal clan; one's own la and life firm: good. | the subjects' land affairs go wrong: the servant's la declines, great losses to wealth, grazing blocked, enjoyments poor. | honour the gods, take fortune, ransom the la, send tormas. No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
