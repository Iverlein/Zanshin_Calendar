# DTD: the rite of Avalokiteśvara, and the last of four watches

Dam tshig rdo rje's instruction (the Mongolian manuscript MW1NLM4232, pp. 155–158; the Lanzhou print, vol. 10, pp. 219–220; both machine readings) is, like KCM, a rite for asking in a dream: before an image of the Noble Lord of the World at a good time, offerings blessed, the three mantras twenty-one times, the left palm blessed and breathed on, the prayer to be shown tonight's matter clearly, then sleep on the right side with the head to the south. The time rule is the text's own: the night is divided into four parts (ཆ་བཞི, cha bzhi), and the examination must be of the last watch (ཐུན་རྗེས་མ, thun rjes ma); the signs are not to be forgotten before they are judged. Nothing is said of a bad dream or of any day of the month, and no image of a dream is judged: the text says only "the way of judging is then known".
