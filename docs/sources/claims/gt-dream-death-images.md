# The bad dreams of the sick that mark the lord of death: every image in the Drathang's verse and what it marks

The bad dreams of a sick person that the Explanatory Tantra (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud), ch. 7, names as the signs that the lord of death (འཆི་བདག, 'chi bdag) has the sick, in the Drathang print (gt:i42-43), image by image. The verse gives one verdict for all of them: a sign of falling into the lord of death's power (a rider's journey to the south is said to be death itself). It reads for the sick dreamer only (the claim gt-dream-death-rule gives who dies of them). The last column is what Byang pa's gloss (bjp:p177-181) adds from *Moonbeams* (ཟླ་ཟེར, zla zer) on the sixth chapter: the illness a dream points to. That is a commentary's reading, not the root verse's, and it exists for the images listed only. The tantra's line is gt:i42-43; the number is the line of its verse in the order of the print.

| Image | What it marks (the verse) | A commentary adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dreams of a cat (བྱི་ལ, byi la), a monkey, a tiger or a fox, or riding a corpse (རོ, ro) | the lord of death has seized the sick | none |

| A shoot (ལྕུག་མ, lcug ma) grows from the crown with a bird's nest on it | in the lord of death's power | a thorny shoot, a reed or a palm grown in the heart (not on the crown): a tumour (སྐྲན, skran) |
| A palm (ཏཱ་ལ, tā la) or a thorny tree grows in the heart | in the lord of death's power | a tumour |
| A lotus (པདྨ, padma) drawn out of the heart | in the lord of death's power | with a fire that does not burn, a burnt offering, butter smeared on and sitting naked: leprosy (མཛེ, mdze) |

| Devoured by fish | in the lord of death's power | none |
| Finding iron or gold; trading; losing a fight | in the lord of death's power | none |

| Sitting naked | in the lord of death's power | with the lotus and the fire: leprosy |
| The hair and beard shaved off | in the lord of death's power | none |
| Drinking beer with the dead (གཤིན, gshin) and being dragged along | in the lord of death's power | drinking much fat with the low-born: urine disease (གཅིན་ནད, gcin nad); led off by the dead as one dances: a spirit of the dead (རྗེད་བྱེད, rjed byed) |
| Adorned in red clothes and a red garland, dancing with the dead | in the lord of death's power | a red body, red clothes and garland and a woman drawn along laughing: death by blood and bile; dancing with fiends: madness |

The Drathang's verse is 31 verse lines long in this order; it has not "the sun and moon seized" or "a black woman with long breasts", which Byang pa takes from *Moonbeams*, not from the root verse. Where the Drathang differs from a commentary by a syllable, gt-dream-verse-variants.
