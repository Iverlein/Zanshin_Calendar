# «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད», the late afternoon: the time the ransoms are sent

WB's ransom lists (vol. 1, pp. 431–433 and 443–444) send the ransom at «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད» (e.g. «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད་རྩག་རྩེར་ལ། ཤར་མོ་ཞིག་གིས་སྣ་དྲངས་ནས། ལྷོ་ཡི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་གཏང་བར་བྱ»), and the mewa list of p. 431 sets the green at «མྱུར་སྨད», the whites at sunrise, black and blue at sunset.

BDRC's full-text search for «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད» (12 hits, 2026-11-01) finds the phrase as a time of day in other texts. The clearest is a Nyingma list of the twelve times of the day and night in a collection of the Dge mang monastery (etext I4PD4312, pp. 268–269), in the order: dawn (ཐོ་རངས་སྐྱ་རེངས), sunrise (ཉི་མ་ཤར་ཙ་ན), when the sun grows warm (ཉི་མ་དྲོས་པ་ན), the middle of the day (ཉི་མ་ཕྱེད་ཚལ), just past the middle (ཉི་མ་ཕྱེད་ཡོལ་ནས), then «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད», then evening, when the sun goes down (དགོངས་མོ་ཉི་མ་ནུབ་ཁ་ཟེར), then dusk, the small hours, midnight, past midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, ཕྱེད་ཡོལ), and before dawn. The same text, p. 275, lists "sunrise, sun warm, midday, evening, «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད»". So «མྱུར་སྨད» is the late afternoon, after the sun has turned past the middle and before it sets. A second witness, Takla Padma Blodan's *Gnyan bcos* as quoted in a medical collection (etext I1KG20686, p. 166), says to guard travellers «ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད་ཕན་ཆད», "from «myur smad» on", with the evening following; and two biographies (Brag dkar ba's and a life of Dharmeshvara) set it as the hour between the day and the evening watch («ཉི་མ་མྱུར་སྨད་མཚམས»).

WB's own order of the mewa times (whites at sunrise, yellow and reds at noon, green at «མྱུར་སྨད», black and blue at sunset) agrees: green falls between noon and sunset. The dictionaries (Steinert's 64, scanned 2026-11-01) have no entry for the compound; «མྱུར» is "swift" and «སྨད» "lower, latter part", which gives "the latter part, when the sun is hurrying down".

State: etext only, neighbours of WB (Nyingma and medical), a witness to the meaning of the word and not a rank above WB.
