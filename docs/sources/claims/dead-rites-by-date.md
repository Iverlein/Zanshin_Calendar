# Death on each lunar date: whom it falls on and what is done

WB vol. 2, pp. 116–118 (read line by line on the Gser thang reprint, 2026-10-23; the spells in full in the passage; the full table with the beings and the spells' names is in the passage). For a death on any month's date, the line names a being met or set out, the person after the dead on whom it falls rough or bad, beings to be held prisoner, a spell, and an act on the corpse. In short:

| Date | Falls on | Act on or beside the corpse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | small children | the pressing wheel set in the market |
| 2 | young wives | a wooden sword at the enemy; a black ransom beside the corpse |
| 3 | women in their prime | an acacia peg in the ransom; an iron peg in the eyes |
| 4 | young men | an iron man and horse pressed under; a name-card burned on the head |
| 5 | a monk | a saw at the neck |
| 6 | a man in his prime | a *'gong po* effigy under the corpse |
| 7 | a monk | the head turned downward |
| 8 | a wise man | a juniper beam in the right armpit |
| 9 | a nun | an iron *rbad shad* under the neck |
| 10 | a small child | one wrathful eye drawn on the chest |
| 11 | a sinful man | vajra and bell at the neck |
| 12 | a man in his prime | a dough corpse in the armpit |
| 13 | an old woman | incense burned in the armpit, *za ma* given |
| 14 | an old working woman | a swastika drawn on the corpse |
| 15 (full moon) | men and women in turn | a ring and a beam tied at the armpit |
| 16 | a woman | a broom tied to the corpse |
| 17 | a well-dressed bride | sun and moon drawn in butter |
| 18 | a lama or geshe | a golden ornament tied on |
| 19 | a young man | clay votive images struck on the corpse |
| 20 | a woman | fumigated with all fragrances |
| 21 | a young son (*bus pa shar ba*, as read on the scan, 2026-10-23) | fine flowers offered |
| 22 | a mother or old woman | (spell only) |
| 23 | a mother or a man in his prime | the four continents' general circuit |
| 24 | a corpse-companion of the paternal kin | thirteen *sri* objects tied on |
| 25 | a geshe or monk | covered with the five families' images |
| 26 | a well-dressed man | dice-eyes drawn on the body |
| 27 | a small boy of the paternal kin | white silk with vermilion lines |
| 28 | one of the dead's own year | (spell only) |
| 29 | the men and women of the household | the eight classes' wheel hung above; three black substances rolled |
| 30 | all eight kinds of paternal kin | (spell only) |

Mipham's notes: "the remedies of the thirty dates as the text" (p. 517). The 6th, 16th and 26th also carry the grave rule of *kung kung* (p. 112).
