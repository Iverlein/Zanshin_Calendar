# The 65-year table of the sage's and the pig's days and its rule

The 65-year table of the sage's and the pig's days (WB vol. 1, pp. 188–189) is printed in four blocks of 15, 19, 18 and 13 years; each block has seven rows: the sage's date, its desired part, its fifth part (1 in every cell), the year number (*bya ra ba*), the pig's date, its desired part, its fifth part (4 in every cell). The rule: to the rab byung years elapsed give 26, take 65 away; with the remainder take the year's column; the upper is the sage, the lower the pig; the first figure is the date, the second the desired part, the third the fifth part; give the desired part and the fifth part by their numbers to the pure proven mean sun of that date, and the measure appears; look to the earlier or the later date by which part has risen. Cell by cell the table follows a step of 11 in the date (12 when the part comes round to nought) and 1 in the part, but for four cells of the sage's date and part (columns 12, 15, 18 and 25), which are read as printed.
