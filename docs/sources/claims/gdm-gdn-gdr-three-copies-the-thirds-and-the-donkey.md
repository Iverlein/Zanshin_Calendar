# Three Mongolian copies of the dream text read side by side: the thirds of the night, the dawn line and the donkey

The three Mongolian National Library manuscripts read side by side (RD328): the Dga' ldan Byang rtse manuscript (GDM, W1NLM3822 img. 87-112), the copy in W1NLM9044 part 30 (GDN, img. 252-262) and the copy in W1NLM9419 part 7 (GDR, img. 151-157) are three copies of the one text of RST. GDN and GDR share the verses in the same order as GDM: the lama and the Mother's Chöd visualisation collection, the sorting of dreams, the thirds of the night, the good signs, the signs for the practitioner, the bad signs, whose display each is, the dreams of death (GDN img. 255, GDR img. 155), then the examination of death omens by body, speech and mind and the marks of men and women.

What each copy reads where RST and KBS part (machine; eye on the lines, enlarged 3x to 5x):

- the thirds of the night: GDR has «མཚན་མོ་(ཐུན)་གྱི་སུམ་ཆར་བགོས» (the eye finds «ཆར་བགོས», not «གསུམ་ཆ»), as GDM and KBS; GDN writes the number as a numeral (machine «༣») and shows neither spelling plainly. So of the witnesses that can be read, three (GDM, GDR, KBS) have «སུམ་ཆར», RST «གསུམ་ཆ», and the copy in numerals does not decide;
- the three parts: GDR has «སྲོད་ཀྱི་ཆ… གུང་གི་ཆ… ཐོ་རངས་ཆ» without the number; GDM has «གསུམ་ཆ»; GDN has «སྲོད་ཀྱི་སུཾ་ཆ» and numerals for the other two. The number is written in some copies and left out in others;
- the dawn line: GDR has «ཐོ་རངས་དུས་ཀྱི་ནམ་གྱི་ཆ་ལ་ནི» (eye: the syllable before «ཀྱི» is the short one the machine reads «དུས»), as GDM and KBS, but without «གསུམ»; RST's «ཐོ་རངས་ཁ་ཡིན་ནམ་གྱི་གསུམ་ཆ་ལ» stands alone against the three. GDN is not settled (machine «ཐོ་རངས་༤་ཡི» with a numeral where the others have «དུས་ཀྱི»);
- the unsaddled donkey: GDR «རྟ་བོང་སྒ་མེད་ཞོན» (machine; by eye a sa-head), GDM «རྒ» (machine; by eye a ra-head, leaning, not settled), GDN «རྒ» (machine, twice; by eye undecided). The copies are not of one mind and the hands are cursive. «སྒ་མེད» (no saddle) is what RST, KBS and WB have; GDM and GDN read, if the machine is right, «རྒ་མེད», which the scribe may have written for it.

So the Mongolian witnesses do not settle which of «སུམ་ཆར» and «གསུམ་ཆ» is earlier: GDM and GDR follow the wording KBS quotes, RST's etext stands apart in both lines, and GDN, the copy that carries Klong rdol bla ma's name in its colophon (gdn-colophons-klong-rdol-and-a-second-hand), writes numerals where the question lies.
