# WB ch. 15 ends with the rule for stacking and postponing a month by the day the moon rises

The last verses (machine reading of p. 183): though the systems have many ways of drawing the postponing and bringing forward of the month, in this system, by the measure of the middle one told earlier, on whichever skipped day (གནམ) the moon rises, that month is piled up (བརྩེགས); when it rises on the first lunar day, that month is postponed to the next. 'This is pure and established; the maker comes out when the months increase.' The first lines give omens by weekday (Friday, Saturday, the sun) and by many planets of the lunar day or of the house meeting 'rising' or 'seated' (good harvest and peace against disease and strife), and a rule for the black month by the animal that rises; these are given as the verse runs and are not interpreted here.
