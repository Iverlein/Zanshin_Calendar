# Mipham: the great occasions multiply through blessing and devotion; the eclipse is the special time for mantra practice

Two statements of Mipham (rank 2) bear on why and when practice counts for more.

Why: in his summary of the tenets chapter of the Wish-fulfilling Treasury (mgz.21:p135, read on the scan) he answers the view that time does everything, and adds that on the great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) the difference of multiplying arises from two things, the buddhas' blessing and the followers' devotion, and so is unlike the doing of time. The multiplied effect of a good day is, for him, the work of blessing and devotion, not a power of the date.

When: in his Activities of Mañjuśrī (mgz.33:p51, read on the scan) the eclipse is "the special time at which accomplishment is achieved at once": activities of every kind are to be accomplished then, whether or not the deity's practice has been completed before; the eclipse is reckoned in advance, the image and offerings are set out, and for as long as the planet holds the sun or moon the practitioner stays one-pointedly in the yoga of the deity and the mantra.

- Days: great occasions, unnamed; eclipses of sun and moon.
- Practice: at an eclipse, the yoga and mantra of one's yidam through its whole length.
- For whom: a mantra practitioner; not addressed to lay people apart.
- Figures: none. He gives no multiplier, and neither passage lists which days the great occasions are, so the White Beryl's "ordinary good occasions" (wb.1:p62) are not identified by them.
