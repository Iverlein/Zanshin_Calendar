# A meditation hand-book cuts day and night each into four by the season's own length

A meditation instruction in the *Rin chen gter mdzod chen mo* (the same words in Ngag dbang dpal bzang's collected works) fixes the watches by the season, not by a clock: a day-and-night has eight watch-times, "following the season's long or short day-and-night" they are divided into four of the day and four of the night; the day's are measured by the shadow of a post, the night's and the inner ones by the count of breaths (7,200 to a watch); each watch is cut in two as sixteen small watches (ཐུན་ཆུང, thun chung).

So where a watch-bound is stated at all, a night's four watches are quarters of that night's own length (long in winter, short in summer), not four spans of fixed hours. It is the one place read that says so; it is a meditation hand-book and not a dream text, and it does not mention the Svapnohana.
