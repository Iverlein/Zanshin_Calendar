# A Mongolian copy of the dream text carries Klong rdol bla ma's name and a second writer's colophon

The copy of the dream text in the Mongolian National Library volume W1NLM9044 (part 30, MW1NLM9044_O1NLM9044_030, images 252-263; the part BDRC's catalogue lists as the dream text's copy, part 9, MW1NLM9044_O1NLM9044_009, holds a rite for the king spirit and is not it) closes with two sentences of writership, on image 262 (machine reading, the shapes read by eye at 3x and 5x):

- after the verses on the signs of women, line 1: «ཅེས་པ་འདི་ཕར་ཀློང་རྡོལ་བླ་མ་ངག་དབང་ཀློང་དེས་བྲིས་པའོ», "this was written by Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang ..." The end of the name is not settled: the machine reads «ཀློང་དེས» where RST has «བློ་བཟང་གིས». The first words and «བྲིས་པའོ» are plain on the scan. This is the colophon RST gives, in a copy that is a manuscript: so the text reaches us with Klong rdol bla ma's name in a hand copy as well as in the etext;
- line 7, after the verses on the marks of men (a block on people by the year of birth follows it): «ཅེས་པ་འདི་སྔ་སུམ་པ་མཁན་པོས་བྲིས་པའོ», "this was written by the abbot ..." (the machine's «སྔ་སུཾ་པ» is not settled; the last three syllables, «མཁན་པོས་བྲིས་པའོ», are plain). Whether «སུམ་པ་མཁན་པོ» is Sum pa mkhan po Ye shes dpal 'byor (1704-1788) is not shown by the scan and is not asserted. It marks the part before it as another writer's piece in the collection.

No date stands on the page; the page after (img. 263) is empty. The copy in W1NLM9419 (GDR) ends at img. 157 with «སྨན་དང་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་དོན་བཞིན་འཆི་ལྟས་རྫོགས་སོ» and no name or date. The Dga' ldan Byang rtse manuscript (GDM) has no ending: its last leaf (BDRC's note on img. 113: "Final pages are missing 14 A") is lost. So the claim of rst-is-klong-rdols-dream-text, that Klong rdol bla ma is the compiler or last writer, now has a manuscript copy that bears his name; that he wrote the verses first, or that GDM, which follows KBS's wording against RST, is older than the copy, is not shown.
