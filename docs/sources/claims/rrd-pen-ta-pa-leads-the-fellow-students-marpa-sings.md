# Marpa sings the song of the dream of Maitrīpa; Pen ta pa leads the fellow students and is a lama of Nepal in the Life (RD402)

In the witnesses read the song of the three watches of the night (evening habit, midnight the work of Māra, dawn the gods' prophecy) is Marpa's own, and Pen ta pa (also written Pe dha ba, Pen ḍa pa, Paṇḍa pa) is the one it is addressed to and who leads the fellow students before whom it is sung. In the Life of Gtsang smyon (rrd.23:p296-298) the verses open by addressing the lord who keeps the ascetic's conduct, Pen ta pa, and name the fellow students and the yoginis in the rows; the closing lines say that this was offered to the fellow students with Pen ta pa leading, and that in the Rin chen tshul temple in Nepal Lord Maitrīpa had come in a dream and shown the meaning of the symbols of the great seal. The grammar of «bla ma Pe dha bas dbu mdzad kyi mched grogs rnams la rtogs pa phul ba» (rrd.23:p102-104) puts Pen ta pa as the leader of the group, Marpa as the one who offers. Of Pen ta pa the Life says: he is a lama, called so, with whom Marpa stays in the Rin chen tshul temple of Nepal and hears the Ral gcig ma abhisamaya, the Bdud rtsi 'od ldan sadhana and some scattered completion-stage teachings (rrd.23:p292), and in the second month of the search for Nāropa he is taken as a companion on the way to India (rrd.23:p262); he is named beside the Nepalese Spyi ther ba (rrd.23, etext p202, where he is the one who says the Tibetans are like oxen; p303, where Marpa offers gold to both). His dates, his birthplace and his lineage are not given on the pages read.
