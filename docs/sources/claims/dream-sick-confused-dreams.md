The word «ཟ་ཟི» is left as printed; its sense is taken from the context (claims st-dream-unripe-fever, st-dream-unripe-pestilence). Where ST and WB ch. 25 both read the dream, ST's row is the one shown.

What is done for the sick dreamer (RD229, 2026-11-09; the claims st-dream-unripe-fever, st-dream-unripe-pestilence, st-dream-rims-cham): none of the three chapters treats the dream itself. The unripe fever (ch. 15, st:p185-186) and the unripe pestilence (ch. 23, st:p222-223) are ripened with the warm, mild decoction of the four manu (མ་ནུ་བཞི) or of the three fruits with sle tres, with light food, boiled water and a conduct between cool and warm; the cham of the pestilence (ch. 27, st:p241-242b) with fasting, boiled water and the decoction of the three fruits, sle tres, tig ta, ma nu and hong len as powder. The dream marks the stage (the unripe pestilence only, the first stage), so the picker shows it as a sign of the illness and no remedy of its own; the remedy row of the illness is the chapter's, not this image's.
