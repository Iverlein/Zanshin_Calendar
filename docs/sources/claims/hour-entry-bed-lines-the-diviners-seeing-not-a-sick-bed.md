# The hour entries' bed and pillow lines: four copies, the verb of seeing, no word of illness; «ཚིག་པ་ཟ» doubtful (RD396, q1082)

The bed and pillow lines of the hour's iron entry (kd.3:p588-589) are four etext copies of one text (the White Beryl, its Gser thang print, the Kun 'dus chen mo, the commentary Dpyod ldan snying nor), and none of them says whose night it is (RD396, BDRC's exact phrases, 2026-11-11). The lines are in the form «'dug» ("is seen to be, sits"), the verb that marks what a speaker sees: "at times (he) is seen eating burnt food [or: being burnt]; he sits on an ash heap...". Taken as the diviner's patter on the pebble that has fallen, they describe the asker (or the one for whom he asks) as the diviner sees him, sitting among ashes on a dark-blue mat with a wooden pillow, and continue with the dream, the dead forefathers and the knife, the god a white man on a white horse: a picture in the manner of the other hour entries, which give the asker's look, house, kin, dreams and the god. As the sleep of a sick one the lines would need a word for illness or lying; there is none. The sense of «ཚིག་པ་ཟ» (tshig pa za) is doubtful: «ཚིག་པ» is "burnt" or "anger" in the lexica, and the two other places of the phrase in BDRC (Tāranātha's life; Pu to ba's long instruction, «snying nas rang tshig pa za bar 'dug») use it of a person scorched in heart, so "eating burnt food" is one reading and "being scorched, pained" another. The question whether the lines speak of the asker's own last night stays open: nothing in the four texts or the lexica says; the form of the verb favours the diviner's seeing of the asker, not a note of a sick one's sleep.
