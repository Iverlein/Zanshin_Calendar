# Mipham's dream mantras and the Skul byed ma rite ask for a dream; they give no meanings, name sources and a few times

Mipham's list of mantras for "dreams and experience practice" (mph.128:i189-192) and his rite of the goddess Skul byed ma (mph.25:i374-376) are the practice of asking for a dream, not a list of what a dream shows. Each entry says what to recite, over what (a water jar, rue-root «ru rta», chang, lamp butter), how many times (108 is the common number, ten thousand to a hundred thousand for the preparatory count), that the face or feet are washed or rubbed, and that one lies down without speaking, the purpose in mind: "in the dream it shows what is good and what is not good" (དགེ་མི་དགེ).

The only times named are for the practice, not for reading a dream: "at bedtime" (ཉལ་ཁར, ཉལ་དུས), the first to the eighth of the month for the dream to stay clear for thirteen years, and, in the Tantra of the Great Black One, the eighth or the fourteenth. The rite of Skul byed ma has no hour; its colophon dates the writing (wood-dog 1874, tenth month, the evening of the waxing tenth), not the dream.

Sources named inside the text: the Realization of the Good Jewel (Nor bu bzang po'i rtogs pa) for the goddess Śramaṇā's dream divination; the Tantra of Mañjuśrī the hero, the one accomplishment ('Jam dpal dpa' bo gcig grub kyi rgyud) for the rest and for the rite's own colophon; the Nyang treasure Khros nag; the Tantra of the Great Black One (Nag po chen po'i rgyud); and "treasures and others" unnamed. The same Skul byed ma mantra, with the same instructions, stands in both texts: the be bum quotes it from the Mañjuśrī tantra.

No dream image is given a meaning here. Read by machine (etext of the 2008 reset and Yigdzin-1 for the be bum; Yigdzin-1 alone for the Chengdu volume), not on the eye.
