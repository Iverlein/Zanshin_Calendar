# A white enemy pebble is the bad one in WB and in Mipham; UT's «white enemy good» differs

Answered 2026-11-01. Mipham's gloss on WB's five doors of bad luck (wb.2:p483, on the scan) defines «དགྲ་དར» (dgra dar), "the enemy rising", as each one's enemy pebble white («དགྲ་དར་ཞེས་སོ་སོའི་དགྲ་རྡེལ་དཀར་ནའོ»), and counts it among the findings that make an aspect bad when they meet (it alone is "no great fault"). WB's own verdicts on the hundred agree (wb.1:p329-331): the life force's enemy pebble white, «གཅོང་དང་ནད་གཞི་རྙིང་པ་འཛེམ། ཟས་ངན་དམུ་ཆུ་འོང་བའི་ངོ», beware leprosy and old illness, bad food, dropsy comes; the body's enemy pebble white, «ནད་མོད་དབང་པོ་སྐྱོན་ཅན་འོང», illness and faulty senses; and the black enemy is good. The *Utpala Garland* (ut:p153-155, etext) reads the enemy pebbles white as good, «དགྲ་རྡེལ་རྣམས་ནི་དཀར་བ་ན། རྒས་ཁ་བུ་དང་ནོར་གྱིས་ཕྱུག། དགྲ་དང་རྐུན་མས་འཚེ་བ་མེད», rich in sons and wealth in old age, no harm from enemies and thieves, and black as the loss of what one has. So the karma reckoning over the match (whose findings are each spouse's birth hundred) takes the white enemy pebble as the one that makes the aspect bad, by WB and by Mipham, and UT, a manual of a later hand and not the rank of either, stands aside as a conflict. This is the sense of the older claim [karma-reckoning-aspect-bad](karma-reckoning-aspect-bad.md), whose three bearings it settles for the white enemy pebble.
