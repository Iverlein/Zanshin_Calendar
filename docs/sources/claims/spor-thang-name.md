# The Spor thang is the Chinese reckoning's tradition that WB, Mipham and KD's charts all name

The Spor thang (གཙུག་ལག་སྤོར་ཐང, gtsug lag spor thang) of the sheets is the name of the Chinese-derived (elemental) reckoning's tradition, not one text that can be fetched: KD's two charts that cite it do so as "from the Spor thang" (p. 718: the hundred mixed pebbles; the second chart of the page too) and, on p. 726, "from the Spor thang *reckoning text*" (རྩིས་གཞུང་), the household chart of seven members; neither names an author, a volume or a page, and the lent scans of the sheets' title leaves and the colophon of KD vol. 5 add no work behind the name.

The name is the one Mipham gives WB's notes: they are "notes on the hard points of the Spor thang" (WB vol. 2, p. 539; verdicts.md, *The notes*), and the opening of the notes (p. 474) says "of the boundless systems of the Gtsug lag spor thang, I write a few clarifications of the hard places of the widely known old Chinese reckoning in the Phugpa way, the White Beryl". The title of the *Beautiful Rosary of Jewels* (*Nor bu'i phreng mdzes*, verdicts.md) likewise says it takes the 136 pebbles "from the Spor thang" as WB's intent. So the *Spor thang* is the body of the Chinese pebble reckonings that WB's notes and KD's charts both name as their ground: the sheets do not rest on WB for the name, WB's notes present themselves as clarifications of it. The sheets draw on it for charts WB does not draw (the board of a hundred, the household's black-pebble round of five and of seven); that the charts are the reckoners' own drawing of its rules is KD's, not a printed text's: no other print of these two charts was found (BDRC full-text searches for the chart titles, RD28 and RD54).
