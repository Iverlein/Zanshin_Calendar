# Klong rdol, five kinds of dawn dream and the waking feeling that decides

From the dream examined at dawn the article (pp. 70–79) sorts Klong rdol bla ma's verses into five kinds of dream, which are five readings of one verse text, not five times: (1) signs of good fortune; (2) bad omens, for a practitioner mostly obstacles on the way to the higher realms and liberation; (3) the dream that shows a spirit's magic display, by the kind of spirit; (4) a religious practitioner's dream (signs of accomplishment and realisation); (5) death omens. The article adds, apart from the verses, that dreams are judged by what the dreamer's own life and culture hold, a point of its author and not of the text.

A dream's feeling on waking also decides in the verses of the third kind: a dream without imprint that is marvellous and leaves the dreamer glad on waking brings good fruit; one that leaves him uneasy brings bad; a frightening dream that makes the hair stand is the magic of a demon; a dream that returns again and again is either the power of a mantra or the display of the eight classes (སྡེ་བརྒྱད, sde brgyad), and is to be examined to tell which.
