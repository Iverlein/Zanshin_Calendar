# Why the 8th, 14th and 15th: the Four Great Kings' servants, sons and the kings themselves look over the world

The reason the Tengyur gives for the 8th, the 14th and the 15th: the *Lokaprajñapti* (འཇིག་རྟེན་གཞག་པ, 'jig rten gzhag pa), bam po 3 (ljz:i41-47, etext only), says that on the eighth day of the half-month the hired servants of the Four Great Kings (རྒྱལ་པོ་ཆེན་པོ་བཞི, rgyal po chen po bzhi), on the fourteenth their sons and on the fifteenth the Four Great Kings themselves go through the whole world and look whether many people honour mother and father, ascetics and brahmins and the elders of the family, make merit, give, keep the fast day (གསོ་སྦྱོང, gso sbyong) and hold to morality. They report to the Thirty-three gods in the hall Sudharmā: if such people are many the gods rejoice, the host of the gods grows and that of the asuras dwindles; if few, the reverse.

- Days: the 8th, 14th and 15th "of the half-month", that is of each half; and, in Śakra's verse, the half-month of miracles.
- Practice named: the fast of eight branches (གསོ་སྦྱོང་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པ, gso sbyong yan lag brgyad pa), with giving, merit and respect for parents and elders.
- For whom: human beings in general.
- Benefit: Śakra's verse promises that the keeper becomes like him; the Buddha calls the verse ill spoken in Śakra's mouth and well spoken in an arhat's, so the promised likeness is to the arhat, not to the god.
- Tradition: common (the Abhidharma collection of the Tengyur). No Nyingma author was found quoting it in this reading.

The same account of the 15th stands in the sūtra extracts of the *Vyākhyāyukti* and its commentary (seen in search snippets only, not read).
