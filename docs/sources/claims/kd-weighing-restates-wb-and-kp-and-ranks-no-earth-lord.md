# KD vol. 1 pp. 498-499 restates the order of strength; no earth lord in it

Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan (the abbot of Nalendra, in the closing part of his account of the almanac, KD vol. 1 pp. 498–499) states the weighing of a day's results as WB's verse and KP's prose have it (weighing.md): the date has one quality, the planet four times, the mansion eight times, the combination period ten million times; where the planet and mansion differ the planet leads, mansion against date the mansion, combination against effect the fiercer effect, the day against the time the time; where none are equal the concord (འཕྲོད) leads, and where there is no special case (དམིགས་བསལ) the general grouping (ཕྱོགས་བསྡེབས); then the difference of strength is counted. The order he gives is Rāhu, the weekday, the mansion, the date, the effect, the combination, the day, each stronger than the next, with the instant combination period above all because the power of all is complete in it.

What his text adds to KP and WB: (1) two verses on the person's own weekday and mansions (a Jupiter or life-planet day, the bla, srog and dbang mansions good and the keg, bdud and gshed mansions bad), set as examples of the particular case overriding the general, with the line «a treatise's general rule being fixed, the particular becomes strong»; (2) the counsel that when the chief factors are good, some of the bad kinds are only guarded against and the obscuration and decline remedies are applied as the occasion allows, because if a harm is true its turning back is true; (3) the date's earth lords are not among the factors he ranks. The page of the almanac he explains lists the earth lords among the day's entries (kd.1:p495), and his reason for reckoning them with the date's course (kd-date-earth-lords-keep-the-dates-span) is time, not weight. Read from the etext (weighing.md records a reading of the same pages on the scan, 2026-10-09); the etext's wording agrees with that reading.
