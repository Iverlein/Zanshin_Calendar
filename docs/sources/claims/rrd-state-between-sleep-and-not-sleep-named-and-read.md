# The state between sleep and not-sleep: named, and read as experience or hindrance, not as a sign (RD388)

In the texts read, the state of a practitioner on the edge of sleep, aware of himself and with visions, is named by the phrase "sleep and not-sleep" (གཉིད་དང་མ་གཉིད, gnyid dang ma gnyid), with "border" (སོ་མཚམས, so mtshams) where it is placed; its visions are called experience (ཉམས, nyams), and in two texts the clear light of experience (ཉམས་ཀྱི་འོད་གསལ, nyams kyi 'od gsal) against the clear light of realisation. Pure vision (དག་སྣང, dag snang) names a deity's or dakini's blessing seen. None of the five passages reads such a vision as an omen: Karma chags med takes the light and the clairvoyant-seeming sight as a hindrance if it breeds pride; the Nyang Dzogchen instruction takes the hazy habit-appearances there as a gauge of the practice; the Karmapa's verses list it among his experiences. The dream is named apart from it in each. No text found fixes the state to an hour of the night. Etext only; the heading and author of three of the volumes were not checked.
