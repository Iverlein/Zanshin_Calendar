# Dreams that foretell death: the Indian works beside Klong rdol's verses

The verses on death-deceiving by an Indian paṇḍita, with their explanation in Mkhyen brtse's collected works (CBL), and the medical work SDS list dreams that foretell death which return in Klong rdol bla ma's verses quoted by KBS (kbs-death-omen-dreams): riding a donkey or a monkey south or away and not coming back or waking there; a woman in black; a red garland of flowers and red clothes (CBL: karavira flowers, found in a dream or worn, death within eight months; SDS: a garland of red flowers and red clothes); climbing a heap of ash, a stupa or an ant-hill; being carried off riding a donkey, pig, she-camel or buffalo; darkness, a prison or a pit; eating and drinking with the dead or with the corpse-watcher (SDS: a gshin dog, honey and oil). The times differ: CBL says six months for most, three months for a man in black with an iron staff sitting before the sleeper, eight months for the red scent, garland or clothes, a year for being eaten or carried off; KBS gives no times in the verses. Klong rdol bla ma lived 1719-1794, while the two other works are, by their own lines, translations of Indian works; so the Tibetan verses may be a later form of a list already fixed, but this is a comparison of contents, not a proof of descent. All read in the etext only.
