# The serpent's place for the first stroke is its walk by the house-day, not the day's mansion

In the rite of the ground the place of the first stroke of the serpent Lto-'phye (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ, lto 'phye) is reckoned by the house-day within the season, not by the day's mansion (ཁྱིམ་ཞག, khyim zhag: the days of the house-year of 360, three house-months of 30 days to a season, as on WB p. 281). Padma dkar po's collected works (vol. 13 of BDRC's etext, pp. 211–213) say that the serpent, lying in the middle of a square of ninety cells a side, moves one cell a house-day: on the first day of the first autumn month his head is at the first cell of the east edge (at its north-east end, «དབང་ལྡན», dbang ldan) and his tail at the last cell of the north-west edge (the wind's, «རླུང་», rlung); in the three autumn months head east, face south; winter head south, face west; spring head west, face north; summer head north, face east. The first stroke goes in front of the hood and neck (merit); the cells where the body lies, and the back, are harmful (the verse of the harms is on p. 282). Longchenpa's *Dri med 'od zer* (lcm:p181-182, quoting the *Bam brgyad kyi 'grel pa*) and Sādhuputra (sdp:p238-241) give only the season's four headings; the *Bod spyi bshad* (bsb:p435-436) says only that the serpent's body "moves with the course of the planets and mansions" and that the work is begun on an accomplishing mansion. No text found gives the serpent's place by mansion. The *Bod spyi bshad*'s words are best read as a loose account of the same walk.
The mansion fixes only the day of beginning (the accomplishing mansions); the place of the stroke comes from the serpent's walk by the house-day. Both are told apart, as the claim earth-lords-place-beside-day says. Padma dkar po is not in the registry yet: its passage is quoted only in the section of RD114 of wb.2:p282, from BDRC's etext, which is not a scan.
