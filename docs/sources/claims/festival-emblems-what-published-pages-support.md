# What the published pages support of the eleven festivals' painted emblems

Read on 2026-11-24 in the browser (Himalayan Art Resources, Wikipedia, Lotsawa House's Tibetan text; [festivals.md](../festivals.md), RD408). No page read says how a festival is painted, so no emblem is confirmed as the festival's own picture; only whether the thing belongs to the event or is a Tibetan emblem.

- Supported as the event's content or a Tibetan emblem: the eight auspicious symbols (བཀྲ་ཤིས་རྟགས་བརྒྱད, bkra shis rtags brgyad) with the treasure vase and parasol among them; the twin miracle's fire above and water below, with six colours (Chötrul Düchen); the wheel of Dharma (ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ, chos kyi 'khor lo) with deer, on the Jokhang's roof; a white elephant with a white lotus entering Māyādevī's right side; the triple ladder with rainbows of the descent (Lhabab Düchen); Tsongkhapa's sword and book on flower blossoms (HAR); the torma (གཏོར་མ, gtor ma), red and triangular for protectors, used against obstacles at the New Year.
- Not supported by any page read: the Kālacakra mandala's rings of the elements; the flowering branches, the bud and the seven lotuses of the birth; the bodhi leaf and the vajra seat for Saga Dawa as a frame; the juniper smoke as painted for Zamling Chisang; the six tusks of the elephant; the gold, silver and crystal of the stair; a flame on Tsongkhapa's sword; the lamps for Gaden Ngamchö; a wreath of fire for Gutor.
- Their frames, by the plan's own rule, are a plain aureole in the day's colours.
