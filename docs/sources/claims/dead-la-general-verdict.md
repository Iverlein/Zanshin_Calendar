# The la pebbles' general verdict: the board's shape

WB's first of three verdicts on the twelve la pebbles (vol. 2, p. 52) reads the board's pattern by images; Mipham's notes say no commentary identifies each image, and it is judged from the general shape that the members' own years white and their sevenths black make over the household (p. 502).

| Shape | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| the old like an empty nit blown away (མི་རྒན་སེ་སྟོང་བུད་འདྲ) | the line's head broken at the neck, a stream cut at its source; decline great from the eldest down, the good elders vanish like mist |
| like stars at dawn | decline small, the seat held, few hold wisdom |
| like black wild dogs chasing | of the living some rise [?], some went abroad and did not return, some entered religion; on the dead's grave: forefathers' skulls incomplete, carried off by bird, beast or dog, or misused; an empty cut-off with no corpse held |
| like a black snake chopped up | harm by nāgas and earth lords; many seized by leprosy and wasting illness (གཅོང, gcong) |
| like a male yak-cow hybrid (མཛོ, mdzo) led by the nose | brides mixed in too long broke the kin from within |
| like a lone man on the road | a man killed by the blade not subdued, a ghost sees the graves; deaths by the blade, faulty brides, deaths by stabbing |
| upper pebbles empty | the male line declines the more the emptier |
| lower pebbles empty | the female line cut off [?] |
| pebbles cut off in the middle | the kin splits in two or three at its grave, goes to two or three districts, defilement and strife within; three or four cuts the same |

The notes define "empty": not one pebble on the four sides of the great cut-off. "Upper", "lower" and "middle" are not defined in the passages read; no remedy is given for the general verdict.
