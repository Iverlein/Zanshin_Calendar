# The scholars and works the chapter draws its tables from

The chapter names whom it draws on, each with a table of his own: the sage Nor bzang rgya mtsho, the general-wealth table of the day and lunar day (p. 73); the sage Don grub dbang rgyal, from the *Skol po dkon rin zab pa'i gnad* (as read: «སྐོལ་པོ་དཀོན་རིན་ཟབ་པའི་གནད»), the day-night sun-and-moon table by the water-taste (*chu ro*) and the rock-taste (*ri ro*) (p. 74); the great pandit Dkon mchog chos grags, the *byed grub* dhruva tables, the planets' and the sun's (pp. 74–75); the Nepalese scholars' Kara-myang reckoning, translated in two sections by Dharmapāla's Grags seng, which Shes rab 'phul extended (p. 75); and the Padma dkar po's *Zhal lung* (named as the pure intended meaning that earlier scholars' impure tables and broken-foot tables are not, p. 75). The Phug school (*phug lugs*) is named as the school whose famed and needed table kinds the chapter sets out (p. 74).
