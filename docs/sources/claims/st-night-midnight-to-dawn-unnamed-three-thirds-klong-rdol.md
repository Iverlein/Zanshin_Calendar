# No commentary names the stretch from midnight to dawn; the three-humours verse, Klong rdol's thirds and Bya mdo's two authorities

No commentary on the Four Tantras that was read (Byang pa, Rin spungs pa, the Drikung dka' mchan, Skyem pa Tshe dbang, Zur mkhar ba, the Blue Beryl, the Moonbeams; etexts, 2026-11-10) names the stretch from midnight to dawn or gives it a rule of its own. The times they name are dusk (སྲོད), midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད), the first part (མཚན་སྟོད) and dawn (ཐོ་རངས); the stretch between midnight and dawn is not a unit in any of them. The searches that bear it (BDRC, 2026-11-10): the terms མཚན་བར, མཚན་ཕྱི, མཚན་མཇུག, མཚན་ཐ་མ, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཕྱི, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ནས, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཚུན, ནམ་གུང, མཚན་ཕྱེད, ནམ་ཕྱེད་འདས in the seven etexts near the word རྨི་ལམ: no hit.

What is found beside them (st:p31-32c).
- A humours' verse for the dream's content: dusk phlegm, midnight bile, dawn wind, quoted by Zur mkhar ba (p. 354) and the Blue Beryl (p. 211) as "said much in the Tantra" and found in Rang byung rdo rje's and Kong sprul's writings as "from the Tantras". It names midnight and dawn as two times, not a stretch. The Root Tantra's own rhythm (Blue Beryl p. 75) puts bile at midnight and wind at the evening and dawn.
- Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang's verse (q848): the night in three thirds, dusk (the earlier habit-traces), midnight (demons' play, nothing fixed), dawn (the one that clarifies what arises later, so judging it is precious). Its «ཐོ་རངས་གསུམ་ཆ་ཕྱིས་འབྱུང་གསལ་བྱེད་ཡིན» (ACIP etext p. 3, colophon: Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang) means "the dawn third is the clarifier of what arises later"; ཕྱིས་འབྱུང་ is what comes afterwards, the verse's next lines say that the dawn dream's good or bad fruit arises, which is why judging it is precious; གསུམ་ཆ་ is a third of the night by the watches (ཐུན). The verse opens by saying it sets out what Ma gcig's Zab mo gcod kyi dmigs 'bum teaches, so its source is a Chöd text, not a medical one. It is the same rule as LGD's and RST's three thirds in the pool, and the dawn third is the only one of the three for the fruit.
- Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal (q901; search snippet only, the etext is closed): he quotes the Klong rdol line "as it is said" (ji skad du) and then, with «rgyud rang las», "the Tantra itself", the Four Tantras' first line. He thus puts the two side by side as two authorities for the dawn dream, the second named the Tantra's and the first not; he does not read them as one. Whether he draws a joint rule of them (dusk and midnight without fruit, dawn with) the snippet does not show.
- «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ནས་ཐོ་རངས་ཀྱི་བར» as a phrase for the span: found in practice texts (snippets: Tāranātha's commentaries on the Guhyasamāja's five stages and the Kālacakra's purification, Marpa's Hevajra texts, a graded-path manual) as the time of seizing the clear light of sleep; not in the commentaries on the Four Tantras.

Later books keep Zur mkhar ba's «nam phyed yan chad» sentence (a Gso bya lus kyi rnam bshad, a Gso ba rig pa volume, Padma rdo rje's work on sleep and dreams, a periodical article: BDRC's search for «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད་དུ་རྨི་ལམ» finds seven etexts, among them the Blue Beryl in two etexts, Zur mkhar ba, and these), and none of the hits gives a name to the later half of the night.
