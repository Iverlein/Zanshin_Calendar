# WB ch. 9 holds that the moon's whole seizure lasts fifteen chu tshod, from experience and the great commentary

The colophon (machine reading of pp. 68–69) says that observers who set a stake and watched the crescent moon through a gap in the clouds saw half seized or about a third seized over some six chu tshod, and that the Abridged Tantra's great commentary (Khams chapter, verse 6) gives the seizing as the moon's sixty-chu-tshod disk, a whole seizure at the end of sixty, a half where half the enjoyment is pure, and the keeping company with the planet up to a quarter of the chu tshod entered. From this, since an asterism has sixty chu tshod, a quarter of fifteen is what the commentary directly shows, and the author says experience agrees that fifteen chu tshod come on the moon's whole seizure. For the sun he holds the sun is smaller than the moon by twelve parts and that the abridged reckoning's faults accumulate, and the test of seizing or not seizing is made by the five piled dots (ཐིག་ལེ་ལྔ་བརྩེགས); rules for the moon on a void day and for the 'red mouth' marking are given in a separate text.
