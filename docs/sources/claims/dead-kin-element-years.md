# The dead's paternal kin by element and the members' years

WB reads the living household when the dead is taken out by the paternal kin's (ཕ་ཚན, pha tshan) element and how many members' birth years fall on each animal (vol. 2, pp. 51–52). First, in general: many four executioners (གཤེད་བཞི, gshed bzhi) and sevenths (བདུན་ཟུར, bdun zur) among the members' years bring strife in the end. Then, entry by entry (input: the clan element; the count of living members' year animals):

| Kin element | Many years | One or two years | Reading | Remedy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| water | pig, rat | horse, snake | demons and ogres (སྲིན, srin) have brought water into the house | a separate grave for the dead |
| iron | bird, monkey | tiger, hare | the sandalwood trunk ringed by axe-bearing demons, "the axe has struck the tree" | move the grave site and the house site |
| fire | horse, snake | bird, monkey | "a copper needle fallen into the smithy's coals" | move the grave site, part |
| earth | ox, sheep | rat, pig | demons and *srin* toppled a mountain at "the spring of glory" | set apart [?] and flee |
| wood | tiger, hare | few ox, sheep, dog, dragon | "the glorious armour belt fallen into the hearth" | keep the levies of troops apart |

In each row the many years are of the kin's own element and the few of the element it overcomes (water over fire, iron over wood, fire over iron, earth over water, wood over earth); the text states the rows, not this rule. "Many" and "one or two" are not given as counts; the readings are images, the remedies acts.
