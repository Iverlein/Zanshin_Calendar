# The Men-Tsee-Khang's full almanac names no buddha or practice beside the days; the great occasions stand in the month's head

The Men-Tsee-Khang's full almanac (ལོ་ཐོ, lo tho) of the iron dragon year 2000 (mta:p84-100, read on the scan for its months 3 and 4) prints no buddha, deity, vow or practice beside any day. Its day cells hold the reckoning only: date and weekday, mansion, the pair of elements, yoga and karaṇa with the hours of their change, the day's animal, trigram and number, the spirits that move, a weather omen. The cells of the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th, 29th and 30th of month 3 and of the 7th, 8th, 10th and 15th of month 4 were read for it.

The religious days stand in the month's head, as the White Beryl rules (wb.1:p174-178) and as its model almanac does (wb.1:p160b, wb.1:p162b): in month 3 the fifteenth, the teaching of the Kālacakra; in month 4 the seventh, the Buddha's birth, and the fifteenth, his awakening and his passing. The heads name no tenth day, no twenty-fifth, no eighth and no new moon, and attach no practice or multiplied merit to the occasions.

So the marks of the month's days, the Medicine Buddha on the 8th, Padmasambhava on the 10th and the rest, are no part of the Phugpa almanac as the Men-Tsee-Khang prints it in full; they belong to the practice calendars of the schools (festivals.md, *What Nyingma houses' calendars mark*) and to texts such as RCZ. An almanac of a Nyingma house was not read: BDRC's catalogue was not searched for one in this session.
