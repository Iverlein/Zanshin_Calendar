# p. 69: the thirteen ways of funeral are five pairs and three bars; the thirteen who may not be offered food stay unnamed

The fourth examination's «འདུར་ལུགས་བཅུ་གསུམ» (p. 69, the thirteen ways of funeral) lists ten as five pairs (the sky buries the earth and the reverse; the *gdags* the *sribs* and the reverse; the right the left and the reverse; the head the body and the reverse; the upper end the lower and the reverse) and then three who may not hold the seven-day rites (one with great obstacles towards all the living; one who has not sent out sons and sisters; parents and elder brothers, who may not bury): ten and three are the thirteen. That is a reading of the count, not stated by the text. The «ཟན་གཏད་མི་རུང་བཅུ་གསུམ» (the thirteen not to be offered food) are named only by one, «ཟན་བརྣངས་ལར་ན་བརྣངས་ནས་ཤི» (one who choked on pulled food and died); the same words stand in the Gser thang etext and the 1996 etext and nowhere else BDRC's full-text search reaches; the thirteen kinds of corpse of the seventh examination (p. 72) are the obvious thirteen, but the verse does not say so and none of the old, the infant, the blade… is called one who may not be given food. The notes (p. 503) only reorder the phrase.
