# The title of the Turning back of the bad dream has «ལྟ་བ» in every print read, the text says «ལྟས»: not settled (RD395, q1090)

In the Derge Tengyur and in the Dpe bsdur ma the title of the Avalokiteśvara rite is spelt «རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ལྟ་བ་ངན་པ་རྣམ་པར་བཟློག་པ» with «ལྟ་བ» (lta ba), in the colophon of the Derge (VE23703_1389 p. 309), in the contents of the Dpe bsdur ma (etext p. 28) and in the closing line of its text (p. 632), and in the two catalogues (svo:i816-817). The word of the title is thus the same in every print read, so a slip of the cataloguers' pen is excluded.

Whether the print means «ལྟ་བ» "view" or «ལྟས» "sign" cannot be shown. In the lexica «lta ba ngan pa» is the fixed term "bad view" (kudṛṣṭi: Mahāvyutpatti, Negi, Hopkins) and «ltas ngan» the "bad omen" (durnimitta, apaśakuna: Negi), the two kept apart. In the text of the rite the thing turned back is «ལྟས» (ལྟས་གང་བྱུང་, ལྟས་ངན་རྣམ་པར་བཤིག), and neighbouring Avalokiteśvara texts of the same volume speak of «ལྟས་ངན» as what he destroys (pp. 317, 353). The title therefore either uses «ལྟ་བ» in the sense of «ལྟས», a spelling which the Tibetan scribes of the title kept, or means the "bad view" of a dream (a bad way of seeing it); the rite itself speaks of signs only. I take the title to name the bad signs a sleeper sees, as the text does, and the question stays a doubt for a reader of the tradition.
