# «ངན་པ་ཐ་ཆད», utterly bad, is the bottom class, not a count

"Utterly bad" (ངན་པ་ཐ་ཆད, ngan pa tha chad; ཐ་ཆད, tha chad, "the very last") names the bottom class of a graded set, below the merely bad, not a count of pebbles.

- WB grades the letters' elements (vol. 2, p. 41, wb.2:p39-41, machine): great and small elements agreeing best, the great agreeing middling, the small agreeing last (ཐ་མ, tha ma), disagreeing «ཐ་ཆད». It grades the ten meetings of the planet's and mansion's elements (vol. 2, p. 333, wb.2:p331-333, on the scan): three good, three that sustain, three bad and one «ཐ་ཆད», the meeting of fire and water. Of the directions of journeys by planet and mansion (vol. 2, p. 393, wb.2:p391-393, machine), the bad are «ཐ་ཆད» or somewhat virtuous.
- The regent's questioner (ys:i094, on the scan) calls the match's mewa meetings named the body cut (ལུས་ཆད, lus chad), the last class of the hidden *gi gong*, «ངན་པ་ཐ་ཆད».
- In BDRC's full-text search «ངན་པ་ཐ་ཆད» has 68 hits, nearly all outside the reckoning texts, in the sense "the lowest, the basest" (Tāranātha's «སྐྱེ་བོ་ངན་པ་ཐ་ཆད», 2026-10-25).

So in reply 111 (ys:i467-468) "two utterly bad" lives are two lives of the lowest class. The reply itself makes the class binary: each life reckoning is gathered into one verdict pebble, white or black, by its white and black pebbles; it gives no count, and no reckoning text found names "all black" as the condition.
