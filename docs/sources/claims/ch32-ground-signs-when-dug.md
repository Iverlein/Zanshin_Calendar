# Ch. 32: the signs of a dug ground

WB vol. 2, ch. 32, pp. 282–283 (img. 288–289, machine): the signs of the ground when it is dug at the building site. A pit is dug a cubit deep. A stone found means a great wind; a bone, piercing pain; a potsherd, dumbness; straw with ash, disease; hair, ash or wood, small harms. The colour of the earth: white for the warrior class, yellow for lords, red for commoners, blue for brahmins, black for outcastes, oily and pleasant earth as the sign of attainment. The hole filled again with its own earth: if the earth is more than the hole, good; if it does not fill, poor. Filled with water: the ground that holds the water well is good, low ground is avoided; water that sounds shows the nāgas afraid, and then a treasure vase is hidden. After the test the signs are wiped out. On p. 283: a hole found in the ground is stopped with a stone from the same part; rotten cloth or wood bodes the death of the father's kin; ashes are carried away by the wind; a stone with a mark, the nāgas angered. Doubts: p. 283's lines on women and the 'bearers' («བུད་མེད་བུས་པ») are not understood.
