# Mipham's counts of the trigram remedies and of his thirteen classes: what the arithmetic allows

Mipham's line (p. 517) counts the trigram remedies as eight identifying pebbles, one per trigram, and for whichever it is six: its general remedy, its sum of slips, the white, black and speckled rites 'counted one each' («གཏོ་དཀར་ནག་ཁྲ་གསུམ་རེ་རེ་ནས་བརྩི») and the smith's paper-roll remedy. For q213 the notes count the three rites as three pebbles, each by itself, not as one verdict's colour. Arithmetic for q214: 8 + 6 = 14, WB's number (p. 50) and the notes' first count; «བཅོ་ལྔ» needs one more and the notes name no fifteenth: not decided, and no source outside WB and the notes found. For q297: the notes' classes with counts 14 (or 15), 10, 24, nine spud pa with six rgyug, 9, 20, 13, the thirty dates (30, as the text), 8, 27, 17, 24 reach 202 only with 14 and 6, or 15 and 5, so the sum cannot tell them apart; the thirteen classes may come from counting the spud pa and the rgyug as two, or the four ra remedies apart from the thirteen: not decided.
