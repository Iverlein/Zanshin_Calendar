# «[རྙོངས]་ཚད» on WB p. 435 is the turbid fever (rnyogs tshad): the fevers run in the Instruction Tantra's order

The seven fevers WB lists on p. 435 by the split of the five pebbles run in the order of the Instruction Tantra's seven fever chapters (Serthang, contents and st:p202): the grown (fire four, earth one: རྒྱས་ཚད, p. 187), the empty (སྟོངས་ཚད, p. 191), the hidden (གབ་ཚད, p. 195), the old (རྙིངས་ཚད, p. 199), the fifth (fire four, wind one), the scattered (འགྲམས་ཚད, p. 205) and the disturbed (འཁྱུགས་ཚད, p. 210); the Tantra's fifth is the turbid fever (རྙོགས་ཚད, rnyogs tshad, p. 202). So the word WB's prints show as «[རྙོངས]་ཚད» (the 1996 Yigdzin-1 reading; the reprint's ink clot; the Taikhang «སྙོངས») stands where the turbid fever stands, and is that fever, not a repeat of the empty fever above it. The signs agree: WB gives for it «རྡུལ་མང་པགས་རྩེ་སྣ་ཁྲག་འོང་», dust (in the urine), nose blood coming; the Tantra's special signs are «རྡུལ་བརྩེ་སྣ་ཁྲག་གཡོ», urine with dust and nose blood stirred. The Tantra names it for the stirred spring or sand-and-water (a fever badly treated), and the Tshig mdzod chen mo has «rnyogs tshad» as the fever of sand and water churned together, making the body turbid. The final letter of WB's word (ངས against the Tantra's གས) is not told on the prints, and the Tantra's «རྙོགས» is the word taken; it does not rest on the letters alone. Etext only for the Tantra's page (the page image 241 confirms the page and its number, 202); the dictionaries and the order are what the reading rests on.
