# The union of times: 21,600 breaths in twelve parts, the sun's and the moon's remainders

WB's chapter eight reckons the "union of times" (*dus sbyor*) in twelve parts a day: a day and night (a *phrugs*, one whole turn) holds 21,600 breaths (*dbugs*), and the quantity obtained by dividing it among the twelve houses (the signs) is the enjoyment (*longs spyod*) of one union of times, that is 1,800 breaths. To test the division of the union of times by the five parts, the common figure is divided by 900 (and by 600 for the three-part union); the quantity found cannot be above two (three-part: above three); of the two, the sun is lord of the one and the moon of the two: by the sun a son is born, by the moon a daughter. For the twelve and the thirty parts the common *dhruva* is divided by 150 and by 60. See p. 52 (the 900 and 600, the sun and the moon) and p. 55 (21,600 and the twelve).
