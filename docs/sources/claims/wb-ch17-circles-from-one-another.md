# Ch. 17: finding one of the three circles from another

WB's chapter 17 teaches how to get one of the three circles of day from another by the difference between their circles and everlasting enjoyments (wb.1:p196, continuing from p195). When the house-day or tithi is the larger and the smaller is sought, the rule is done twice on one side: all the places of the one are multiplied by 2 and divided by 67; the other is found by subtracting. When the smaller is given and the larger sought, the places are multiplied by 2 as before and divided by 65, and the other is made by giving. For the solar day and the tithi, from smaller to larger the places are multiplied by 177 and divided by 11,135 and the other made by giving; from larger to smaller they are multiplied by 177 and then divided by 11,312, and the other made by subtracting. For the house-day against the solar day, the places are multiplied by 2,153 and divided by 149,209 (larger to smaller) or by a further constant, printed 147,056 (smaller to larger). The text closes the harder cases with a general rule: the remainders of the upper places multiply the lower, and the divisor parts are mixed and drawn out (p. 197).
