# The circles: the second, the white snow lioness, for spiritual friends

The second of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the white snow lioness, is read for spiritual friends (lamas). Its starting point: the hare of the enemy element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: counting upward from the hare of the enemy element of the one reckoned and turning there: whether the golden vajra has gone up into the sky; whether the vase of inlaid gems has slipped from the hand; whether the crystal rosary has fallen into the ashes; whether the turquoise maṇḍala is crushed at its centre; whether the lion throne has toppled from its base: five.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the golden vajra has not gone up into the sky, no obstacle to the life; the deathless vajra body attained, appearance and existence gathered under his power, the lama's blessing entered: good. | the golden vajra has gone up into the sky: an obstacle to the life before his power reaches its height. | strive at approach and accomplishment, great confession and purification (སྤོང་དག, spong dag), reading the "hundred thousand" (འབུམ, 'bum) and raising the rites. |
| Body | the vase not slipped from his hand: the saffron robes, the victory banner raised, blessing, enjoyments, life and glory grow. | the vase slips from his hand: the Dharma line short, a severe illness to his body, no fit disciples: bad. | accomplish the dhāraṇīs, turn back the demons (བདུད, bdud), change the body and renew the vows, recite the *Tog gzungs* and the hundredfold counts. |
| Power | the thread of the crystal rosary not broken: he plays in the magic dance of the profound meaning; disciples and enjoyments flourish greatly. | the thread broken: disciples, activity and enjoyments do not flourish, great obstacles to helping others, losses of food and wealth. | diligent recitation, wide giving of food and wealth, bestowing empowerments; a threefold confession and purification is good. |
| Luck | the precious turquoise maṇḍala not ruined at its rim: fame and qualities like flowers in bloom, students and disciples flourish: good. | ruined at its rim: gossip and the fear of slander and tongues. | offerings before the three supports, offering robes and praying, clearing the narrow paths, teaching the Dharma: strive at white virtue. |
| Mewa | the lion throne not empty: the deathless body achieved, the stream of nectar-blessing flows, activity spreads in the ten directions: good. | the lion throne empty: an obstacle to the life, a great risk of having to move his seat. | offering a prayer for long life, a long-life practice, and the great practice of the *Bka' brgyad* with thread-cross and turning back. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
