# Snow-rain and frost on set lunar dates

WB vol. 2, pp. 30–31: snow-rain (ཁ་ཆར, kha char) on set days of the lunar months, each with what follows. On p. 31 most entries read what follows if it does *not* fall: first spring 1st (a good year), 12th (frost, middling), 25th (bad); middle spring 16th and 18th (famine), 15th (a warm winter [?]), the 24th if it falls (great drought); last spring 8th and 20th (illness), 20th (drought); first summer 7th (wind); middle summer 7th and 20th (sap dries); last summer 30th (late rain), 6th (livestock illness), 27th (a bad year); first autumn 4th (bad for livestock), 7th and 8th (great drought), 18th (red wind), 24th (fierce hail); middle autumn 24th (human illness), 26th (wind); last autumn 15th (wind), 26th (the year's tail short); first winter at its start (falling: inner strife [?]), 5th (water scarce), 10th (people do not settle); middle winter 4th and 11th (not understood), 6th (drought); last winter 11th (the rich grow poor), 14th (a warm winter), 3rd (great red wind). A whitening of the high mountains or snow on the bare slopes counts as falling.

On p. 30, what follows if it does fall: first summer 15th (drought, strife where it falls); middle summer 8th (heavy rain), 15th (drought); last summer 8th (people and livestock gather [?]), 15th (strife calms), 22nd (good; not falling, bad for the borderlands); first autumn 8th, then frost on horses' hair on the 28th; middle autumn 8th, then frost on mice (5th), vultures (13th), the *gzhung mo* (14th), stags (23rd), foxes (26th), donkeys (28th); last autumn 8th, the season's hoarfrost.

The days are lunar dates; whether it falls is observed.
