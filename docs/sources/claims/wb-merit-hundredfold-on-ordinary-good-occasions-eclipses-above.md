# WB ch. 9: virtue is multiplied a hundredfold on the ordinary good occasions, far more at an eclipse, and evil likewise

The White Beryl (rank 1) states its one rule of multiplied merit in ch. 9, on eclipses, as the ninth of the ten points on the lunar eclipse (vol. 1, pp. 62-63, read on the scan): the difference in the multiplying of virtue is a hundredfold on the ordinary good occasions (དུས་བཟང་དཀྱུས་རྣམས, dus bzang dkyus rnams), seven bye ba (seventy million) at a lunar eclipse and a hundred thousand bye ba at a solar eclipse.

What stands above the ordinary good occasions is therefore the two eclipses, and nothing else is named. The passage does not say which days the ordinary good occasions are: no date, festival or phase of the moon is listed there, and the word "ordinary" (དཀྱུས, dkyus) only sets them against the eclipses. That they are the full and new moon, the eighth or the festivals written at the month's head is not said in ch. 9.

The grounds given: the Victor taught it at length in his scriptures, in the sūtras of provisional meaning, the Sūtra of the Sun and Moon above all; in the definitive meaning, the Kālacakra, the winds enter the side channels and the central channel at an eclipse, so that generation and completion stage practice, self-entry and the like increase the wholesome, and that is the true reason. For whom: nobody is singled out; the acts named are tantric practice, but the multiplying is of virtue in general. The last lines add that evil deeds increase in the same way at these times, as the authoritative scriptures and treatises say.

For the solar eclipse the chapter does not repeat the point: its ninth, the difference of merit, and its tenth, the results, are said to be as explained above for the moon, in the main common to sun and moon (wb.1:p67-69, a machine reading).
