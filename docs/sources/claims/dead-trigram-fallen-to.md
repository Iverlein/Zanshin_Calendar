# The dead's trigram in ch. 29 is the trigram fallen to: the descended trigram at death

In the tenth examination's strikes (WB vol. 2, p. 89), "born rat, dragon or monkey, li or kham reached, if one dies: the strike of the three Tuesdays gathered": the regent's reply 152 (ys:i513-514) reads the dead's trigram there as the trigram *fallen to* (བབས, babs): "a man born rat, dragon or monkey whose trigram has fallen on li or kham", that is the descended trigram (བབ་སྤར, bab spar) at death, not the birth trigram. In the trigram repetition of the same page ("when it has fallen on the *do gcig*, and the *ma klungs*' trigram, and year, month, day and hour, four, are stacked") he names both: the *do gcig* fallen on dwa, the birth (*ma klungs*) on dwa, and the year, month, day and hour of death bird or monkey, of the trigram's element. Mipham's note on the line (wb.2:p504-512) has the dead's birth trigram and descended trigram of the year of death the same.

So where ch. 29 says "died on li" of the dead, the author of WB means the trigram the dead had descended to, at least in the tenth examination. That the ninth examination's provisions («ལིར་ཤི་ན», pp. 75–77) and the notes' "the year of death's trigram and mewa" (p. 503) mean the same is an inference from the same idiom in the same chapter (questions 260, 283).
