# None of the five cited texts names a watch of the night or a day for the dream; what each names as a remedy (RD373)

None of the five texts the Sngags rim chen mo cites for the dawn dream and its remedies names a watch of the night, a day of the month or a season: the Common Tantra, the Garland, Mar me mdzad bzang po, Klu byang and Dārikapa all have the pupil asked at dawn for the dream of the night, whatever part of the night it came in (sct:p295-297, vjv:p230-231, mmb:p155-156, nbv:p273-274, trt:p418-419). The rule that the first three watches are not certain and the fourth is used is Tsong kha pa's own and the Mañjuśrī root tantra's four-watch verse that he quotes beside it (mmk-snr-quotes-the-verse-and-adds-the-reason); the cited rites of the pupil's preparation do not give it. What each names, for the Sngags rim chen mo's use:

| Text | A watch | A day | A remedy of the bad dream |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common Tantra | no | no | the pacifying fire offering, one hundred and eight times |
| Garland | no | no | the whirling mantra of amṛta |
| Mar me mdzad bzang po | no | no | the All-Doer and the means of application; the Ender of Obstructors is not in the lines (mmb-remedy-all-doer-not-ender-of-obstructors) |
| Klu byang | no | no | the talk of selflessness |
| Dārikapa | no | no | for the pupil the All-Doer; for the teacher the seven acts |
