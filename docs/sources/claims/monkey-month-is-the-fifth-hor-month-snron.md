# The monkey month of the tenth-day texts is the fifth Hor month, Snron, in the Phugpa tradition

The monkey month (སྤྲེལ་ཟླ, sprel zla) of the tenth-day texts is the fifth Hor month (ཧོར་ཟླ་ལྔ་པ, hor zla lnga pa), the month of Snron (སྣྲོན, snron). Zhe chen rgyal tshab says so of the Phugpa tradition (ཕུག་ལུགས, phug lugs): «སྤྲེལ་ཟླའི་ངོས་འཛིན་ཡང་། ཕུག་ལུགས་རྗེས་འབྲང་དབུས་གཙང་མཁས་པ་ཡོངས་ལ་གྲགས་ཤིང་གྲུབ་པ་ནི་ཧོར་ཟླ་ལྔ་པ་སྣྲོན་གྱི་ཟླ་བ་འདི་ཉིད་ཡིན་པས», and adds that Gter bdag gling pa, father and sons, held the tenth-day offering of the time (དུས་མཆོད, dus mchod) in that month (zgp.13:p541, read on the scan). The White Beryl's model almanac heads the same month «རགས་པ་སྣྲོན་ཉ … ཧོར་ཟླ་ལྔ་པ་རྒྱ་རྩིས་སྟོན་ར … སྤྲེལ་ཟླ», the full moon in Snron, the fifth Hor month, the first autumn month of the Chinese reckoning, the monkey month (wb.1:p154-171), which is what q1166 found for the dākinī's monkey month in Rtsa gsum gling pa's life. Padma gling pa's text agrees on the season: it calls the tiger, snake, monkey and pig months "the four first" (ར་བ་བཞི, ra ba bzhi), the hare, horse, bird and mouse months "the four middle" and the dragon, sheep, dog and ox months "the four last" (pgl.1:p835-837), the first, middle and last months of the four seasons as the White Beryl's almanac counts them by the Chinese reckoning.

The tenth of the monkey month is therefore the tenth of the fifth Hor month in the Phugpa calendar, and the monkey year's is the day on which the Guru comes himself (pgl.1:p835-837; claim tenth-day-monkey-month-monkey-year). 'Jigs med gling pa's "sixth Hor month" for the birth is a month later (claim tenth-day-months-jigme-lingpa-one-month-later-than-the-animal-lists).
