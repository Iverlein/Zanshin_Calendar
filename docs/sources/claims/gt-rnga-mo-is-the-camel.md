# རྔ་མོ in ch. 7's verse: the camel

The animal «རྔ་མོ» (rnga mo) of the third line of ch. 7's dream verse (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, a medical dictionary on BDRC): the camel; Byang pa's and Rin spungs pa's «རྔ་མོང» is the same word.

**The dictionaries.** རྔ་མོ (rnga mo): the camel (Jim Valby, Ives Waldo, Dan Martin, the Mahāvyutpatti and Lokesh Chandra's Sanskrit lists, Negi: uṣṭra; Hopkins); the Tshig mdzod chen mo: "like rnga mong"; the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs: a large beast with a long neck and one or two humps, a yellow-brown coat, a good animal for riding and for loads, also called rnga mong; the Brda dkrol gser gyi me long: the archaic form of rnga mong, the long-necked animal, and also an archaic word for milk (འོ་མ, 'o ma); Dan Martin also has "milk" for it. རྔ་མོང (rnga mong): the camel in all (the Tshig mdzod chen mo lists its names, the wheel-necked, the hanging-lipped, the wind-loving). The Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo (BDRC I00EGS1017132, etext OCR) has «རྔ་མོ» as another spelling of རྔ་མོང, with the camel's names, and «རྔ་མོང» as a grass-eater of great height.

**The sense.** In «མ་ཧེ་རྟ་ཕག་བོང་བུ་རྔ་མོ་ལ། གཅེར་བུར་ཞོན་ནས་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་འགྲོ་རྨིས་འཆི» the animals are the ridden beasts (buffalo, horse, pig, donkey), and "milk" does not suit a verb of riding; so the camel. The Dag tshig's remark that it is ridden agrees. Khro ru's «ཇ་མོ» with his gloss "female dzo" (ཇོ་མོ) is not a dictionary form: ཇོ་མོ is "lady, nun", the female dzo is མཛོ་མོ (mdzo mo), and no dictionary has ཇ་མོ; in the medical dictionary ཇ་མ (ja ma) is the tail of a horse or yak. The reading of Khro ru's word stays a question of his print, but the Drathang's animal is the camel (q858). Rank: moderate (a dictionary sense and the verb of riding).
