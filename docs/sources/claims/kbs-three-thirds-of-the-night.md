# Klong rdol, the night's three thirds, and only the dawn third is read

Klong rdol bla ma (Ngag dbang blo bzang, a Dge lugs lama of Chab mdo; BDRC gives 1719–1794) wrote, as the article says in the 18th century, a text on examining the omens of dreams; the article (pp. 68–70) quotes his rule that the night's dreams are not all examined alike. He divides the night into three thirds (ཐུན་གྱི་སུམ་ཆ, thun gyi sum cha) and gives each its character:

- the evening third (སྲོད, srod): the imprint (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) of what was done, attended to, eaten, said and met in the last three days; no omen. A dream dreamed again and again belongs here;
- the middle third (གུང, gung, "midnight"): the magic display (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ, cho 'phrul) of spirits, flesh-eaters, asuras and demons moving about; whatever appears, good or bad, the wise say has nothing fixed in it;
- the dawn third (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs): the dream from which the good or bad fruit is examined; this one alone is read.

So his rule gives no span of days or months for the fruit; the time is only which third of the night the dream came in. The article reads «ཐུན», watch, as the same word as the third and counts three, where Vibhūticandra's Svapnohana counts four watches with the dawn apart (svo:p268-271); how the thirds map to clock times is not given, and the article's remark that the thirds match sleep research is its own opinion.

The verses are in the ACIP etext RST pp. 3-4 (rst:p1-8), which ends with Klong rdol bla ma's colophon (rst-is-klong-rdols-dream-text); the etext adds that part of the evening third is the deeds of former lives (rst-and-kbs-wording).
