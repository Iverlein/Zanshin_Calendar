# Marpa's spirit dreams (horse, dog, hag, woman) and 'Jig rten mgon po's male and female spirit dreams by posture are not equated in either text; the overlap is a woman's dream and the loss of the drop (RD403, q1119)

RD403 (q1119). The two teachings say different things and do not refer to one another. 'Jig rten mgon po (dds.24:p190-191): lying on the left side hatred grows and the dream of a male spirit (ཕོ་གདོན) comes with obstacles; lying on the back desire grows and the magic of a female spirit (མོ་གདོན) and the loss of the drop (ཐིག་ལེ་ཉམས་པ) among the obstacles; lying facing west dullness grows and the earth lord's magic comes in the dream. It names no image of the spirit and ties the drop only to the woman spirit (the back). Marpa's inner-heat manual (dds.19:p185, p187-189, p190-191) lists nine causes of the drop's spilling; the third is the force of spirits (གདོན), known if the dream brings a horse, a dog, an ugly old woman who carries one off, or a woman and desire is acted out in it (the dream itself is the sign), and the cure is compassion, giving the spirits what they like, resting in the great seal, and tying a thread. It names no posture and no male or female spirit. A different cause, the seventh, is spilling by habit (བག་ཆགས): the dream of lying with a beautiful young woman, or with a woman who once kept company, or an unlovely old one (dds.19:p190-191).

So the answer is that the text does not say the dreams are the same: the only overlap is that both tie a woman's dream, and the loss of the drop, to a spirit. Whether the «woman» spirit lying on the back is the horse, dog, hag or woman of Marpa's list is not stated by either; it would be a reader's join. The male spirit's dream has no drop in 'Jig rten mgon po and no counterpart in Marpa's list.
