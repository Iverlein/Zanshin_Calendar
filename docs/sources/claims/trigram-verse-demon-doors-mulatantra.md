# The trigram verse on the four demon doors: unnamed in WB, the mulatantra in the Mirror

# The trigram verse "li kham the end pressed" is quoted by WB without a source and by the Mirror of Mañjughoṣa's Mind as «mūlatantra»

For question 658. The verse that the *Mirror of Mañjughoṣa's Mind* (MW3CN2232; BDRC etext I3CN2345, etext p. 293) quotes «མུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ་ལས། ལི་ཁམ་མཐའ་མནན་ཕོ་ལ་རྩུབ། །དྭ་ཟིན་མཐའ་མནན་མོ་ལ་རྩུབ། །ཁེན་ཟོན་མཐའ་མནན་རོ་འཛིན་རྗེས། །གིན་ཁོན་མཐའ་མནན་དོང་ཡང་བརྐོ» stands, four lines of it, in WB itself: vol. 1, in the year-of-age obstacle chapter's rites for the heap (etext p. 392 of the Gser thang reprint, I3CN5028; found by BDRC's exact full-text search 2026-11-07, «ལི་ཁམ་མཐའ་མནན» two hits, and in the etext), introduced «གཞན་ཡང་སྤུངས་པའི་རིམ་པ་ལ། བདུད་ཀྱི་སྒོ་མོ་རྣམ་བཞི་ཞེས། ལི་ཁམ་མཐའ་མནན་ཕོ་ལ་རྩུབ…», "again, on the order of the heap, 'the four doors of the demon', so: li and kham, the end pressed, is harsh on a man…". WB gives it with «ཞེས», "so (it) says", and names no text. The Mirror has the same four lines under the heading «ཡང་གཅིག་བདུད་ཀྱི་སྒོ་མོ་ཆེན་པོ་བཞི» ("one more, the four great doors of the demon") and sends them to «མུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ». So the «Mūlatantra» of the astrological manuals, for this verse, is the source of a verse on the four demon doors (the four pairs of trigrams, Li–Kham, Dwa–Zin, Khen–Zon, Gin–Khon, each pressing at the end), quoted by two manuals, one of them WB's; which root text it is is not said in either. The printed page of the WB passage was not checked (etext p. 392; the reprint's image = page + 7 would put it on vol. 1, p. 385, in ch. 24's range).

The other verse of p. 450, «རང་གིས་གོ་ཆ་མ་བརྩམས་ན། །རང་གིས་བྱས་པ་རང་ལ་བརྡེག», is in neither the Mirror's etext nor WB vol. 1's (searched 2026-11-07); so whether the astrologers' «Mūlatantra» is one text for both verses is not shown.

INV-1114 (2026-11-14): the verse «རང་གིས་གོ་ཆ་མ་བརྩམས་ན…» is found only in WB vol. 2 itself (BDRC exact search: Gser thang I3CN5029 etext p. 456, the 1996 print I1KG12907, and the Zhol-block do shal commentary MW1KG1617, I1KG1710, which repeats WB's verse with the name garbled «མུལ་ཏཅུ»), introduced there by «དེ་ཡང་མུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ་ལས», 'and that, from the Mūlatantra'. Its context is the rites against earth lords and the warning that a rite done without arming oneself (go cha, གོ་ཆ) strikes back at the maker, so it is a rite text's saying, not a trigram verse; WB names the Mūlatantra for this verse, and the Mirror names it for the demon-door verse, which WB gives without a source. The two verses stand in different subjects in the two manuals; nothing shows the one text. The question stays open: which root text, if any single one, is not said.

The other hits of «མཱུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ་ལས» in BDRC's search (2 hits, 2026-11-07) are not astrological: a Nyingma collection's rite «མཉྫུཤྲཱི་མཱུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ་ལས» for a mantra (Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po, gsung 'bum, MW3PD1002) and a Nyingma bka' ma text's «རྒྱུད་མཱུ་ལ་ཏནྟྲ་ལས། དུས་ཀྱི་བརྡ་ནི་ཤེས་བྱས་ནས» (MW1PD100944): the name is used for a root text of many tantras, so the spelling alone identifies nothing.
