# The dreams that mark the nāga spirits' illness (ch. 81)

The dreams that mark the illness of the harmful nāga spirits (གདུག་པ་ཀླུའི་གདོན་ནད, gdug pa klu'i gdon nad), ch. 81 of the Instruction Tantra.

In the chapter on treating it, the identification of the illness is the first of the means of examining, and its general signs are examined by the outer, the inner and the secret. The outer sign is the appearing: dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and the marks of body, speech and mind. The dreams listed are of water, trees, a reed or the like (ནིའུ་གསིང), frogs, fish, worms, bees and other creatures; of things sticking to the body; of sitting in a crevice; of yaks, dzo and beasts of prey chasing; of narrow doors and passes and of falling into a pit; of the sun setting, earth turning to dust and hail; of a temple shaking; and of women drawing the body's essence out. The text calls this the sign of being struck by a spirit (གདོན, gdon). The passage names the nāga only through its chapter: the chapter's illness is the black water-humour (ཆུ་སེར་ནག་པོ, chu ser nag po), called the illness of mdze (མཛེ). The list holds no dream by lunar day, weekday or hour. A remedy for the dreams is not given in the passage; the chapter's treatments are later (not read here). Several of the creatures' names are unsettled. The chapter is the eighty-first by its colophon, p. 357 of the print (img. 369); the passage is on pp. 350–351 (img. 362–363) (st:p350-351).
