# The dreams that mark the nāga spirits' illness (ch. 81)

The dreams that mark the illness of the harmful nāga spirits (གདུག་པ་ཀླུའི་གདོན་ནད, gdug pa klu'i gdon nad), ch. 81 of the Instruction Tantra.

In the chapter on treating it, the identification of the illness is the first of the means of examining, and its general signs are examined by the outer, the inner and the secret. The outer sign is the appearing: dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and the marks of body, speech and mind. The dreams listed are of water, trees, a green meadow (ནིའུ་གསིང, ni'u gsing), frogs and tadpoles (སྦལ་ལྗོང, sbal ljong), fish and snakes (ཉ་སྦྲུལ, nya sbrul), insects and bees (འབུ་སྦྲང, 'bu sbrang), a scorpion and a spider (སྡིག་སྡོམ, sdig sdom) and the like; of things sticking to the body; of sitting in a crevice; of yaks, dzo and beasts of prey chasing; of narrow doors and passes and of falling into a pit; of the sun setting, earth turning to dust and hail; of arrows and eggs; of a temple shaking; and of women drawing the body's essence out. The text calls this the sign of being struck by a spirit (གདོན, gdon). The passage names the nāga only through its chapter: the chapter's illness is the black water-humour (ཆུ་སེར་ནག་པོ, chu ser nag po), called the illness of mdze (མཛེ). The list holds no dream by lunar day, weekday or hour. A remedy for the dreams is not given in the passage; the chapter's treatments are later (not read here). The creatures' names are settled on the scan and in the dictionaries (RD227): the scan reads fish and snakes (ཉ་སྦྲུལ), not the etext's ལྕོལ, and the Drathang print agrees; the senses of ནིའུ་གསིང (a meadow), སྦལ་ལྗོང (tadpoles), འབུ་སྦྲང, སྡིག་སྡོམ (scorpion and spider) and སྒོ་ང (egg) are the dictionaries'. Two words stay unsettled and carry no verdict: ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ, and the print's ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ (the temple shaking; the Drathang machine text has ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ). The chapter is the eighty-first by its colophon, p. 357 of the print (img. 369); the passage is on pp. 350–351 (img. 362–363) (st:p350-351).
