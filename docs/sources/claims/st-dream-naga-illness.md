# The dreams that mark the nāga spirits' illness (ch. 81)

The dreams that mark the illness of the harmful nāga spirits (གདུག་པ་ཀླུའི་གདོན་ནད, gdug pa klu'i gdon nad), ch. 81 of the Instruction Tantra.

In the chapter on treating it, the identification of the illness is the first of the means of examining, and its general signs are examined by the outer, the inner and the secret. The outer sign is the appearing: dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and the marks of body, speech and mind. The dreams listed are of water, trees, a green meadow (ནིའུ་གསིང, ni'u gsing), frogs and tadpoles (སྦལ་ལྗོང, sbal ljong), fish and snakes (ཉ་སྦྲུལ, nya sbrul), insects and bees (འབུ་སྦྲང, 'bu sbrang), a scorpion and a spider (སྡིག་སྡོམ, sdig sdom) and the like; of things sticking to the body; of sitting in a crevice; of yaks, dzo and beasts of prey chasing; of narrow doors and passes and of falling into a pit; of the sun setting, earth turning to dust and hail; of arrows and eggs; of a temple shaking; and of women drawing the body's essence out. The text calls this the sign of being struck by a spirit (གདོན, gdon). The passage names the nāga only through its chapter: the chapter's illness is the black water-humour (ཆུ་སེར་ནག་པོ, chu ser nag po), called the illness of mdze (མཛེ). The list holds no dream by lunar day, weekday or hour. A remedy for the dreams is not given in the passage; the chapter's treatments are later (not read here). The creatures' names are settled on the scan and in the dictionaries (RD227): the scan reads fish and snakes (ཉ་སྦྲུལ), not the etext's ལྕོལ, and the Drathang print agrees; the senses of ནིའུ་གསིང (a meadow), སྦལ་ལྗོང (tadpoles), འབུ་སྦྲང, སྡིག་སྡོམ (scorpion and spider) and སྒོ་ང (egg) are the dictionaries'. Two words stay unsettled and carry no verdict: ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ, and the print's ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ (the temple shaking; the Drathang machine text has ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ). The chapter is the eighty-first by its colophon, p. 357 of the print (img. 369); the passage is on pp. 350–351 (img. 362–363) (st:p350-351).

What the commentaries add (RD255, 2026-11-09; etext only). Khro ru Mkhan po Tshe rnam (a modern author; `ktr:p329-332`) and the 'Bri gung *Dka' mchan* (`bdk:p580-581`) sort the dreams of the list by what each marks, and agree in most. Water, trees and the green meadow: falling within the aim of a harm sent at one (བཏད་དུ་ཚུད་པ; the Drikung gtad du tshud pa; RD271 corrects the earlier "pledge": claim st-gtad-du-tshud-pa-under-a-sent-harm). The creatures (frog and tadpole, snake, worm, bee-grub, scorpion, spider, in Khro ru also the «horse-la» རྟ་བླ and the ant): the Drikung calls them the general sign of the flesh-eaters (ཤ་ཟ, sha za); things sticking to the body, sitting in a crevice and being chased by yak, dzo and beasts of prey: the signs of a spirit (གདོན). Narrow doors and passes, falling into a pit: being pressed down (མནན་པར་ཚུད་པ). The sun setting, dust, hail, arrows (Khro ru: the general sign of «byad kha»), an egg, a temple that shakes: the sign of a male spirit (ཕོ་གདོན) in both; the Drikung's gloss on the egg, the arrow and the others is not in the etext. Women drawing out the body's essence: the sign of a female spirit (མོ་གདོན) in both. So the list is not of the nāga's illness alone: Khro ru and the Drikung read it as the dreams of being struck by several kinds of spirit, the nāga's among them; the text of the chapter itself says only that they are the sign of being struck by a spirit. The sorting by kind of spirit is older than Khro ru: the 18th-century glosses written into Sde srid's Supplement (`mbl:p521-522`, Blo bzang dbang rgyal) are the ones Khro ru gives. Khro ru's creatures' list has the spelling སྦུལ་པ and ལྕོང་མོ, and his verse has ལྕོང (lcong) where the Serthang print has ལྗོང (ljong), a spelling the Supplement also has (`lth:p325`).

RD271 (2026-11-09, dictionaries and BDRC etexts): of the list's unexplained words, ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ་ is "the temple's clay statues move" (st-lder-bzo-is-the-temples-clay-statues), ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ is "the time felt by the god", against "the time felt by oneself" (st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god), and Khro ru's རྟ་བླ is the centipede (st-rta-bla-is-the-centipede).
