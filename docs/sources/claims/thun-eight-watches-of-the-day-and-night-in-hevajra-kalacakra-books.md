# The Hevajra and Kālacakra books count eight equal watches to the day-and-night, four by night

The tantric commentaries on the Hevajra and the Kālacakra count the day-and-night as eight watches (ཐུན, thun; in these books also ཐུན་ཚོད, thun tshod, "watch-time"): four by day and four by night. Five authors say it in the etexts: Ratnākaraśānti's *Muktāvalī* (a verse: "the night, four watches, [and] the day; a quarter of a watch is a water-hour"), the fourth Zhwa dmar, Padma dkar po, Rang byung rdo rje and Chos kyi grags pa.

In this breath reckoning the watches are equal and the day-and-night is a fixed whole: a day-and-night has 64 dbyug gu (a short measure of the breath), two dbyug gu make one water-hour (ཆུ་ཚོད, chu tshod), so 32 water-hours; a quarter of a watch is one water-hour, so a watch is four of these; each watch is two "moves" (འཕོ་བ, 'pho ba), and the move is called the half-watch (ཐུན་ཕྱེད, thun phyed): sixteen moves to the day-and-night. The water-hour here is not the 60-to-the-day-and-night of the astronomical reckoning.

For the questions on WB: the vocabulary is the same as WB's. WB vol. 1 p. 135 divides the day-and-night measure by eight (the same sentence's ཐུན་ཕྱེད, the half-watch, "arises" on the wheel) and vol. 2 p. 403 divides the day-and-night into eight watches, "a planet and a nāga at each". The reading of q811 that fits is one division of the whole day-and-night into eight, four by day and four by night; the Rāhu of p. 101 (four by day, four by night) says the same. This is our inference from the shared words; none of the commentaries read says WB takes the division from them, and WB's own wording on p. 403 does not say "four and four".
