# A rainbow is read by the night part, dusk and the junctures (Garga, in Klong rdol's abridgement)

In the same abridgement the rainbow (འཇའ, 'ja) has two rules of time and place. In the night part (མཚན་མོའི་ཆ, mtshan mo'i cha) a rainbow means the king dies, strife, illness and famine ruin the country. At dusk (སྲོད, srod) a rainbow standing in the east harms the king, in the west the chief of men; a five-coloured one standing in the east is followed by rain, from south to west it harms the king and the company of the monkey (སྤྲེའུ, spre'u) and the butcher or slayer (བཤན་པ, bshan pa, so read, uncertain). A rainbow standing at the junction of the three times (དུས་གསུམ་མཚམས, dus gsum mtshams) makes that year good; at the time of good planets (གཟའ, gza') and the like it brings the king peace and the destruction of thieves; and within seven days after it, even a bad rain gives good fruit.

Colours and shapes are read apart from time: parasol, banner, chariot or wheel are auspicious; staff, club, spear tip or bow, a skeleton or aquatic shapes harm the country; many white layers, a monk dies; fire-red or yellow, fear. The rule gives no date and no lunar day.
