# RRT: the bad sign that is good for one practice, by deity

RRT pp. 189–191 (etext). A bad sign is bad for every practice but one. The table gives the practice for which it is good and bad for the rest:
- naked, burning, vomiting, bleeding, bathing, eating one's own flesh, bowing to images and the lama, receiving empowerment: good for the supreme practice (མཆོག་གི་སྒྲུབ་པ, mchog gi sgrub pa);
- no good weapon, a stupa under the ground, sun and moon falling, many naked women: Lord of Death (གཤིན་རྗེ, gshin rje);
- a red river crossed, a long precipice, sitting in blood: Hayagrīva (རྟ་མཆོག, rta mchog);
- many grey-white ashes: Vajrakīla (ཕུར་པ, phur pa);
- a great red wind, a donkey, black darkness: the Mothers (མ་མོ, ma mo); flower rain, dawn, the sun rising are the reverse: bad for the Mothers, good for the rest;
- a lion coming to eat one, a book read in the dark, hail: Vajrapāṇi (ཕྱག་རྡོར, phyag rdor);
- sleepiness: the hurled cake (ཟོར, zor);
- the great male organ, Īśvara showing himself, standing: the non-Buddhist (མུ་སྟེགས, mu stegs) practice;
- herds of horses and donkeys: the btsan and the yakṣa king (བཙན, btsan; གནོད་སྦྱིན, gnod sbyin);
- cold, a hungry belly: the hail practice (སེར་བ, ser ba);
- a dark plain, a corpse moving: the casting (གཏད, gtad);
- a lone Ācārya, Mon people, monks and the like: bad for the wisdom deity, good for the proud spirit practice (དྲེགས་པ, dregs pa).
