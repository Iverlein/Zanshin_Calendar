# The circles: the starting point, the count and the five pebbles

How a circle of ch. 27 is laid (WB vol. 2, p. 3; the notes, vol. 2, p. 533):

- The starting point is a year animal of a named element relation to the one reckoned: the first circle "the bird of the enemy of the reckoned person's element". The notes name that element: «བརྩི་བྱའི་ཁམས་སྟེ་དབང་ཐང་གི་དགྲ་སོགས», the reckoned person's element, that is, the enemy (and the rest) of his power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang). The tenth circle's fine way says the same in its own words: "the pig of the same power as the one reckoned" (p. 10).
- From that year, count upward (གྱེན, gyen) as many years as he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa, and each is fought by the reckoned person's own: five pebbles to a circle.
- The mewa pebble: the mewa reached by counting upward from the reckoned person's body mewa and from the starting point's mewa that agrees with it in the mewa sequence (the notes' words; how the two are combined is not spelled out).
- The tenth circle has a rough and a fine way; in the fine one the servant's starting point is taken from the master's year, the notes observe, though the text could be read as the servant's own. The thirteenth is called "of rise and decline" but is fought as the others, the rise and decline read from the good or bad of the fighting.
- Thirteen circles of five make 65 pebbles; WB counts 301 root and 501 sign pebbles for the chapter, 802 (p. 3), without a breakdown.
