# WB ch. 1 opens with homages, then eight 'definite teachings' against the calculators of the day

The introduction's verse (machine reading, wording unsettled in places) runs through six and then seven verses of good fortune (to the six-armed Protector, to Magzor Gyalmo, to Tsongkhapa), then numbered homages (ཕྱག་བྱ) from the first, to the Buddha, through the Dharma and the Sangha, the kings of Shambhala (the 'number of the mountains', that is seven, and the twenty-five), to the writers of the tradition, up to a fifteenth, and then eight 'definite teachings' (ངེས་བསྟན). The fifteenth homage and the eight teachings are the author's complaint: calculators of his day, proud and unlearned, hold to old errors and to rough 'at random' (མུ་ཅོར) reckonings; the Phugpa system (ཕུག་པའི་ལུགས) is set against them as the unspoiled one, its hand-tradition (ཕྱག་རྒྱུན) 'an unerring sun'. The people named in the homages are not identified here: a lord of knowledge Smon-'gro-pa, a translator-scholar, Padma Karpo, a man of Dumpo (ལྡུམ་པོ་བ), and a 'Dran-dbang Don-grub-dbang-gi-rgyal-po' whose name is not settled.
