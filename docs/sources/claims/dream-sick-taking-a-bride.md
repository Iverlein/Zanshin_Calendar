# Taking a bride

Image group: the dreamer ill. Read by 1 text: Four Tantras (for a sick dreamer).
Offered only when the dreamer is ill: every row is a reading for a sick dreamer (the Four Tantras above WB ch. 25).

| Text | Verdict (tone) | Passage | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Four Tantras (for a sick dreamer) | taking a bride (བག་མ་ལེན་པ, bag ma len pa; the Drathang spells it ལན་པ, lan pa): a sign that the lord of death (འཆི་བདག, 'chi bdag) has seized the sick; dreamed constantly by the sick it means death, for one without illness it is not certain, and the rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) frees him (bad) | `gt:i42-43` | machine |

RD271 (2026-11-09): lan pa is a known spelling of len pa, taking a bride, found in two other etexts in this very phrase (gt-bag-ma-lan-pa-is-bag-ma-len-pa). RD270 (2026-11-09) read the word by eye on the Chag-po-ri print I3CN1711: ལེན་པ, the slanting vowel stroke over the la plain (good), where the Drathang's machine reading has ལན་པ (gt-dream-verse-chag-po-ri-prints). The line stands after the levy in the verse; the two are separate images.

The verse is the Explanatory Tantra's (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud) ch. 7 on the Drathang print (RD268); the Serthang scan lacks the page. The rule on who dies, the cause and the six kinds of dream are in gt-dream-death-rule, the whole list in gt-dream-death-images, the one-syllable differences from the four commentaries in gt-dream-verse-variants. This image claim was added by RD274 (2026-11-09) so that the picker holds every image of the verse; the Drathang passage is a machine reading, the lines read by eye in part (gt:i42-43).
