# The pegs and works on each mewa at the funeral feast

The pegs (སྦོམ་གཏའ, sbom gta') and works set on each mewa at the funeral feast (WB vol. 2, p. 127, read on the scan of the Gser thang reprint):

| Mewa | What is set there | Whom it guards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 white | an arrow with white silk | the reckoner |
| 2 black | the peg itself, "able for all" | — |
| 3 blue | a ram's skull with five-coloured wool | mother and child |
| 4 green | a stick with five ferments tied | the beer-maker |
| 5 yellow | five colours, a turtle drawn | the carriers |
| 6 white | an image of a god and of a dancer | the young and the priest |
| 7 red | seven bowls of water, a pig's skull with silks | the women and the butchers |
| 9 red | a red spear adorned with a rib of meat | the *btsad po* and the patron |
| 9 red | an arrow with a mirror and nine eye-boards | the elders and their wealth |

The eight-white has no peg in this list; the print sets two on the nine-red. Then, by the mewa, the works: on the eight-white the good assembly; on the seven-red all red (blood) work, the funeral meat cut; on the six-white the *se* rite (སེ་བཅའ, se bca') with the earth lords' images and the words of truth; on one's own five-yellow the carriers' share; on the four-green the beer share and the offerings; on the three-blue no speech; on the two-black the peg; from the one-white the speech. Distribution faces the eight-white, never the nine-red and the earth lords who take offence at wealth (Sebyi and the others). Mipham's notes (p. 521) give the same works as nine pebbles measured in fathoms from the east wall (eight in all, the three-blue's "no speech" not counted).
