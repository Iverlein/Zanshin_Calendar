# Rig 'dzin Chos kyi grags pa's life: the nāga king comes in the midnight session (nam gung); the remedy is to change what is brought to his image; one telling, not a rule (RD391)

RD391. In the first-person life of Rig 'dzin Chos kyi grags pa, the visit of a spirit is placed at midnight, in the session called nam gung (ནམ་གུང). On the night of the second, after the signs of the pox lord's touch and a clear sight of the vidyā-king Pawo Kyangdrub in the first dusk session (srod thun), the nāga king Gzi chen comes in the nam gung, says that meat and beer taken to his image at Dbu ru Ka tshal made him unwell and caused the nāga diseases and quarrels of Mal gro, and asks that none be brought and that the image be filled with medicines, jewels, silks and grains; the author later does so at Ka tshal. The same life, a night or two earlier, has the lord of the pox (Go zhal mo) arrive in «the dream of sleep» and answer his questions (dds.49:p110-112). The telling joins the nāga's coming at nam gung with «the earlier dream of dusk» (srod kyi rmi lam) and then recalls Padmasambhava's account of how Khri srong's nāga harm was cured by taking the Nāgarakṣa protection from a stupa's vase; which dream the dusk dream is the words alone do not settle; RD403 (dds.50:p6-8, claim dds-chos-kyi-grags-pa-dusk-sight-brings-the-naga-story-and-nam-gung) reads it as the dusk-session sight of the vidyā-king, in the short account Nāgarakṣa himself. «Nam gung» is the midnight session: the same text names the midday session nyin gung and the early night nam srod and says that after the nam gung session the author fell asleep and dreamed (p114). So this life places a spirit's visit at midnight, once, in a narration, and the remedy is an act: changing what is brought to the place and filling the image. It is not a rule that the middle-of-the-night dream belongs to the spirits.
