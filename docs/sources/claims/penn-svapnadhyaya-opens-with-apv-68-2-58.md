# The Penn Svapnādhyāya begins with the Atharva-pariśiṣṭa's verse, in the form its manuscripts give (RD357)

Verse 58 of the Atharva-pariśiṣṭa 68 (the Svapnādhyāya of Kroṣṭuki) is printed by Bolling and von Negelein as "svapnās tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ | dvitīye 'ṣṭasu māseṣu tṛtīye tu tad-ardha-bhāk" (apv:p445, read on the page image); their apparatus (apv:p456) says that **all their manuscripts read *svapnas*** and that three of them read "ca 'ṣṭamāseṣu" and one "cāṣṭasu māseṣu": the eight months of the second watch are in every witness. The Penn copies (pn1871:i2b, pn2730:i0, pn673:i1) open the span verse with "svapnas tu prathame yāme", the Brahmavaivarta with "svapnas tu prathame yāme" too (bvp:p662-665, bvg:p3), Item 687 with "svapnās tu … saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ" as the editors print it. So the first half-line of the family is the Parisiṣṭa's, in the Penn Item 687 down to the words "saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ".

In the Parisiṣṭa the third span is "half of that" (tad-ardha-bhāk, four months or half a year, not said) and the cows' release a month (māsiko go-visarge; no variant marked on it, apv:p456), the dawn at once; the Penn copies have three months for the third, a month for the fourth, ten days at dawn and "at once" at the cows' release: the Parisiṣṭa's series and the Penn series share the opening and the eight months, differ from the third span on. The attributions differ: the Parisiṣṭa's chapter names two sources, the appendix of the Śukra-ācāra by Padmayoni and the Svapnādhyāya of Kroṣṭuki taught to Śaunaka (apv, the editors' summary of 68.1); the Penn copies call theirs Bṛhaspati's (pn1871:i4).

Doubt: the editors' sigla (A–E, T, Roth) are the Parisiṣṭa's manuscripts and printings, not the Penn ones; the shared opening is a parallel of wording, not a proof of descent.
