# The sky-bird's days: forty, the kite and she mo

WB vol. 2, pp. 29–30, with two Tibetan dictionaries (the *Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho* and the *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, in the Steinert collection, entries *bya zhag*, *khrums zhag*, *she mo*, *'ol ba*): the sky-bird's days (བྱ་ཞག, bya zhag) are counted after the twenty-fourth day from the winter solstice, and last forty days, not thirty: the mother bird (བྱ་མ, bya ma) 6, the wing's corner 6, the shoulder 3, the neck's hollow (མཇིང་ཁུང, mjing khung) 7, the face 3, the wing's tip 5 (the dictionaries write «གཤོག་རྗེ», WB «གཤོག་རྩེ»), and the chick (བྱ་བུ, bya bu) 10; the dictionaries explain the whole count by the bird's brooding, from the egg until the young are formed. The thirty of WB's text are the first six parts; "in ten days the chick arrives" is the seventh part, so the chick's ten days are days 31–40 of the count, and «སོ་གཉིས་བར་དུ» ("up to 32"), which falls inside them, can only name the 32nd day of the bird's count (two days into the chick's), not a span of 32 days. After the forty come *khrums* (the bird's brooding, 12 days; *khrums zhag bcu gnyis*, the dictionaries) and WB's *shol po* (half a month), *smyung mo* (9) and *she mo* (7); the dictionaries say *she mo* is the time when the *'ol ba* bird arrives, and name *'ol ba* the black kite or mouse-hawk (བྱ་ནེ་ལེ, bya ne le; ཕིང་ཀྱུར་མ, phing kyur ma). So the «འོལ་བ» of p. 28's marks of the season is the kite; chapter 28 puts its coming at the spring equinox. *Smyung mo* is in no dictionary searched; *shol po* is one, but as a tree (*sbyar pa*, willow or poplar; *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, Valby, Ives Waldo), which gives no span of days (question 325, 2026-11-14); *Khrums*, which as a word also names the mansions Bhādrapadā, is here the 12 days of that name, as in the dictionary.
