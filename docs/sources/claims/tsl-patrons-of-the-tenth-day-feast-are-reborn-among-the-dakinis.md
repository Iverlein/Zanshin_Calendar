# Rtsa gsum gling pa's life: those who gave the feast substances when the tenth-day offering was made are born in the dākinīs' palace

In a vision of a dākinī land in his life (vol. 30, image 265, printed p. 254, read on the scan; the sentence's beginning on image 264 in the etext), Rtsa gsum gling pa (rank 2) asks who the people of the great maṇḍala's palace are. The dākinīs answer that they are those who, when the offering of the tenth (ཚེས་བཅུ, tshes bcu) was being made, acted as patrons of good feast substances (ཚོགས་རྫས, tshogs rdzas) and made the aspiration to be born there; the dancing boys are those who danced the feast dance when a maṇḍala of the Vajra vehicle was opened, and prayed with faith.

It is the one place read where the lineage's texts name a fruit of the tenth-day offering for the giver, and the giver is a patron, the part a lay person takes: providing the substances of the feast and making an aspiration. The life is a narrative, not a rule; the benefit named is rebirth among the dākinīs. With tsl.30:i287 (the dākinīs come to the gathering on the tenth and other good occasions) it shows the tenth-day feast offering as the lineage's standing observance.
