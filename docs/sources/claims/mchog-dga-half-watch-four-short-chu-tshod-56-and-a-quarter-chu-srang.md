# The Mchog dga's four chu tshod to a half-watch are short ones of 56¼ chu srang: 4 x 56.25 = 225 = an eighth of the 30-chu-tshod day (RD338, q968)

The Mchog dga' is open: Phug pa Lhun grub rgya mtsho's commentary on the Dbyangs 'char is printed whole in the Kun 'dus chen mo, vol. 3, etext pp. 21-505, and its chapter 9 sets out the half-watch Rāhus on etext pp. 449-454 (printed 429-434). Its words, in Tibetan, are in [svo:p271z](../corpus/svo/svo.md#svo-p271z). What they settle about the four chu tshod:

- **The four is not a round figure and the half-watches are not thirty-two against thirty.** The Mchog dga' says outright that "four and four dbyu gu to a half-watch" (the text calls the unit dbyu gu, and chu tshod where it narrates) is said with an equal day and night in mind, not as a fixed rule: whatever the lengths, eight half-watches by day and eight by night are kept, and the length of each is the day's (or the night's) measure divided by eight, as its chapter 2 explains.
- **The unit is a short chu tshod.** The four are not the usual sixty-to-a-day-and-night chu tshod: the Mchog dga' says the unit is the Kālacakra's 56 and a quarter chu srang taken as one chu tshod, so that it "does not tally" with the sixty-chu-tshod day. In that count a day-and-night holds sixty-four of them (the Karma pa gsum pa's volume 13, etext p. 207, says so in as many words: sixty-four chu tshod and thirty-two moments).
- **The arithmetic.** An equinox's day is 30 ordinary chu tshod, 1800 chu srang. Divided by eight: 225 chu srang, 3 chu tshod 45 chu srang, 3 and three quarters chu tshod. Four short chu tshod: 4 x 56.25 = 225 chu srang. The same. Eight of them: 8 x 225 = 1800 chu srang = 30 ordinary chu tshod, so at the equinox the eight half-watches of the day fill the day exactly and the sixteen of the day-and-night fill the 60. As the sum of short ones: 8 x 4 = 32 short chu tshod by day, 64 in the day-and-night, 64 x 56.25 = 3600 chu srang = 60 ordinary chu tshod. The thirty-two of q968 is thirty-two short chu tshod, not thirty-two ordinary ones. The Kālacakra great commentary's sixteen half-watches of 3 and three quarters chu tshod each ([svo:p271y](../corpus/svo/svo.md#svo-p271y)) give the same length.
- **What the Mchog dga' does not say.** It names no nāga in these pages: its half-watch makers are eight Rāhus, one for each planet that is the day's, one direction each, and the planet-nāga pairs of the Dbyangs 'char verse ([svo:p271w](../corpus/svo/svo.md#svo-p271w)) are not in them. The day's planet gives the starting direction (east for the Sun, other directions for the others, p. 451); by night the order runs the other way round; and a list (p. 453, the verse of p. 454) gives for each day-planet the half-watch in which the fierce Rāhu of Rāhu arises, the verse multiplying the eighth by that place (6, 4, 3, 5, 8, 2, 7 by day; 4, 6, 7, 5, 2, 8, 3 by night).
- **State.** From the etext only; the scan is lent (archive.org) and was not opened. The unit's «ང་དྲུག» is read 56 (the 50 of «ང་» and 6); the sum 4 x 56.25 = 225 = 1800/8 and the 64 of the other work support the reading.
