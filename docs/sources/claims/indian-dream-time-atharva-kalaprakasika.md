# The time of a dream's fruit by the watches of the night: the Atharva-Veda appendix 68 (eight months), the Kālaprakāśikā (six months) and the Svapnohana (RD263)

RD263 (2026-11-09) read the Indian texts that Wayman 1967 reports ([svo-indian-frame-of-watch-rule](svo-indian-frame-of-watch-rule.md)) in the text layers of published editions on the Internet Archive. Two of the three rules of dream time by the watches of the night are in front of us in the original or the translation; they differ from what Wayman reports, and the Svapnohana's spans (svo:p268) turn out to follow the older of the two.

The Atharva-Veda appendix 68, the *Svapnādhyāya* (apv:p445, Bolling and Negelein 1910, three editions agreeing; the chapter names as its sources a text ascribed to Padmayoni and the dream book of Kroṣṭuki taught to Śaunaka), gives five times for the night: a dream of the first watch (*yāma*) ripens in a year (*saṃvatsara-vipākin*), of the second in eight months (*dvitīye 'ṣṭasu māseṣu*), of the third it takes half of that (*tad-ardha-bhāk*: the verse does not say whether half of the eight months or half of the year), at the time the cows are let out (*go-visarga*) in a month (*māsika*), at dawn at once (*sadyaḥ-pāka*, *prabhātika*); "the time is told separately for the five states of the night" (*pañcasv avasthāsu*). The *Kālaprakāśikā* in Iyer's translation (kps:p232-236) has a year, six months, a month, twelve days before dawn and a day before sunrise.

So the "six months" that Wayman gives for the Atharva-Veda's second watch is the *Kālaprakāśikā*'s number: the appendix reads eight months in every witness, including the variant readings of its manuscripts. The Svapnohana (a year, eight months, three months, half a month, ten days at dawn) agrees with the appendix in the number of times (five), in the year and the eight months, and in a third span that is shorter than the second; it parts from the appendix in the later spans (three months and half a month against half of the second and a month) and from the *Kālaprakāśikā* in the second span. None of the three ties its spans to a lunar day, a weekday or a month: they run by the watch of the night alone.

The edition of the *Kālaprakāśikā* printed the Sanskrit above the translation, but the text layers of the four scans read have no Devanagari; its verse was not reached, and the author of the work is not named in the front matter as read. The sentence "of two dreams the later prevails" is in both the appendix (68.2.57) and the *Kālaprakāśikā*.
