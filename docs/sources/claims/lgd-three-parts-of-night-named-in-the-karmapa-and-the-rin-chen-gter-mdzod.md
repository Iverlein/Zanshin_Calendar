# Dusk, midnight and dawn in the third Karmapa's verses and the Rin chen gter mdzod: the same three names, midnight's character differs, no hours

Three older texts name the same three parts of the night as LGD, and none gives them hours or watches (RD355, 2026-11-10, etext; the first is the third Karmapa's verses on dreams, Rang byung rdo rje's collected works vol. 5, etext p. 57, the second the instruction on the four bardos in the Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 27, the third LGD's own page on three copies).

The Karmapa's verses give a saying of Marpa: the dream of dusk (སྲོད, srod) is, in general, habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags); the dream of midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) is the power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang); whatever comes at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) is true. LGD agrees on dusk (habit) and on dawn (the dream of omen, to be examined) and differs on midnight: «གུང» (gung) there is the time of the afflictions (ཉོན་མོངས་པ, nyon mongs pa), here of spirits. The word for midnight differs too, «ནམ་ཕྱེད» against «གུང»; the Rin chen gter mdzod's bardo instruction writes «ནམ་གུང» (nam gung) for the middle of the night and likens it to the dream, dusk to the dullness (གཏི་མུག, gti mug) in which appearance sets, and dawn to the rising of thoughts. So «གུང» is the middle of the night, as the commentary-less names of RST suggest, not noon; and the three names are the common ones of the tradition. LGD's copies (the Rin chen gter mdzod img. 985 by eye, the Sbas-yul manuscript img. 145 and the Ngodrup print img. 307 by Yigdzin-1) write the same three names and add no hour, watch or measure.

For q806: the tradition answered here names thirds of the night, not hours or the four watches of the Svapnohana; none of the three texts counts a span, so what «dusk», «midnight» and «early dawn» cover is not fixed by any text read. A search of BDRC's full text for a span (2026-11-10, the phrases in the open questions' dead ends) found none.
