# The dream verses of Marpa's Hevajra rite are the verses of 'Deceiving Death' chapter 1, not the Hevajra Tantra's (Toh 417, 418 have no dream sign of death) and only in part the Saṃvarodaya's; the rite names no source (RD382)

RD382, q1066. The dream verses that stand in Marpa's Hevajra mandala rite as the bad signs of the empowerment night, from «ལས་ལྡོག་པ་སྤངས་པ་ནི» to «དེ་ནི་ཟླ་བ་བརྒྱད་ན་འཆི» (dds.19:p53-55), are the verses at the end of chapter 1 of Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa's 'Deceiving Death' (Derge Tengyur etext, cbs:p247-249; the Dpe bsdur ma and Mkhyen brtse copies are in the pool as cbd and cbl). Compared sentence by sentence (the etexts' spaces and the rite's page heads taken out), the two agree line for line in the same order: sand or ashes and the temple's post, the donkey going south and not returning, the monkey going south, the ant-hill, the black girl «bound» (the rite «bcings pas 'ching ba yin», the Tengyur «bcings pas 'chi ba ni»), Time-Night and the seven days, the woman in black and the karavīra garland, darkness and precipice and prison, falling from a tree, trees on the body, the monkey rider going east and his «last night», the eaters in a year, the man with an iron staff in three months, red scent in six, red scent or garland or clothes in eight. The differences are spellings («nag» for «gnag», «sprel» spelt out, «ka ra bi ra'i 'phreng ba»), two dropped lines («as before» after the first climbing verse, «his life likewise» after the donkey), and the opening: the Tengyur begins with the kovidāra and karavīra flowers and then «mi ni gang zhig rmi lam mthar», the rite with «ལས་ལྡོག་པ་སྤངས་པ་ནི» (words not understood). The rite is therefore a copy of a version of 'Deceiving Death', not a version of the Hevajra Tantra and not of the Saṃvarodaya.

The Hevajra Tantra has no dream verse of death. The Derge Kanjur etext of Toh 417 and 418 (volume VE4CZ5369_I1KG9206, [p5]-[p62], read by word search and by the places found) has «རྨི་ལམ» in five places, each a simile («like a dream», «like a mute's dream», «the teaching as a dream»), «འཆི་བ» once (of the poison that kills), and none of «དུས་མཚན», «ཟླ་བ་དྲུག», «ཞག་བདུན», «ཟླ་བ་གསུམ», «ལོ་གཅིག», «གཤིན་རྗེ» (in the sense of a death sign) or the lists of figures; BDRC's search of the whole Kanjur etext for the lines «བུ་མོ་ནག་མོ་རབ་འཁྲོས་ནས», «ཞག་བདུན་ནས་ནི་འཆི་བྱེད་ཡིན» and «ཀ་ར་བི་རའི་འཕྲེང་བ་རྨིས» finds them in the Tengyur's 'Deceiving Death', its Mkhyen brtse copy, the Marpa and 'Bri lugs rites and the Gdams ngag mdzod teaching, in no Kanjur volume. Snellgrove's Sanskrit of the Hevajra was not opened.

Of the rite's signs, the Saṃvarodaya's chapter 19 (dds.25:p580-583) has the sand and ashes and temple post, the donkey and monkey going south with the ant-hill and the dust-heap, the woman in black as Time-Night (see the Sanskrit claim), the eaters in a year and the red clothes in six months; the rest, and every number of days and months beyond these, is 'Deceiving Death''s. The same borrowing runs the other way for the body signs: 'Deceiving Death' chapter 1 has the Saṃvarodaya's verses on the pierced soles, the heart and throat (two and a half months), the black semen of the first of the waxing fortnight, the five-streamed urine and the shadow with no head or hands, reworked (Derge etext VE23703_1344, [p241]-[p247]). That the Tengyur text is the later of the two (and made from the tantra) is what a modern study, as quoted by a secondary summary of Schneider's 2010 edition (pp. 44-45), says; the quotation was not opened and the order is not established here. Its Tibetan translation, by Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna and Rin chen bzang po, is put in 1042/3 by Schneider's conjecture according to Szántó (2020, p. 171, open access); the Saṃvarodaya's Tibetan was made by the Indian abbot named «gzhan la phan pa mtha' yas» and the translator Rgyus Bande Smon lam grags and checked on a manuscript of Vanaratna by the translator Gzhon nu dpal (colophon, Derge etext [p621], Toh 373).

The rite names no source for the verses. Its colophon (dds.19:p95-96) says it was «begun» at Lho brag Chu khyer, that the instruction was found among Nāropa's pupils, and a second note, after the colophon, names «the venerable Rngog» as telling of a Lho pa instruction and an old copy changed in a few words; whether this is the lineage of Marpa's pupil Rngog is not said. Tsong kha pa and the Vajrāvalī rite name the Sdom 'byung for the three verses they quote (dds.16:p11-13, dds.17:p392-393), which are the ones that are in the Saṃvarodaya.
