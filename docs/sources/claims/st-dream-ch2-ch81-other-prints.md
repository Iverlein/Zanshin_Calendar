# The boy's dream (ch. 2) and the nāga illness dream list (ch. 81) on the Zhol and Derge prints and in the Drikung commentary

Ch. 2, the line «ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་» (the body is light, and if in her dreams, clear or plural, she sees a man: a boy). The Zhol print (BDRC I5910, 2280 px, six lines a page) has it on img. 44 and the Derge (I00JW501163, 7504 px) on img. 28 (the print's folio 26); Yigdzin-1 reads the line on both exactly as the Serthang scan has it. By eye on line 4 of each page: Derge at 1.6×, ཡང (ཡ over ང), རྨི་ལམ and དག (ད over ག) clear (good); Zhol at 5× on a bilevel scan, ཡང and དག legible (moderate). With the Serthang and the Drathang that makes four prints with the same letters, and on none of them does ཡང read as «also» by letter; དག stays plural or «clear» by sense (q789 and q818 stay open). The Drikung commentary's etext (MW1KG24729, I1KG24731, p. 64270 on) quotes the line with the same words; no print or commentary read so far has a variant. The Chag-po-ri print I1KG13487 holds the chapter on img. 35–36 (the conception and the months), but its reader text of img. 36 breaks off before the birth lines and the dense six lines were not read by eye: not settled on that print.

Ch. 81, the dream list. The Zhol print has the whole list on one page, img. 483 (five lines a page, from «རྨི་ལམ་ཆུ་དང་ཤིང་དང་ནིའུ་གསིང་དང་» to «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས», the next lines on img. 484). Yigdzin-1 on it: ནིའུ་གསིང, སྦལ་ལྗོངས (a ས after ལྗོང, where the Serthang scan has སྦལ་ལྗོང), ཉ་སྦྲུལ (not ལྕོལ: the Serthang etext's ལྕོལ is an OCR slip, as RD227 found on the scan), འབུ་སྦྲང, སྡིག་སྡོམ, ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ (བཟོ as the Serthang print, not སོ) and ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ (ལྷས as printed). The Zhol glyphs on this page are crowded and the cut is bilevel: at 3× the syllables of the list were not told apart by eye, so these are the machine's readings only. The Derge and the Chag-po-ri copy of ch. 81 were not reached (the Zhol's is img. 483 of 806 images; by proportion the Derge's would be near img. 440 and the Chag-po-ri's near img. 442, not looked at).

The Drikung commentary (Chos kyi grags pa, the Four Tantras' difficult points, etext MW1KG24729, I1KG24731 p. 816403 ff., a 2014 edition: facts only, no wording) has the same list with ནིའུ [ནེའུ] གསིང, ཉ་སྦྲུལ, སྡིག་སྡོམ and ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ, and sorts it by what each dream marks: water, trees and the ni'u gsing for being «put in hand» (གཏད་དུ་ཚུད་པ, the sense of the phrase not settled); frogs and tadpoles, fish and snakes, insects and bees, scorpion and spider for the general sign of the flesh-eating spirits (ཤ་ཟ, sha za); sticking to the body, sitting in a crevice and being chased by yaks, dzo and beasts of prey for a spirit (གདོན, gdon); narrow doors and passes and falling into a pit for being pressed down; arrows, eggs and a temple shaking for a male spirit (ཕོ་གདོན) and women drawing out the body's essence for a female one (མོ་གདོན). «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» has no gloss of its own there. So the creatures are the water-dwellers and the small flesh-eaters' creatures, which answers q817's first half and the nature of the four words of q791 in sense (tadpoles, frogs, fish, snakes, insects, scorpion, spider), but not the grammar of the last clause. The Grwa pa mngon shes etext (MW16778, 1982) has the list as the Serthang etext does.
