# The circles: the twelfth, the golden vase, for stored goods

The twelfth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the golden vase, is read for gold, silver, copper, iron, turquoise: stored goods. Its starting point: the pig of the friend element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: drawn from the pig of the friend element of the one reckoned: whether grease has settled on the golden king of mountains; whether the mirror is stained with rust and dirt; whether the turquoise lake of great light is breached; whether newcomers have entered the inlaid jewelled bowl («ཡོལ་གོ»); whether the hundred-bird and hundred-thousand-bird copper eye is blind; whether the cord of the fortune-turquoise's eye is cut; whether the hanging copper pot is rolled into the hearth.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the golden mirror free of stain: food, wealth and enjoyments from before; now goods spent are an inexhaustible treasure. | the mirror tarnished with rust and dirt: bad; dry goods decline; or for the sake of one shining thing, a *chags 'dre* or *'gong po* spirit follows. | press down the *god sri* and recite the *Nor rgyun*. |
| Body | no fence entered round the turquoise lake of great light: good; the body adorned with turquoise and ornaments; ornaments, clothes and enjoyments grow: good. | the fence entered, the mouth of fortune breached, the shining things lost to outsiders: bad. | summon fortune and accomplish the god of wealth, restore glory and expel downward («ཐུར་འབུད»). |
| Power | no new fence entered in the inlaid bowl: good; the father's consecrated goods enjoyed by the son; wealth from every side gathers: good. | entered: though father and mother had enjoyments, the son lets them decline; whatever he does, a sign of poverty: dry goods and fortune decline. | put the power-raising wheel in the storeroom; a fortune rite with offerings at the hearth is good. |
| Luck | the eye-cord of the fortune-turquoise not cut: dry goods good; a famous line from before; the son more famous than the father; dry goods spent cannot be exhausted. | the white silk eye-cord cut: bad; a shining jewel slips to others. | restore the luck and press down the *chung sri*; recite the *Stobs po che* and do the fumigation and clearing. |
| Mewa | the hanging copper pot not rolled into the hearth: whether held before or come now, la and fortune gather in the shining wealth; dry goods and stored wealth grow; the treasury fills without hoarding. | rolled into the hearth: bad; wealth with fortune or food with nourishment slips to others, fortune declines; the records feared lost to others, or great loss of wealth. | turn back the white and black flaring mouths («ཁ་འབར»), summon fortune fiercely. If it meets the nine-eye it is grave: be careful. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
