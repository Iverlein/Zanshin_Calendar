# The chapter's colophon: what the author draws from Nor bzang rgya mtsho and what he settles himself

The chapter closes with the author's own words (vol. 1, p. 189), after the work's full title (*Phug lugs rtsis kyi legs bshad mkhas pa'i mgul rgyan bai ḍūr dkar po'i do shal dpyod ldan snying nor*): the solstice wheel is taken from Nor bzang rgya mtsho's wheel of the *mda' ro*, whose single cell does the work of sixty wheels; the solstice, the sage and pig days, the comet with its omens and the golden pillar (*gtsug gser*) are put together from the author's own experience and a few new settlements of hard points; the earlier reckoners of Chinese or Indian descent did not put the Tibetan reckoning base to such use, so the elapsed years are kept few; and the making of the sage's and pig's days, reckoned from the present eight (*rab brgyad*), is seen to be a little confused, which is also settled.
