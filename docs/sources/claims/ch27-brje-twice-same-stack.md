# Ch. 27: «བརྗེ» twice in its set, the same stack on both scans, the begging verse a syllable short

In the reckonings in four (WB vol. 2, p. 23), the word «བརྗེ» stands twice in the second set, and both times it is the same stack on both scans: in the set's head «དེ་བཞིན་བརྗེའི་རྩིས་སྐོར་བཞི» (l. 22, the head marked with dots below the line) and in the power's black verse «ནག་ནང་བརྗེ་གྲོང་སྤྲང་བྱེད» (l. 25). On the 1996 scan (I1KG12907 img. 31, enlarged 9 to 10 times) the two stacks are a ra over a ja with the vowel e and match stroke for stroke; on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 29, 3.5 to 5 times) likewise. No other reading of the letters (a ba, a tsa, a u) is on either scan.

The black verse has six syllables, «ནག་ནང་བརྗེ་གྲོང་སྤྲང་བྱེད», where every other verse of the set has seven (its white verse «མ་ཞིག་བསྙེན་བཀུར་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས», the next black verse «ནག་ན་བདུད་མོའི་བར་ཆད་འབྱུང»), on both scans. Read as it stands, «བརྗེ» is the one who goes begging in the villages, the subject of the verse and so the set's person. A syllable may have dropped, or «ནང» stand for «ན» with a syllable lost: the scans do not say which.

Question 365 is narrowed so far: the word is «བརྗེ» on both scans and in both places, and in the second it names the person of the set. What person «བརྗེ» spells, the monk (བཙུན, btsun) of the chapter's close «སྔགས་བཙུན» or the spiritual friend (དགེ་བའི་བཤེས, dge ba'i bshes) of Zhechen Gyaltsab's list, is still not shown.
