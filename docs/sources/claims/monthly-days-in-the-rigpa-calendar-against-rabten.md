# The monthly days of the Rigpa students' calendar are Karma chags med's list: six days against Edition Rabten's five

The calendar that students of Rigpa publish after Rigpa's printed Tibetan calendar (tibetanbuddhistcalendar.org, read in the browser on 2026-11-16; festivals.md, *What Nyingma houses' calendars mark*) marks seven things on six days of every month. They are the buddhas and deities of Karma chags med's verse (rcz:p286-288), day for day:

| Day | The calendar's mark | RCZ | Edition Rabten, now in the app (SPEC §5.8) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8th | Medicine Buddha day and Tārā day | the Medicine Buddha comes; White Tārā guards the field | a day for the eight precepts |
| 10th | Guru Rinpoche day | Padmasambhava and the heroes and ḍākinīs | a day for the feast (ཚོགས, tshogs) |
| 15th | Amitābha Buddha day | the time of Amitābha | the full moon: the eight precepts, the monks' confession |
| 25th | Ḍākinī day | "the tenth of the waning half", the same deities as the 10th | a day for the feast |
| 29th | the Dharma protectors' day | all the Protector's kind roam | — |
| 30th | (Śākyamuni) Buddha day | Śākyamuni with the thousand buddhas | the new moon: the eight precepts, the monks' confession |

What differs from Rabten's five days: the 29th, the protectors' day, is a sixth day; every day has a buddha or deity where Rabten names a vow or a rite; the fifteenth is Amitābha's and the new moon Śākyamuni's, RCZ's order and not the one DTN reports (dtn:i316). The note in the Larung print of Longchenpa's commentary counts the same six days, the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th, 29th and the new moon, as the tantric practitioner's "six times" (lcb:p568). When a date is skipped the calendar moves the mark to the next day, and when a date is doubled it stands on the first; it says it follows the Men-Tsee-Khang in this.

- Days: the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th, 29th and 30th of every lunar month.
- Practice: named only by the day's buddha or deity; the calendar's day page gives a praise of that buddha.
- For whom: any practitioner; the calendar speaks to followers of all four schools.
- Tradition: a calendar of a Nyingma community, with a list whose written form is a Karma Kagyu master's (RCZ).

The printed Rigpa calendar itself was not seen; its own page names sojong, Tārā days, Guru Rinpoche days and the great festivals among what it marks.
