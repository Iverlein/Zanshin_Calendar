# What WB does not say: no day of the month is set apart for the restoring rite, the one-day vow, the feast or the tenth-day offering

In the two volumes of the White Beryl (rank 1) no passage found sets a day of the month apart for a religious observance of its own. The BDRC etext of both volumes was searched for the restoring rite (གསོ་སྦྱོང, gso sbyong), the one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas), the fast (སྨྱུང་གནས, smyung gnas), the feast gathering (ཚོགས་འཁོར, tshogs 'khor), the feast offering (ཚོགས་མཆོད, tshogs mchod), the offering of the times (དུས་མཆོད, dus mchod), the good occasions (དུས་བཟང, dus bzang) and the tenth (ཚེས་བཅུ, tshes bcu); a search of an etext can miss a word the OCR broke, so the finding is "not found", not "proven absent".

What the hits are:

- The restoring rite and the fast occur once, as a remedy and not on a date: among the remedies for the harm of the twelve vowels, "for a day or a month do the restoring rite, the Amoghapāśa fast" (wb.2:p305-307).
- The one-day vow occurs as a condition of a work and as a trait of character: the maker of an image is to hold vows or to have taken the one-day vow (wb.2:p291-294); Mañjuśrī's words ask the reckoner to keep a layman's vows or the one-day vow (wb.2:p449); a birth under the twelfth mansion "delights in the one-day vow"; a remedy of an obstacle year is to change the name and keep the one-day vow's restraint. None names a day.
- The good occasions occur in the rule of merit (claim wb-merit-hundredfold-on-ordinary-good-occasions-eclipses-above) and in ch. 32's rites for the earth lords, where offerings are made "on good occasions" without a date (wb.2:p291-294).
- The tenth occurs as Padmasambhava's birth date in the chronology of ch. 3, the tenth of the monkey month of a wood-monkey year at sunrise (wb.1:p23), and in reckonings; no offering or feast is tied to it.
- The eighth and the twenty-fifth occur once with a religious fruit, in ch. 34's list for bathing: on the eighth of the months of Kṛttikā and Mṛgaśiras sins are cleansed in particular, and on the twenty-fifth of "rgyal rta" wisdom grows (wb.2:p404, the last lines, read again on the scan on 2026-11-16). It is a rule for the work of bathing, not a day of practice.
- The thirty dates of ch. 33 (wb.2:p297-302) list works good and bad on each date, religious works among them (claim wb-religious-works-by-lunar-date), and call the 8th, 15th, 22nd and 30th the four perilous times of the month, on which falling ill is dangerous and great gatherings and grand or forceful undertakings are bad. The 8th is perilous because Indra holds a feast of Dharma among the gods on it.

So WB gives the calendar's festivals (claim wb-great-occasions-written-at-the-month-heads), the multiplying of merit at eclipses and on good occasions it does not list, and works by date; the observance days of the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th and 30th as days of vows, feast and offering are not in it.
