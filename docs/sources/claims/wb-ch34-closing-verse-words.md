# Ch. 34's closing verse: «ཨ་འཐས» is a word, «དར་གུད» rise and decline, «དམར་ནག» is not in the chapter

«ཨ་འཐས» (a thas) is a word of its own: Tshig mdzod chen mo, Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho, the dag yig gsar bsgrigs and the Golden Mirror of terms (brda dkrol gser gyi me long) have the headword, 'to take as solid and true, clinging to the real as hard'; Rangjung Yeshe and Valby 'fixation, grasping at things as real'; the dictionaries' own example is «ཨ་འཐས་བདེན་ཞེན». So «ཨ་འཐས་བདེན་ཞེན་འཆིང་བྲལ་བ» is 'freed from the bonds of clinging to things as solid and true', which fits the line before it. «དར་གུད» (dar gud) is rise and decline: the dag yig gsar bsgrigs lists it with «གྱོང་གུད» as a use of «གུད» (to fall off, wear away); the 1996 etext's notes (p. 491, etext only) say of a black red-mark «ཁ་དམར་ནག་ན་གུད་ཟེར», that it is called 'gud', decline. On «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» (khyad par du) the dictionaries give only 'especially, in particular, chiefly' (Rangjung Yeshe, Hopkins, Valby): it picks out the last member of the list; no source gives it a rank or a weight. «དམར་ནག» (dmar nag, dark red) does not occur as a term in the 1996 etext between p. 386 and p. 436 except in the colophon itself (locate.py find), so no work of ch. 34 is shown to carry the colophon's red-and-black kinds under that name; whether the colophon means the red-black verdict of the reckonings or something taken from the Svarodaya is not told. «ས་རོད་ཡའི་རྒྱུད» reads as svarodaya by sound only; no dictionary or etext found has the tantra under that spelling.
