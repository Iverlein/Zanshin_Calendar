# The in-laws' trigrams: daughter-in-law and mother-in-law

The trigram point of the match (WB vol. 1, p. 362) says the in-laws'
trigrams fighting is bad («…་སྤར་ཁ་འཐབ་ན་ངན»). Mipham's notes (vol. 2,
p. 526) say who they are: «གནས་མ», that is the daughter-in-law (མནའ་མ,
mna' ma), and the son's mother, the mother-in-law: when the birth trigrams
of these two meet as the demon's cut (བདུད་གཅོད, bdud gcod) and the like
and fight, it is bad.

So the bride's birth trigram is tried against the groom's mother's, on the
pairs the spouses' own trigrams are tried on (the demon's cut "and the
like"); WB gives no rite for it at this place.

The spelling of the second syllable, «སྐྱུག» or «སྒྱུག», is not settled
on the scans: its stack is filled with ink in both impressions of the 1996
typesetting (WB p. 362, l. 15; the notes p. 526, ll. 2–3), blurred in the
Zhol print, and in Mipham's own print of the notes the subjoined letter
looks like ka rather than ga at a low resolution; ch. 30 prints «གནས་སྒྱུག»
(vol. 2, p. 152). The person meant is the same either way.
