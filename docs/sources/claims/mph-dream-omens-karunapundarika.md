# Dream images with readings in Mipham's Śākyamuni compilation: the omen that comes before (sngar ltas)

Where Mipham does give dream images a meaning, it is in the Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka stories he retells in his Śākyamuni compilation (mph.101:i42-43, mph.101:i44-45, mph.101:i46, mph.101:i220-221, mph.101:i233): a dream sign is an "omen that comes before" (སྔ་ལྟས, sngar ltas), the Buddha, "one skilled in the examination of dream signs" (རྨི་ལྟས་ཀྱི་དཔྱད་པ་ལ་མཁས་པ) or a "sign-man" (མཚན་མཁན, mtshan mkhan) reads it image by image, and the reading is a prophecy of what will come to the dreamer or to others. Examples read: lotuses and sun-rays entering the mouth, a body growing large and clear: prophecy to awakening; sitting on filth, vomiting filth, striking the sky with the tongue, sitting on a lotus, climbing a peak, many bowing: becoming king; a pig's face, blood, being eaten and growing again: the long suffering of those who seek the kingdom of gods and men; losing the eyes and their growing again, a house carried off and set up again, a crest taken and given back: someone will beg the king's body.

These are narrative dreams of a sūtra, with their readings, and they are not a table of signs; no hour of the night, no day and no season is named, the sūtra's own name (Snying rje pad ma dkar po'i mdo) is given by the compilation on its images 32 and 159, and the dreams are not tied to a practice. Dreams on three successive nights occur once (mph.101:i233) and the "sign-man" answers each time that it is so. Machine and etext readings; the scan was not read by eye. The compilation has more dreams (images 162, 356, 386, 398) not read here.
