# The Svapna-īkṣā: the three parts of the night and what each holds

Advayavajra's *Svapna-īkṣā* divides the night into three parts (ཆ, cha) and gives each a role, for the practitioner who has lain down on a kuśa seat with fragrant grass for a pillow, a bound jātaka garland, a mantra recited over a young girl's milk and the eyes anointed: in the first part the habitual traces (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) are stirred; in the second the hungry ghosts (ཡི་དྭགས, yi dwags) walk; in the last the dream is examined and one lies down (p. 261). So the first two parts are not read, and the third is the part where the dream counts. The text gives no time of fulfilment for any dream, in any part: no span, no day, no month. It does not number the parts as watches (ཐུན, thun); the word is not used.
