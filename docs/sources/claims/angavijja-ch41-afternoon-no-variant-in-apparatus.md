# The Aṅgavijjā's apparatus at chs. 41, 42 and 53 has no note on the bright beginning's time of day; the afternoon of ch. 41 is the print's reading in all the editor's manuscripts (RD312)

The apparatus under the three pages of Puṇyavijaya's print that hold the six-fold list of the part of the day (ch. 41 p. 185, ch. 42 p. 190, ch. 53 p. 210) was read at full size: it has no note on "avaraṇhesu", the word that stands for the bright beginning in ch. 41 where chs. 42 and 53 have "puvvaṇhe". The editor records the variants of his seven manuscripts (Haṃ, Ta, Saṃ, a Patan manuscript of the Loṃbaḍī-pāḍā store whose letter the text layer garbles, Mo, the editor's own, and Si) under the text, "Haṃ. Ta. vinā" meaning "in the others except Haṃ and Ta"; a word on which he records nothing reads, by his practice, as the print has it in them all. So the afternoon of ch. 41 is not a slip of one scribe of one manuscript that the editor passed over: it is the reading of the manuscripts he used, or an uncorrected agreement, since the apparatus gives only what differs. It remains that two of the three lists give the forenoon, that ch. 41's gives the afternoon twice (for the bright beginning and the bright ends) and no forenoon, and that the lists differ in the middle (midday in chs. 41 and 42, midnight in ch. 53). Which is the original wording is not settled from the print; a collation of the manuscripts at ch. 41 p. 185 line 28 is what would settle it.

The one note that does bear on the bright beginning (note 10 at p. 185 line 29) concerns another word, "ādimūlīyesu": the manuscripts other than Haṃ and Ta read "ādimūlesu", the form ch. 42 prints.
