# No Chinese counterpart of the table 夢をみてかたらぬ日 found; two neighbouring Chinese rules

No Chinese counterpart of the Edo table 夢をみてかたらぬ日 (twelve months, each with two groups of day branches, closing "of dreams on these days neither good nor bad is told") was found in the Chinese texts read in RD375. What was found is two things near it: the Dunhuang rule that a bad dream is not told on waking and, left untold for three days, becomes treasure (cdb:i13-16), and the almanac's per-day lists of months for the omen of a dream seen on that day (cdb:box1). Neither has the table's shape or its content, and neither says what the slash in the Edo cells divides, so q785 stays open for the Chinese side; q786 and q916 are not touched (they ask about Japanese prints). The silence rule and the day-and-month dream omens are two Chinese strands that a Japanese compiler could have joined; no Chinese text read does so. Texts checked without a rule of this kind are listed in the section's Searched and not found.
