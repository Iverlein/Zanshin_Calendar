# In the oath-bound Great Man's rite the god who comes alongside and the demon born together are the victim's two attendants (q1006)

In the rite of killing in the cycle of the oath-bound Great Man (Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38, lgd.38:p13) the enemy aimed at is parted from «the god who comes alongside» (མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་ལྷ, mnyam por 'ong ba'i lha), and «the demon born together» (ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད, lhan cig skyes pa'i bdud) is turned into the oath-bound's messenger who draws out his life and heart. The pair are the victim's two attendants, a god to protect him and a demon to bring him down; the same pairing stands in a second rite, the lower rite of the Tshe sgrub gsang ba kun 'dus: «protecting god, be released now; hand him over to the demon who comes alongside; entrust him to the lord of death of inescapable karma» (BDRC snippet in Wylie, VE1ER121). The words are crossed in the two kinds of text: here the god is the one «who comes alongside» and the demon the one «born together», while LGD p. 970 has «the god born together with oneself» and «the demon who comes alongside» (lgd:p970).

So the use of the rites of killing is that of a person's attendant pair, not Chöd's self-clinging; but the two senses do not exclude each other, since Chöd's gloss (the demon who comes alongside is one's own co-born ignorance, the karmic spirit) is the same companion seen from within. Which of the two LGD means is not shown by the rite: LGD sets the pair beside «the tricks of outer gods and spirits in tumult» in a list of what disturbs dreams, and it names no rite of killing.
