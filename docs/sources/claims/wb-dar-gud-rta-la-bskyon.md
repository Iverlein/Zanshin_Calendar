# The «ཏ» of «དར་གུད་ཏ་ལ་བསྐྱོན» is the horse (རྟ)

P. 334's «དར་གུད་ཏ་ལ་བསྐྱོན་ནས་བརྩི» (wb.1:p334) is printed so on the 1996 typesetting and on the Gser thang reprint (BDRC's full-text search for the string finds exactly these two, 2026-11-01), but the Zhol print reads «དར་གུད་རྟ་ལ་བསྐྱོན་ནས་བརྩི» (wbz:i433, by eye, the stacked རྟ), and so does the manuscript copy of WB in the *Bod lugs gso rig rtsa che'i dpe rnying kun btus* (MW2PD17386_D19206, search snippet «dar gud rta la bskyon nas brtsi»). So «ཏ» is a slip for «རྟ», the horse, and the clause is the same as the page's own closing «བཅུ་གཉིས་རྟ་ལ་བསྐྱོན་ནས་བརྟག», "examine them mounted on the twelve horses": rise and decline is reckoned by mounting each pebble on the horses, the twelve animals, as the rest of the page does with the hundred. «བསྐྱོན» is the verb "to mount" (to set on a horse). Nothing is said of what exactly rides on which; the page's two clauses are one instruction.
