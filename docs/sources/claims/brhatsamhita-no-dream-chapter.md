# Varāhamihira's Bṛhatsaṃhitā and Yogayātrā have no chapter on dreams (RD263)

Varāhamihira's *Bṛhatsaṃhitā* has no chapter on dreams: the English translation of Iyer (1884, bsm:p8-12) was searched whole for "dream" and the Sanskrit *Yogayātrā* (yyt:p188) for *svapna*. In the *Bṛhatsaṃhitā* reading dreams is a skill of the king's astrologer (ch. 2), the evil of bad dreams is said to vanish when the astrologer's reckoning of the moon's course is heard (ch. 2 v. 23), and in the chapter on the king's bath at Puṣya (Iyer's ch. XLVII) Varāhamihira says that the dreams dreamt on the night spent at the spot are explained in his *Yogayātrā*. The *Yogayātrā*, which has no dream chapter either, says only, at the end of its thirteenth chapter on omens at departure, that the omens told there hold also in dreams.

Nothing in the three places gives the time of a dream's fruit or ties a dream to a lunar day, a weekday, a month or a season; the "dream chapter of Varāhamihira's *Bṛhatsaṃhitā*" that the row of RD263 expected does not exist. The Atharva-Veda appendix is the Indian text that does (apv:p445, [indian-dream-time-atharva-kalaprakasika](indian-dream-time-atharva-kalaprakasika.md)).
