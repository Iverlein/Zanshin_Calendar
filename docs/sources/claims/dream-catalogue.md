# The dream images: every image with each text's verdict, WB first (RD216)

The catalogue of the dream images, built from the passages RD59, RD207–RD212 read, so that the picker of block 73 (SPEC section S2) can be made without reading the corpus again. One claim per image (`claims/dream-<name>.md`) names the texts that read it and each text's verdict with its passage and the passage's state; this file indexes them. It keeps no net verdict: no text sums images, and nothing here calls a dream good or bad as a whole.

**Order inside a claim.** WB ch. 30 first (rank 1, the app's `MomentSign.DREAM` rows are its thirteen groups, never copied); then the Four Tantras' rows (ST), marked as readings for a sick dreamer; then WB ch. 25's lines on the sick, under ST where both read one dream (claim dream-st-above-wb25); then the rank-3 texts, which no source outranks: LGD (scan), SVO, SVI, SKP, RST, KBS, RRT, the scan before the machine before the etext.

**State of each passage** is carried into every row: scan (every quoted syllable read on the page image), machine (Yigdzin-1 only, the layout checked by eye), etext (the printed etext, not read on a scan). SVO's and SVI's passages are etext or machine; RD207's pp. 268–269 are etext only, p. 270–271 machine, and SVI is etext only. SKP's two texts, KCM and DTD are machine; RST and RRT are etext; KBS is an article quoting Klong rdol's verses (machine); his own text is RST (rst-is-klong-rdols-dream-text: the same verses, ACIP etext). WB ch. 30 p. 144 is scan; the Four Tantras' dream passages are scan, except ch. 7's list of the bad dreams of the sick, which is the Drathang print (gt:i42-43, a machine reading, the lines read by eye in part: RD268), whose rows are marked M (the five unsettled places of the verse read by eye on the Chag-po-ri prints, RD270: gt-dream-verse-chag-po-ri-prints), and the commentaries' glosses on it (etext); WB ch. 25 pp. 428–429 scan, pp. 429–433 machine except the four dream lines (pp. 428, 430, 430–431, 431), read on the scan (RD230); LGD is scan except its rite pp. 961–966 (machine); the Palri Parkhang print of LGD (machine) agrees with it in every verdict and differs in a few words (lgd-palri-print-against-rd208).

**Cells of the table at the end:** the tone (G good, B bad, X mixed or turning, N a sign only) and the state (S scan, M machine, E etext), `?` where the verdict rests on an unsettled word. Texts that give an image more than one row show each.

**Texts with no image rows.** KCM and DTD are rites for asking a dream (KCM's prophecy sign is the one row); RBG is King Kṛki's eighteen dreams, no method (claim rbg-eighteen-dreams-of-the-latter-times); KDL has no dream in it (kdl-no-dream-rule); MDP's dream part is not reachable (RD221); SKP's second text repeats RST's verses (skp-second-text-is-the-rst-verses) and adds no rows. Time rules, which are RD215's and RD209's and, for the Indian texts, RD263's, RD265's and RD269's, are not repeated image by image: they are in the table "The watch rules by text" at the end of this file, and in dream-manuals-time-rules, svo-watches-and-spans, lgd-dream-times-of-night, kbs-three-thirds-of-the-night and st-dream-night-first-part-dawn.

**Rules that are not images:** dream-mixed-order, dream-spirit-classes, dream-practice-signs, dream-st-above-wb25; the remedy per text once: dream-remedies; the images on an unsettled word: dream-unsettled-words.

**RD274 (2026-11-09)** brought this catalogue in line with RD227–RD273: the Four Tantras' ch. 7 list of the bad dreams of the sick (gt-dream-death-images) now has an image claim or a row in the claim of its image for every dream of the verse (13 new claims, 11 claims with a new Four Tantras row); the creature names, the clay statues, the camel, the centipede and the other words RD227, RD255, RD264 and RD271 read are written into their image claims and out of dream-unsettled-words; the Svapnohana's "horse" of the seized creatures is a centipede; the pregnant woman's dream of a man is a sign of a boy, not of illness; the table of the watch rules by text is new.

## The watch rules by text (RD274)

For the night chips of S2: which text divides the night how, what it says of the dream's fruit, and in which print and state the rule stands. **No text read fixes a watch or a third by the clock**; WB counts four watches by night and three in one list of the night's names but bounds none (night-watches), and no text ties a span to a lunar day, a weekday, a nakṣatra or a month: the only clock is the part of the night, the hour of dawn and the hour of the cows' release (indian-dream-spans-by-watch-nine-texts). WB ch. 30 gives no rule of the watch for a dream. The Four Tantras' rule is first, on medicine; the Tibetan manuals follow, then the Svapnohana and the Indian texts it belongs with. "Cows" is the hour of the cows' release (*go-visarga*); "dawn" the hour before sunrise.

Notes for the chips. (1) The Tibetan rules are of two kinds: a rule of which part counts (the Four Tantras' first part and dawn; LGD's and RST's three thirds; DTD's last of four) and a rule of how long the fruit takes (the Svapnohana and its Indian relatives); the Four Tantras give no span and the three-thirds rule none, and the Svapnohana does not say that the earlier dream is empty, so the two kinds are not read as one rule. (2) All of them make dawn the surest time. A four-chip night (the Svapnohana's) and a three-chip night (LGD, RST, KBS) need different bounds; none of the texts gives them. (3) Where two dreams come in a night, the later counts, and after a good dream one does not sleep again (the Atharva appendix, the Agni, the Matsya, the Bṛhadyātrā, the Svapnacintāmaṇi, the Kālaprakāśikā; the Brahmavaivarta: sleep after a good dream loses its fruit). (4) The Adbhutasāgara (adb:p513-515) quotes the Parāśara series (a year, half a year, three months, a month), the Bṛhadyātrā verse and the Matsya and Viṣṇudharmottara verse, and adds no series of its own; the Aṅgavijjā and the Bṛhadyātrā tie the *kind* of dream to the season and the fortnight of its seeing, not the span ([angavijja-dream-time-fortnight-season](angavijja-dream-time-fortnight-season.md)). (5) Doubts: the Matsya, the Agni and the Brahmavaivarta each rest on one print; the Atharva appendix and the Svapnohana's own line were not read again on the images by RD269; the modern Tibetan books that quote the Svapnohana (svo-modern-tibetan-quotations) tie dawn to the tiger's time, a reader's reading and not the text's.
