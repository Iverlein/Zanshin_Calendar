# The labels at the end of the classes of p. 425: colour of a colour, and the sunny and shady sides

The lines of WB p. 425 that name each class by a pair of colours stand at the end of the rites of the class they follow, and name the class's place as the colour of a colour. As the print gives them, class by class:

| Class (counts of the fifteen, notes) | Words at the end of its rites |
| --- | --- |
| a third more white | none; its rites end «དཀོན་ཅོག་གསུམ་པོ་མཆོད་རྣམས་སོ» |
| a fourth more white | «ལྔ་སུམ་གཅིག་གི་ནད་རྣམས་ཞི། ཅུང་ཟད་ཁྲ་དང་དཀར་ཁྲ་ཡན» (a little speckled, and white-speckled upward) |
| a fifth more white | «དཀར་པོའི་ནག་པོ» at its head: the black of the white |
| a sixth more white | «འདི་ཡང་དཀར་པོའི་ནག་གཅིག་འགྲོ»: this too goes as the one black of the white |
| white and black half | «རྡེལ་དཀར་ཁྲ་བོའི་དཀར་པོ་ཡིན»: the white pebbles, the white of the speckled |
| a sixth more black | «ཁྲ་བོའི་ཁྲ་བོ་ཡིན» after its first rites, the speckled of the speckled; «ཁྲ་བོའི་ནག» after the second, the black of the speckled |
| a fifth more black | «ནག་པོའི་དཀར»: the white of the black |
| a fourth, a third more black | none |

So the labels do grade the classes by a second colour inside the first: white of white, black of white, white of speckled, speckled of speckled, black of speckled, white of black. Three zones (white, speckled, black) each with three grades would give nine, as many as the classes; the labels fill only six of the nine places and cover seven of the nine classes, and the text does not say that this is the scheme, so it is not taken as one. The notes (wb.2:p495-496) give no word on the labels.

The two lines of p. 425, l. 6–7 are read with the help of a word from WB's ch. 26: «གདགས་སྲིབས» is the sunny and shady sides of the ground (wb.1:p462-463, the garuḍa-head point's sixth pebble: black, «གདགས་སྲིབས་གོ་ལོག»: reversed, bad for the local lord and house owner). So «ནད་པའི་ལུས་རྡེལ་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀར། གདགས་སྲིབས་གོ་ལོག་ཐང་ཤིང་འབྲས། མ་བབས་གཏོ་ནག་བཅོས་ཁ་བསྒྱུར» says: the sick person's body pebbles very white, yet the sunny and shady sides turned about, the plank-tree's result has not come down: change the treatment with the black ransom. The notes (wb.2:p495-496) say only "the body pebbles very white is not good either". «ལྔ་སུམ་གཅིག་གི་ནད» (the fourth-white rite's last line but one) is glossed nowhere; its three numbers may be the fifth, third and one of something, and no text found names what. All words read on the scan (reprint img. 420, rows 6–11), as RD41 had them.
