# The Edo sixty-day cells against the Yongle dadian's day omens (eighteen days) and P.3908's branch chapter: no agreement beyond chance

Only eighteen of the sixty days can be set beside the Yongle dadian: the surviving volumes of its entry 日 that give the day-by-day lines are 20181 and 20182 (甲辰 to 辛酉); the other volumes of the entry that survive (20121, 20122, 20139: 諸家選日 7, 8, 25, and 20197: 83) are arranged by kind of work (building, earth-breaking, marriage, nine palaces) and not by day, and have no line of dreams (夢 occurs 0 times in 20121, 20122 and 20139, and in 20197 only in stories of dreams); the volumes with the days 甲子 to 癸卯 and 壬戌 to 癸亥 are lost. The table sets for each of the eighteen days the Yongle dadian's 占夢 omen (雷英發's lines, cdb:box1), the Edo cell of the 六甲の夢うらなひ (mdz:i426556b) and the line of P.3908's chapter 19 for the day's branch (cdb:i13-16). The tone, good (+), bad (-) or none/mixed (0), is my coding of each wording and is only a way to count; cells of the Edo table read in part are marked (part).

Counted by tone, on the fifteen days where both sides are not "0": Yongle and Edo agree on 7 (丁未, 甲寅, 乙卯, 丁巳, 戊午, 己未, 庚申) and differ on 8 (甲辰, 乙巳, 丙午, 戊申, 己酉, 壬子, 癸丑, 辛酉); three days (庚戌, 辛亥, 丙辰) cannot be coded. Edo against P.3908's branch line: agree on 9 (甲辰, 丙午, 丁巳, 戊午, 己未, 壬子, 癸丑, 甲寅, 乙卯), differ on 6 (乙巳, 丁未, 戊申, 庚申, 己酉, 辛酉), and three are uncoded. Both counts are what chance would give from a coin, and no Edo cell repeats a Yongle omen in kind (mourning, fire, a stove, a traveller, a letter against the Edo person, direction and outcome) or the P.3908 branch line: the Edo cell names a quarter and a person, which neither Chinese table does, and the Edo cells of one branch differ (the five 申 days give a guest, a marriage, a relation, a quiet, good news) where P.3908 gives one line for the branch. The Edo table does not follow the Yongle day lists or P.3908's branch chapter, by direction, by person or by outcome. Doubts: the Yongle omens were checked on the page images for 甲辰, 丙午, 丁未 and 辛亥 only, the others come from the Wikisource transcription; the tone coding is mine; seven Edo cells are partly read; the other forty-two days could not be compared because the Chinese text does not survive.
