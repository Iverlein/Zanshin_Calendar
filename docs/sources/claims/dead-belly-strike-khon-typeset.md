# «ལུག་ལོ་ཁོ་ན་ཤི» in the notes is «ཁོན་ཤི»: the belly strike's eight

In the eight great strikes of repetition (WB vol. 2, p. 86) the first, the demon's belly strike (བདུད་ཀྱི་ཁོག་འགྲམ, bdud kyi khog 'gram), is a death on the trigram of the dead's own sign; the root text's «ལུག་ལོ་ཁོན་ལ་ཤི་བ་སོགས» says "a sheep year dying on *khon*, and the rest", and Mipham's note (p. 506, etext and the Gser thang reprint's etext, which BDRC's search finds spelled alike) prints «ལུག་ལོ་ཁོ་ན་ཤི་སོགས་བརྒྱད» ("... the sheep year *kho na* [*khon*] dying, and the rest, eight"). «ཁོ་ན» is a typesetting of «ཁོན» (*khon*, the south-west trigram): the note is counting the eight deaths of each sign on its own trigram (*li*: horse and snake, *khon*: sheep, and so on), the eight of the root's own list, which «ཁོ་ན», "alone", does not give, and the note goes on to the nine more deaths of the strike when the trigram has fallen on *li*, nineteen pebbles in all. The root text, LT (p. 59, «ལུག་ལོ་ཁོན་ལ་ཤི་བ་སོགས») and the note's own context agree. Answered.
