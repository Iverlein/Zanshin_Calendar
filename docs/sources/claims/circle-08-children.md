# The circles: the eighth, the blazing jewel, for children

The eighth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the blazing jewel, is read for small children. Its starting point: the tiger of the enemy element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: drawn upward from the tiger of the enemy element of the one reckoned: whether the swastika fence is broken at the waist; whether the golden fish has come out onto dry land; whether the little garment has been carried off by water; whether the children's playground is empty; whether the toy bow and arrow (སེ་མདའ་སེ་གཞུ, se mda' se gzhu) are broken.
