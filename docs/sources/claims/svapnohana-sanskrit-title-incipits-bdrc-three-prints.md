# The Sanskrit title line of the Svapnohana in BDRC's three print records, and what svapna + ūhana gives (q834, q890, q994, q1088)

BDRC's work records (purl.bdrc.io/resource/<ID>.ttl, read 2026-11-14) carry the title line after «རྒྱ་གར་སྐད་དུ» as an *incipit title*, a transcription of the print's own first line, not a Sanskrit title of the catalogue: Derge MW23703_1749 «s+wab+nA u hA na» (language tag sa-x-ewts; the stack ba, བྣ), Narthang MW2KG5015_1416 «s+wap+nA u hA nA» (pa, པྣ), Peking MW1KG13126_2621 «swap+na u hA nA» (pa, པྣ; the record has no language tag). The Peking record's neighbour MW1KG13126_2620 (the Deceiving Death) shows the cataloguers copy the print's Sanskrit with its faults («mri tu sany+dzA no pa de sha»), so the incipits are readings of the prints, not corrections. Two of the three give pa.

The Sanskrit reads as *svapna* (dream) + *ūhana*: Monier-Williams has ūha «consideration, deliberation, examination, supposition, inference» and ūhana «deliberation, reasoning» (Cologne MW, entries 223,2, read 2026-11-14); a + ū makes o, *svapn-ohana*, «examination of dreams», the Tibetan title rmi lam brtag pa (examining dreams) rendered by sense. The third stack is ཨུ (u) for the o of the sandhi, the fourth ཧཱ (hā), the fifth ན or ནཱ. *Svapnoddhāra* would need a doubled dh and a long ā after it; the print's stack after ཨུ is ཧ, so it is not that. The stack with pa (པྣ = pna) is the one that spells *svapna*; ba (བྣ) gives no Sanskrit word and is read as a slip of the Derge, which agrees with the Derge colophon «སྭབྣཱ» of the pool.

This fixes the Sanskrit reading; it does not read the Peking glyph, which the scans at 3200 px leave undecided (q1088). 84000 lists Toh 1749 with the title *Svapnohana* (claim svo-sanskrit-original-not-named-in-indian-sources).

Also found: the colophon field of the Narthang etext record (MW2KG5015_1416) and of the Peking record (MW1KG13126_2621) reads «rmi lam ngan pa bzlog pa rdzogs so» before the Vibhūticandra line, where the Derge record has «rmi lam brtag pa rdzogs so»; the pool's eye reading of the Narthang manuscript (img. 287 line 3) and the Peking print (img. 318 line 6) has «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་རྫོགས་སོ». Either the cataloguers' colophon is run into the title of the Turning back of the bad dream (a different work, MW1KG13126_3608 and MW23703_2787, by Ajitamitragupta, translated by Gnubs Byams pa'i dpal bzang, with a colophon of its own), or the two prints differ from the Derge; unsettled, scan-bound.
