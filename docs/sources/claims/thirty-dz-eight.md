# The thirty: the Moonbeams' eight pebbles, without the progressed year

The *Moonbeams* (DZ ch. 2, img. 033–034) lays a starting point's eight pebbles this way, without any count through the years of the one reckoned:

1. Take the point's pair; if the one reckoned was born in a male year, start from the pair's male year, if in a female year, from its female year.
2. From that year count four places up and four down, seven up and seven down, eleven up and eleven down, and mark each place counted upward with a white pebble and each counted downward with a black one.
3. The eight fighting pebbles: the life force of the fourth up and of the fourth down, each fought by the reckoned person's life force; the body of the seventh up and down, by his body; the power of the eleventh up and down, by his power; the luck of the pair's own two years, male and female, by his luck.
4. 30 points × 8 = 240 pebbles; the verdict by the commentary *The Magic Sieve* (འཕྲུལ་གྱི་མིག་ཚགས, 'phrul gyi mig tshags), the one WB's colophon keeps (vol. 1, p. 472).

WB (vol. 1, p. 453) counts first from the pair's year as many places as the years of the one reckoned to a progressed year (ལོག་མེན, log men) and fights from there: life force at the fourth up and the seventh up, body at the fourth down and the seventh down, power at the eleventh up, luck at the eleventh down, and the progressed year's own power and luck. The *Moonbeams*' way is the one the notes report from "elsewhere", fighting "without turning the progressed year" (vol. 2, p. 533; an inference from the description, the notes name no text). It differs from WB in where the count starts and in which aspect each place carries, so it is not a source for WB's way; it shows only a way in which the eight pebbles come as four pairs of one aspect each, up and down (question 306).
