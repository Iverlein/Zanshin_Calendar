# Dreams as the distant, uncertain signs of death, and the remedy by grade

How the Four Tantras rank dreams among the signs of death, and what is done for each grade.

The Explanatory Tantra (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud), ch. 7, on the signs of the body's destruction, closes by sorting the signs of death by their nearness. Dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and changes of bearing are the distant and uncertain signs: they are turned back by gathering merit, by recitation and by the practice of the yidam deity (ཡི་དམ, yi dam). The near and certain signs call for the death ransom (འཆི་བསླུ, 'chi bslu). For the very near signs nothing turns them back. The text gives the reason for learning the signs: without them the choice of what to take up and give up in treatment is blocked. This is the only place in the pool where the Four Tantras say of a dream as such what is done; the dream is not named by its image or its watch of the night, and nothing ties it to a date. The chapter is the seventh by its colophon, p. 34 of the print (img. 44); the etext's «ལས» for «ལྟས» in the line on the very near signs is a misprint of the etext (st:p34).

What the two commentaries add (RD229, 2026-11-09; the etext only). Byang pa (`bjp:p197`) repeats the remedies: merit, recitation and the yidam made threefold turn the distant signs back, and for the near ones the death ransom, tsha tsha and the ransom of the la (བླ་བསླུ, bla bslu) are done. Rin spungs pa (`rsp:p229-230`) names the dreams that count (bad ones, seen by an ordinary person or a sick one) and gives each kind of person his means: the practitioner generates the yidam, recites, rests in the great seal, gives the aggregates as a meal to the six classes and gives away what he holds dear; the layman offers to the Three Jewels, gives alms, reads the Buddha's word and does the long-life rite and the yidam's approach; for the very near signs there is nothing to turn back and one purifies the veil by good accumulation.

What the three commentaries add on the dreams themselves (RD255, 2026-11-09; etext only). Chapter 7 does not stop at the closing lines: before them the Explanatory Tantra lists the bad dreams of the sick that show the lord of death has them (riding a cat, monkey, tiger, fox or corpse, or a buffalo, horse, pig, donkey naked to the south; a tree or lotus growing from the heart; falling into a pit, lying in a charnel ground, the head cut off; entering the womb again, sinking in mud; drinking with the dead, dancing with the dead in red clothes), gives their cause, says who dies of them, and names six kinds of dream. These verses are quoted and glossed in Byang pa (`bjp:p177-181`), Rin spungs pa (`rsp:p208-211`) and Khro ru (`ktr:p303-307`, a modern author); the Serthang pages of the verses themselves are not yet read in the pool. The 'Bri gung *Dka' mchan* (Chos kyi grags pa, 1595–1659; `bdk:p53-54`, `bdk:p57`) has the same verses with small words worked in, and the same rule, in a few lines. The four agree on the rule. Such dreams are seen because the illness blocks the opening of the mind (ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག, yid 'jug bu ga); the sick who dream them constantly die before long; for the healthy the outcome is not certain, and even for the sick, if the white-side rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro), gathering merit, reading and meditation are done as a means of turning back, the harm is escaped. A sixfold division follows: dreams of what was seen, heard or felt, of a prayer to a chosen deity, of what comes true as dreamt, and of illness; a dream of the early or middle night is forgotten and has no fruit, a clear dawn dream unbroken by sleep surely gives its fruit (Khro ru; Byang pa and Rin spungs pa quote *Moonbeams* for the same). Byang pa and Rin spungs pa add, from *Moonbeams*, the illness each bad dream points to (blood and bile, a great swelling, a tumour, leprosy, urine disease, madness, blindness). Rin spungs pa's verse reads «རྨི་ལམ་མི་ཟད་པ» (dreams that are not used up) where Byang pa, Khro ru and the 'Bri gung quotation read «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ» (not good).

The close of the chapter in Khro ru (`ktr:p323-324`) repeats the grades of remedy as the other two do: dreams, long drawn-out changes of bearing and uncertain signs are turned back by merit, reading, the practice of the chosen deity and the yidam threefold beyond the former; the near and certain signs by the death ransom and the life rites, which may turn death back though it is sure; the very near sign, the stages of dissolving seen in fact, by nothing, for the life's projecting force is used up.
