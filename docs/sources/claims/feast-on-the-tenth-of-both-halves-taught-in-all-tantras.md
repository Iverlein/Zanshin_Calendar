# Dpal sprul: the feast gathering on the tenth of the waxing and of the waning half is taught in all the tantras, new and old

Dpal sprul (dpk.1:p380-381) says that to hold the feast gathering (ཚོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ, tshogs kyi 'khor lo), "the festival of the heroes and heroines", on the tenth of the waxing half and on the tenth of the waning half, that is the twenty-fifth, is taught in all the tantra classes of the new and the old translations, and gives three authorities: the Root Tantra of Cakrasaṃvara (བདེ་མཆོག་རྩ་རྒྱུད, bde mchog rtsa rgyud), "on the tenth of the waning half and the tenth of the waxing half of the month, quickly, with effort, make the offering"; the Yoginīsañcāra (རྣལ་འབྱོར་ཀུན་སྤྱོད, rnal 'byor kun spyod), "on the tenth of the months, the middle winter month and the rest, make great offering"; and a verse of Longchenpa, "on the tenth, in the day and the night of the waxing and the waning half, strive in approach and accomplishment, the feast offering, fulfilment and confession". From these he draws that the daytime of the waxing tenth and the night of the waning tenth are great occasions, because the special accomplishments come swiftly then.

- Days: the 10th (by day) and the 25th (by night) of every lunar month.
- Practice: the feast gathering; with Longchenpa's verse also approach and accomplishment, fulfilment and confession (བསྐང་བཤགས, bskang bshags); for the followers of the Guru, on the waxing tenth the periodic offering and prayer before his image.
- For whom: "those who follow the Guru", without a division into lay and ordained; the text is written for all who honour the Guru's images.
- Benefit named: benefit beyond measure of the prayer on the waxing tenth; swift special accomplishments on both tenths.
- Tradition: stated as common to the new and the old schools, by a Nyingma author; given as teaching of the tantras and strong advice ("of certain importance"), not as a counted commitment.

Read in the etext only (an OCR of the typeset page); the scan is on archive.org and was not opened, and the three quotations were not traced to the tantras or to Longchenpa's work. The passage does not call the twenty-fifth "the day of the dākinīs".
