# མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད in Machik's Chöd and Kongtrul: the demon that arrives as a companion, one's own self-clinging

«མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» (mnyam por 'ong ba'i bdud, the demon who comes alongside) is glossed by the texts themselves as the demon of self-clinging, one's own co-born ignorance (RD307, 2026-11-10; BDRC's exact-string search and the etexts' windows; no scan opened, so the quotations stand as etext).

**Machik's Chöd, the gloss in so many words.** The life of Pha Dampa Sangye and Machik Labdron (Gser thang etext I3CN4779, etext pp. 392–393) and the Chöd instruction that repeats it (I1KG24707, etext pp. 153–154; BDRC's search finds the same sentence in nine Chöd and Machik texts) divide the harmful into the three outer (enemies who appear, hindrances who harm, circumstances that cut in) and the inner ones, the first of which is the karmic spirit (ལས་གདོན, las gdon): «ལས་གདོན་ཞེས་པ་ནི། ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་མ་རིག་པའམ། ངའོ་སྙམ་པའི་བདག་འཛིན་ནམ། མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཅེས་སམ། མིང་གང་ལྟར་བཏགས་ཀྱང་…» — "the karmic spirit is the co-born ignorance (ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་མ་རིག་པ, lhan cig skyes pa'i ma rig pa), or the self-clinging that thinks «I am» (ངའོ་སྙམ་པའི་བདག་འཛིན, nga'o snyam pa'i bdag 'dzin), or the one called the demon who comes alongside; whatever name it is given, it does not let one reach liberation, it is the root that makes all faults and lies hidden in the subtle mind from beginningless time, setting one to bad deeds alone". A further Chöd instruction (the Nye brgyud khrid yig, *Legs bshad bdud rtsi'i…*, a BDRC snippet only) puts it from the other side: that which, among the kinds of demon, sets the mind only to bad deeds from beginningless time «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཅེས་ཀྱང་བྱ་སྟེ། དེ་ནི་ལས་ཀྱི་གདོན་ཡིན།» is also called the demon who comes alongside; it is the karmic spirit.

**Kongtrul's Bima la'i zab tig (I1KG13062, etext p. 364), Mkhyen brtse's collected works (the same sentence, MW3JT13384).** The stain that spoils, being no other than awareness itself, is «སྔར་ནས་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ་ལྟར་མཉམ་འགྲོགས་ཀྱི་ཚུལ་དུ་བྱུང་བ» — "as the so-called demon who comes alongside from the first, it has arisen in the manner of a companion (མཉམ་འགྲོགས, mnyam 'grogs)", and is known as the one self (བདག་ཉིད་གཅིག་པ) and the co-born ignorance. So the name means the demon that arrives as a companion, and the texts' own explanation of «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་མ་རིག་པ» is the same thing.

**What it settles for LGD p. 970.** The pair of the line «རང་དང་ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ་དང༔ མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་དང༔» is therefore the god and the demon each person is born with: the Chöd gloss makes the second one's own ignorance and self-clinging, in the figure of a companion; the folk figure (lgd-co-born-god-and-companion-demon-keep-white-and-black-pebbles) makes it the one who keeps the black count of one's sins. These are not two different beings: the one figure is taught in the two registers, as a doctrine in Chöd and as a person in the death narrative. LGD itself glosses neither. Rank: strong that the name is the Chöd's «self-clinging» demon; moderate that LGD's author meant that sense and not the narrative's, since LGD sets it beside the outer spirits' tricks (so a cause among the causes of dreams, not a thing to be cut).

**Not the same as ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད.** The co-born demon of the Chöd's lists (ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད, lhan cig skyes pa'i bdud) was not asked for again; the phrase in the Machik passage is «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་མ་རིག་པ» (co-born ignorance), and the demon is named «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད», so Machik's text itself writes the second name, not the first.
