# The measure of ch. 18 set out: the two chains, 32 against 30, and the Kālacakra and Indian units beside it (RD378)

The measure in ch. 18 (Derge etext pp. 438-439) gives two chains and a count, and they can be set out as one scale; the tantra itself does not set them together, so what follows is arithmetic on its words, not its statement.

- Chain of the se gol: 100 se gol (སེ་གོལ) make 1 rtsa rgyun (རྩ་རྒྱུན); 4 rtsa rgyun make 1 chu tshod (ཆུ་ཚོད); 4 chu tshod make 1 watch (ཐུན). So a chu tshod is 400 se gol and a watch 1600.
- Chain of the eye: 10 openings and closings of the eye make 1 skad cig (སྐད་ཅིག); 10 skad cig make 1 yud tsam (ཡུད་ཙམ); 4 yud tsam make 1 watch. So a yud tsam is 100 blinks and a watch 400.
- Eight watches (four by day, four by night) make the day-and-night: 8 x 4 = 32 chu tshod, or 32 yud tsam, or 3200 blinks, or 12800 se gol.
- The line on p. 438 before the measure says the yud tsam are thirty to the day-and-night. 32 against 30: the same ratio as 16 against 15, a watch of 3 and three quarters yud tsam (30/8 = 3.75) against the watch of four.
- If both chains give the same watch, then one chu tshod equals one yud tsam (400 se gol = 100 blinks), so one blink is four se gol; the text calls the eye's opening and the se gol's swift pace two things, so a se gol finer than a blink is likely, but it is not said.
- Every figure has a counterpart in the pool's Kālacakra and Hevajra books: the Hevajra and Kālacakra books count 32 water-hours (chu tshod) to the day-and-night and a watch of four (claim thun-eight-watches-of-the-day-and-night-in-hevajra-kalacakra-books); the Kālacakra's short chu tshod of 56 and a quarter chu srang are 64 to the day-and-night with 32 yud tsam, two chu tshod to a yud tsam (Karma pa gsum pa vol. 13, in svo:p271z), a watch being 8 short chu tshod or 4 yud tsam; the ordinary count has 60 chu tshod and 30 yud tsam to the day-and-night (the same passage). So "four chu tshod a watch" is the 32-count, "four yud tsam" the 32 of the short count and "thirty yud tsam" the ordinary count.
- Against the Indian units: a ghaṭikā or nāḍikā is 1/60 of the day-and-night (24 minutes), a muhūrta 1/30 (two ghaṭikā, 48 minutes) and a yāma or prahara 1/8 (3 hours, 7.5 ghaṭikā, 3.75 muhūrta) in the Sūrya Siddhānta's chain as the English Wikipedia lists it (read 2026-11-10, a second-hand source; Manusmṛti 1.64 is cited there for 30 muhūrtas to the day-and-night). So the tantra's yud tsam as one thirtieth is the muhūrta, and a watch of four yud tsam is the yāma rounded to a whole number; its chu tshod cannot be the ghaṭikā (a yāma holds 7.5 of those, not four), it is instead the same length as its yud tsam, about 45 minutes in an equal day and night (24 h / 32).
