# The Kalacakra Vimalaprabha divides its eight watches at midnight and noon, the shape of the Cakrasamvara commentary's clause; no text names the start of the water-hours (q1149)

In the Kālacakra commentary Vimalaprabhā (Derge etext VE23703_1328, svo:i538) the eight watches (ཐུན་ཚོད, thun tshod) of the day and night are told as four "from midnight to noon" (མཚན་མོ་ཕྱེད་ནས་ཉི་མ་གུང་གི་བར་དུ) and four "from noon to the end of midnight" (ཉི་མ་གུང་ནས་མཚན་མོ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་མཐར་ཐུག་པར), the first half going east, south, west, north, the second the other way. That is the shape of the Cakrasaṃvara commentary's clause (svo:i525: in the sun's part, "from midnight for as long as there is sun", four; likewise in the moon's, four): the first four watches there are midnight to noon, and the commentary borrows the Hevajra's measure of the water-hour (svo:i523) in the same breath. So the Cakrasaṃvara commentary's own wording leaves open whether the first watch begins at midnight, but the Kālacakra commentary, a Tengyur commentary that counts the same way, puts the dividing points at midnight and noon.

The caution: on p. 546 of the same Vimalaprabhā the watches are numbered from noon (the seventh lies after midnight, the eighth ends at sunrise), so the ordinal of a watch and the point from which the halves run are two things in that text. Neither the Vimalaprabhā passages nor the Cakrasaṃvara commentary names a moment from which the sixty water-hours (chu tshod) of a day and night are counted. Open: that start.
