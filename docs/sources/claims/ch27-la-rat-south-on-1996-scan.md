# Ch. 27: the rat's la goes south («ལྷོར») on the 1996 print's scan

In the wandering of the la, the life-spirit (བླ, bla), by the year (WB vol. 2, p. 16, l. 23), the rat verse «བྱི་བའི་ལོ་པ་བླ་ལྷོར་འཁྱམས» reads «ལྷོར», to the south, on the 1996 print's own scan (I1KG12907 img. 24, the line enlarged 8 to 14 times). Set side by side at 12 times, the stack after «བླ» has the shape of the pig verse's «ལྷོ» in «བླ་ནི་ལྷོ་ཡི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འཁྱམས» two lines below: the letter ལ above, a lower letter hooked at the left with a falling tail, and the vowel o. It is unlike the «ལྟ» of «བལྟས» on l. 6 of the same page. Yigdzin-1 reads «ལྷོར» on this scan. On the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 22) the same stack is a solid blot at 6 times, as RD62 found; on the Taikhang (I5878) it was not told either (RD57).

So the letter is settled on the 1996 print as far as its scan allows, and the four verses stand on every print read as rat south, snake north, horse north, pig south. Question 363 is left with the sense alone: whether the tradition means the two pairs so, or one of each is a carving error that all the prints share.
