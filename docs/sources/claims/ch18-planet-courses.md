# A planet's crooked or backward course through the mansions: what follows (ch. 18)

After the planets' lists the chapter tells what follows when a planet goes through the mansions by a crooked or a backward course. Told in general: a backward course gives no rain, a dry year and the death of those wounded by weapons; a crooked course from a mansion harms the people and trades that mansion governs (from Gre the crops, from Hasta and Citrā lions and venomous snakes, from Viśākhā and Anurādhā kidney disease and the snakes); between Ārdrā and Maghā fighting, famine and disease; from Aśvinī, Dhaniṣṭhā and Mūla a turn back harms merchants, doctors, boatmen and creatures of the water; Mercury turning back gives either great rain or none with famine; a swift course makes Tibet uneasy. The subjects of the clauses are not always stated in the text and the machine reading of the mansion names was found unreliable in places; the planets' courses are not to be built on from this passage before it is read letter by letter on the scan.
