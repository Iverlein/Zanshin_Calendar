# Wangkhyi's close read on the scan, and the animal's day in other manuals

The close of the Wangkhyi paragraph on p. 210 reads on the scan, in three steps: on the days of the four last months of the seasons one does not face the bad direction; in particular not on the grave days (དུར་ཉི); and most of all one holds the day-time of where he stays (གནས་པའི་ཉི་དུས). In the Bhutan collection's «གླང་གི་ཉི་མར» the animal's name before «ཉི་མ» is the day of that animal (a rite on the ox day), and its table of the year's lords writes «ཝང་ཁྱི་འབྲུག་ཉི་དུས» as one cell. So the «གླང་གི་ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» of Wangkhyi's summer line is most likely the ox day and its time, not an ox hour of every day of the month; no text glosses «ཉི་དུས», no copy of WB reads otherwise, and the ox-hour-of-every-day reading is not excluded. Question 29's answer stays as the earlier claim has it (wb-wangkhyi-day-time), now with the closing lines read on the scan.
