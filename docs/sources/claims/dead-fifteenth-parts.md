# The dead's fifteenth examination: its parts and what selects each

WB's fifteenth examination of the dead (vol. 2, ch. 29, pp. 128–142) reckons
nothing new: it is the rites that close the reckoning, chosen by what the
earlier examinations and the dead's own data give. Its size (p. 51, read on
the scan for RD16) is 58 pebbles: 4 result pebbles, and 54 identifying ones,
the trigram's provisions 8, the provisions "above" 11, searching for the
ground 23, the preliminaries 12. Mipham's notes (p. 522) name the parts.

What selects each part:

- **The grave provisions** (དུར་རྫོངས, dur rdzongs): the dead's trigram
  (གཤིན་སྤར, gshin spar; the notes' word) selects one of eight blocks
  (pp. 129–132); each block holds the same eleven entries, the eleven of the
  size ([dead-grave-provisions-by-trigram](dead-grave-provisions-by-trigram.md)).
  A general address (pp. 128–129) and a common ending (p. 132) frame it.
- **Searching for the ground** (ས་འཚོལ, sa 'tshol, pp. 133–135): one
  sequence for every death; its tools and the remedies of the year's
  trigram, planet and mansion, "whichever they are", each reckoned from its
  form (notes, p. 522); the elements' gods asked to cut illness off from the
  kin of their element ([dead-ground-elements-kin](dead-ground-elements-kin.md)).
- **The five graves** (pp. 135–137): the kind of grave, found by the eighth
  examination (pp. 74–75), selects one chant: the great (old) grave,
  mountain, water, fire, earth, or the new iron *bsgrub* grave
  ([dead-five-graves-lands](dead-five-graves-lands.md)).
- **The small grave** (pp. 137–138): used when the dead is a child
  (སྲིའུ, sri'u), to call it back to the same parents.
- **Digging the chamber and opening the dead's door** (pp. 138–139): for
  every grave; the chamber's cleansing is not needed for a new grave.
- **Cutting the joined hands of the living and the dead** (pp. 139–140):
  for every death; closed by road omens
  ([dead-joined-hands-omens](dead-joined-hands-omens.md)).
- **Calling back the fortune** (བཀྲ་གཡང་འགུགས, bkra g.yang 'gugs,
  pp. 141–142): for an old person who died with fortune (བཀྲ་རྒན, bkra
  rgan: long life, spouse living, grandchildren seen).

None of the parts has a verdict of its own; each is a rite to be done,
chosen by the dead's trigram, the kind of grave, and whether the dead is a
child or an old person with fortune.
