# The black small feet: the table's own pattern and its three misfits

WB vol. 2, pp. 38–39: the black small feet pair each year animal with a season-month, a time of day and a link. Read as a table, the months of the dragon to the pig run one after the other from the middle of summer to the last of winter (dragon mid-summer, snake last summer, horse first autumn, sheep mid-autumn, monkey last autumn, bird first winter, dog mid-winter, pig last winter), and the tiger's «last spring» fits the same run: the n-th year animal (rat 1 … pig 12) takes the n-th month counted from the first spring month, by the Chinese count of the chapter. By that rule the rat should have the first spring month, the ox the middle one and the hare the first month of summer; the print has the rat «middle spring», the ox «last spring» (the same as the tiger) and the hare «first spring». The three misfits are the ones the question named (no year with first summer, the tiger repeating the ox), and the run's own order makes them printer's or copyist's slips, but the rule is a reading of the table's pattern, not a statement of WB's or Mipham's, and the blocks carve the print as it stands.

The links run backwards from ignorance at the rat through the pig's formation: rat ignorance, ox aging and death, tiger birth, hare becoming, dragon grasping, snake craving, horse feeling, sheep contact, monkey the six sense fields, bird name and form, dog consciousness, pig formation. By that order the ox's printed «ཀ་ཤི» stands for aging and death (རྒ་ཤི, rga shi) and the snake's «རེག» (contact), which the sheep already has, for craving (སྲེད་པ, sred pa). The times follow the day, dawn to dusk; for the hare's «རྡེལ་འགོ་དྲོས» the places between «the sun reaches the valleys» and noon are the morning warmth, and p. 38's own words for the morning are «འབལ་དྲོས» (first warmth) and «དྲོས་ཆེན» (great warmth, glossed by the dictionaries as the time before midday): the hare's time is a morning warmth; the exact word stays open.
