# The Brahmavaivarta's Kāṇva "book of merit" is not traced to a known Vedic book; the Purāṇa uses the Kāṇva branch beside the Sāmaveda (RD291)

The Brahmavaivarta's line that the good dream is set out "in the Kāṇva branch, in the lovely book of merit" of the Sāmaveda (skd:p470-471) names no book that is known by that title; RD291 (2026-11-10) found none in the sources open to it.

- **The Purāṇa itself uses the Kāṇva branch as a source of rites and hymns, and joins it with the Sāmaveda.** In the Basu translation (1920, the Sacred Books of the Hindus vol. XXIV; Internet Archive item mmxy_the-scared-books-of-hindus-the-brahma-vaivarta-puranam-by-major-b-d-basu-volume-, text layer) the Prakṛtikhaṇḍa gives the meditation, worship or hymn of Sarasvatī, of the Earth and of Dakṣiṇā "as narrated by" or "as stated by" the Kāṇva branch (the text layer's lines 7989, 8195, 9168, 9181, 16836), and the Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa has a gopīs' month-long vow and a hymn "of the Kāṇva branch" (Jivananda 1888, Internet Archive item xnrj_brahmavaivarta-purana-volume-2-jibananda-vidyasagara-1888-jivananda-vidyasagar-calcutta, text layer, which does not mark its pages; the Sanskrit reads, in a poor layer, "kaṇvaśākhoktām iṣṭaṃ ca vrataṃ" and, the next verse, "sāmavedokta-dhyānena dhyātvā kṛṣṇaṃ parātparam", then "kaṇvaśākhoktastotreṇa tuṣṭuvuḥ parameśvarīm"). So the Purāṇa treats the Kāṇva branch, in its own usage, as one whose sayings go with the Sāmavedic meditation, though the Kāṇva branch is by the usual tradition the White Yajurveda's (Kāṇva Śākhā, Wikipedia).
- **The "book of merit" (puṇyakāṇḍa) is not the name of a known Veda section.** No catalogue, edition or study open to RD291 names a *puṇyakāṇḍa* of the Kāṇva branch; the Purāṇic line is a claim of authority for the chapter, as the Purāṇa also claims it for its other chapters by "as told by the Kāṇva branch". What the line means is not settled; it is not a reference RD291 could trace to a Vedic text.
- Unread: Ganga Sagar Rai's article on the schools of the Sāmaveda in the Purāṇas (Purāṇa Bulletin 8, 1966, 115–134, named in Rocher's bibliography) and Bonazzoli's studies of the Purāṇa's attitude to the Vedas (Purāṇa 17, 1975); neither is open.

Doubt: the Sanskrit lines come from a text layer that is poor in Devanagari and was not read on the page images; the English is Basu's, a translation, not the Sanskrit.
