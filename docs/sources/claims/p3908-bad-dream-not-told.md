# P.3908: a bad dream is not told on waking, and not told for three days turns to treasure; bad dreams only, no day or month

The Dunhuang dream book P.3908 ends with a chapter on driving off bad dreams (攘惡夢章, chapter 23) that holds the one rule of silence found in a Chinese dream book: a person who has a bad dream in the night is not to tell it to anyone when rising in the morning, but with a pure mind writes an ink charm, puts it under the foot of the bed where no one knows, and recites a spell; and the chapter states that a bad dream not told for three days turns into pearls and treasures (夫人惡夢三日不說化為珠寶). The rule is about bad dreams only, runs from the waking hour and has no table of months or days: it does not say, as the table 夢を見て語らざる日 of the Edo almanacs does, that a dream of either kind (善悪ともに) seen on certain days is not told. The manuscript is a Tang-to-Five-Dynasties booklet; its date is not settled on the page.
