# UT's chase upward and downward is not WB's up-three count; the same sign-pair harms

# UT's chase upward and downward is not WB's «ཡར་གསུམ» count, but its year-store pairs name the same harms as p. 172's four counts

For question 529. What UT (etext pp. 224-226, printed pp. 202-204) calls the chase upward and the chase downward (ཡར་དེད, མར་དེད) numbers the fifteen places of its board: one's own place first, then the child, the woman, the father's and the uncle's brothers and the rest, each with what must not fall on it; the downward chase is the same places in a second order. It counts no years from a landing. WB's «ཡར་གསུམ་སོགས» (notes p. 537; the counts of p. 172, wb.2:p172-173) are the counts three up, three down, eight up and eight down from the sign where a count has landed, along the sign circle. So they are not one count read twice.

What they share is the harms and their sign pairs. UT's third chase gives each place a year-store, a pair of opposed signs with a harm: ox and sheep, the heap and the lawsuit (the second place); the bird and hare, separation (the fourth); the dog and dragon, the sky door (the fifth); the pig and snake, the slayer (the sixth); tiger and monkey, the seven corners (the third) (ut:p224-226). WB p. 172's counts end in the same names: three down, ox or sheep landing: the heap and the lawsuit («འཕུང་གྱོད»); eight up, dog or dragon: the sky door breaks; eight down, pig or snake: the earth door breaks, "to be counted as the sky door"; three up, bird or monkey: separation (wb.2:p172-173; machine text of the 1996 print, which the etext of the print agrees with). The pairs agree for the ox-sheep and dog-dragon, the harm of the bird for separation and the pig-snake's door agree in kind; UT puts the slayer where WB has the earth door. So the two texts draw on one set of sign-pair harms, UT keying them to the board's places by number and WB to counts from a landing; and Sprugtön's reckoning has the same four counts, five up and down and eight up and down, as the rope and the stake and the two distances (wb.2:p22-23), whose rite UT gives just before the chase («the sky's rope cut», «the earth's stake pulled out»).

So the answer to the question as asked: not the same count; the two use the same sign-pair harms. Which of the notes' «ཡར་གསུམ» counts is the one that lands on UT's third or fourth place is not told by either text.
