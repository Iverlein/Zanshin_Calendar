# WB ch. 13: the three sme ba of each year and the wind-horse element, years 1–30

The right half of WB's table of the sixty years (pp. 105, 107, 109, years 1 to 30) has, after the year's row, the element of the wind-horse (ཀླུང་རྟ, klung rta) and three sme ba (སྨེ་ཕྲེང, sme phreng) rings, headed first, second and third; the remaining columns hold words and a three-part column of numbers with merged cells that were not read.

Read on the scan row by row, the digits obey a single rule. In year n of the cycle (n = 1 for the first year, རབ་བྱུང) the three numbers are the first, second and third of three sequences that each fall by one every year, going from 1 to 9 again: year 1 has 7, 1, 4; year 2 has 6, 9, 3; year 3 has 5, 8, 2; year 4 has 4, 7, 1; year 5 has 3, 6, 9; year 9 has 8, 2, 5; year 19 has 7, 1, 4 again; year 30 has 5, 8, 2. The first number is ((8 − n) mod 9), the second ((2 − n) mod 9), the third ((5 − n) mod 9), each read as 1 to 9. The element of the wind-horse goes round a cycle of four, fire, wood, water, iron, with fire for years 1, 5, 9 and so on and iron for years 4, 8, 12 and so on.

Rows 1 to 8 and 9 to 19 were read digit by digit on the scan and the rule found from them; the rule was then compared with every row of the three pages. A row that disagreed would have been a misread digit; none did.
