# Mar me mdzad bzang po's rite names the All-Doer, not the Ender of Obstructors, which the Sngags rim chen mo says he names (RD373)

The Sngags rim chen mo says that Mar me mdzad bzang po (Dīpaṃkarabhadra) says the Ender of Obstructors (བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད, bgegs mthar byed) destroys the obstructors of a bad dream. In his rite of the Guhyasamāja mandala in 450 verses (Derge Bstan 'gyur etext VE23703_1358, pp. 139-175; the Peking print's etext has the same lines) the dawn passage at [p156] says only: "whatever dreams you see, tell all of it to me at dawn; for the guarding and the blessing and the rest recite there the All-Doer (ལས་ཀུན་བྱེད་པ, las kun byed pa); having asked about the dream, good and bad, destroy the non-virtue by the means of application (ཐབས་སྦྱོར, thabs sbyor)" (mmb:p155-156). The Ender of Obstructors does not stand in these lines or elsewhere in the text, as searched by its name in the etext ([p41], [p51]-[p57] and [p335]-[p336] of the volume belong to other texts of the volume). Where the Ender of Obstructors does stand near a dream is in Kṛṣṇa's (Nag po pa's) Guhyasamāja rite in the Derge etext VE23703_1351 at [p505] (a mantra said over the tooth-sticks, not a remedy of a dream), and the Mañjuśrī root tantra's remedy of a bad dream is the wrathful king's mantra, not the Ender of Obstructors (mmk-bad-dream-remedy-wrathful-king-mantra).

So the text of Mar me mdzad bzang po that was found does not say what the Sngags rim chen mo says it says. Either Tsong kha pa reads the All-Doer or the means of application as the Ender of Obstructors, or he cites another work of Mar me mdzad bzang po, or his copy read otherwise. This is a conflict between a quotation and its source, not settled here.
