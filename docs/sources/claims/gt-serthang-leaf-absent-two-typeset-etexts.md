# The Serthang print's ch. 7 leaf is absent from its whole digitization, no second Serthang copy on BDRC; two typeset etexts add brjed ngas and mi bzang ba

RD296 looked for a second scan or etext of the Serthang print (BDRC MW3CN5036, the Larung Gar typeset edition, 611 pages, scan I3CN5038, etext IE0OPIFEF6EACA) and found none: the work record WA3CN5036 lists that one instance only, and BDRC's catalogue search for the title with the publisher (གསེར་ཐང་བླ་རུང་ལྔ་རིག་ནང་བསྟན་སློབ་གླིང, gser thang bla rung lnga rig nang bstan slob gling) or the place (གསེར་རྟ, gser rta) returns it alone. The leaf is absent from the print's own digitization, not only from one scan: on the scan the printed page numbers run 30 (img. 43), 34 (img. 44), 33 (img. 45), 34 (img. 46), 35 (img. 47), so printed pp. 31 and 32, which carry the opening of ch. 7 and the bad-dream verse, were never photographed, and p. 34 stands twice; the etext, made image by image, repeats the same page at its pp. 44 and 46 and has p. 45 (the near signs) next. A search of all 622 etext pages with the spaces and the tsheg stripped finds none of «རྨི་ལམ་བྱི་ལ», «སྤྲེའུ་སྟག», «འཆི་བདག་ཟིན», «དུར་ཁྲོད་ཉལ», «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ», «རྨི་ལམ་མི་ཟད་པ», «མཚན་སྟོད»; «བརྗེད་ངས» and «བརྗེད་ངེས» occur only in other chapters' lines (pp. 169, 182, 183, 473 and 266, the signs of an illness of the mind), not in the night rule. So q850 and q887 stay open for the Serthang print: nothing on BDRC reads that leaf.

The count by print is extended by two typeset etexts that carry the whole verse, read as texts (OCR of a printed book, not by eye on a scan): the Lhasa 1982 Bod ljongs mi dmangs dpe skrun khang «རྒྱུད་བཞི» (MW16778, etext IE0OPI0F224AAA, pp. 49–50) and the Beijing Mi rigs dpe skrun khang Explanatory Tantra (MW1KG25027, IE0OPI17695C92, pp. 44–45). Both read «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད་ངས་འབྲས་བུ་མེད», so ངས stands on ten witnesses now and ངེས on none that is a Four Tantras print; line 17 reads «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ» in the Lhasa etext and «མི་བཟང་པ» in the Beijing one, never «མི་བཟད་པ» or «མི་ཟད་པ»; both have «བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད་བག་མ་ལེན་པ», «པདྨ་ཕྱུང», «མགོ་ཆག» (the Lhasa etext's «རྔ་མོ», the Beijing's «རྔ་མོང»). The typeset editions thus stand with the Drathang and the Derge for བཟང་, and add nothing to the split of the Chag-po-ri and Zhol prints for བཟད་; as OCR text they are a weaker witness than a scan and do not move the count of eye readings (four prints each way, RD275, RD279).
