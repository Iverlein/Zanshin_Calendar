# The Sakya manual's «no dream comes» in the second and third watches says that no dream occurs, because of deep sleep; it is not a rule of counting (RD394, q1072)

In Nam mkha' dpal bzang's Vajrāvalī manual the words are «ཐུན་གཉིས་པ་དང་གསུམ་པ་ནམ་གུང་གི་ཆ་གཉིད་འཐུག་པོ་ཞེས་རྨི་ལམ་མི་འབྱུང» (rsk:p459-461): the second and third watches, the midnight part, are «deep sleep» (གཉིད་འཐུག་པོ, gnyid 'thug po), and so a dream does not arise (མི་འབྱུང, mi 'byung). The verb is «to arise, to occur» (འབྱུང), and the cause given is the sleep itself, so the sentence says that in the middle of the night, sleeping deeply, no dream occurs; it does not say that a dream occurs and is not counted. The same manual says of the first watch (the evening part, mixed with the day's appearances and a little unclear) and of the last (the dawn part, the dream very clear, so it must be examined) in the vocabulary of how clear a dream is, not of how true. So the reading «none is to be counted» has no word in the sentence to rest on; the manual treats the middle watches as empty of dreams, and it quotes the root tantra's four-watch verse (phlegm, bile, wind, the fourth true) just before without using its bile and wind for the second and third watches, nor remarking that the tantra lists dreams for them (mmk.1:p399-400, mmk.1:p400-401). That the manual and the tantra are at odds is therefore left unsaid by the manual itself; the tantra's second and third watches have signs of bile and wind, the manual's have none (rsk:p459-461, mmk.1:p399-400, mmk.1:p400-401).
