# The household round: who is counted and what each brings

The household round (the Chinese pebble round, རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར, rgya nag rdel skor) is a reckoning of the year of age for a whole household (བཟའ་ཚང, bza' tshang).

- **Who.** Every person of the house, as many as it holds; not fewer than four (the notes, vol. 2, p. 490). Some reckoners add those gathered there from abroad or in the house that year; the notes report this and do not adopt it.
- **What each brings.** Five pebbles: the four deciding pebbles (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod) of that person's year of age, one each for life force, body, power and luck, each gathered from the twenty-four of cutting and tearing (WB vol. 1, p. 385; the notes, p. 490); and one pebble of the colour of that person's mewa of the year (WB «སྨེ་བ་ཁམས་རྡེལ»; the notes «བབ་སྨེའི་མདོག་རྡེལ»; the *Moonbeams* «རང་རང་ཁམས་མཐུན་ཁ་དོག»).
- **The order.** Men first, eldest to youngest, then women the same; of two in one year, the one born earlier first; if not known, the bigger or the older (the notes, p. 490).
- **Each column.** Body, life force, power, luck, mewa, top to bottom: the life force's and the body's rows are exchanged "as the oral instruction" (the notes, p. 490; the laying of five on the same page has the body row first).

So for every member the round needs the birth year (the four aspects and the mewa of the year of age), the gender and, between two of one year, who was born first.
