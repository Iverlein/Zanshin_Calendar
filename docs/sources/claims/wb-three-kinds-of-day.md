# The three kinds of day: tithi-day, solar day, house-day

WB's chapter 17 opens with the three kinds of day it analyses: the tithi-day (ཚེས་ཞག, tshes zhag), the solar day (ཉིན་ཞག, nyin zhag) and the house-day (ཁྱིམ་ཞག, khyim zhag). The tithi-day is one of the fifteen parts into which the moon's waxing or waning is divided, taken when that part is complete. The solar day is the span from seeing the lines of the hand at rising to seeing them at the next rising. The house-day is the time the sun takes over one house (sign) divided by thirty; the sun's 360 days make one house-year, and the circle of the house-days is fixed at that number. Each planet has its own division of years, months and days; the sun's are the pattern. The tithi-day of this chapter is a part of the moon's circle and the house-day a part of the sun's, not the weekday or the date of the almanac.
