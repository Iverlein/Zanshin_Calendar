# The Svapnohana's «བགྲོད་མིན་ལ་ཞུགས» is the Agnipurāṇa's agamyāgamana, an image of its own

# The Svapnohana's «བགྲོད་མིན་ལ་ཞུགས»: an image of its own, the Agnipurāṇa's agamyāgamana

In the second verse of the Svapnohana, «མཚོན་ཆས་རྡེག་དང་སྲོག་ཆགས་ཕྲ་མོས་ལུས་ཁེབས་དང་། དེ་བཞིན་ལུས་ཀྱི་ལྗན་ལྗིན་རྣམ་པར་དག་པ་དང་། བཤང་བའི་རྗེས་སུ་འཇུག་དང་ལུས་ལས་ཁྲག་འཛག་དང་། རྨི་ལམ་རྣམས་ན་བགྲོད་མིན་ལ་ཞུགས་ཤིས་པའོ», the clause «བགྲོད་མིན་ལ་ཞུགས» (bgrod min la zhugs) is read here as a sixth member of the list, not as a limit on the other five: "in dreams: struck with weapons, the body covered with small creatures, the body's dirt washed away, following excrement, blood flowing, and entering what is not to be gone to: auspicious" (inv-k, 2026-11-14).

**The parallel.** The Agnipurāṇa's Svapnādhyāya (agp:p282, read on the scan) closes its good dreams with «घृतविष्ठानुलेपो वा अगम्यागमनं तथा» (v. 31): smearing with ghee or excrement, "and agamyāgamana", going to what is not to be gone to (a woman who is forbidden). The word agamya is «བགྲོད་མིན» (bgrod min, "not to be travelled") and gamana the going, here «ཞུགས» (zhugs, "entering"). The same chapter gives the Svapnohana's other terms in the good dreams: blood seen or bathed in (vv. 24–25; the Svapnohana's blood flowing), excrement smeared (v. 31; "following excrement"), wiping or cleansing (parimārjana, v. 22; the washing of the body's dirt), and the ten-day span of the dawn dream, the year, months and half-month of the watches (agp:p280-281; the Svapnohana's own first verse). The two texts therefore draw on one Indian list, and in it the images the Svapnohana calls auspicious are not read as bad.

**What it settles.** The clause names an act, not a condition; the verdict «ཤིས་པའོ» closes the list, and the line stands with Indian practice against the Tibetan texts that read the same images as bad. It does not say that the Derge verse is a translation of the Agni verse (the Agni has no weapons or creatures on the body), nor what the translator meant by «ཞུགས» beyond the word's ordinary sense; the reading is an inference from the parallel, not a gloss by a commentary, which was not found.
