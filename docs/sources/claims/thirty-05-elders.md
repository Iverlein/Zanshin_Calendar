# The thirty: the fifth point, the elephant of the earth, for the chief elders

The fifth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the elephant of the earth (ས་ཡི་གླང་ཆེན, sa yi glang chen), is read for the chief elders (ཕ་རྒན་གཙོ་བོ, pha rgan gtso bo). Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether a chief son comes to the chief father | A great chief son comes to the great chief father | The chief's line is cut, his seat is at risk of being empty, the country's people fare badly; or a tiger-like father with a fox-like («ཝ», wa) line of sons | The queen's turning of the crowd (རྒྱལ་མོ་ཁྲོམ་བསྒྱུར, rgyal mo khrom bsgyur) |
| 2 | Whether the chief's seat is empty | Not empty | The chief's seat is empty; bad for the country in general | The queen's turning of the crowd helps |
| 3 | Whether the town of the staff-holders (སྤ་འཁར་ཅན, spa 'khar can) is full | Full, good | The staff-holders' council is not filled, its mouth turned down; a great risk of head and foot reversed; blame and quarrels; bad for the country's people | The general thread-cross (མདོས, mdos) of the four continents; if that fails, signs (མཚན་མ, mtshan ma) and the weapon rite against enemies (དགྲ་ཟོར, dgra zor) |
| 4 | Whether the lord's punishment comes | The country's lord is kind; aims achieved, good news heard | The lord's punishment and quarrels come | Press down the enemy sri (དགྲ་སྲི, dgra sri); the enemy pit (དགྲ་བྲུབ, dgra brub) helps |
| 5 | Whether the crystal fortress (ཤེལ་གྱི་འགྱིང་མཁར, shel gyi 'gying mkhar) is broken | Not broken; merit and wealth grow | Broken: bad for the country, fields, castle and estate, above all for the sons of aunts and sisters | — |
| 6 | Whether rivalry with companions comes | Companions and near kin are kind | Through idle talk, those who did no wrong rise as enemies against one's own counsel | — |
| 7 | Whether the many mouths speak of emptiness | No ground for gossip (མི་ཁ, mi kha) | Many people talk of emptiness; gossip and quarrels in plenty | The enemy pit and turning gossip by rites |
| 8 | Whether the counsel is deceived by demons | Wishes fulfilled, no obstacle to what is planned | The counsel deceived by demons: the three courtiers (དྲུང་གསུམ, drung gsum) rise as enemies, bad counsel of every kind, and what is in the heart goes to the lungs («གློར་འགྲོ», glor 'gro; read: goes astray) | — |

Whose year is reckoned: the person's own (the notes, vol. 2, p. 533: «གཞན་རྣམས་སོ་སོའི་ལོ་རྟ»).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
