# The rat day's «སྦང་མ» is the beer's mash, read so on the Gser thang reprint

On the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 230, p. 224, by eye 2026-11-01) the line before the rat day's reads «ཆང་མ་བྱིས་པ་དག་ལ་དང་། སྦང་མ་ཕུད་གཏོར་ཡོལ་བས་སྒྲིབ། ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན།» with «སྦང་མ» (sbang ma), without the subjoined ra: the Zhol and Taikhang reading of question 440 is the reprint's too, and «སྦྲང་མ» (the bee) is the variant. «སྦང་མ» is the mash of the brewed beer, the dregs after brewing (Rangjung Yeshe, no. 2: leftover grain after beer has been brewed; Ives Waldo, no. 8; Dag yig gsar bsgrigs: the husk dregs after beer is pressed; Tshig mdzod chen mo: the residue of pressed beer), so the beer's mash is the first portion scattered and a cloth is drawn over it or the jar; no bee or honey. Narrows questions 440, 447 and 404. «ཡོ» (yo) alone is «crooked, askew» (Dag yig gsar bsgrigs: kha mig yo ba, a mouth or eye awry; tibetanlanguage.school) or «all» (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Dag yig); which fits «ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན» stays open (questions 439, 446).
