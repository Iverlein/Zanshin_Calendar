# Yu thog Snying thig: in the dream of spirits the trained dreamer overcomes the animal by the next stronger one and each class by its antidote deity; no part of the night (RD391)

RD391. The Yu thog Snying thig's completion-stage text Sku gsum rang shar (the three bodies self-arisen), in its dream-yoga instruction, trains the dreamer who holds the dream to change what appears: fire does not burn, water does not carry away, a precipice is not fallen from; water is turned into fire and fire into water; every being seen is meditated as the yidam and multiplied. For taming spirits (gdon) each class has its antidote deity, kings Hayagrīva, planets Vajrapāṇi, nāgas Nāgarakṣa; and in the dream of spirits (gdon gyi rmi lam) the animal seen is overcome by the next stronger one, a dog by a wolf, a wolf by a tiger, a tiger by a lion, and any other animal by whichever antidote suits (dds.47:p293-295). The dream of spirits is a kind of dream the trained dreamer meets, a practice for the sleeper who already recognises the dream, and no part of the night is named. The same days-and-night practice is taught by day too ("this is not a dream"), so the cure is practice and not waiting for dawn. The etext names no author for the text.
