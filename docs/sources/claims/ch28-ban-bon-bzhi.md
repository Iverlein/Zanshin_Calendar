# The four of bon and the four of ban: WB's groups of the eight trigrams

WB vol. 2, p. 39 (ordination) names the group «བོན་བཞི» (spelled «བན་བཞི» a few lines on) beside four trigrams; the names belong to WB's own grouping of the eight trigrams in vol. 1: in ch. 21 (p. 256, img. 266) the eight trigrams, "split into eight faces", are called the four watching eyes (བལྟ་མིག་བཞི, blta mig bzhi), the four striking horns (བརྡུང་རྭ་བཞི, brdung rwa bzhi), the *ban* four and the *bon* four (བན་བཞི་བོན་བཞི); and ch. 20's verse (p. 233, img. 243, BDRC etext only, not read on the scan: the 1996 scan is too small) gives the members: the eyes *li kham dwa zin*, the horns *khen khon gin zon*, and, as the etext reads, the *ban* four *khen khon zin dwa* and the *bon* four, whose line begins «ཡིན་» (probably «བོན»), *li kham gin zon*. So «བོན་བཞི» and «བན་བཞི» are one of two complementary fours of trigrams, here spelled alike in p. 39 («བོན་བཞིར», first, with the vowel, read on the Gser thang reprint, I3CN5029 img. 045, 2×; «བན་བཞིར» later); the ordination rule's own list of four (khon, gin, dwa, zin) crosses both fours. What the person is "born in" is a trigram of that four (by the birth reckoning's trigram), not an animal or a time. The members of the fours are a reading of an etext line and wait for the scan.
