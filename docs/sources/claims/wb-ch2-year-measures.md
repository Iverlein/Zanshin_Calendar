# WB ch. 2 gives the year's measures: 360 breaths to a chu tshod, 21,600 breaths a day, 360 sign-days and 365 civil days

The coarse arrangement of ch. 2 (machine reading of p. 14): three hundred and sixty in-and-out breaths fill one chu tshod (ཆུ་ཚོད); a day and night has twenty-one thousand six hundred breaths (ཉིས་ཁྲི་སྟོང་དྲུག་བརྒྱ); thirty days make the month and twelve months the year. The days of the year are counted in two ways: civil days (ཉིན་ཞག) three hundred and sixty-five, and the days of the house (ཁྱིམ་ཞག, the zodiac's going round) three hundred and sixty, which the text calls the same as a human year. A further line on the asterism's motion (a thousand and six hundred and twenty chu tshod) and one on the days of the lunar day (three hundred and 'one', the wording unsettled) are not settled and are not relied on.
