# In the Mañjuśrī root tantra's «བྱ་བ་དུས་ཀྱི་རིམ་པ» the Tibetan is krama, «sama» is mnyam pa (RD395, q1048, q1032)

In the lines «རྨི་ལམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ཡོད་པ་སྤངས། བྱ་བ་དུས་ཀྱི་རིམ་པ་ཉིད།» of the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa the Tibetan «རིམ་པ» (rim pa) is krama, "order", not sama: the lexica (Mahāvyutpatti, Yogācārabhūmi glossary, Hopkins, Negi) give rim pa = krama, and sama is «མཉམ་པ» (mnyam pa). The Sanskrit text layer's garbled word «समश्» may be «क्रमश्» with the first letters misread, and the same edition has the compound kāla-krama a chapter later (ch. 22, «eṣa kāla-kramo yoge», "this is the order of times in the practice"). «སྤངས» is varjita or parivarjita ("set aside"), compatible with the layer's «सखवितपू».

So the lines say: "therefore in every way the dream-named is set aside, and the order of the times of the act was pointed out by the seers of what is so". Whether «all dreams» or only the «dream-named» kind is set aside is not decided by the lexica: «ཞེས་བྱ་ཡོད་པ» is "the so-called", which leans to the second. The Sanskrit was read in a machine text layer, not on the page image; this is a likelier reading, not a settled one.

**Corrected 2026-11-13 (q1136).** GRETIL's keying of the Gaṇapati Śāstrī edition reads «kriyākālasamaś caiva» in verse 15.46 (claim `mmk-gretil-reads-kriyakalasama-verse-15-46`): the Sanskrit of that edition has sama, not krama. That the Tibetan «རིམ་པ» renders krama stays true of the lexica; that the edition's word is krama does not. Whether the manuscripts, and the translators' copy, had kramaś or samaś is open.
