# Whose year for the thirty's subjects and horses

The regent's replies 137 and 138 (ys:i494-496, ys:i496) settle two of the points the notes name (wb.2:p532-533):

- **The tenth, subjects and retinue** (འབངས་འཁོར, 'bangs 'khor): in general the lord's (དཔོན, dpon) year, because the checks read the retinue's agreement, the servants' loyalty and their sweeping away of wealth as good or weak for the lord. But when one of the lord's household, not the lord, asks about subjects for his own wish, the asker's own year.
- **The eleventh, horses**: the year of whoever asks, the chief owner, the groom (ཨ་དྲུང, a drung) or a buyer alike, each for his own matter, and the results are not mixed.

The rule under both, as stated in reply 138: the reckoning is "in the power of whoever asks". That it extends to the other points the notes pass over (12–13, 16, 18–20, 22, 26–30; question 303) is an inference, not stated.
