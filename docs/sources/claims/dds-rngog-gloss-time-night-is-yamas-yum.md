# Rngog Zhe sdang rdo rje's exposition glosses the black girl as Yama's mother (yum) with seven days and the monkey rider's «last night» as death at once; the watches are habit, Māra, the deity (RD382)

RD382. In Rngog Zhe sdang rdo rje's running exposition of the verses of 'Deceiving Death', «'Chi ba bslu thabs rin chen sgron me» (the volume of the collected works of Rngog and his pupils, Mes po'i shul bzhag; dds.27:p41-44), the glosses on the dream signs say two things that the verses do not. The black girl whom a man dreams of binding him is «Time» (the etext prints «dus mtshams», the reader takes dus mtshan), and «she is Yama's mother (or consort, ཡུམ, yum), so it is seven days»; and the monkey rider who goes east and wakes has the «last night» (མཚན་མོ་ཐ་མ), that is, he dies directly after that waking («de ma thag tu 'chi»). Time-Night is therefore, in this earliest exposition read, a figure of Yama's household with a span of seven days attached by the verse, as the medical commentary (dds.20:p213-220) and the Sakya praises (the existing claim on «'o yug pa») also take her; she is not a part of the night.

The lecture also keeps the three parts of the night for dreams: the evening (srod) dream is habit-imprint («one dreams what one did today»), the midnight (nam phyed) dream is Māra's («Māra's obstacle»), the dawn (tho rangs) dream the chosen deity's teaching, so one prays and then examines. That is the order of the Karmapa's verses (dds.18:p57) apart from the midnight dream, which there is the power of spirits; both give no span of life to a watch. The Tengyur verses themselves tie no sign to a watch: the only watch rule of the Tengyur is the Svapnohana's (svo).

The etext is noisy (words run together, glosses inside verses), read as etext only. The date of the exposition is not given by the page; the volume's section heading names Rngog Zhe sdang rdo rje.
