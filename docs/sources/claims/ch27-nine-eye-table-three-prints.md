# The nine-eye table of p. 16 reads alike on three prints; ten rows of eleven obey the rule

# The nine-eye table of p. 16 reads the same on the 1996 scan, the reprint and the Zhol print: ten rows of eleven obey the element rule

Yigdzin-1 over the three prints of p. 16 (the 1996 scan I1KG12907 img. 24, the Gser thang reprint I3CN5029 img. 22, the Zhol print I1KG1710 img. 649) gives the eleven rows «X ཤར་ན Y ཚུད» in the same words on all three: the wood male rat «ཤིང་ཕོ་བྱི་བ་ཤར»: the woman of the earth tiger or hare; the wood ox: the earth hare «ས་ཡོས»; the fire tiger «མེ་སྟག»: the iron tiger; the earth hare «ས་ཡོས»: the water bird; the earth snake: the water horse; the iron horse: the wood dragon; the iron sheep: the wood snake; the water monkey: the fire monkey; the water bird: the fire bird; the wood dog: the earth monkey; the wood pig: the wood bird «ཤིང་བྱ». The only difference between the readers is the vowel of the third row, «མི་སྟག» on the 1996 scan, «མེ་སྟག» on the other two; MITRA on the Zhol print gives «མེ་སྟག» ([wb.2:p16c]). Held against the element rule (the first year's element overcomes the second's: wood the earth, fire the iron, earth the water, iron the wood, water the fire) ten rows obey it and the wood pig's does not, as [ch27-nine-eye-sky-years-by-element](ch27-nine-eye-sky-years-by-element.md) found on the reprint alone. The rule fixes the two doubted words: the row of the iron tiger needs a fire year over it («མེ་སྟག»; «མི» alone names no element), the row of the water bird an earth year («ས་ཡོས», not the fire hare, which water overcomes). «མེ་སྟག» occurs thirty times in WB's etexts and «མི་སྟག» once, in an unrelated line (vol. 2 p. 228). The wood pig's «ཤིང་བྱ» is on all three prints as the machine reads them, so a slip for «ས་བྱ» (earth bird, as the rule asks) would be common to every print: the word stays as printed, the rule broken once, no text says why.
