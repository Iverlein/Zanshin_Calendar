# Dpal sprul's calendar verses: the results of the lunar days (the eleventh good to 'bog the vows) and the series of the great festivals

Dpal sprul's own verses on the results of the lunar days (dpk.2:p138-140) give the days' character, the first to the fifteenth, with the waning half the same: the first all aims succeed, the second a great feast, the third every work good, the fourth uncertain, the fifth profit, the sixth death in prison for one who seeks an aim, the seventh every wish, the eighth fear, the ninth the guest dies, the tenth good omens go before, the eleventh "good to 'bog the vows" (སྡོམ་པ་འབོག་པ་བཟང, sdom pa 'bog pa bzang), the twelfth foolishness, the thirteenth great strength, the fourteenth a thief, the fifteenth travel is given up. The verse after the days, the yogas and the karaṇas gives the great festivals (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) of the Conqueror's blessing by the Hor months' names: the eighth to the fifteenth of the Rgyal month (the sitting under the Bodhi tree), the waxing half of the Mchu month (the miracles), the full moon of Saga (the awakening), the fourth of the Chu stod month (the turning of the wheel), the full moon of Tha skar (the descent from the gods' abode), the eighth from the Dbo month (the entry into the womb), the full moon of Nag (the Kālacakra root tantra) and the eighth of the Smin drug month (the nirvana), with the reason: "because the Dharmas differ in how they are regarded, the difference in the multiplication of virtue and vice is great".

- Days: the 11th (and by "apply the same to the waning half", the 26th) is the one day said to be good for the vows, with the verb 'bog left unglossed (a question in the report of this reading); no word for the 25th or the other practice days.
- It is a calendar-reading verse for sums of days, not a lay instruction; no practice (fast, offering) is tied to the days.
- Rank 3; the verse is Dpal sprul's own composition for the calendar manual (printed pp. 138-140), read in the etext only.
