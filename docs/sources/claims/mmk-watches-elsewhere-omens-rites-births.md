# The watches in the other chapters: earthquake omens, rites, births; never tied to a humour (RD372)

The "watches" (ཐུན) are used elsewhere in the root tantra, never for a dream. In the 18th chapter the omens of an earthquake are read by the watch of the night in which it shakes (Derge etext [p436]-[p437]): it names a first, a second and a third watch, each with a beginning, a middle, a decline and an end, and in between midnight (མཚན་ཕྱེད), a "middle watch" (བར་མའི་ཐུན), dawn (ཐོ་རངས) and sunrise; the meanings are weather, war, disease, the death of a king or a good year (and are not read here beyond what the page says). Rites are timed by watch: the second watch for recitation until the blaze appears ([p451], [p454], [p456], the first watch having passed), one watch of the night for a fire offering ([p456]), the first watch of the night in the bright half and the full moon for a good presence to appear ([p503]). Births are sorted by the last watch of the night, its middle watch and the sun's half-watch ([p430]-[p432]). In none of these is a watch tied to a humour. The scheme of three watches before midnight and dawn in the omens, against four in the dream verse, is a doubt (the dream verse may count a fourth between midnight and dawn).
