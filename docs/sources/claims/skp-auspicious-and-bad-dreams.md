# SKP, first text: the verdict of each dream image, no time rule

The first text of the bundle under Sakya Paṇḍita's name (pp. 40–50, machine reading) is a verse list of dream images, each with its verdict: the auspicious ones (lion, high mountain, white horse, armour, sitting on a mountain top, fruit trees, elephant, banners, the dragon's voice, the king's word, white elephant, clothes given, a field with grain, stūpas and images made, the Buddhas seen, a throne, a great river crossed, sun and moon touching the body) bring health, wealth and the end of sins; the bad ones (black clothes and black meat, nakedness, a burning house, door or monastery, a red sun, dry rivers, collapse of a temple or stūpa, a pit, a black woman, beasts and birds of prey, a broken rosary, vomiting) bring illness, loss, a death among kin or a spirit's harm. It gives no watch of the night, no lunar day and no remedy. The title's attribution to Sakya Paṇḍita is not the colophon's (the Śākya monk Kun dga' rgyal mtshan dpal bzang po, at Sa skya).
