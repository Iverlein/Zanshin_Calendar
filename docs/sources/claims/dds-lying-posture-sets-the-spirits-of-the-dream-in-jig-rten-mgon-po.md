# 'Jig rten mgon po: the way one lies sets the dream of the earth lord, of a male or a female spirit; the cure is the lion's posture, no hour named (RD391)

RD391. In 'Jig rten mgon po's Great Assembly Teaching (Tshogs chos chen mo Rin chen 'brug sgra), put down by the monk Dar ma tshul khrims at 'Bri gung dpal gyi nags khrod from the lord's words, the third of the three root physical disciplines ('khrul 'khor), lying down, ties the dream to the way the sleeper lies: the right side down and the head to the north is right; lying facing west brings dullness and, in dreams, the magic of the earth lord (sa bdag) that cuts in between; the left side down brings hatred, the dream of a male spirit (pho gdon) and obstacles; the back brings desire, the magic of a female spirit (mo gdon) and obstacles such as the loss of the drop. The cure is the lion's lying posture, the Buddha's own at his passing, with the absorption in which the awakening mind is undivided (dds.24:p190-191; the same words in the Drikung collected works vol. 4, dds.48:p220-221). The spirits' dream is here a result of posture, with no hour of the night and no rite beyond the posture and the absorption. Beside Marpa's inner-heat manual (dds.19:p187-189), where the dream of a horse, dog or woman is the spirit's work and the cure compassion and emptiness, and the Yu thog instruction (dds.47:p293-295), where the dream of spirits is tamed by the antidote, this is the third Kagyu-side account of the spirits' dream, and like the others it names no part of the night.
