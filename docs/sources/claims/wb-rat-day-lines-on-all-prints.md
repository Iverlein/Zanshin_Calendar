# The rat day's lines are the same on every print; the Bhutan collection's table adds «ཁ་ཡོ་ནས»

The rat day's three remedy lines of p. 224 stand the same on the 1996 print, the Gser thang reprint, the Zhol print and the Taikhang print; the one place where the prints may differ is the ra under the head of the first word, «སྦྲང་མ» (bee) or «སྦང་མ», which the Gser thang reprint and the 1996 etext show and the Zhol and Taikhang readings do not (the stack is not told apart by eye on the last two). A compendium from Bhutan (Thimphu 1981) carries a shortened verse of the same lord in which the second and third lines are WB's own words, «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན», without the first-portion and cloth lines, and a table of the twelve days that gives the rat's remedy as «ཁ་ཡོ་ནས། འདྲེན།», so «ཡོ་ནས» is the printed pair with a syllable before it in the table. The dog day beside it (a corpse on the threshold, remedy: the threshold wrapped in cloth) shows that «ཐེམ་བུ» is the house threshold and the rat day's line is a rule for where the corpse is not set down. The sense of «སྦྲང་མ་ཕུད» and of «ཡོ་ནས» is still not settled, and the notes of vol. 2 (pp. 474–545, which end with the black reckoning of ch. 22–30) do not gloss ch. 31's lines.
