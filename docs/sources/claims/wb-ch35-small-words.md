# Ch. 35's small words: the topknot feat, «གུད་སྦས» on p. 448, the palm and the root tantra

p. 440, «ཐོར་ཚུགས» (thor tshugs): the topknot, hair bound up on the crown (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Valby, the Mahavyutpatti's śikhābandha). Negi's Tibetan-Sanskrit dictionary quotes the Lalitavistara list of a prince's skills with it as an art (kalā): «མཆོངས་པ་དང་…ཐོར་ཚུགས་དང་…སྤོས་སྦྱར་བ་ལ་སོགས་པའི་སྒྱུ་རྩལ», leaping, …, making the topknot, … mixing perfumes and the like. So the feat in WB's list «ཐོར་ཚུགས་གོམ་སྟབས་ལྕགས་ཀྱུས་བསྒྱུར» is most likely the art of dressing the topknot; read only in Negi's quotation, not in the Lalitavistara. p. 448, line 24: on the 1996 print (img. 456, line 24 enlarged twice) the words are «རྟོགས་དཀའ་གུད་སྦས་ཀུན་བསལ་བའི»; the stack read «རྡུལ» is «སྦས» and the machine «སྔས» is wrong. «གུད་སྦས» is the word of ch. 34's colophon (wb-ch34-gud-sbas-hidden-part), the hidden part: the White Beryl clears away all the hidden that is hard to realise. The reprint (img. 454) was not cut again. p. 453: the 1996 etext reads «ཧ་ལ་ལམ་དུ་བསྒྱུར་བ་འདྲ», the Zhol etext (I1KG1710, p. 1237) «ཏ་ལ་ལལམ་དུ…» and the earlier scan reading is «ཏ་ལ»; «ཏ་ལ» is the palm tree (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Tshig mdzod chen mo: it does not grow again once its top is cut), «ཧ་ལ» poison (Rangjung Yeshe). No source read glosses the saying 'turning the palm into a road'; the word is ཏ་ལ, the image unsettled. p. 450: the Mūlatantra's name is garbled in both etexts («མུ་ལ་ཏནྟཱ», «མུལ་ཏཅུ») and its lines «རང་གིས་གོ་ཆ་མ་བརྩམས་ན། །རང་གིས་བྱས་པ་རང་ལ་བརྡེག» are in no other etext of the pool; which root tantra is meant is open.
