# The nine-eye's two rounds: the year of age, the executioner signs and the trigram at each age

The nine-eye strike (དགུ་མིག, dgu mig, WB vol. 2, pp. 87–88) counts, at ages 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63, 72 and 81, the person's sign of age on **two rounds**, the male round (ཕོ་སྐོར, pho skor) and the female round (མོ་སྐོར, mo skor), and strikes when the round reaches an executioner (གཤེད, gshed). The manual of the Chinese reckoning by Lha mo tshul khrims (LT, pp. 93–94) gives the count: the man's round starts at the rat of the same power element as the person (བྱི་བ, byi ba) and the woman's at the bird (བྱ, bya) of that power, each counted "upward" (གྱེན་དུ, gyen du), which the animals named show to be backward through the twelve signs, with the starting sign as the first, so that age *n* falls on the *n*th sign; since *n* is taken from 1 for the starting sign, the age is the year of age (ལོ, lo), the nominal age counted from birth as the first year, not full years. The nine ages thus fall only on the four signs of the executioners (dragon, sheep, dog, ox): male dragon, sheep, dog, ox, dragon, sheep, dog, ox, dragon; female ox, dragon, sheep, dog, ox, dragon, sheep, dog, ox. Each is set on a trigram (the "inner" one): male *li*, *khon*, *dwa*, *khen*, *kham*, *gin*, *zin*, *zon*, *li*; female *kham*, *khen*, *dwa*, *khon*, *li*, *zon*, *zin*, *gin*, *kham*. WB's pairs of trigrams for each age are the union of the two rounds' (9: *li* and *kham*; 18: *khen* or *khon*; 27: *dwa*; 36: *khen* or *khon*; 45: *kham* and *li*; 54: *gin* and *zon*; 72: *gin* or *zon*; 81: *li* and *kham*) and agree in all but one place: at 63 LT sets both rounds on *zin* where WB says *dwa* or *zin*. LT is a modern witness, so this is its table read beside WB's, not WB's own word on the start.
