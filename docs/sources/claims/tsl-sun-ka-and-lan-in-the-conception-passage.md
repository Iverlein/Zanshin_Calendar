# «སུན་ཀ» and «ལན་འདུག» in the conception passage: a harm turned back by a rite, and a debt of the place

In the conception passage the people first say the dreams are «བྱད་ཁ» (byad kha, a curse of sorcery), then, after the same dreams came a third time with the woman pregnant, a household voice says they are «སུན་ཀ» (sun ka); the Tibetan counter-rite for it is «སུན་ཟློག» (sun zlog), "turning back the sun", done by the mantra-holders (i38). The first is turned back by «བྱད་བཟློག» (byad bzlog) in i37. The two stand in the same pattern, a harm named and a rite that turns it back, so «སུན» is the harm laid on the child or the house by the dream's coming, and «སུན་ཀ» is "a harm" as the question reads it; I cannot give the word's second syllable from any source (it may be the «ཁ» of «བྱད་ཁ», read ka). «སུན» of «སུན་འབྱིན», refuting, is another word in the same sound. «ལན་འདུག» in the diviner's answer, "the place where you live has a «ལན»", is read with «ལན» as the debt or retaliation of «ལན་ཆགས» (lan chags), the creditor's claim, the word the volumes use for the karmic debts of spirits and persons and that a torma and a rite are offered to (vol. 27 images 28, 151; vol. 28 images 67, 263); the advice that follows, "move the root soon", is the usual answer to a place's claim. In i505 of the same volume «བོན་བྱད་ལན» are three harms named together, sorcery of the Bon, byad and lan. It is a reading from the company the word keeps, not a definition from a dictionary.

On the scan (vol. 30 img. 38, printed p. 27) «སུན» is the same stack in «སུན་ཀ» and in «སུན་ཟློག» two lines below; the second syllable of «སུན་ཀ» is a small ཀ; the page is a low-resolution image and the line of «ལན་འདུག» was not read on it.
