# A dawn rite of Avalokiteśvara after a fast turns back a bad dream; no part of the night named (RD381)

A rite for a bad dream in the Tengyur's Avalokiteśvara run, "the Turning back of the bad dream", is done at the glow of the dawn after a fast (གསོ་སྦྱོང་ཡན་ལག): face east in clean clothes with the face washed and a flower garland, take refuge, set an image of Avalokiteśvara before one, make the yoga of the thousand-armed sword-bearing form, recite the three wheels, burn agaru and give a thousand tormas; this is said to destroy bad omens like cutting, striking and burning. It names no part of the night, no lunar day and no list of dream images. The maker of the run is Ajitamitragupta (Derge catalogue) and the translator Gnubs Byams pa'i dpal bzang po. A companion piece, "the Showing of dreams", asks for a dream after a fast of one to three days and shows it at dawn. Both are etext readings with doubtful words in the weapons and the mantra's tail.
