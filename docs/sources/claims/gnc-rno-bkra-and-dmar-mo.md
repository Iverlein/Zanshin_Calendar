# «Dpal rno bkra» names the cycle of the Gnam chos the rites are drawn from; «ma mar mo» is «dmar mo» (RD286)

«Dpal rno bkra» is the name of a cycle, not of a place or a person met in the dream text. BDRC lists the Gnam chos' own titles under it: the vol. 1 section "Rno bkra" (partIndex 7 of MW21578, pp. 435–576) holds, among thirty sections, the Great Compassionate One's examination of dreams (ff. 61r–61v, pp. 557–558, letter sa), the Mtsho rgyal text (ff. 61v–63v, pp. 558–561, letter ha, titled «dpal rno bkra las rmi lam brtags pa gsar ba lung bstan ma», "the new prophecy of the examination of dreams from the Dpal rno bkra") and sections named «dpal rno bkra'i them byang nam mkha'i lde mig», «rno bkra'i phyi sgrub», «rno bkra'i lo rgyus» (pp. 493–494), «dpal rno bkra'i thugs rje chen po'i gsol 'debs». BDRC's full-text search for «དཔལ་རྣོ་བཀྲ» finds 32 titles, all of the form "(Gnam chos) dpal rno bkra las …" in the Gnam chos prints (MW21578, the manuscript MW1ER167) and one collection from Zhe chen, and one for «རྣོ་བཀྲའི་ལོ་རྒྱུས», whose full title reads «gnam chos thugs kyi gter kha snyan brgyud zab mo las dpal rno bkra'i lo rgyus», the history of the Dpal rno bkra from the Gnam chos' "deep oral transmission, the heart's treasure-store". So the heading says that the rites that follow are taken from the Dpal rno bkra part of the treasure; what the name means, and why this part of the Gnam chos is so called, is told in that history (pp. 493–494, not read) and nowhere in the pages read.

The scan of p. 558 settles the machine's «ང་མར་མོ»: it is «དམར་མོ» (dmar mo), the line breaking after «དམར». The rite is to recite the mantra over «དམར་མོ་འདས་པའི་གདུང་ཐལ», the cremation ashes of a dead dmar mo, and later (machine, line 2 end read «དེ་ནས་དམར་མོ» on the scan) to smear these ashes on the five places of the body. The word is the feminine of the colour name; whether it means a red woman, a red one as a name of a being (the same section has «king kang dmar po» and «chos skyong rgyal po rnam sras dmar po», both with the masculine dmar po) or the colour of the ashes, nothing on the page says. The pool has no lexicon entry to settle it: the dictionaries of Steinert's collection were not at hand (the 4.5 GB clone is not in the scratch), so «dmar mo» is not looked up there.

**RD315: the history is read; «dmar mo» stays open.** The history of the Dpal rno bkra (vol. 1 pp. 493–494, gnc.1:p493-494, machine with the layout checked by eye) answers what the name stands for, though not what the word means: «Rno bkra» is the name of a volume (glegs bam) that the Great Compassionate One gave, after all the Buddhas had spoken it, to the queen 'Od ldan rgyal mo when she asked; the giving was met by an earthquake and hail, the lesser gods died or were struck down, the middling fainted, the great fled; the text warns that a wrong view of it brings the wrathful Hayagrīva's crushing of body and la, that its name alone frees from the lower realms, and says it was spoken in person by the Great Orgyan on the 23rd of a month of a fire-monkey year. The Dpal rno bkra is therefore the Gnam chos' name for the teaching cycle that comes from this volume, and the dream examination of p. 558 is one of its sections; the word «rno bkra» itself is not glossed in the history. BDRC's full-text search for «དམར་མོའི་གདུང་ཐལ» and «དམར་མོ་ཤི་བའི་རོ་བསྲེགས་པའི་ཐལ» (2026-11-10) finds no hit either; «dmar mo» is not explained anywhere read.
