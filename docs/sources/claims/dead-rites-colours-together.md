# The white, speckled and black rites are parts of one trigram's rite, not a choice by colour

On pp. 105–108 WB gives, for each of the eight trigrams in turn, an ordinary remedy, then "the white rite", "the speckled one" and "the black rite" one after the other, each with its own recipient, food and fee, and then the grave-breaking and the years cut: they read as parts of one rite done together, and no line on pp. 104–126 says that the colour of the thirteenth point's verdict, or of any pebble, picks one of them. Mipham's notes (p. 517) count them as three pebbles among each trigram's six ("the white, black and speckled rites counted one each"), which fits that reading: each is a remedy pebble of its own. How full the whole is turns on the household's means (p. 114: full or short, the *g.yo 'khrug* and chanted rite if the means suffice, each one's root rite if not). The verdict of the thirteenth point sizes the rites great, middling or small (p. 50); which colour asks for which size is still not stated anywhere on these pages. Narrows questions 212 and 213; it does not answer them.
