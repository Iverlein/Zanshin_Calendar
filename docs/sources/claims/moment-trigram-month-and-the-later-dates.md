# The moment's trigram: the month's triad, and the later dates, against the Vaiḍūrya's «ལོ་ལ»

The *Vaiḍūrya* volume's line (quoted in ys.2:i016-017) «སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་ལོ་ལ་སྤར་ཁ་ལི་ཡིན་པས་ཟླ་བ་ཡང་ལི། ཚེས་ཀྱང་གཅིག་ལ་ལི། དུས་ཚོད་ཀྱང་ནམ་ལངས་ལི་ཡིན» says, with «ལོ་ལ» ("for the year"), that when the year is of the tiger, horse and dog triad its trigram is li, so the month, the first date and the dawn hour are li too: a chain from the year down to the hour. The pool cannot tell from it alone whether the triad is the year's or the month's; what the other witnesses say:

- WB's own almanac chapter gives the rule with no noun for the animal and sets it beside the mewa by month (the first four months 1 white, the middle four 4 green, the last four 7 red): the date's trigram and mewa "rise on the dates" by the month's animal (WB vol. 1, p. 178, etext only; lunar-day-signs.md).
- Mipham's notes (wb.2:p535-537, read on the scan) have the same clause with the noun set in it: «སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་ཟླ་བ་ལི་ཡིས་ཚེས», the tiger, horse and dog *months* begin their dates with li, and then, from that date, "turning in the order of the trigrams" (འབྱུང་ལུགས, 'byung lugs), the trigram of each date is taken as that date's first, the day's span divided into eight parts, one trigram rising in each, and the night likewise. So after the first date the trigram moves on one place for each date, and within a date it runs through the eight in the same order.
- A verse WB gives as "another's view" in the household reckoning has the same four-step chain, year, month, date and hour, each led by li (the year and month of the horse and snake [«རྟ་སྦྲུལ»], the date of a spring hare date, the dawn hour li; vol. 1, p. 301, etext only); the *Vaiḍūrya*'s chain is of this kind.
- The regent (reply 157) does not say which animals; he quotes the line only to say the Phugpa practice rests on Duhar Nagpo.

So the notes and the almanac's rule, read the triad as the month's, with the date's trigram rotating forward by date and the hour's by eighths of the day and night. The *Vaiḍūrya*'s «ལོ་ལ» is a doubt, not a conflict: read as the year's, it would make the first month li only in years of that triad, which no other text says. No print of the volume was found (the full-text search finds the line only in two prints of the regent's replies); see the dead end in ys.2:i016-017's file.
