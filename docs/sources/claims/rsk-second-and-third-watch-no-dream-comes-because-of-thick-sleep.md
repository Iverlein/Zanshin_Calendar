# The Sakya manual's «no dream comes» in the second and third watches means that none arises, for thick sleep; the last watch alone is examined (q1072)

The Sakya manual (Nam mkha' dpal bzang) says, after quoting the Mañjuśrī root tantra's four watches (phlegm, bile, wind, true): the first watch, the evening part, is mixed with the habits of the day's appearances and is a little unclear (ཐུན་དང་པོ་སྲོད་ཀྱི་ཆ་ཉིན་བར་གྱི་སྣང་བའི་བག་ཆགས་དང་འདྲེས་ཅུང་ཟད་མི་གསལ); the second and third watches are the midnight part, thick sleep, so no dream comes (ཐུན་གཉིས་པ་དང་གསུམ་པ་ནམ་གུང་གི་ཆ་གཉིད་འཐུག་པོ་ཞེས་རྨི་ལམ་མི་འབྱུང); the last watch, the dawn part, has the dream very clear, so it is to be examined (rsk:p459-461). «མི་འབྱུང» says that no dream arises, and the reason is given: the sleep is thick. It does not say that a dream of those watches is not to be counted; it is the manual's account of why the last watch is the one examined, and it passes over the root tantra's bile and wind dreams of the middle watches. The Drukpa manuals reach the same practice by another reason (dkb:p163-164: the first three of the four parts of the night are not certain, so the examination is in the fourth).
