# Rin spungs pa's «ལུས་ཡང་ཤིང་ཆེ་བ» on the boy sign is a second word on the body, not the raised right side

Rin spungs pa's gloss of ch. 2's boy sign (st:p21) is «མའི་ལུས་གཡས་མཐོ་ཞིང་། མའི་རྨི་ལམ་དུའང་སྐྱེས་པ་ཡང་ཡང་མཐོང་བ་དང་། མ་དེ་ཉིད་ལུས་ཡང་ཤིང་ཆེ་བ། ནུ་ཞོའང་གཡས་པ་སྔ་ལ་འབབ་པར་འགྱུར་རོ» (Dri med kun gsal, BDRC I4707, etext p. 83, printed p. 57; etext only). The question was whether «ཆེ་བ» (che ba) there is the raised right side or a second property of the body.

What the page settles. The raised right side has its own clause before the dream: «མའི་ལུས་གཡས་མཐོ་ཞིང་», "the mother's body high on the right". The next clause begins again with «མ་དེ་ཉིད», "the mother herself", and then has «ལུས་ཡང་ཤིང་ཆེ་བ», "the body light and large". So ཆེ་བ is not the raised side of the first clause; it stands with ཡང (light) as a second word on the body. The girl's gloss on the same page pairs the raised left flank with «ལུས་ལྕི་བའི་ཉམས་དང་ལྡན་ཞིང་», "with the feeling of a heavy body", and no «ཆེ་བ»: ཡང is the light that stands against the heavy, as in Byang pa (boy «ལུས་ཡང་ཞིང་», girl «ལུས་ལྕི་བ», mixed «རེས་འགའ་ཡང་ཞིང་རེས་འགའ་ལྕི་བ»), and ཆེ་བ is Rin spungs pa's addition. Byang pa and the Blue Beryl (body «རང་བཞིན་གྱི་ཡང་བ», by nature light) have no ཆེ་བ.

What it does not settle. Where the word comes from and how it is meant. The Moonbeams, on the Eight Limbs' parallel verse, has «དཀུ་གཡས་ལོགས་མཐོ་ཞིང་ཆེ་བ», the flank on the right high and large, so ཆེ་བ there goes with the raised flank; Rin spungs pa may have moved it to the body, or meant the body large with child, or stout; his own sentence does not say. BDRC's exact search for «ལུས་ཡང་ཤིང་ཆེ་བ» finds the sentence in one place only (a chapter etext of Dri med kun gsal itself): no other commentary has it (2026-11-10). The Tantra's line has no ཆེ་བ.
