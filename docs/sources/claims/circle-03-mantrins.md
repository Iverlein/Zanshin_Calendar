# The circles: the third, the yellow snake, for mantra-holders

The third of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the yellow snake of existence, is read for mantra-holders. Its starting point: the snake of the friend element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: whether the golden vajra has gone up into the sky; whether the bell's sweet voice is lost; whether the maṇḍala's lustre has faded; whether the blade-tip of the ritual dagger (ཕུར་པ, phur pa) is blunted; whether the ḍākinīs' punishment comes.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the golden vajra not gone up into the sky, majestic: life firm, a good holder of the line; even without diligence in approach and accomplishment, blessing and power gather like clouds; no break of the pledges: supremely good. | the golden vajra has leaked up into the sky: though the mantra line was good at first, there is a fear that the line-holder will be cut off midway; by the fault of a broken or polluted pledge an obstacle to life may come. | approach and accomplishment, the dagger practice, recitation, fulfilment and confession to the protectors, ablution, the rite of mending broken pledges. |
| Body | the bell's sweet voice not broken: no illness, a good line-holder; appearance and existence gathered under his power. | the bell's voice broken and spoiled: bodily illness, the mantrin's line corrupted; strive now at empowerment and approach as he may, his power is small. | paint or erect an image of the yidam, take the feast-torma and self-entry, the rite of fulfilling what is broken, clearing the path with *tsatsa*, reading the *Tog gzungs*, reciting the "hundred thousand" aloud. |
| Power | the maṇḍala's lustre has not faded and turns to light; the son better than the father; by fierce mantras the eight classes and spirits are made servants, enemies and obstructors liberated into the space of reality. | the lustre fades: though the father struck his dagger into rock, the son cannot even into a sand-hill; great obstacles when he brings out his power; food and wealth lost; a risk the retreat house will stand empty. | move the place of practice and strive at meditation, recite fierce mantras, read the *Rin chen g.yang ra brtsegs mdo*, strive at the rites that restore the power. |
| Luck | the dagger's blade not blunted: all enemies and obstructors pressed down and liberated swiftly by the power of wrathful action; fame and power at their height. | the blade blunted: the line was good before, but on oneself gossip and much talk, the curses, dagger-blows and spells of others may come. | the rite of turning back spells, fulfilling and confessing, ablution, transforming the crowd, the ransom rite against gossip, offering a hundred thousand lamps and the like. |
| Mewa | the ḍākinīs' word not cut, the sound of *hūṃ* undiminished like the sun rising in the sky; a good line; the enemy's spells and curses do not come, and if they come are surely turned back; young knowledge-holders flourish. | the ḍākinīs' punishment comes, the *hūṃ* diminishes: from now on curses fall on the knowledge-holder; the time has come for the jewels, the yidam and protectors one relies on to be shot with arrows and stones (that is, offended); a spell is buried in a spring or a stūpa, so rises and declines mixed before. | rot the spells in their nest, turn the hurled daggers back by their face, strike down the fierce mantra's own executioner, strive at the profound instructions, teach and do fulfilment and confession fiercely. If it meets the nine-eye it is very bad. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
