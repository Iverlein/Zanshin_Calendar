# The Mañjuśrī root tantra's verse of the four watches: phlegm, bile, wind, the fourth true (RD370)

The Derge Kanjur's Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa ('Jam dpal rtsa rgyud, Toh 543), in its 15th chapter, gives one verse of four lines that names four watches (ཐུན, thun) of the night for a dream and what each is: the dream of the first watch comes from phlegm (བད་ཀན, bad kan), that of the second from bile (མཁྲིས་པ, mkhris pa), described as «འཇིག་རྟེན་ལ་ཡོད་སྨད་པ་ཡིན» (blamed, of the world; the sense is a doubt), that of the third from wind (རླུང, rlung), and the fourth "comes true" (བདེན་པར་འབྱུང་བ, bden par 'byung ba). The Sanskrit of Gaṇapati Śāstrī's edition has the same four lines, with "born of truth" (satya-sambhavā) for the fourth; its Devanagari was read only in OCR, so the Sanskrit words are a doubt. So the rule of the four watches that the Sngags rim chen mo cites is in the root tantra, and the root tantra counts the watches of the night as four: it says "the first", "the second", "the third" and "the fourth" (དང་པོའི་ཐུན, གཉིས་པ, གསུམ་པ, བཞི་པ) and in the detailed lists "the first watch", "the second watch", "the third watch". The verse does not say how long a watch is, nor where the night's four parts begin; the rite before it names the first watch only (mmk.1:p397).
