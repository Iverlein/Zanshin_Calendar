# The black hal khyi's five mothers and five sons hold 22 days each, 110 in all

On p. 185 the black hal khyi (no. 16) goes with five mothers and five sons, who move two by two (mother and son together); when each pair stays twenty-two days the round is a hundred and ten days (5 x 22). A note in small type gives a hundred and four days for the Rin chen kun 'dus, the Lung kun 'dus and the Lha rgyud gsal sgron; the Stong shes says the same as the note. The print numbers nine places, 17 to 25, after the hal khyi's own 16. Where the pairs sit is not said on the page: WB names the seat of the hal khyi alone (the previous year's wind-horse). Settles the first half of the old question 36; the second half stays open.
