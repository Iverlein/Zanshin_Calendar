# The circles: the fifth, the elephant, for ministers and officials

The fifth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the steadfast elephant, is read for uncles, ministers and officials. Its starting point: the ox of the friend element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points, turned from the ox of the friend element of the one reckoned: whether parted from the lord's affection; whether the god's punishment has come; whether the records' gold and turquoise are lost; whether the magic arrow and spear are broken; whether the ministers' council seat is empty.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | not parted from the king's loving affection: he carries out the lord's work, his intelligence knows no end, the subjects set in happiness and glory; no obstacle now: good. | parted: though the forefathers flourished, an obstacle to life has come, a sri is there; the minister's line may be cut off. | press down the sri with the hundred hurled weapons of *kham*; turn back the enemy, read the "hundred thousand" against the enemy sri (དགྲ་སྲི, dgra sri). |
| Body | the god one relies on sends no punishment and protects; the minister's line flourishes, the war god (དགྲ་ལྷ, dgra lha) stands behind as friend: good. | the protectors' punishment: a chronic illness, or a burden beyond strength, an obstacle of illness, weapon or wound; a sri has risen, a sign the clan's line will be cut. | honour the gods, press down the sri, the ransom rite against gossip, fasten a sri-protection. |
| Power | the records' gold and turquoise not lost: food, wealth, enjoyments and all wishes grow. | the records and gold and turquoise lost, somewhat bad; a great flourishing at first, but short; a fierce punishment from the lord of men, or loss of food and wealth, a risk of rise and decline among the subjects. | summon fortune, press down the sri, recite the *Nor rgyun* and *Gser 'od rnam rgyal*. |
| Luck | arrow and spear unbroken, courage great; life long, illness rare, wishes fulfilled. | arrow and spear broken: the la (བླ, bla) of the father and uncles and of oneself declined and lost; an obstacle to life feared. | ransom the la, the gods' praise, summon fortune and ransom the life. |
| Mewa | the minister's council seat not empty, great fame; his seat of law not empty, the minister keeps the king's realm; subjects in concord. | the council seat empty: hostile talk, the policy fails, fear of losing records, gold and turquoise; risk of death for nephew, sons and sisters. | press down gossip and stir the pit, recite the five great dhāraṇīs much. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
