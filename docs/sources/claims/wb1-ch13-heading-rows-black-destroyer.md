# Ch. 13, pp. 142-143: the heading rows are the black earth-destroyer's dates by time of day, 28 of 30 dates settled by the verse

The twelve heading cells over the first strip charts (p. 142, six cells; p. 143, six cells) are legible on the Gser thang reprint at 3x to 6x, unlike the ring charts' narrow cells, and what they are is settled by a check: they are the dates-by-time table of the earth's destroyer, the black one (sa'i phung byed nag mo), the verse of vol. 2, p. 222 (year-month-earth-lords.md, the five further lords, no. 3), which gives each of the thirty dates a time of day. Each cell names a time (nam langs, nyi shar, nyi phyed, myur smad, sa srod, srod with nam byings, nam phyed, nam phyed yol, tho rengs, nyi nub) and lists the dates that go with it.

Every cell read agrees with the verse: daybreak 13 and 26, sunrise 14, noon 1, 15 and 27, late afternoon 16 and 29, nightfall 19 and 30, evening and deep night 2, 4 and 11, midnight 3, 10, 12, 17, 18, 20 and 25, after midnight 22, dawn 6, 8, 9, 21, 23 and 28, sunset 24. That is 28 of the 30 dates, each once. The two missing, 5 and 7 (the morning's warmth and the sun's full strength), belong to the two unsettled places: the cell headed nyi rgas on p. 142 (two glyphs that look like 15, a repeat of the noon cell's, where the verse needs 5 and 7) and one glyph above 24 in p. 143's sunset cell. The check turns the strip charts' heading rows from numerals taken from a blot into dates the text itself spells out; it does not tell what the chart cells below them (nang bdud, snang sel and the rest) are filled by.
