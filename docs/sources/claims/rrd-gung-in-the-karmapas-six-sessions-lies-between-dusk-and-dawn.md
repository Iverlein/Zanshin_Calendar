# In the Karmapa's counsel for retreat «gung» is the fifth of six sessions, after dusk and before dawn, for the clear light (RD384)

In the third Karmapa's own counsel for retreat the day is cut into six sessions, and «གུང» (gung) is the one between dusk and dawn in which the clear light is trained (RD384, 2026-11-10, etext only).

In the counsel in vol. 5 (etext p. 111-112) the person who practises "divides into six sessions (ཐུན་དྲུག, thun drug)": the early morning (སྔ་དྲོ, snga dro) for body exercise and holding the breath; the gro kha (གྲོ་ཁ, the time of day is a doubt) for resting in the lama's yoga and looking at the mind's essence; the afternoon (ཉི་མ་རྒས, nyi ma rgas) for body exercise and breath again; "then at dusk (སྲོད, srod) the yoga of the yidam, mantra, torma"; "then at gung (གུང) train in the clear light (འོད་གསལ, 'od gsal)"; "then at the time of dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) the limbs of purifying sin and gathering merit, and the lama's yoga". So in the Karmapa's own word gung is the fifth session, after dusk and before dawn, and is the session of the night for the clear light, the practice of the sleeping hours; it is not noon. The counsel does not say "the middle of the night" in words; the place of gung between dusk and dawn is from the order of the sessions.

The same three names as in the dream verses (rrd.5:p57-59), where the middle part is «ནམ་ཕྱེད» (nam phyed): the two works use two words for the middle of the night, and no text of his says one for the other; the order of the sessions is what makes the equation likely.
