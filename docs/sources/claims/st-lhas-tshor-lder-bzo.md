# ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ and ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ in ST ch. 81: what the other prints and the commentaries read

The two phrases of ch. 81's dream lines that the Serthang scan leaves unsettled, as the commentaries and the other prints read them (RD255, 2026-11-09; etext only, no scan opened), and, in the last section, what the Bhutan print's and the 2005 Supplement's scans read (RD264, 2026-11-09).

**ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ (lhas tshor dus te).** No witness read in the etext has ལུས་ for ལྷས་: the Serthang print (`st:p350-351`), the Lhasa 1982 print (BDRC MW16778, a search snippet), the 'Bri gung *Dka' mchan* (`bdk:p580-581`), Sde srid's Supplement in both prints (`lth:p325`, `lhq:p509`), Khro ru (`ktr:p329-332`), the Bhutan print (`mnb:p267`) and the commentaries that follow Khro ru all keep ལྷས་, and BDRC's full-text search for «ལུས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» found no hit. So the guess that ལྷས་ is a misprint for ལུས་ finds no support in these texts. The Supplement's 2005 print (`lth:p325`) and the Bhutan print (`mnb:p267`; OCR) read ངུས་ (ngus) for དུས་ (dus), a variant in two witnesses, against དུས in the Supplement's 1991 print (`lhq:p509`), the Serthang scan, the Lhasa 1982 print and every commentary; whether ངུས is the prints' own or the OCR's fault is open until the scans are read. No commentary says what ལྷས་ is. The 18th-century annotator of Sde srid's Supplement, Sman pa Blo bzang dbang rgyal (`mbl:p521-522`), has the gloss «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» (the time of the feeling) written into the line, and Khro ru's gloss is the same in nearly the same words. Khro ru takes the clause whole as «the time of the feeling» (ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས, with the nominal ཚོར་བ, "feeling"), the time in which the signs listed are the sign of a spirit striking, and says of the sister clause «རང་ཚོར་དུས་ཡིན་» that it is the time one feels oneself, so that one is sure to be struck by a spirit (`ktr:p329-332`, p. 332). The Drikung has the verse without a gloss.

**ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ (lder bzo 'gul).** The spelling with བཟོ (bzo) is the Serthang scan's, the Lhasa 1982 print's (search snippet), the Drikung's (`bdk:p580-581`), Khro ru's (`ktr:p329-332`) and Sde srid's Supplement's in both prints (`lth:p325`, `lhq:p509`). The spelling with སོ (so) is the Bhutan print's (`mnb:p267`, OCR) and, as a machine reading, the Drathang print's (`st:p350-351`). Two spellings, then, and the weight of the witnesses read is on བཟོ. No commentary says what ལྡེར་ (lder) is: Khro ru glosses the three words together as «a temple shaking» (ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ་བ, with འགུལ་བ, "moves"), a sign of a male spirit (ཕོ་གདོན), and the Drikung puts the same gloss inside the line.

## What the scans of the other prints read (RD264, 2026-11-09)

Three prints, each on its scan by eye at 5–16×, against the Serthang scan (`st:p350-351`; the Serthang crops read again). The cited passages are `mnb:p267` (the Bhutan print of the Instruction Tantra, img. 267) and `lth:p325` (the 2005 print of Sde srid's Supplement, p. 325, archive.org's lending copy).

**ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ.** Every scan read agrees: the Serthang scan, the Bhutan scan and the 2005 Supplement's scan all have ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ; ལྷས is ལྷ with ས on each, and ལུས is nowhere. The variant ངུས (ngus) of the RD255 claim above has no scan behind it: it is in the Bhutan print's etext and in the 2005 etext only, and on both scans the syllable is དུས (ད, ུ, ས). So ངུས is the OCR's fault in two etexts, not a reading of either print. What ལྷས means is still said by no text; the commentaries' gloss «the time of the feeling» (Khro ru and the annotator of the Supplement) stays the only help.

**ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ / ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ.** The scans split by tradition, not by chance. བཟོ: the Serthang scan (a boxed བ and ཟ under ོ) and the 2005 Supplement's scan (typeset, the prefix བ and ཟ with ོ clear), with the 1991 Supplement and the Drikung, Khro ru and Sde srid's etexts. སོ: the Bhutan scan (one glyph ས with ོ and no separate བ, img. 267, 16× with the contrast stretched) and, as a machine reading, the Drathang woodblock print. So the Bhutan print and the Drathang print, two prints of the Instruction Tantra, read ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ, and the Serthang print and the Supplement (which versifies the chapter) read ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ. No text and no scan gives a sense for ལྡེར (lder) alone; Khro ru's «a temple shaking» for the three words is the only gloss.

The sister clause on the Bhutan scan reads «རང་ཚོར་དུས་ཡིན» (the etext's ངང་ is again its OCR; moderate confidence for the first letter, `mnb:p267`), so the pair ལྷས་ཚོར་ / རང་ཚོར་ stands in that print as in the Serthang print.

What is unchanged: the guess that ལྷས is a misprint for ལུས finds no scan or etext that has ལུས, so it has no support.

## RD271 (2026-11-09)

The dictionaries and BDRC's search say more than the commentaries: ལྡེར་བཟོ (lder bzo) is a clay statue, so the phrase is "the temple's clay statues move", and ལྡེར་སོ is a known spelling of it (st-lder-bzo-is-the-temples-clay-statues); ལྷས is the god in the agent case, "felt by the god", against རང་ཚོར་, "felt by oneself" (st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god).
