# བྲོ་པའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད in ch. 7's verse: a levy of the bro being demanded; bro ba'i is the common spelling

The Drathang line «བྲོ་པའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད་བག་མ་ལན་པ» (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, BDRC etexts, no scan opened): the commentaries and the other prints say «བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད་བག་མ་ལེན་པ»; the words are the levy of the "bro" and taking a bride.

**Which letters.** BDRC's exact search for «བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ» has 23 hits: the 'Gso rig 'bum bzhi, the Bshad pa'i rgyud in two etexts, the 'Bri gung *Dka' mchan*, the Mes po'i zhal lung, the Bon Kangyur (a witness), a medical dictionary's index and others, all «བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད» ('bro ba'i khral 'ded) with ba and with འདེད; «བྲོ་པའི་ཁྲལ» has four hits, among them Byang pa's *Bdud rtsi'i chu rgyun* (whose verse has «བྲོ་པའི་ཁྲལ་འདོད་བག་མ་ལེན་པ», 'dod with o) and the Brda yig ming don gsal ba'i sgron me's list of words that begin with bro. So the Drathang's pa and Byang pa's 'dod are two minority spellings; «འདེད» is the common one (the verb "to drive, to collect (a debt, a levy)", Ives Waldo, Rangjung Yeshe, the Verbinator).

**The words.** བྲོ (bro) in the Tshig mdzod chen mo has four senses: the dance step, an oath, an old word for illness, and provisions for a journey (the last marked as a regional sense); the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs: the food one eats on the road. བྲོ་བ (bro ba) is the taste, the desire. ཁྲལ (khral): a tax, levy, forced service, duty; also grief (Rangjung Yeshe and others). བྲོ་པ (bro pa) is a dancer (Ives Waldo; Tshig mdzod: one who dances the bro). Khro ru's gloss, "the share of food and goods" (read in the Four Tantras' commentaries already pooled), goes with the provisions sense. The Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo (a medical dictionary, BDRC I00EGS1017132, an etext read as OCR; the entry's columns are run together by the OCR) has «བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད» as a headword glossed with meat levy and the like, demanded, as in the hand cut off, the fine of the law, the tax imposed; a modern medical glossary (Krung go'i gso rig kun 'dus, a search snippet) explains it as the heavy exaction of «bro ba»'s levy and quotes the *'Grel chen dri med kun gsal*.

**The sense.** "A levy of provisions (or the like) being demanded", an exaction as of a government; a dream of being taxed, with taking a bride and the other life events of the list. The dancer's tax (བྲོ་པ) is no text's; the dictionaries do not decide between "provisions" and "an oath", and the glosses agree on the levy. The line is a sign in a list of unlucky events, not a rule; which "bro" it is is not settled in the sources found.
