# WB ch. 13: the nine spirits placed round the twelve animals (pp. 122–125)

Pages 122 to 125 of WB's chapter 13 hold twelve framed strips, one for each animal in the order rat, ox, tiger, hare, dragon, snake, horse, sheep, monkey, bird, dog, pig (the woodcut names the animal; there are no captions). Round the woodcut run twelve cells (four on the left, two above, two below, four on the right), and in every strip nine of them hold the nine names, each exactly once: sme ba bla mkhyen (སྨེ་བ་བླ་མཁྱེན), ka la ro zan (ཀ་ལ་རོ་ཟན), gshed po (གཤེད་པོ), rgyal po mtshon thogs (རྒྱལ་པོ་མཚོན་ཐོགས), nang bdud (ནང་བདུད), gshin rje (གཤིན་རྗེ), byis pa rta bdud (བྱིས་པ་རྟ་བདུད), stag gzig (སྟག་གཟིག), nag mo (ནག་མོ). Which cell holds which name differs from strip to strip; the placement of every strip is in the passage, and it has not been compared with any other table of the chapter. This claim records only that the placement is a table of twelve strips of nine names, with the cells in the passage.

What the strips are for is not said on these pages. Earlier readings of the chapter list the same nine names among the year's earth lords and their charts; a reader should compare the strips with those, and not infer a rule here.
