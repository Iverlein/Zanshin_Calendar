# The eight branches of the one-day vow

The eight branches of the one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas), in Mnga' ris paN chen's root verse with Lo chen Dharma shrI's commentary (dpn:i70, scan), after the *Treasury* and its commentary (akb:i365-366, etext):

| Branch | Gives up | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | the four roots; Lo chen names "unchastity and the rest", the commentary on the *Treasury* counts the four "down to giving up false speech" | morality |
| 5 | beer, intoxicating drink | heedfulness |
| 6 | large and high beds or seats | ascetic observance |
| 7 | dance, song and music, with garlands, perfume and ointment | ascetic observance |
| 8 | food at the wrong time (in the afternoon) | ascetic observance |

The eight are pledged to the end of one day and night, not for good; kept for life they make the lay devotee of Gomin's kind (གོ་མིའི་དགེ་བསྙེན, go mi'i dge bsnyen; dpn:i70-71, etext). The four roots are, in Lo chen's order of the lay devotee's roots (dpn:i71, etext): taking life, taking what is not given, wrong sexual conduct, false speech; for the one-day vow unchastity stands for wrong conduct, as in the root text's list of the novice's ten trainings (killing, stealing, unchastity, lying; etext p. 9, q1277).
