# What Zhabs dkar tells lay patrons: the maṇi always, prostrations and circumambulation when free, the fasting practice and the one-day vow on the full moon, the new moon and the eighth

Zhabs dkar ba Tshogs drug rang grol's last advice, as the account of the end of his life reports it (zkt:p582, etext only), tells the patrons (ཡོན་བདག, yon bdag), the lay people of his following, in one run what to do:

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| always | no theft or robbery, no pointless quarrels, help in the village's good and bad times; no killing of beings without need; kindness to household, servants, the dog and the cattle; a gift to every beggar at the door, "Dharma done in passing" |
| whenever there is time | prostrations and circumambulations (ཕྱག་སྐོར, phyag skor), and whatever recitation one knows |
| always | counting the maṇi (མ་ཎི, ma Ni) |
| the full moon, the new moon and the eighth, "the three" («ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ») | the fasting practice (སྨྱུང་གནས, smyung gnas) and the one-day vow of eight branches (བསྙེན་གནས་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པ, bsnyen gnas yan lag brgyad pa) |

- For whom: lay patrons; the monks are told their college's discipline, the mantra holders (སྔགས་པ, sngags pa) the tenth day and the Dharma sessions without a break.
- Benefit: a verse he quotes, that the way of men practised well leaves the gods' land not far and, relied on, liberation near.
- Tradition: the advice speaks in turn to the monks of the colleges and retreat centres, to the mantra holders and to the patrons; the collection is listed by BDRC under Zhabs dkar's name (P287, 1781–1851). His school is not stated on the pages read.

Only the fast days are tied to lunar days; the maṇi, prostrations, circumambulation and giving are for every day. No lamp offering and no freeing of lives is named here. Rank 3; etext only, the page image was not served.
