# The notes' nine spud pa and six rgyug are WB's six spud of the going and the nine spud pa rite, with the ku kung three

«སྤུད་པ་དགུ་རྒྱུག་དྲུག» in Mipham's note (wb.2:p517) is the notes' summary of WB's own count on p. 51, where the remedy pebbles run: the trigram's fourteen; the year's trigram and the rest, ten; the sweeping of the corpse's traces, twenty-four; «རྒྱུ་ཡི་སྤུད་དྲུག», the six *spud* of the going; the mewa's nine (etext p. 59, printed p. 51). So «རྒྱུག» is the notes' spelling of «རྒྱུ་ཡི» "of the going" (the dead's going), and the six are WB's six *spud*; «སྤུད་པ་དགུ» is the name of a rite of WB p. 111, "the nine *spud pa* of China" (རྒྱ་ཡི་སྤུད་པ་དགུ་གདུག་གཏོ), done for a death on the four executioners or on *zin* (the rite is in the passage of p. 111, ch29-rites). The contents list of the notes' colophon names the same two, «གཤིན་རྒྱུའི་སྦུད་པ་དགུ», "the nine *sbud pa* of the dead's going" (wb.2:p544, a reading of the 1996 etext). The notes' clause then lists the deaths: the four executioners and *zin* (WB p. 111), entering the grave, and «ཀུ་ཀུང་ལ་གསུམ», the three for *ku kung*: WB p. 112 gives the *kung kung* after-strike in three, the four *ra ba* dying on the 6th a sky grave, the four *'bring* on the 16th a middle grave, the four *tha chung* on the 26th an earth grave. The *Garland of Blue Utpalas* has a different three for the same name, the *ku kung* "bad after" on *khon*, "following after" on *khen* and "turned back after" on *gin* (ut:p302-304). What a *spud* is in itself no text gives (the dictionaries and BDRC were searched for it in earlier readings).
