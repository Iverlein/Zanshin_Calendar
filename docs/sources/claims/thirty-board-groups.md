# The thirty: the eight pebbles in three groups, and the order of the checks

The eight pebbles of a starting point are also read as a board in three groups (WB vol. 1, p. 471; the notes, vol. 2, p. 533): the first, third and fifth are the upper three, the place of the forebears and the first part of life; the second, fourth and sixth the lower three, oneself and the last part of life; the seventh and eighth the middle, harm by spirits and wealth. Within a group of three the greater share decides: two white and one black read good (the notes, p. 533).

That this numbering is the order of the eight fighting pebbles of p. 453 is an inference, not a statement of the text: the upward places (the fourth, eleventh and seventh upward) stand first, third and fifth, the downward places second, fourth and sixth, and the year reached itself, with its power and luck, seventh and eighth, so "upper" falls on the pebbles counted upward and "lower" on those counted downward, and Dharmaśrī's *Moonbeams* lays a white pebble at an upward place and a black at a downward one (question 97). If it holds, the eight numbered checks of each point are the eight fighting pebbles in this order (claim thirty-eight-fighters). The readings do not all agree with the grouping: in several points the first two checks both speak of the forefathers' or the father's and uncles' place (points 1, 2, 6). Which pebble answers which check stays open (question 306); the words «ཡར» and «མར» tie the board's "three up" and "three down" to the pebbles counted up and down (claim thirty-check-order-yar-mar).
