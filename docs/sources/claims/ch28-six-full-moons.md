# The Pleiades against the full moon of the tenth month

WB vol. 2, pp. 27–28: on the 15th of the tenth month, in the evening as the moon rises, the place of the Pleiades (སྨིན་དྲུག, smin drug) against the moon reads the year: ahead of the moon, a bad year, bad for livestock, human illness; behind it, strife and wind; south of it, a great drought, grass and wood scarce, famine; north of it, a good year, much rain, peace, the farm wins; close together, very good. The figure on p. 28 gives the same.

The date is the calendar's; the Pleiades' place against the full moon follows from the moon's position, though the text treats it as something seen. The text calls it "the six full moons" and names only the tenth month's.
