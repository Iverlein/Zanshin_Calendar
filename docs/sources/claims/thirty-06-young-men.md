# The thirty: the sixth point, the striped Chinese tiger, for young men

The sixth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the striped Chinese tiger (རྒྱ་སྟག་ཁྲ་བོ, rgya stag khra bo), is read for young men (སྐྱེས་དར, skyes dar). Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the hat-helmet has fallen into the hearth («ཐབ་ཏུ», thab tu; the reading has «རྦབ», the precipice) | It has not fallen down the precipice | It has fallen: a great rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud) in the father's and uncles' place; in one's own place much shame | — |
| 2 | Whether the supreme white bow is broken | Not broken, good | The bow is broken, bad for oneself | Carry the protection that raises the life force (སྲོག་བསྐྱེད་བསྲུང་བ, srog bskyed bsrung ba) |
| 3 | Whether the weapon has been carried off by the btsan spirit (བཙན, btsan) | Courage is complete; the sharp knife is not eaten by the btsan | The weapon sheath is carried off by the btsan: bad for the long knife and the short; little courage | The rite of raising the life force and the like |
| 4 | Whether the mount's saddle has fled | It has not | The mount's saddle has fled: harm comes to the innocent («མ་ཉེས་མགོ་ནས་ཁམ་ཆུ», not understood) and thieves come | Press down the enemy sri (དགྲ་སྲི, dgra sri) |
| 5 | Whether the spear is broken at its middle | The spear is not broken; good for sons and grandsons | The spear is broken at its middle, bad | Service rites (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) for sons and grandsons |
| 6 | Whether the stripe of the tiger skin has faded | Good for sons, sisters, wives and the tiger skin's place | The stripe has faded: bad for sons, sisters, wives and the tiger skin's place | — |
| 7 | Whether the leopard-skin bow case has parted from the bow | Not parted; good for food, wealth and clothes | Parted: food and wealth there now and gone now | Send the golden-light protecting prosperity (གཡང་སྐྱབས, g.yang skyabs) and the livestock ransom (གླུད, glud); that helps greatly |
| 8 | Whether the strap of the cane shield (སྤ་ཕུབ, spa phub) is cut | Not cut; good for servants and retinue | Cut: bad for subjects, retinue and servants | — |

Whose year is reckoned: the person's own (the notes, vol. 2, p. 533: «གཞན་རྣམས་སོ་སོའི་ལོ་རྟ»).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
