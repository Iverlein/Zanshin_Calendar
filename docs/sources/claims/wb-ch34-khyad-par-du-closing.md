# ཁྱད་པར་དུ in the closing verse singles out the person's rise and decline, no ranking stated

«ཁྱད་པར་དུ» in ch. 34's closing verse (p. 428) is "above all", "in particular": the line «ས་བདག་བཟང་ངན་ཁྱད་པར་དུ། ལས་བརྩམས་གང་ཟག་དེའི་དར་གུད། དེ་ལ་གནོད་པའི་སྐྱོན་ལ་སོགས། ཞིབ་ཏུ་བརྟགས་ལ་ཁ་དམར་སྨྲ།» singles the person's own rise and decline and what harms him out as the member to examine with most care, then gives the instruction to examine closely and give the verdict. The verse states no weights for the other members, and neither the Bhutan digest nor Dpal bsdus's *Rmad byung* (its chapter on rise and decline, p. 317: the rise and decline of the time, the fortune and the verdict matched with each other, «ཕན་ཚུན་བསྟུན་ཏེ་ཁ་དམར་སྨྲ») restates it as a ranking. So what the scan allows: the person's rise and decline is marked as the especially examined item; that it outranks every other factor in the weighing is not said. Unsettled beyond that.

RD66 (2026-10-28): the Bhutan digest (etext pp. 125–126) ends its own list of what is examined, nearly the same members, in the same order, as WB's closing verse, with «ཁྱད་པར་བྱེ་བྲག་གང་ཟག་སོ་སོ་ཡི། །ལས་བརྩམས་དེ་ལ་གནོད་པའི་སྐྱོན་ལ་སོགས། །ཞིབ་ཏུ་བརྟག་ལ་ཁ་དམར་མཁས་པས་སྨྲ།» (the etext's spelling is damaged; read so): "the particulars of each several person's beginning of the work, the faults that harm it and the rest: examine closely, and the skilled give the verdict." It puts the earth lords' good and bad (the «ས་བདག» of «ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་རྒྱུ་བའི་ས་བདག») before this line and writes «ཁྱད་པར་བྱེ་བྲག», the particulars, the distinctions, where WB writes «ཁྱད་པར་དུ». So the digest, the one reader of the verse found, takes the word as the turn from the general list to the case of each person, not as a weight over the list. This is the etext only, not read on a scan. Mipham's notes (the 1996 etext's pp. 482–549 searched) do not say anything of it. The question is settled as far as the texts go: «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» marks the person's own case as the particular last member; a rank over the others is not read.
