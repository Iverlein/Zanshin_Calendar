# མོ་ཕྱྭ in ch. 91: divination and its omen, one compound

The pair མོ་ཕྱྭ (mo phywa) heading the remedies of the spirit in ch. 91 (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, BDRC etexts, no scan opened): divination and the omen or fortune that it gives, one stock compound, not two remedies.

**The dictionaries.** ཕྱྭ (phywa) is a headword in Rangjung Yeshe (good fortune, luck), Jim Valby (luck; the positive force of a person), Ives Waldo (a sign, a divination; luck, fate), Dan Martin (lots and prophecy; a god of the world's fortunes; the one who reads omens), the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs (what is foretold by throwing the dice, and the fortune, གཡང, g.yang, of wealth and happiness), the Bod rgya nang don rig pa'i tshig mdzod (omen, fortune) and the Brda dkrol gser gyi me long (a worldly god; the diviner and the reader of omens, with the Zhi byed and Rin chen spungs pa glosses). The Tshig mdzod chen mo (no. 25) gives the first sense, omen (ལྟས, ltas), with the stock phrases «དགྲ་ཕྱྭ», «གྲོག་ཕྱྭ», «མོ་ཕྱྭ» (mo phywa, the divination's omen) and «ཕྱྭ་བཟང་པོ་འདུག» (a good omen is there), the second sense fortune (གཡང). So «མོ་ཕྱྭ» is named as a compound in the dictionary itself.

**BDRC's search.** «མོ་ཕྱྭ» has 146 hits, mostly in ritual texts where it comes beside «ཕྱྭ་གཡང» (omen and fortune); the pair is common outside medicine.

**For the passage.** In `st:p493` the remedies of the spirit are then divination with its omen (མོ་ཕྱྭ), the rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro), the dependent link (རྟེན་འབྲེལ, rten 'brel) and the mantra, so the "four" of q822 is a count of the earlier reading, not of the text; the second item asked for there is not a separate remedy. The sense does not need the earlier reading ཕྱུ, which no dictionary or etext has in this place. The Drathang root verse has the same pair without the brackets (`st:p493`).
