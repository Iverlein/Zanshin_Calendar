# The seven dream lines of the Nag rtsis part of Kun 'dus vol. 3 are WB's lines: each tied to the birth, the pebbles, the hour's element or the illness, none to a date

The Kun 'dus chen mo vol. 3 holds seven dream lines in Kun bzang blo gros's *Garland of Stars* (རྒྱུ་སྐར་ཕྲེང་བ, rgyu skar phreng ba), the supplement to the *Moonbeams* that restates the White Beryl's (WB) reckonings. Each is one line in a long stock of results, tied to something other than the day, and each has the same words in WB (BDRC's exact-phrase search finds every one in WB's 1996 etext, MW2CZ8040, and in its Gser thang reprint MW3CN5026; WB's page is the etext's image minus ten in vol. 1 and minus eight in vol. 2).

What the table shows: the dream is tied, in the Nag rtsis books, to the dreamer's birth (year, mewa), to the pebbles cast, to the element of the hour of the asking, or to the animal and mewa of an illness. Of the seven only the first and the fourth name a thing dreamt (a precipice; lying down «byes su»); the others say how the dream is, troubled, many, confused, rough, or compare goods to a dream. The Kun 'dus lines are read in the etext only. The three ties that are to a day or an hour (the hour's iron; the ox of the day; the mewa of falling ill) give no image. WB's page for the third and fourth rows was read in the etext only here; the pool's passages of wb.1:p428b, p431 and wb.2:p157-161 were read on the scan.
