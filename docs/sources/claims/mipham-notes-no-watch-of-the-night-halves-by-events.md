# Mipham's notes name no watch of the night; WB and the notes cut it by sunset, midnight and dawn

Mipham's notes on WB (vol. 2 pp. 474–541, etext pp. 482–549) contain no word for a watch of the night: a search of the etext for ཐུན, ནམ་ཕྱེད, མཚན་མོ, སྲོད, ཐོ་རངས, ཐོ་རེངས and ཉིན་མཚན finds ཐུན only in the hand reckoning's list (ཐུན་ཀཱང་, p. 539; ཐུན་མཚམས་རཱ་ཧུ and ཐུན་ཕྱེད, p. 542) and no bound of a watch.

The only bound of a part of the night he states is p. 532, on the sky dog's facing: "in the three autumn months, from sunset to midnight (ཉི་ནུབ་ནས་ནམ་ཕྱེད་བར)", repeating WB vol. 1 p. 375, where the day-and-night is cut at sunset, midnight, sunrise (dawn) and noon, the night into two halves. So the clock-like words WB and Mipham use for the night are events: sunset, midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད), dawn (ནམ་ལངས, ཐོ་རེངས); no watch.

Mipham's other writings, found by BDRC search of the phrases, use the sūtra's watches of the night only in a rite and a life-story (མཚན་ཐུན་གསུམ, "three night sessions" of a Mañjuśrī sādhana; «ནུབ་མོའི་ཐུན་དང་པོ», the first watch of the night in the *Nye ba'i sras brgyad rtogs brjod*): sessions, not bounds. The full text of his collected works was not searched by author.
