# The five graves: elements, combatants, verdict and on whom harm falls

The eighth examination: which of the five graves of the scattered reckoning the dead obtains (wb.2:p74-75; the notes, wb.2:p500-501, wb.2:p503-504).

The graves are the elements: burning fire, hiding in earth earth, sending to the mountain wood, *sgrub* (the corpse cut up) iron, casting into water water; the new school's five are the Indian fire, the Nepalese water, the Oḍḍiyāna substance, the Chinese *sgrub* and the Tibetan earth burials. Thirteen combatants decide each (the notes): the dead's clan element ("the king's row-seat"), the power of the year, month, day and hour of death, the elements of the planet and the mansion of the day of death, the dead's life force, body, power and luck, and the mewa and trigram of death; each fights the grave's element by the five relations (WB p. 47). The burial goes to the side whose verdict is white: where white are more, the twenty-five great gods, people and goods grow; where black, the twenty-five demons, tears and illness of people and cattle.

Where the trigram's pebble is black, harm falls on: *li*, the woman with the parching pan; *khon*, the woman with the food trough; *dwa*, the copper pot at the fire-dog; *khen*, the lord of the day lifting the food share; *kham*, the beer-woman with the ladle; *gin*, the reader on the throne; *zin*, the feast's equipment; *zon*, the woman grinding parched grain. The mewa falls on widows and orphans: 1 the king's queen, 2 the widow carrying the corpse, 3 the orphan and beer-woman, 4 the beer-woman with the ladle, 5 the one with the kneading-bell, 6 the Bon priest, 7 the deer-carver, 8 the work companion, 9 the appointed woman.
