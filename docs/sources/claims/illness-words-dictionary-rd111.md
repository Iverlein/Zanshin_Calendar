# Illness words: «བསྔོ», «བླངས་ལན», «གདོན་རྐྱེན» in the dictionaries and BDRC's search

Three small points on words, from the Steinert dictionaries (the clone of 2026-10, searched 2026-11-01) and BDRC's full-text search; none gives a gloss in a text, so each is a narrowing only.

«བསྔོ་རུ་མ་བཏུབ» (WB vol. 1, p. 419; question 195). The dictionaries have «བསྔོ» as the future of «སྔོ», to dedicate, transfer, distribute, share, turn over to (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo), and «ru» as a nominalising or dative-locative particle; with «བཏུབ», "can", the clause reads "white and black that cannot be assigned or shared out" as an inference. Searches of «བསྔོ་རུ་མ་བཏུབ» found only WB's own verse, as before.

«བླངས་ལན་པའི» (the notes, vol. 2, p. 497, l. 2; question 232). Hopkins's list of tense forms gives «བླང / ལེན / བླངས / ལོངས» for "to take": «ལེན» (len) is the present stem and «བླངས» the past. So «བླངས་ལན་པའི» is most simply the two stems side by side, "taken and being taken", and the clause means "the time in which the breath of the three elements is generally taken, past and present", that is, the time of the reckoning at large, not a separate element. This is a grammatical reading, not a gloss found in a text. BDRC's search for «བླངས་ལན་པ» has no hit anywhere (0 of 0).

«གདོན་རྐྱེན» (WB vol. 1, p. 438; question 401). The dictionaries give «གདོན» an evil spirit that causes disease, and «རྐྱེན» a condition or adversity, misfortune, accident. BDRC finds «སུམ་ཆ་སུམ་གཉིས་གདོན་རྐྱེན་ནད» (and «གདོན་རྐྱེན་ནད») only in WB's Gser thang reprint (MW3CN5026 etext, p. 438); no other text, and no gloss. So the question stands: spirits and conditions added to the humoral illness, or an illness of its own.
