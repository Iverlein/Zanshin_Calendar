# The string after Om on LGD p. 962 is in an ornamental letter and is not read

The first line of LGD's p. 962 (the opening of the rite, after the page-opening Om) carries a string of six glyphs in a heavy ornamental letter between two small ornaments, then the ordinary-letter homage «namo guru deva ḍākinī hūṃ». The string stands in the same place, in the same letter, in the Palri Parkhang print (printed p. 526), so it belongs to the text of both prints and is not a flaw of one scan. Read glyph by glyph at 10-fold, the six forms are near ཧ or ད, ཀྱ or ཉ, ཏ or ན, ཀ, ས with a subjoined letter, and ཧ or ཀ with a u-vowel; none can be fixed, and the two machine readings differ in all but three letters. The string is therefore not recorded as text, and it is not known whether it is a seed-syllable string, a mantra in ornamental (lantsa-like) form or an invocation; its place, between the Om and the homage, is that of an opening invocation. The ordinary witnesses of the same line (the Zhe chen, Kazi and Sbas-yul prints, the Shechen etext) were not opened at this line, so whether any of them writes it in plain letters is not known.

**Four copies (RD355, 2026-11-10).** The string is in the Rin chen gter mdzod (I1KG57 img. 981), the Palri Parkhang print (I01CT0067 img. 534), the Ngodrup and Sherab Drimay print (I1867 img. 304, coarsely cut) and the Sbas-yul manuscript (I1LT3208 img. 142, a black string after a red glyph, between two small ornaments), each time between the opening Om and the homage «namo guru deva ḍākinī hūṃ»; the six shapes agree in order in the three I compared at 5-fold (the Rin chen gter mdzod, Palri and Sbas-yul), and the Ngodrup cut shows the same order. So the string is a part of the text, copied by hand as well as printed, and no copy writes it in plain letters. The machine readings of the copies differ again (Yigdzin-1 on the Sbas-yul line «ཌཌྷཌ་ཥཌ་ཌྷཌ», on the Ngodrup line «ཊཀཱཊིའམ།སྔ»; with the earlier readings of the Rin chen gter mdzod, none repeats another), so no reading is recorded. What the string is, a seed-syllable string, a mantra written in an ornamental or secret script or an invocation, stays open (q807, q875); the Zhe chen and Kazi scans, restricted by BDRC to a window that starts inside the text, could not be opened at this line.

**The Shechen etext marks it with six placeholders, and so opens other texts (RD355, 2026-11-10, etext VE1KG14_042 and _037, no scan of the other texts).** Where the print has the string, the etext of vol. 42 (and the copy in vol. 37) has a run of six «ྈ» (U+0F88) between the title and «ན་མོ་གུ་རུ་དྷེ་ཝ་ཌཱ་ཀི་ནཱི་ཧཱུྃ»: six marks for six glyphs, so the typist saw six forms and wrote none; the mark is the etext's stand-in for what it did not read (it fills the diagrams of the volume's mandala texts, in runs of 5 to 20). The same homage is preceded by such a run in other texts of the Lama Gongdü in the volume, 7 marks before the homage of the text that ends «གཞན་དོན་མཐོང་གྲོལ་འཁོར་བ་སུན་འབྱིན», 8 before that of «མཐོང་གྲོལ་གྱི་ཙཀྲ་བྲི་བའི་ཡིག་ཆུང་» and 8 before that of «སྔགས་འབུམ་དགོས་འདོད་ཀུན་འབྱུང་གི་རིམ་པ» (all «ན་མོ་གུ་རུ་དྷེ་ཝ་དྷཱ་ཀི་ནཱི་ཧཱུྃ»), and 6 marks open the dream text of Rat+na gling pa that follows (idx 644 of the etext). So an ornamental string before the homage is the opening of seven texts of this volume, its length varying with the text, and not a trait of the dream text alone (RD403 corrected this: the cycle's four texts in the Shechen vol. 7 have none, see claim dds-lama-gongdu-placeholder-run-is-in-seven-texts-of-vol-42-and-in-none-of-vol-7); whether the string is the same in each (the glyph shapes of those pages were not compared on the scan) is not known.
