# The Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya's Śārīrasthāna 6 has the early night late and the cows' release at once, no numbers; the Śabdakalpadruma's line is its own (RD295)

The line the Śabdakalpadruma quotes as "Vāgbhaṭa, Śārīrasthāna, ch. 6" is the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya*'s (Śārīrasthāna 6, vv. 61–64 of Kunte's print, 1880, pp. 655–656 with Aruṇadatta's commentary; read on the page images by RD295). The word RD272 could not read in the dictionary's line on the early night is "cirāt" (late): "a dream of the early night gives its fruit late", and the next line says that a dream seen at the time of the cows' release (*go-sarga*; the dictionary's print has *gomarge*) gives a great fruit that same day. Aruṇadatta glosses the first as little and long delayed. The physician also counts as fruitless the dreams of the day, of one's own nature, forgotten, very long or very short, besides the first five of the seven kinds, and has a dream turned to little fruit by gifts, burnt offerings and recitations. Next verse: a good dream seen after a bad one in the same place gives the good fruit.

The passage thus has only two ends of the night, early (late, small) and the cows' release (at once, great), and no numbers: it is the shape of the series (far for the first watch, near for the dawn) without the series. The Tibetan side is the *Svapnohana*'s graded series (svo:p271h).

RD312 (2026-11-10): the Nirṇayasāgara edition of 1939 and five further prints read gosarge, none gomārge (claim ahr-gosarge-no-gomarge-in-six-prints).
