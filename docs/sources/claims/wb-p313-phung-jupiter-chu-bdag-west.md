# WB p. 313: «ཕུང» is Jupiter after the Lamp, «ཆུ་བདག» the west

The third triad's «ཕུང» (p. 313) is Jupiter, and the lord of water (ཆུ་བདག, chu bdag) of the same page is the west, Saturn's quarter. The *Lamp of Tibetan Astrology*'s preliminaries (lt:p9-11, lt:p10-11, read on the scan) give the same four triads of animals word for word, with «ཕུར་བུས» (phur bu, Jupiter) where WB has «ཕུང»: tiger-horse-dog Saturn, pig-sheep-hare the Moon, bird-ox-snake Jupiter, rat-dragon-monkey Mars. WB's «ཕུང» is then Jupiter written short for «ཕུར»; the 1996 etext carries «ཕུང» as well (p. 321 of its images), so the print and the reprint agree and the slip, if it is one, is old. No dictionary lists «ཕུང» as a planet (Steinert's dictionaries, searched 2026-11-01: only "heap, ruin, skandha"). For «ཆུ་བདག»: Ives Waldo and the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* give it as "west" (nub phyogs) and as the ocean, 84000's glossary as Varuṇa, the governor of the western direction. With the eight planets set round from the east in the order of the verse (Sun east, Venus south-east, Mars south, Rāhu south-west, Saturn west, Moon north-west, Mercury north, Jupiter north-east), the fifth is Saturn: "the Sun in the east is good, the west bad" is the opposite pair, and the verse means that the planet facing the sun's quarter is the one to avoid. The placing of the other planets is by the order of the words, not stated. What «མདུན་གཏད» fixes in «མ་རྒྱབ་གྲོགས་མདུན་གཏད་པ་གཅེས» stays unsaid by the page: the friend is to be in front, nothing behind.
