# The midnight-dream death-sign teaching is the companion method of the Breakthrough transference in the Lho brag pa four sheets of the whispered lineage (RD374)

RD374 (2026-11-10, BDRC etexts, the Gdams ngag mdzod page read also on the scan). The teaching of the midnight dream (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ, nam phyed kyi rmi lam brtag pa; the Gya skag mountain, the naked donkey rider) is one text in six places: in the Gdams ngag mdzod (Shechen), the Bka' brgyud sngags mdzod nag 'gros, the 'Brug pa chos mdzod, the Lho brag Mar pa lo tsa'i gsung 'bum (Dpal brtsegs, vol. 4), and two Lho brag pa collections, always as the end of the Lho brag pa transference text, after the sentence closing the root «'pho ba spyi rtol» («the special oral instruction of the noble one», གནད་ཀྱི་གདམས་པ་ཁྱད་པར་འཕགས་པའི་ཞལ་གདམས་སོ) and before the next text. The Gdams ngag mdzod and the Bka' brgyud sngags mdzod end it «the companion method of the Breakthrough transference» (འཕོ་བ་སྤྱི་རྟོལ་མའི་ལྷན་ཐབས, 'pho ba spyi rtol ma'i lhan thabs, dds.9:p173-174, dds.22:p1085-1114). The collection is «Lho brag pa's special instruction, the four sheets of the whispered lineage» (ཤོག་དྲིལ་བཞི, shog dril bzhi, Marpa's transmission from Nāropa and Maitrīpa as the history tells it): the Breakthrough transference, the Net-eyed wheel of channels and winds, Cutting the bardo by mind and the Great Seal condensed in words (dds.22:p1085-1114). So the teaching is a companion text of Marpa's transference teaching, not one of the four root texts (the Breakthrough's root verses in the Gdams ngag mdzod volume close with «spyi skong wa ta» and carry no death signs).

The prints differ in one word of the dream: the Gdams ngag mdzod etext reads «མེ་ཏོག་དམར་པོ་ཐོབ་པ» («getting a red flower»; the first words of the scan's second page also read ཐོབ་པ), the other Shechen etext (BDRC VE23605_008), the Bka' brgyud sngags mdzod, the 'Brug pa chos mdzod and the Marpa volume «ཐུ་བ» / «འཐུ་བ» («picking»), as does Tilopa's Tika in the Ras chung collection (dds.15:p119-121). The mountain is «རྒྱ་སྐག» (rgya skag) in these prints and «རྒྱ་སྐྱེག» (rgya skyeg) in the Ras chung collection.
