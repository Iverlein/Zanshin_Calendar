# The night's three watches (thun gsum): the Bodhisattva-vow and Vinaya scheme, WB's night of awakening, LGD's thirds

The three watches (ཐུན་གསུམ, thun gsum) of the night are the Buddhist scheme of the Bodhisattva-vow and Vinaya literature, with no clock bounds. Tsong kha pa's *Byang chub gzhung lam* (etext p. 475) and an Abhidharmasamuccaya commentary (p. 541, quoting the root verse "the wise one ... in the night's upper and lower watches abides ... even sleeping, mindful") say: dividing the night into three watches, one lies down in the middle one only, and in the first and last, as in the day, one recites and practises yoga. A Sakya Vinaya treatise argues when the dawn (སྐྱ་རེངས, skya rengs) comes against these watches: some say in the middle of the last watch of the three (or from the middle of the seventh of nine parts); the author says from the middle of the eighth of nine, and cites the *Bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* commentary for "the first half of the last watch, when the king's drum is near". No hours are given and the parts are not said to be equal.

WB uses the same words for the Buddha's night (vol. 1 p. 22: dusk སྲོད, the first part of the night ནམ་གྱི་ཆ་སྟོད, the middle གུང, then dawn ཐོ་རངས; and p. 23: «གུང་ཐུན», the middle watch of the night of the fifteenth). The names dusk, middle (midnight) and early dawn of LGD (q806) and of RST are these three, so LGD's three parts follow the three-watch scheme's names and not the Svapnohana's four; no text read fixes any of them by hours.

For q810: WB's epithet of the night «ཐུན་གསུམ» (vol. 1 p. 17) matches this scheme in words; no text read says that it rests on it (WB lists it among the night's names without comment). It is not WB's own four-watch count of p. 101.
