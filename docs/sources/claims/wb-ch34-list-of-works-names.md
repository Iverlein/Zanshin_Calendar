# The names skyas, sba phyar, shwa rags and sen in ch. 34's list of works

The four names of ch. 34's list of works (p. 378, read on the Gser thang reprint, img. 384, with the texts of the works themselves):

- «སྐྱས» (24, «སྐྱས་བྱེད་པ»): the doing of *skyas*, gifts; the work's own verse (p. 400) opens «སྐྱས་ཀྱི་བྱ་བར»; KP's box 23 has «སྐྱས་བྱེད་པ», glossed in kun-phan-me-long.md as giving gifts. WB's day-animal verses set it among the bride-taking and seeking-business works (dog day «བག་མ་དོན་གཉེར་སྐྱས», tiger day beside robbery), and the Bhutan digest lists it between sowing and the tent (p. 357) and as «བྲ་ཉེ་སྐྱས་འདེབས» in its table (p. 408). "Dowries" is not in any text read and stays unsettled; "gifts" is.
- «སྦ་ཕྱར» (17): sewing (འཚེམ་པ) tents or covers: the work's own head is «སྦ་དང་ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ་པ» and its verse «ཕུར་བུ སྦ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ་པ་བཟང་», with «འཚེམ» good where «འབུབས་པ» is bad on the fire-four and the reverse on the Pleiades. The first syllable is «སྦ» on the list (p. 378, scan) and on the work's head; the mansion verses write «སྦྲ་ཕྱར». What «ཕྱར» and «ཕྱ་ཐེར» are is not settled: a hoisted flag or awning is likely, not proved.
- «ཤྭ་རགས» (49): a dike: the work is «ཤྭ་རགས་བརྩིག་དང་ཆུ་བོ་བསྲུང་», building a dike and guarding against the river, and the mansion and day-animal verses name the same work «ཆུ་རགས» beside ponds, pits and tree-planting. Settled as dike.
- «སེན» (50): nails (སེན་མོ): the work's own head is «བཟོ་དང་སྐྲ་སེན་འབྲེག་པ», crafts and cutting hair and nails, and the verse has «སྐྲ་དང་སེན་མོ་འབྲེག་པ». On p. 378 the list reads «བཟོ་སྒྲ་སེན» in the machine reading; the stack under བཟོ་ is sa with ra and an indistinct head letter, and the sense with p. 420 gives སྐྲ་, hair: crafts, hair and nails.

RD66 (2026-10-28), the lines on the prints and in the manuals:

- The list on p. 378 on three prints: the 1996 print (img. 386, 703 px), the Gser thang reprint (img. 384) and the Zhol print (WBZ img. 1135, Yigdzin-1 only; its numerals sit in small cartouches above the syllables and its line of 17 and 24 is not read on the scan). On both clear prints work 24 reads «སྐྱས», with no vowel sign; the 1996 *etext*'s «སྐྱེས» there is the etext's error, not the print's. Work 49 reads «ཤྭ་རགས» on both (the etext has «ཤུ་རགས»). Work 50's «བཟོ་སྐྲ་སེན» (or «སྒྲ»): the two prints show the same stack and neither shows its head letter. Work 17: both prints show the same stack for the syllable the machine reads «སྦ»; whether it carries a subjoined ra cannot be told at their size. The Zhol print's machine reading of the list has «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ»; the typeset Bhutan digest (etext pp. 357, 385) «སྦྲ་དང་ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ་པ»; and WB's own verses in the 1996 etext, «ཕྱ་ཐེར་སྦྲ་འཚེམ» (p. 317), «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ» (pp. 327, 334) all have «སྦྲ» (*sbra*, a tent); only one verse at [p382] has «སྒྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ», an etext misreading by its look. So the word is most likely *sbra phyar*, tents and the fluttering things sewn with them; «ཕྱར» is the word of «བ་དན་འཕྱར» (work 19, hoisting a banner) and of Mipham's «དར་དཀར་ཕྱར་འདྲ», "like a white silk fluttering" (1996 etext [p504]), but «ཕྱ་ཐེར» itself is glossed nowhere read.
- «སྐྱས» (24) is a gift or offering made to someone, and the text uses it where the bride has no part: in the 1996 etext's day verses it stands beside the dead («གཤིན་ལས་སྐྱས་དང་རྩེད་མོ་བྱ», [p320]; «རོ་དྲང་ཤིད་དང་སྐྱས», [p336]; «དུར་འདེབས་དུར་ཁ་འབྱེད་དང་སྐྱས», [p329]), the calling of life («ཚེ་འགུགས་སྐྱས», [p321]) and the transfer of a life («འཕོ་སྐྱས», [pp334, 370]), as well as beside taking a bride («བག་མ་དོན་གཉེར་སྐྱས», [p366]). The manuals use «སྐྱས་འདེབས» for the gift made to the king-spirit and the *sri*: Lha mo tshul khrims (LT) etext pp. 97 («རྒྱལ་པོ་སྐྱས་འདེབས») and 168 («སྲི་སྐྱས་འདེབས»), the Bhutan digest p. 48 («རྒྱལ་པོ་སྐྱས་འདེབས», «འཕུང་སྲི་སྐྱས་འདོད»). So «སྐྱས» is gifts and offerings in general, given to the dead and to the spirits as well as between the living; no text read ties it to the bride, and "dowry" is not a reading the texts allow. (All these are etext readings.)
- «ཤྭ་རགས» (49) and «སེན» (50): no manual glosses them; the earlier readings stand (a dike; the nails, with hair from the head of the work on p. 420).
