# A dream tested on the road names the fifteenth day and the waxing half; the valley is found next dawn (TSL life)

When Rtsa gsum gling pa "tested" a dream (རྨི་ལམ་བརྟགས་པ, rmi lam brtags pa, i169) on the road to a treasure place, he asked the dream's own speaker for the "hour, day and date" (དུས་ཚོད་ཚེས་གྲངས) and got a day and a rule: the god Gze rgyal Lha btsan 'bar ba said to come to the valley-mouth "when the waxing half has passed a little" (ཡར་ཙམ་འདས་པའི་མཚམས); that on the fifteenth day (བཅོ་ལྔའི་ཚེས) in the market's middle a woman with matted topknot would name the place; that "when the fifteen has passed the signs are complete" (i170). The test of the dream was then made on waking at dawn, by the road: «སྔར་གྱི་རྨི་ལམ་ལྟ་བུའི་ལུང་པ་འདུག» (a valley like the earlier dream was there). A dream is thus tied to a date by its speaker's words, and confirmed by the ground on the next day; the page gives the tie, not a general rule on which day dreams are true.
