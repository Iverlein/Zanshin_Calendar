# The Drikung dka' mchan takes the Moonbeams' outsider; Skyem pa reads a man of qualities and neither states the physician's rule; the Blue Beryl's clause lists the Drukpa yogis, monks and the white-robed mantra-holders of Khri srong lde btsan's two seats

The Drikung dka' mchan (BDK, etext p. 79) takes the Moonbeams' word for ya mtshan can: the outsider living in a kind of vow-observance, and sets beside it a Tantra's list of outsider sects ending with the word, «these are seen as going outside». Skyem pa Tshe dbang (etext p. 187) does not: for him the ya mtshan can messenger is one with qualities above others, the dge sbyong one gone forth with a virtuous mind, the one of like kind calm and tamed and striving for others, and the opposites are the flawed, the unvirtuous and «a butcher and the like». Neither commentary, at this verse, states a rule that the physician does not go or does not treat; both gloss only the verse's list of the physician's own states (rough mind, ill-omened speech, cutting, tearing down, the offering to the dead, the burnt offering) at which a messenger's coming is the sick one's sign of death.

So of the commentaries read, the outsider reading stands in the Moonbeams, Byang pa and the Drikung (which names the Moonbeams); Rin spungs pa and Skyem pa read a monk or a man of qualities; the Blue Beryl gives both (gt:i41b).

The Blue Beryl's broken clause (etext pp. 198-199, printed pp. 170-171) reads in the etext as a list of those of a like party to the outsiders: the Drukpa school's realised ones who hold «རྦད་མགོ», «དྲྭ་ཕད», the thighbone trumpet and bone ornaments (the first two words are printed so and not glossed in the etexts read: «རྦད་མགོ་དང་དྲྭ་ཕད» has one hit in BDRC's full-text search, in this volume); the monks who follow Shakyamuni; and, in Tibet, the «གོས་དཀར་ལྕང་ལོའི་སྡེ», the white-robed long-haired communities, of the two seats of offering of the lord Khri srong lde btsan (the etext's «གས་[གོས]དཀར་ལྕང་ལའི་[ལོའི]»). The term «གོས་དཀར་ལྕང་ལོའི་སྡེ» has 120 hits in BDRC's full-text search; in one snippet (Ngag dbang dpal bzang, MW22946, VE22946_001) it stands as the second of «the king's two kinds of offering-recipients», after the saffron-robed ordained (in Wylie in BDRC's snippet: «ngur smrig 'dzin pa'i sde dang, sngags pa rdo rje bdud 'joms sogs gos dkar lcang lo'i sde ste rgyal po'i mchod gnas rnam pa gnyis»), so the Blue Beryl's two seats are the ordained community and the white-robed mantra-holders' community. This is a reading of the term from that snippet, not a gloss in the Blue Beryl.
