# The three dream rites of the 'Brong rtse be'u bum name no author; the Śramaṇā rite is the text of the First Dalai Lama's collected works (RD360)

The print gives the three rites (Śramaṇā, Śākyamuni, White Tārā) with no author, compiler or colophon (tdr:p338-340e, tdr:p337-345e); in their own voice they say only "so it is said" and "the lama says, and it is established by experience", naming no lama. The editors say the book holds instructions of "other holy forebears" and give a lineage for the book's instructions (Gu ru, Ye shes mtsho rgyal, … Mkhas grub Dge legs dpal, Lha btsun Rin chen rgya mtsho; tdr:p15-23e), a lineage the book itself ties to a medical instruction on the voice and not to the dream set. What can be said: (1) the Śramaṇā rite is word for word the text that stands in the First Dalai Lama's collected works, both prints (srm.6:i275-279, srm.6:p386-389; its homage verse and its sentence "none like this instruction of Śramaṇā, an emanation of Tārā, … by experience" are the same), to whom BDRC ascribes it (abstract work WA0XLB767AF25E921, author P80 = Dge 'dun grub pa), on the strength of its place in the collection and not of a colophon; (2) Klong rdol bla ma's record of teachings received lists «རྨི་ལམ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལྷ་མོ་ཤྲ་མ་ཎའི་སྒོ་ནས་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ» among the items of volume nga of that collection, beside the lion-faced one's sādhana (kgy:p64-65), the volume after it beginning with the examination of the signs of death; (3) the Śākyamuni and the White Tārā rites were not found in any other etext: BDRC's search for the Śākyamuni mantra «ཨོཾ་བུདྷ་ཙུ་རུ་མཱ་ཙུ་རུ་ཀེ་སྭཱ» and the Tārā title finds the 'Brong rtse etext only. So the Śramaṇā rite is attributed, on the collection, to Dge 'dun grub pa; the Śākyamuni and Tārā rites have no attribution; whether Lha btsun or the First Dalai Lama's collection came first is not known. State: etext; the Śramaṇā text of the Gsung 'bum on the scan.
