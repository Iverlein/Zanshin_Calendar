# Ch. 27: the unread words in Steinert's 64 dictionaries, and ZG's «ཚང་ཟླ»

Headwords sought by Wylie in Steinert's 64 dictionaries (a clone of github.com/christiansteinert/tibetan-dictionary, `locate.py dict`) for ch. 27's unread words, with what the lines on the page then allow.

- «ན་ཟླ་སྟོན་མོ» (p. 9, l. 16: «བྱིས་སྟོན་ན་ཟླ་སྟོན་མོ་གཏང», "at the children's feast give the *na zla ston mo*"). «ན་ཟླ» (na zla) is "of equal age; young friend" (Rangjung Yeshe), "of the same age" (Ives-Waldo), «ལོ་མཉམ་པ» "of the same year" (the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* and the *Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho*), "companions born together" (Negi's Sanskrit dictionary); «སྟོན་མོ» (ston mo) is a feast or banquet (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, the *Tshig mdzod chen mo*: a feast of joy, the gathering of guests). The words together are the age-mates' feast, a feast for children of the child's own age, given at the children's feast of the ransom rite. Settled as to the words; the rite's detail (who is fed, what is offered) is not given by any dictionary.
- «ཚད་ཟླ་མག་སྐུད» (p. 9, l. 6). «ཚད་ཟླ» is a headword only in Valby ("brother"); the headwords give none for «མག་སྐུད» or «མག་རྒུད». Zhechen Gyaltsab's verse for the same black pebble reads «ཚང་ཟླ་མག་ / སྐུད་བུ་ཚར་ངན» (zg:p352, I4PD2444 img. 352, the first line's end «ནག་ན་ཚང་ཟླ་མག» and the next line's «སྐུད་བུ་ཚར་ངན» read on the scan at 3.5 times), so the first word of the 1996 print and the reprint, «ཚད», is likely a misprint of «ཚང» (nga for da). «ཚང་ཟླ» (tshang zla) is "nest material; comrades, fellow-students" (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby), "the siblings and companions" (*Tshig mdzod chen mo*); «མག་པ» (mag pa) is the bridegroom or son-in-law (the daughter's partner, *Tshig mdzod chen mo*), «སྐུད» (skud) "thread, yarn" and, in Rangjung Yeshe, "one's husband's male relatives", and WB's own list of kin (vol. 2, printed p. 94, etext p. 102) has «མག་སྐུད» among the relatives to whom a death is sent (etext only). The line then reads: the household's comrades, the line of sons-in-law (and the child's side?) bad, among the black pebble's ills (water source dried, plague, the household moves). ZG's «སྐུད» goes with the 1996 print against the Taikhang's «རྒུད» (decline; [ch27-taikhang-doubtful-letters](ch27-taikhang-doubtful-letters.md)); both make sense, so which the Taikhang block carves stays by its own scan, and «མག» is the son-in-law's side either way. «བུ་ཚར» has no headword.
- «འུར་ཞོགས» (p. 17, l. 26). «འུར་ཞོགས» ('ur zhogs) is a headword: "startling talk, noise (which brings on shying)" (Ives-Waldo), "alarming talk" (*Tshig mdzod chen mo*, «དཀྲོག་གཏམ»), with «འུར་ཞིང» a related headword; «བུར་ཞོགས» and «ཞུར་ཞོགས» have none. So where the first letter of the Taikhang's syllable is box-shaped («བ» or «འ») the word with a meaning is «འུར་ཞོགས»; the old rendering "raid" is replaced by "alarm, an uproar of talk": within ten days an alarm, and long and short «ར་མདའ» (goat-horn arrows) come. The letter on the Taikhang stays unread by eye.
- «ལྕོང་མོའི་ཛ[?]་མས» (p. 17, l. 9). «ལྕོང་མོ» (lcong mo) is the tadpole ("the frog's young with a tail": rnga ma can, *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, *Tshig gsar bsgrigs*; Valby, Ives-Waldo "tadpole"). Two words fit the doubtful syllables: «རྔམས» (rngams), "awesome, height, depth, gleam, splendour" (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives-Waldo, *Tshig mdzod chen mo*), the machine reading of both prints, and «རྔ་མས» (rnga ma, "tail", instrumental), the tadpole's own tail (*Tshig mdzod chen mo*: «རྔ་མ», the tail of the animals; «ལྕོང་མོ» "with a tail"). "The tadpole's splendour stirs the ocean" or "the tadpole's tail stirs the ocean" are both images of a small thing moving the great; the second matches the letters a syllable further than «རྔམས» (RD51 saw two syllables, the second «མས», the first a ཛ-like stack). Not settled; the scan alone decides.
- «ཤིང་ལུག་པ» (p. 20, l. 14). Ives-Waldo has «ཤིང་ལུག་པ» as a headword: "a man born in the wood sheep year", so the last syllable པ is the person-suffix, as the scan has it, and not བ; the wood sheep's class is then "the (one) of the wood sheep year", the cow's or not as the class counts decide ([ch27-turtle-wood-sheep-and-element](ch27-turtle-wood-sheep-and-element.md)); no dictionary says that the wood sheep belongs to the cow.
- «བརྗེ» (p. 23, l. 22): see [ch27-brje-set-zg-spiritual-friend](ch27-brje-set-zg-spiritual-friend.md): exchange only.

None of the 64 dictionaries has «ཚད་ཟླ་མག་སྐུད», «མག་རྒུད» or «བུར་ཞོགས» as a headword.
