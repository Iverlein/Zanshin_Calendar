# «ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» is the day and the hour, «ཉི་དུས» its short form

«ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» is WB's own pair, the day and the hour, and «ཉི་དུས» is its short form; so Wangkhyi's «གླང་གི་ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» (p. 210) is "the ox's day and hour", not the daytime hours of the ox's day. Evidence in WB's own words: (1) p. 376's rule on weighing sets «ཉི་མ» and «དུས་ཚོད» side by side as two factors that can disagree, the hour with the sharp weapon ruling, and the sheets of KD vol. 3 write the four factors as year the king, month the minister, day the soldier, hour the weapon; (2) Mipham's notes on p. 498 write the same pair apart, «ལུག་གི་ཉི་མ་དང་དུས་ཚོད་ལ», "on the sheep's day and hour", for a rite of healing and renaming, and together, «ཉི་མ་དུས་ཚོད» again, for a day and hour that must be free of the seventh corner, the enemy and the grave; (3) p. 99 lists the year, month, day and hour animals to be avoided for a corpse and then says that "in particular the day-time (ཉི་དུས) is held chief", and the Wangkhyi paragraph begins with «ཉི་དུས་ནག་པོ་ཁ་ཡི་ཕྱོགས», the black ཉི་དུས as the direction of the mouth. The almanac's list in vol. 1 (etext pp. 148–150, garbled, machine) writes cells such as «འབྲུག་གྱི་ཉི་དུས། ནུབ་བལྟ།» ("the dragon's nyi dus: looks west"), the same pairing of animal and direction as p. 210. What stays open is only whether the ox's day and the ox's hour both must be ox, or the ox day at any hour, or the ox hour on any day: the notes' «ལུག་གི་ཉི་མ་དང་དུས་ཚོད» reads as both at once (a rite done on a sheep day at the sheep hour), and no text says otherwise. No dictionary of Steinert's collection and no commentary found glosses «ཉི་དུས» (the dead ends below).
