# RD209: which short dream manual gives which time rule

| Manual (state) | What it is | Time rule for when the dream counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KCM, Karma chags med (machine) | rite to ask in a dream | tenth of the waxing or waning moon best; three days of offering first; "the proper time" of the night, not named |
| SKP first text, under Sakya Paṇḍita (machine) | list of images with verdicts | none |
| SKP second text (machine) | the verses of RST, under Vairocana | the dawn third of the night; not when ill, grieving or disturbed |
| DTD, Dam tshig rdo rje, manuscript and print (machine) | rite to ask in a dream, Avalokiteśvara | the last of four watches of the night |
| RBG, the eighteen dreams of the latter times (witness, etext) | King Kṛki's dreams | none (not a method) |
| RST, the ACIP method (etext) | verses, images, death signs | the dawn third only; the middle third is spirits' tricks, the dusk third habit; not when ill, grieving or disturbed |
| RRT, the 'Bri gung extended method (etext) | a practitioner's signs by deity | none; asks which deity is practised |

No manual of this reading gives a span after which the fruit falls, and none carries a lunar-day rule for the ordinary dream beyond KCM's tenth day for the rite. The dawn third (RST, SKP2) and the last of four watches (DTD) agree in making the last part of the night the one that counts, but one divides the night in three and the other in four.

RD274 (2026-11-09): the time rules of the Tibetan manuals above and of the Indian dream texts are set side by side, by text, with state and print, in the table "The watch rules by text" of dream-catalogue, for the picker's night chips; the Four Tantras' two-way rule (the first part of the night forgotten, the clear dawn dream gives fruit) is st-dream-night-first-part-dawn, and RST is Klong rdol's own text, the source of KBS's verses (rst-is-klong-rdols-dream-text).
