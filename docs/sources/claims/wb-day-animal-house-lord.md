# The day animal's earth lord in the house and the dead's burial

The earth lord of the day's animal in the house (WB vol. 2, pp. 224–225, «འདི་ཉིད་ནང་དཔྱད»): the same lord as Kharbu (ཁར་བུ), "the twelve cruel ones of the sun" and "the sky's wheel" is said to dwell in a thing of the house on each day, and the dead's burial offends it when that thing is touched. Rat: the beer jar; ox: the granary and storehouses; tiger: the door; hare: the yoke; dragon: the loom; snake: the rope; horse: the camp (sound); sheep: the trough (dough); monkey: the knife and axe; bird: plates; dog: the step; pig: the pit. For each day the text gives the harm (children, the head of the row, the bearer, the one who opens the soil) and the way out (carry the corpse over the roof or through a hole, cut a second rope in three, make the dough on copper or stone, burn the knives, open the soil with flesh and bone). The same rows are read for the fort and for the time-hours.
