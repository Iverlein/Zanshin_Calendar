# Dpal sprul's collected works and the Yon tan mdzod commentary tie no 8th, 10th, 25th, 29th or 30th to the fast or the one-day vow

Dpal sprul's counsel to lay people on the days, searched whole in his collected works' etext (dpk vols. 1 to 8, BDRC I1PD107144 to I1PD107151) and in the Larung print of the *Words of My Perfect Teacher*, names the days only in these places: the feast of the tenth (dpk.1:p380-381, already in the pool); the not-killing on the fifteenth and the new moon (kzl:i177-178); the offerings on "the full-moon and new-moon days and the six times" (kzl:i315); the multiplier verse and the days' results in his calendar treatises (dpk.2:p51-52, dpk.2:p138-140). The rite of the one-day vow (dpk.3:p135-136) and its closing benefits give no day. His commentary on Jigs med gling pa's *Yon tan mdzod* (the *Yon tan rin po che'i mdzod kyi dka' 'grel Nyin byed snang ba*, in vol. 5 of the etext) is an outline of the three vows with no date in its place on the one-day vow, and the Kālacakra's twenty-five conduct vows (བརྟུལ་ཞུགས་ཉེར་ལྔ) are not a day. In his own life (dpk vol. 1, the etext's [p104]) the biographer has him keep, after a long rite, a day's rite on each of the two days of the full moon and new moon (ཉ་སྟོང་གཉིས, nya stong gnyis) until his death: his own practice, not advice to others.

- No 8th, 10th, 25th, 29th or 30th is tied to the fast, the one-day vow or a refuge practice in his advice to lay people.
