# The thirty: the first point, the garuḍa, for lords, rulers and kings

The first of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the garuḍa (བྱ་ཁྱུང་ག་རུ་ཌ, bya khyung ga ru Da), is read for lords, rulers and kings. Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the blue vault of the sky is torn | Good for eight generations of the forefathers | The vault is torn from its height: a great rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud) in the forefathers' place | — |
| 2 | Whether the helmet has fallen to the ground | Good in the forefathers' place; the helmet is kept, the protector gods lead, wishes are fulfilled without obstacle | The king's helmet falls to the ground; the forefathers die by the blade; bad for the father and the uncles | — |
| 3 | Whether the palace's seat is empty | No illness, free of enemies | The palace's seat is empty; a great risk of leaving one's own country; if it happened before, it is borne; somewhat bad | — |
| 4 | Whether the many mouths of the country's people speak of emptiness | No talk of emptiness; wishes are fulfilled | Gossip and quarrels rise | The crowd thread-cross (ཁྲོམ་མདོས, khrom mdos) and turning back the crowd |
| 5 | Whether the subjects have risen against the lord | Servants obey, no revolt, fame is won | The subjects revolt, servants disobey, nothing comes of it, even the near rise as enemies | As remedy rite (གཏོ, gto), plant the luck tree (ཀླུང་རྟའི་ཤིང, klung rta'i shing) and send the life-castle (སྲོག་མཁར, srog mkhar) and the life ransom (གླུད, glud) |
| 6 | Whether the foot pebble (རྐང་རྡེལ, rkang rdel) is lost in the sky | The foot pebble is not lost; bad people do not rise as enemies | Subjects and bad people rise as enemies | The laying-down rite against gossip (མི་ཁ་སྐྱས་འདེབས, mi kha skyas 'debs) |
| 7 | Whether the tiger's and leopard's stripes and fur have faded | Even if the forefathers turned bad, one's own place is full of happiness | The tiger's stripes have faded: the forefathers good but oneself in decline; what is there now is gone now; what rose declines | The secret *Kun 'dus* (གསང་བ་ཀུན་འདུས, gsang ba kun 'dus) |
| 8 | Whether the throne cushion is empty | The seat is not empty; though the forefathers declined, oneself is good; mastery over the five elements | The cushion's seat is empty too; the forefathers good but oneself in decline; talk of moving from the country; no power over the five elements | Turning back (བཟློག, bzlog), pressing down the enemy sri (དགྲ་སྲི, dgra sri), proclaiming the clearing of all faults (ཉེས་པ་ཀུན་སེལ, nyes pa kun sel) |

Whose year is reckoned: the person's own (the notes, vol. 2, p. 533: «གཞན་རྣམས་སོ་སོའི་ལོ་རྟ»).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
