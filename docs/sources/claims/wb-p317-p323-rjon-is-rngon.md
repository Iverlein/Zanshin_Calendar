# WB pp. 317 and 323: «རྗོན» is «རྔོན», the hunter, by WB's own usage

«རྗོན» in Puṣya's avoid list (p. 317) and as Mūla's deity (p. 323) is «རྔོན», the hunter, in WB's own usage: the two 1996 etext volumes carry «རྗོན» nowhere and «རྔོན» in ten places, five of them «རྔོན་པ», the hunter («རྔོན་པ་རྐུ་ཇག་བརྩམ་པའི་ཚེ», when a hunter, thief or robber sets to work; «རྔོན་པ་དང་ནི་ཇག་པ་ངན»), and the etext types Puṣya's «རྔོན་རྐུ» and Mūla's «ལྷ་ནི་རྔོན་པ་ཅན» with the ང, so «རྔོན་རྐུ» is hunting and theft as in the lists just quoted. The letter under the ར is a ཇ-shaped blot on the Zhol and the reprint and was not told apart from ང on the 1996 scan (I1KG12907 img. 331, 5x, 700 px wide); the count settles it, since a word «རྗོན» that no dictionary of Steinert's set has (q630's earlier search) and WB never writes elsewhere would be the only one of its kind. What the deity is is not told: «ལྷ་ནི་རྔོན་པ་ཅན» is "the deity who has the hunters", and no text of the pool names Mūla's deity otherwise.
