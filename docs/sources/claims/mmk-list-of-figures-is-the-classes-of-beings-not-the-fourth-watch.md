# The list of figures after the marks of the true dream is the Sanskrit rāśi, the classes of beings, and not the fourth watch's dream (RD372)

The list of figures after the line «རྨི་ལམ་བདེན་པར་མཐོང་བ་ནི། མཚན་ཉིད་འདི་དག་གསུངས་པ་ནི།» (Derge etext [p403]-[p404]; Sanskrit pp. 152-153) is not the content of the fourth watch's dream. (1) The Sanskrit says «rāśayo bahusattvānāṃ kathitā» "the classes of the many beings were told", and goes on at once: «bahuprakārā vicitrārthā vividhā karma-varṇitāḥ; teṣu sarveṣu karma ca phalanti guṇavistarāḥ» (all the many, the act with its fruit: the rāśis are the places of birth in which the acts ripen; the Tibetan «རྣམ་པ་མང་པོ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དོན། རྣམ་པ་སྣ་ཚོགས་ལས་བསྟན་པ། དེ་དག་ལས་ནི་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ། ཡོན་ཏན་རྒྱ་ཆེར་སྨན་པར་འགྱུར།»). (2) The Sanskrit's own lists of the «rāśi» elsewhere (mmk.2:p19, the first chapter; mmk.2:p180, the chapter on the planets and asterisms) put the twelve signs of the zodiac (ram, bull, twins, crab, lion, maiden, balance, scorpion, bow, sea-monster, jar, fish) first and then the monkey, the human, the god, the garuḍa, the yakṣa and rākṣasa, the animal, the ghost and the hells, "countless, born of the courses of birth": the classes of beings and states of birth, and the chapter on the planets says "not the planets, not the rāśis, but the act is the cause". The dream chapter's list is that list, cut short, with the Tibetan «ཁྱིམ» (khyim, "house") for «rāśi». (3) The marks «མཚན་ཉིད» (mtshan nyid) of the line before the list are, by the Sanskrit's «vātikeṣv api sattveṣu ... kathitā lakṣaṇā dṛśyate svapnānāṃ satya-darśanā» that stands at the end of the description of the wind-natured, the marks (lakṣaṇa) of the persons just described, closing that section; the Tibetan «རྨི་ལམ་བདེན་པར་མཐོང་བ» is the Sanskrit «svapnānāṃ satya-darśanā», "the seeing (the true) of dreams". So the fourth watch's «true» dream has no list in the root tantra, in either text; the passage that follows says only that a dream shows what the act brings, fierce acts ripen at once in the dream and lasting good acts later (mmk.1:p403). The Tibetan «སྡིག་པ» ("sin") at the eighth place has no Sanskrit word to meet: the dream chapter's OCR leaves it unread, and the lists in chapters 1 and 18 have «vṛścika» (scorpion) there, so it is a doubt whether the Derge had a different Sanskrit word or a slip; the Tibetan «སྦལ་པ» (frog) for «karkaṭa» (crab) is likewise a doubt. The Sanskrit pages (OCR) are machine readings.
