# WB's five places on the day-and-night's watches, set against the Dbyangs 'char's sixteen half-watches and the tantric eight (RD333)

WB names the day-and-night's divisions in five places, and none of them says what the Dbyangs 'char's sixteen half-watches or the tantric books' eight watches are to WB's eight; what they give, on the 1996 print (I1KG12906 for vol. 1, I1KG12907 for vol. 2), is this.

- **Vol. 1 p. 17, ll. 13-14 of 28 (img. 27):** the night from sunset to dawn is named «ཐུན་གསུམ», the three watches, among a run of epithets; day and night «དུས་ཚོད་དྲུག་དྲུག», six times each. Three watches and six times are a different count from the eight and the sixteen; the line gives no bounds ([wb.1:p17b](../corpus/wb/ch02.md#wb-1-p17b), [wb.1:p17c](../corpus/wb/ch02.md#wb-1-p17c)).
- **Vol. 1 p. 101, ll. 23-27 (img. 111):** the session Rāhu is cut «ཉིན་བཞི་མཚན་བཞི», four by day and four by night, and its planets stand at places of that eightfold count (the sun from the middle of the fourth, Jupiter from the middle of «ཐུན་བརྒྱད», the eighth watch) with their stretches in water-hours (chu tshod, 5, 4, 6, 3, 7, 4, 1; thirty together). Here «ཐུན་བརྒྱད» is an ordinal: the eight are numbered in one run, the day taking the first four, which is the tantric books' eight (svo:p271o), not the Dbyangs 'char's eight by day and eight by night (svo:p271x). Earlier on the same page, ll. 6-10, the time-union halves are reckoned two ways, by the day's measure cut by the twelve houses and by the water-srang (chu srang), and the planet is counted «six rounds by day, five by night» ([wb.1:p101b](../corpus/wb/ch12.md#wb-1-p101b), [wb.1:p101c](../corpus/wb/ch12.md#wb-1-p101c)).
- **Vol. 1 p. 135, ll. 4-7 (img. 145):** «ཉིན་མཚན་ཚད་ནི་བརྒྱད་ཀྱིས་བགོས» on the time-wheel of samsara (l. 4) and «ཉིན་མཚན་ཚད་ནི་ཀླུ་ཡིས་བགོས» on the wheel of the vowels by half-watches (l. 7), where the bright half turns the day right and the night left. The unit that arises is the half-watch (ཐུན་ཕྱེད). The wheel being turned by day one way and by night the other is the only pointer to eight by day and eight by night; the text does not say so ([wb.1:p135b](../corpus/wb/ch13.md#wb-1-p135b), [wb.1:p135c](../corpus/wb/ch13.md#wb-1-p135c)).
- **Vol. 1 p. 375, ll. 19-24 (img. 385):** the sky dog's mouth by season in four stretches cut at sunset, midnight, sunrise (or dawn) and noon; no «ཐུན» on the page, so this place is no count of watches at all ([wb.1:p375](../corpus/wb/ch23.md#wb-1-p375)).
- **Vol. 2 p. 403, ll. 2-14 of 28 (img. 411):** WB sets its «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» against earlier writings that, it says, left the own and the other time unexplained and mixed the two senses; the own time has eight watches with eight planet-nāga pairs (the eighth pair Rāhu with the Rigs ldan, so the pairs fill the eight once); the other time has the houses cutting the day's measure and «ཆུ་སྲང་བཞི་རེ», four chu srang each for the fierce Rigs ldan; the fine reckoning is left to the Mchog dga' commentary ([wb.2:p403b](../corpus/wb/ch34.md#wb-2-p403b), [wb.2:p403c](../corpus/wb/ch34.md#wb-2-p403c)).

**Set against the pool.** The Dbyangs 'char verse in the Rnam dpyod (svo:p271w) and the books after it (svo:p271x) cut the day into eight half-watches and the night into eight, sixteen in all; the Hevajra and Kālacakra books (svo:p271o) count eight watches, four and four, each in two moves, and thirty-two water-hours with a water-hour a quarter of a watch. WB's p. 101 numbering (a fourth, a seventh, an eighth place in one run) fits the second; its p. 135 wheels and p. 403 pairs use the first's words (half-watch, nāga) and say «ཉིན་མཚན» for what is divided. WB's own sentence on p. 403 that earlier books mix «own and other» is the one place where it states a reason for its division, and it names no book. This leaves RD322's reading, WB's eight as eight in all, as the one the page of p. 101 supports and the sixteen as what the Dbyangs 'char's commentaries add; it is a reading from the wording, no commentator says it (svo:p271s, corrected in part by RD326).

**Thirty-two against thirty (q968).** WB's own water-hour counts do not meet the Kun 'dus chen mo's four chu tshod to a half-watch: p. 101's stretches sum to thirty chu tshod, p. 403 counts in chu srang, and neither page says how many chu tshod the day-and-night holds or how long a watch is.

**Not found on the scans.** No page of the five names a watch's length in any unit, and none quotes the Dbyangs 'char or the Muktāvalī by name.
