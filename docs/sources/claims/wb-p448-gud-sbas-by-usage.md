# «གུད་སྦས» is WB's own word on p. 448, not «ཧྲུལ»

# «རྟོགས་དཀའ་གུད་སྦས་ཀུན་བསལ་བའི» on p. 448: «གུད་སྦས» is WB's own word, «ཧྲུལ» and «རྡུལ» are not

On p. 448, l. 24 the praise of the book as clearing away «རྟོགས་དཀའ་གུད་སྦས་ཀུན་བསལ་བའི» ("all that is hard to grasp, the hidden part, cleared away") has «གུད་སྦས» (*gud sbas*), the hidden part that WB sets down apart ([wb-ch34-gud-sbas-hidden-part](wb-ch34-gud-sbas-hidden-part.md)). The reading rests on counts of WB's own usage and on two machine readers of the scan, not on a letter cut by eye: the 1996 etext (I1KG12907, p. 448) reads «གུད་སྦས» in this line; Yigdzin-1 and MITRA on the Zhol print read the same ([ch27-zhol-p448-two-readers-sbas](ch27-zhol-p448-two-readers-sbas.md)); and the pair «གུད་སྦས» occurs ten times besides in the two volumes' etexts (vol. 2 printed pp. 90, 142, 379, 428, 459, 468, 469, 471; vol. 1 etext pp. 205 and 461), always meaning what is not openly stated, while «གུད་ཧྲུལ», «གུད་ཡས» and «གུད་རྡུལ» have no hit at all in either volume. «ཧྲུལ» (worn out, rotten) is WB's word for rags and decay (vol. 2 pp. 242, 270, 277, 283), never after «གུད». So the reprint's stack, read by eye as «ཧྲུལ» four times, is the shape of «སྦས» on a heavy bitmap, and the Zhol etext's «ཡས» is its own slip. The meaning of «གུད» alone is not stated by WB; in the pair the sense is "hidden away". Counts of the 1996 etext are an OCR of the typeset print, so the proof is the agreement of the four readers (etext, Yigdzin-1, MITRA, and the ten other uses), against one eye reading of a blot.
