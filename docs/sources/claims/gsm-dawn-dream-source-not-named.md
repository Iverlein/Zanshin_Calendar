# Gtsang sman names no written source for the physician's dawn dream; his opening verses name only his teachers' instructions

In the opening verses of the Zhal gdams do shal (etext pp. 261-262) Gtsang sman Ye shes bzang po says he takes the Four Tantras, the great texts of Indian and Tibetan scholars in succession, the oral instructions and key practices he sought from his teachers, the secret words of the mother and the dakinis and the practices of Yu thog and others, tested by use. He names no text; neither the Moonbeams nor the Siddhasara is mentioned. His first examination, the dream (gsm:p263-264), is the physician's own dream in the dawn before the sick man's messenger comes: a white or blue horse, going to a hilltop or the north-east, meeting known well-dressed people, song and dance, flowers and joy mean the sick will not die whatever the illness; going naked, falling into muddy water, weariness, dead creatures, weeping, bare heads and worn clothes, quarrels, hail, the sun going mean the physician does not go. It joins the Four Tantras' ch. 7 night rule (the dawn dream, clear, gives its fruit) to the physician's setting out, and has no counterpart in the Moonbeams. The second half of q973 stays open: the work's own claim is oral instruction, not a text.
