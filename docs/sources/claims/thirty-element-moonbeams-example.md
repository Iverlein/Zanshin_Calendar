# The element of the thirty and of the reversed year in the Moonbeams' examples

# The element of the thirty and of the reversed year: the examples take the year's own element, for a wood person

Question 259, in part, from the *Moonbeams* as KD prints it.

- The *Moonbeams*' thirty bases (kd.3:p498-500) are written for a wood example: every base carries its relation to wood in brackets, «ཤིང་(རང)» wood (own), «མེ་(བུ)» fire (son), «ས་(གྲོགས)» earth (friend), «ལྕགས་(དགྲ)» iron (enemy), «ཆུ་(མ)» water (mother). It does not say whose element wood is.
- The reversed turning of the year, the *Moonbeams*' own count of the number of years (kd.3:p489-490), settles it for that count: for a wood male rat the man begins from the fire tiger, the woman from the water monkey. Wood's son is fire and its mother is water, so the element used is wood, the year's own (the power, དབང་ཐང, dbang thang), not the rat's body element, water, whose son would be wood and whose mother iron. The notes' remark that the thirty's pairs agree with the element of the year-horse (ལོ་རྟ, lo rta) of the one reckoned (wb.2:p532-533) and the phrase «བརྩི་བྱའི་ཁམས» (claim thirty-element-same-phrase) are read with it.
- The two are the *Moonbeams*' counts and the notes call the thirty's count a different one («as many as the years»); the example is strong for the year's own element and not a proof for WB's thirty.
