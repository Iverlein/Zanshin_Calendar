# The thirty: the tenth point, the palace of the nine continents, for subjects, retinue and servants

The tenth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the palace of the nine continents (ཕོ་བྲང་གླིང་དགུ, pho brang gling dgu), is read for subjects, retinue and servants. Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the five arrows and the one bow are lost | Not lost, good | The five arrows and the bow are lost from the hand: subjects and retinue fall out and cause defilement (དམེ, dme) | — |
| 2 | Whether the servants are deceived by demons | Not deceived, good | The demons deceive the servants' minds: a great risk that what is in their heart goes astray (གློ་བར་འགྲོ, glo bar 'gro) | — |
| 3 | Whether companions steal secrets | Subjects and retinue are loyal and steal no minds; servants grow, good | Companions bind by bribes, and people who steal and lure minds come | Take great care |
| 4 | Whether the yoke of misfortune (བྱུར, byur) is carried | Not carried; merit and wealth grow | The yoke of misfortune is carried, bad | Strive at the rites of expelling misfortune and raising glory (བྱུར་ཕུད་དཔལ་བསྐྱེད, byur phud dpal bskyed) |
| 5 | Whether the mountain of misfortune is lifted into the lap | Never lifted, good | It is lifted, bad | The rite of expelling misfortune and raising glory |
| 6 | Whether the black load of misfortune is carried | Not carried, good | It is carried: others' misfortune falls on oneself | — |
| 7 | Whether the black road of misfortune is walked | Not walked, good | It is walked: blame comes for faults of every kind not committed, and sorrow in the near ones' place | The nine turnings back of sorrow |
| 8 | Whether the she-demon's discord has entered | It has not entered | It has: wealth as if swept by a broom, bad | — |

Whose year is reckoned: If it concerns subjects, the year of the lord who asks (notes, vol. 2, p. 532).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
