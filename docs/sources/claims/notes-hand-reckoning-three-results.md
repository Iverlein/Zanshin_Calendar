# The hand reckoning in the Lu manner: phung, klung and kyis

The hand reckoning of the notes' postscript (WB vol. 2, pp. 538–539), as Mipham's own print gives it (I2029 img. 744–745; wb.2:p538-539b):

- Its name is «ལག་རྩིས་ལཱུ་བཞིན», the hand reckoning "in the Lū manner": the print writes the first word with the long-vowel sign ཱ, as it writes the Sanskrit and Chinese words (བཻ་ཌཱུར), so «ལཱུ» is a foreign word, no Tibetan person or text; the colophon says the method was written down as the Mongol geshe Samten (སོག་པོ་དགེ་བཤེས་བསམ་གཏན) guided it by hand (questions 101 and 580; the 1996 print has «ལུ»). The word stands at the head of img. 744's second line and again on img. 745 (the colophon), «ལཱུ» both times by Yigdzin-1, the first with a hooked sign on the scan that is more than a plain ུ; ཱུ against a subjoined ླུ is not settled on the 1-bit bitmap (RD132, 2026-11-03).
- The method: the year the questioner first thinks of is taken from the ox round the twelve places by the month number, then by the date number, then by the year named, one place back, then «རྒྱན་ཀྭང» (the 1996 «ཐུན་ཀུང»; read by the machine, not settled by eye) and the like; the result is whichever of the last three places falls on the eighth, ninth or tenth count, from rat, horse, bird or hare.
- The three results are named: the eighth is *phung* (ཕུང, the machine and the eye), the ninth *klung* (ཀླུང), the tenth *kyis* (ཀྱཱིས); "by these three alone everything is examined". The 1996 print's «mung, krung, kyis» of question 353 are its misreadings of these. Nothing beyond ten need be counted.
- What the three mean beyond this the page does not say.
