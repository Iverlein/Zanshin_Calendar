# Karma chags med: from dusk Māra's obstacles begin and the wrathful yidam is recited; from midnight the clear light of sleep is held (RD405)

In Karma chags med's verses on the times of the day and night, the evening from dusk to midnight is the time the heroes and ḍākinīs gather (so the torma offering and the feast gathering are good and fierce mantras succeed), and, above all, the evening is the time the Sage subdued Māra (བདུད, bdud) and so the time when Māra's obstacles begin; against them the wrathful yidam's recitation is done. From midnight until the middle of the dawn is the time to take hold of the clear light of sleep (གཉིད་ཀྱི་འོད་གསལ, gnyid kyi 'od gsal); the dawn is the time of the Buddha's awakening and good for practice when the wind is held.

This puts Māra's activity in the first part of the night, up to midnight, and sets the clear light of sleep from midnight on; it does not speak of dreams in this place, and it does not call the midnight dream Māra's. It is the practice-side counterpart of the line on the dream of midnight (rrd.19:p271): the hours that are Māra's are the hours before it. The three other prints read the same, with «བརྩམ» for «རྩོམ» in two of them and «བྱེད» for «ཕྱེད» in one.
