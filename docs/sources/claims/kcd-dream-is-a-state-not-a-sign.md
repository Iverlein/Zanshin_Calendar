# The Kālacakra and the Vimalaprabhā treat the dream as one of four states and as an argument for mind alone, and read no dream image as a sign

In the Kālacakra Tantra (Dus kyi 'khor lo, དུས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ) and the Vimalaprabhā (Dri med 'od, དྲི་མེད་འོད) the dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) is one of four states of the mind, waking, dream, deep sleep and the fourth, each held by one of the Rāhus (སྒྲ་གཅན, sgra gcan), and in the individual chapter the dream body of habit is an argument that mind alone, not atoms or breath, holds pain and bliss. In no passage read in Derge vols. 11 and 12 (the world and individual chapters' commentary, pp. 187-539 and 489-490) is a dream image read as a sign: nothing of the kind «if one dreams X, then Y». The word dream is also used in the opening verses for the ascetic's seed that leaks in dream and in waking (the cause of loss, not a sign).
