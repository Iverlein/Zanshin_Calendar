# RST is Klong rdol bla ma's own dream text, the source of KBS's verses, and holds KDL's text in the same collection

The Asian Legacy Library etext *Rmi ltas sna tshogs brtag thabs*, "methods of examining the various dream signs" (RST; BDRC MW1ALS06558E, etext IE1ALS06558E, 20 pp., open to the etext API), is the dream text of Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang that KBS's verses come from. Three things tie them:

- BDRC gives the work to Klong rdol bla ma (person P22) as its author, and the etext ends «ཞེས་པ་འདི་ཡང་ཀློང་རྡོལ་བླ་མ་ངག་དབང་བློ་བཟང་གིས་བྲིས་པའོ», "this too was written by Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang" (rst:p17-20). The colophon stands after the women's verses and names no section: it closes the collection or its last piece, and the dream part itself (pp. 3-8) carries no author's name;
- BDRC's full-text search of three of KBS's verse lines (the dawn third, the good sign of climbing a high mountain, the opening of the death omens) finds each in this etext; it finds them also in four etexts that are search-only and could not be read (MW21880, the folk collection MDP; MW25126, Padma rdo rje's work on sleep and dreams; MW1AC12, a book on Tibet's old rites; MW1PD95727, a volume on crafts, reckoning and dance), so the verses circulate in other prints. Reading RST pp. 3-8 against kbs:p68-70 and kbs:p72-78 shows the same verses in the same order: the thirds of the night, the good signs, the practitioner's signs and bad omens, the spirit classes, the dreams of death;
- the same etext is the whole text in one: the dream verses (pp. 3-8), the abridgement of Garga's omens that KDL prints without any dream (pp. 8-13, kdl:p235-241), the examination of men (pp. 13-17) and of women (pp. 17-20). So KDL's text and the dream text are two parts of one collection of omens of various kinds, which the title of KDL, "drawn from Klong rdol bla ma's collected works", fits.

The text says itself that it is a retelling: its first verses (rst:p1-8) give "as taught in the Mother's profound Chöd visualisation collection" (མ་གཅིག་ཟབ་མོ་གཅོད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་འབུམ, ma gcig zab mo gcod kyi dmigs 'bum) the sorting of dreams by their fruit. The Mongolian bundle SKP gives the same verses under Vairocana's name (skp-second-text-is-the-rst-verses), so Klong rdol bla ma is the compiler or the last writer of the verses, not necessarily their first author.

The etext is not a scan: no scans of it are on BDRC ("not available"), and the verses were read in the etext only, never on a page image. No print of the text out of his collected works (the gsung 'bum) was found on BDRC: the ACIP etext has no print identified, and KDL's print holds only the Garga part.
