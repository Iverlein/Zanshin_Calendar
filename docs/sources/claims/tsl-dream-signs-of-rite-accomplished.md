# Dreams that show a rite accomplished, in the treasure instruction: by rite, no hour or day

In the compiled volume's section on the recitation of the *Gsang ba zil gnon* cycle, a verse list (its attribution inside the page not stated, probably the treasure's own lines) gives the dreams that show a rite has been accomplished. For the rites of pacifying, enriching and power the dreams include: sun and moon rising; teaching, blowing the conch, beating the drum; being given food and goods; a rainbow on the body; bathing and white clothes; a throne; giving empowerment; wealth and a harvest; irrigating and sowing; flowers of the nāga king; tasting the three whites; climbing up; a vast body filling the world; crossing rivers and building bridges; finding jewels. For the fierce rite's killing: arriving at a house of swords, armour, leading an army and winning, scattering another's head and heart, hunting, flesh and blood, beasts eating a corpse, the enemy put in prison, striking with weapons, hurling the zor, a mountain turned over, the body aflame, a mountain set on fire. The key is the rite done, not the hour: no day or time is named. Before it the same page names waking signs (meeting the deity in person, prophecy, blazing heat, a light body). The list has dream signs shared with the treasure sādhana's two verses (sun and moon rising, climbing up), a fact for the dream books' "adorned with clothes", "mountain", "sun and moon" entries: here they are good signs of practice, read for a practitioner and a rite, not for any dreamer.
