# The Svapnohana colophon names only its translator Vibhūticandra; Ngag dbang is not identified

The Derge Tengyur colophon of the *Svapnohana* (rmi lam brtag pa; etext VE23703_1344, p. 271 line 4) names no author and one man only: "made by the eastern Indian paṇḍita Vibhūticandra as his own translation" (རྒྱ་གར་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་པཎྜི་ཏ་བི་བྷཱུ་ཏི་ཙནྡྲས་རང་འགྱུར་དུ་མཛད་པའོ, rang gyur). The BDRC catalogue gives the work (WA0RT0621, instance MW23703_1749) one creator, role "main author" (rtsom pa po gtso bo): Vibhūticandra (P00EGS1017688, "writer and translator of texts in the Tibetan Tanjur"), an attribution contributed by the ATII project, not an independent witness.

The preceding work in the same volume, the *Deceiving Death* ('chi ba bslu ba), names its author in its colophon (p. 268 line 3, and the etext's repeat) as the teacher Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa (ངག་གི་དབང་ཕྱུག་གྲགས་པ), whom the catalogue (WA0RT0620, creator P8946) gives as Vāgīśvarakīrti; its translators are the Indian abbot Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna and the lotsāba Rin chen bzang po.

The remedy's «ངག་དབང» (p. 271) occurs once in the etext of the volume, in this line, in the short form; the long form is the Deceiving Death's. Nothing in the colophon, the catalogue or the text says whether the short form means the same man, a teacher whose view Vibhūticandra follows, or Vāgīśvara as a name of Mañjuśrī. The BDRC full-text search for «ངག་དབང་དགོངས་པ» finds the same verse in the volume and in two other Tengyur copies (I1PD95859, I1KG10372) and adds no author. So the identification with Vāgīśvarakīrti stays probable by neighbourhood and unsettled (q796 stays open). A reader of the tradition could say whether the abbreviation is current for Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa. State: etext and catalogue; the colophon is not read on the scan.
