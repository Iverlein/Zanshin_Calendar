# The Dbyangs 'char's half-watches are eight by day and eight by night, equal within each (RD326, q946)

The Dbyangs 'char verse says the day-and-night's half-watches are «cut in eight» (བརྒྱད་བགོས) and the Karma Nges don bstan 'dzin book states the reckoning: the day-and-night has four great watches by day and four by night, each halved, so there are eight half-watches by day and eight by night; the Deu dmar piece divides the measure of the day-and-night by the nāga in the same way (sixteen in all, the Kālacakra's sixteen half-watches of the great commentary Dri med 'od); each day's half-watch is an eighth of the day's measure and each night's an eighth of the night's measure («the day's measure and the night's measure, divided by the nāga, is got»: nāga, ཀླུ, klu, stands for eight). Equal, then, within the day and within the night, and unequal between day and night whenever day and night differ in length; the Kun 'dus chen mo, quoting the Mchog dga' commentary on the Dbyangs 'char, gives each half-watch four chu tshod, going right by day and left by night in the bright half. WB's «day-and-night measure is divided by the nāga» (vol. 1 p. 135) is the same wording. This corrects svo:p271s, which took the eight as eight in all and the same count as the Hevajra books' eight watches: the Hevajra and Kālacakra books' eight are the great watches, and the half-watches of the Dbyangs 'char are sixteen. Open: the Dbyangs 'char verse as the 'Bri lugs work quotes it is ambiguous by itself and is settled only by the later books; WB vol. 2 p. 403's «nyin mtshan thun brgyad» (eight watches) may then name the eight of the day, and the eight of the night, and not eight in all. Eight times four chu tshod is thirty-two, against thirty for the day-time at an equinox.
