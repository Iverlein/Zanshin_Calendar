# The Blue Beryl's two places on the first part of the night are two rules, both Zur mkhar ba's sentences copied

The Blue Beryl (Sde srid Sangs rgyas rgya mtsho, part 2, the commentary on the Explanatory Tantra) speaks of the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod) in two places in ch. 7, which are two different rules, not one rule read twice; and the first is Zur mkhar ba's sentence copied. Everything etext only (VEIE0OPICF5AF372_I1PD411; Zur mkhar ba, I8LS19191; 2026-11-10).

(1) Etext p. 209 (printed p. 181): «མཚན་སྟོད་ཅེས་པ་ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད་དུ་རྨི་ལམ་རྨིས་པ…»: the first part of the night, dreaming up to midnight: its fruit is far off and small and weak; the dawn dream with no sleep and no unfitting talk between brings great fruit that day; giving and burnt offering make a bad dream's fruit small. It is the gloss of the Eight Limbs' verse «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་འབྲས་བུ་རིང་…» (the Moonbeams' lines), with the lemma lines left out; Zur mkhar ba p. 351 has the same sentences with the lemma lines in, and it is he who recasts the Moonbeams' «མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད» as «མཚན་སྟོད་ཅེས་པ་ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད»; Byang pa p. 219 and the Moonbeams keep ཕྱེད་པ (st:p31-32b). The fruit of such a dream exists, it is far and small.

(2) Etext p. 216 (printed p. 188), its own comment on the Four Tantras' two lines, after the six kinds of dream: a dream of any of the six in the first part of the night, which is dusk (སྲོད་ལ), is not cut by other sleep, becomes forgotten, and brings neither good nor bad fruit; midnight is of a kind with it; a dream at the close of dawn, with sleep not breaking in again and the dream clear in the mind, brings its fruit for certain. This is word for word Zur mkhar ba p. 356 (one word differs: «གང་དམིགས་ཀྱི» for his «གང་རྨིས་ཀྱི»), so the Blue Beryl's p. 216 is no second witness (st:p31-32).

So the answer to what the Blue Beryl says beyond p. 209 on the Four Tantras' own lines is: p. 216 and p. 217 (the good dreams, citing the Moonbeams by name), and nothing else in the dream passage (pp. 208–211 and 214–218 read line by line; pp. 212–213 only through a search of the whole etext, in which ཐོ་རངས appears on pp. 211 and 216 and ནམ་ཕྱེད on pp. 209, 211 and 216 within this range). The two rules differ in span ("up to midnight", with fruit far off; "dusk, midnight alike", without fruit) and in what the early dream has (a distant, small fruit; none), and neither Zur mkhar ba nor the Blue Beryl reconciles them: each sets them side by side, the first as the Moonbeams' on the Eight Limbs, the second as the explanation of the Tantra's lines. The Blue Beryl copies Zur mkhar ba's text for both and has the second sentence in common with no other commentary read (Byang pa, Rin spungs pa, the Drikung, Skyem pa: st:p31-32).
