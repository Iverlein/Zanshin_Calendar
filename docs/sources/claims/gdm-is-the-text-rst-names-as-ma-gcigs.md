# The Mother's Chöd collection is the text's own first verses: a manuscript holds the whole under that title without an author

A manuscript in the Mongolian National Library, the fourth text of a volume of the Dga' ldan Byang rtse college's learned collection (BDRC MW1NLM3822_O1NLM3822_004, 13 folios, the page after 13b lost; open scans), carries the dream verses of RST under the title "the sorting of all the fruits, good and bad, of night dreams, as taught in the Mother's profound Chöd visualisation collection" (མ་གཅིག་ཟབ་མོ་གཅོད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་འབུམ་ལས་གསུངས་པ་མཚན་ལམ་གྱི་འབྲས་བུ་བཟང་ངན་ཀུན་གྱི་རྣམ་དབྱེ, ma gcig zab mo gcod kyi dmigs 'bum las gsungs pa mtshan lam gyi 'bras bu bzang ngan kun gyi rnam dbye, BDRC's catalogue title). Its first verses are RST's: the lama and the refuges, then "as taught in the Mother's profound Chöd visualisation collection, the sorting of the fruits of night dreams is shown here" (img. 88).

What this settles about the "Chöd source":

- The words "from the Mother's Chöd visualisation collection" are the text's own first verses, in the manuscript as in RST, MDP's p. 138 and Padma rdo rje's quotation; no other passage of a Chöd collection has them. BDRC's exact-phrase search for «ཟབ་མོ་གཅོད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་འབུམ» gives seven hits, all this text (RST, the KBS article's print, MDP, Padma rdo rje's work, the book of Tibet's old rites, the volume of crafts and reckoning, and the manuscript's title); «གཅོད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་འབུམ་ལས་གསུངས་པ» gives the manuscript's title and Padma rdo rje's work. No dream passage of a Chöd collection, and no Chöd text with these verses, was found on BDRC;
- the manuscript's surviving text has the same parts in the same order as RST: the dream verses (img. 88-93), the death omens by body, speech and mind (img. 93-95), Garga's omens in the Tengyur's abridgement (from img. 95: rainbows, great sounds, birds), the examination of men and of women (from img. 102), breaking off in the women's marks at img. 113. No author's name stands in it; the page after 13b, where a colophon like RST's «ཀློང་རྡོལ་བླ་མ་ངག་དབང་བློ་བཟང་གིས་བྲིས་པའོ» could have stood, is lost;
- so there are now two ways the text reaches us: with Klong rdol bla ma's name (RST's colophon, Padma rdo rje's quotation, headed with the name, as BDRC's snippet shows) and without any name in a Mongolian manuscript of a monastic collection. The manuscript is undated and the volume's date is not given in the catalogue read; that it is older than Klong rdol bla ma (1719-1794) is not shown, so the manuscript does not say whether he composed the verses or copied them from the Mother's collection. The claim rst-is-klong-rdols-dream-text, "compiler or last writer, not necessarily the first author", stands.
