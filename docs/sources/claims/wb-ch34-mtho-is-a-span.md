# One mtho is a span of twelve finger-breadths in the dictionaries, which makes the digging figure's six finger-breadths a half mtho

In the Tibetan dictionaries of Steinert's collection (read on 2026-10-28) «མཐོ» (*mtho*) is a measure of length: the span from the tip of the extended thumb to the tip of the extended middle finger, and «མཐོ་གང» (*mtho gang*) is one such span. *Tshig mdzod chen mo* gives it a second sense, the breadth of twelve finger-breadths (སོར་མོ་བཅུ་གཉིས, *sor mo bcu gnyis*); the Rangjung Yeshe dictionary, Ives Waldo's, the *Dung dkar tshig mdzod chen mo*, Mahāvyutpatti's *vitasti* and the *Sgra bye brag tu rtogs byed chen mo* give the same twelve. So by the dictionaries one *mtho* is twelve finger-breadths.

WB's digging figure (p. 387, «ལྟེ་བར་མཐོ་གང་སོར་དྲུག་གནས», the navel one *mtho* and six finger-breadths) then reads: the stake is half a *mtho* («མཐོ་ཕྱེད», six finger-breadths), the face and chest one each (twelve), the navel one and a half (eighteen); the six finger-breadths of the navel are the same half *mtho* as the stake's. That makes the p. 387 example internally even, which a *mtho* of a tenth of an unknown plot does not by itself do. The two readings stay apart: ch. 32's count (wb.2:p281) makes the *mtho* a tenth of the plot and the stake a whole *mtho*; the digging rite's «རང་མཐོ་བཅུ་པའི་ཚད» can be the plot's tenth or ten spans of the man himself (120 finger-breadths). No text read says which a digger measures with. What the sources allow: a *mtho* of twelve finger-breadths is the common sense of the word and fits p. 387's own numbers; the plot's tenth is the sense ch. 32 and the Bhutan digest use. Not decided; the build need not give a length.
