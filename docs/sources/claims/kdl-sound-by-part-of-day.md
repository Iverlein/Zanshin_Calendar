# A sound in the sky is read by the part of the day (Garga, in Klong rdol's abridgement)

The abridgement of Garga's omen examination that Klong rdol bla ma (Ngag dbang blo bzang, 1719–1794) gives, pp. 235–241 of the Bylakuppe collection, reads the great sound (སྒྲ་ཆེན, sgra chen) of the sky by the part of the day in which it is heard, and each part has its own person harmed. In the verse's order: sunrise, the kingdom is ruined («ཉི་མ་ཤར་ཚེ…རྒྱལ་སྲིད་འཇིག»); early morning, the low born (རིགས་ངན); midday, brahmins; sunset, the company of lords, not good; dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs), harm to servants; midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed), a flesh-eating demon (སྲིན་པོ, srin po) is let loose; waking time (ནམ་ལངས, nam langs), harm to guests. A sound between two quarters of the sky: many cattle die. The three junctures (དུས་གསུམ་མཚམས, dus gsum mtshams) are named once more: a great sound in the north then costs a friend or a holy monk his life.

It is a rule for a sound, not for a dream, and the names of the parts of the day are the text's own words; no hour is given for them. The scan's reading is by machine with the flagged words checked; the line of the cattle at midday has two unclear words and is left out here.
