# The sun's klung of p. 186 is a klung-horse counted by the sun, probably the day's

On p. 186 the sun's klung of «ཉི་མའི་ཀླུང» is read «ཀླུང» on the Taikhang crop as on the Gser thang reprint. The sun's name stands for the day in WB's own p. 223 («ཉི་མའི་གདུག་པ་བཅུ་གཉིས», the twelve of the day), and KD vol. 2's verse on the peahen of the breath-taking sets «ཉི་མའི་དབང་ཐང» and «ཉི་མའི་ཀླུང་རྟ» beside the year's king-planet, so the sun's klung is a klung-horse (wind-horse) counted by the sun, probably the day's, of the same kind as the month's «ཟླ་བའི་ཀླུང་རྟ» a Bhutan reckoning uses; this is an inference from the words, not a statement of any text, and which day's klung Langging the red is "produced together with" is not said. The table of the twelve year animals before the line (rat to tiger, ox to sheep, tiger to hare, hare to rat, dragon to dog, snake to bird, horse to monkey, sheep to horse, monkey to dragon, bird to hare, dog to ox, pig to snake) is by years and is not the triads' klung of vol. 1, p. 254. Narrows question 432; leaves its sense open.
