# The thirteen kinds of corpse

The seventh examination's thirteen kinds of corpse and their divisions (wb.2:p72-74; the notes, wb.2:p503-504). The kind decides the corpse's equipment and rites in the ninth examination.

| Kind | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| the old (*bkra*) | once men of 70 and women of 60, now men past 60 and women of 50; in short anyone who has seen a grandchild; 5 or 7 sub-kinds by the old texts |
| the infant (*sri'u*) | six by growth: miscarried; before its voice; some days after its voice; hand reaching its mouth; walking; playing |
| the blade (violent death) | thirteen kinds (lightning, arrow, water, horse, abroad, hanging, frost, choking, …), gathered into four: wild (could not take a drop of water), drawn out (lived some days and was treated), *gri bag* (killed by a spear), "looking yak" (killed by a wild yak or a tiger) |
| childbed (*mtshun*) | best: having borne a boy and a girl; middling: many children not yet separated; last: died as children were being born |
| the boy (*yul lha*) | dead at one (*bya ra*), 2–7 (*zhing sa*), 8–9 (*thon bu*), 10–13 or above (*rnel*) |
| the girl (*mo sman*) | dead at 13–15 the best, at 8–12 the middling, at 3–7 the little |
| the unmarried man (*ro gog*) | dead at twenty without spouse; very impure; hidden in an ant's hole |
| the unmarried woman (*spre shal*) | dead at twenty without household; the same |
| the *bzed* | a man or woman of 15–17 dead without household |
| the heirless | with a son white, with a daughter speckled, with neither black |
| the ill-natured | hanging, *snel* (four: child unborn, after birth, of a child's illness, a husband dying with a child in the womb), poison |
| the kin-slayer's corpse | killing within the household, among in-laws and relatives, or within the paternal clan |
| the leper (*bsha' ya*) | a leper's corpse |

Besides: seven chronic illnesses, seven epidemic ones, seven sudden deaths and seven disabilities, each listed by name, and the low caste, eight. The notes call the thirteen blades "unclear and tangled" and leave the last three to be examined.
