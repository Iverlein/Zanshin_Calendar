# The Vimalaprabhā sets waking against day, dream against the junctures between watches and deep sleep against the great night, a correspondence, not a rule of when one dreams

In the Vimalaprabhā (Dri med 'od, དྲི་མེད་འོད) on the individual chapter (nang gi le'u, ནང་གི་ལེའུ), the mind's three states are set against the parts of the day: the coarse mind, the state of waking (སད་པ, sad pa), has the mark of day; the fine mind, the state of dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam), has the mark of the junctures between watches (ཐུན་མཚམས, thun mtshams); the other fine one, deep sleep (ཤིན་ཏུ་གཉིད་ལོག་པ, shin tu gnyid log pa), has the mark of the great night (མཚན་མོ་ཆེན་པོ, mtshan mo chen po). The fourth state belongs to the moment the seed moves. This is a correspondence of the three states with the day, the twilights and the night, taught for the yogin's body; it does not say when in the night any given dream comes, and it names no lunar day, planet or season. The three phrases are found in the Derge Vimalaprabhā, in the Dpe bsdur ma etext and in Karma pa Mi bskyod rdo rje's quotation by BDRC's exact-string search (hits for «ཕྲ་མོ་ནི་ཐུན་མཚམས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད»), so the wording is not a one-copy slip.
