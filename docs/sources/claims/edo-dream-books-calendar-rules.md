# What two more Edo dream texts say of a date, season or night: seasons and a half-night, never the first dream's night

Two further Edo prints, both open on the NDL's site, name no night of the year for the first dream (初夢): the 夢合延寿袋大成 (1814) mentions it once, as the best moment to see Fuji (ymk:p6), and the 永代大雑書 (1856) never (ezs:p30; its OCR holds no 初夢). What they give are rules of another kind. Seasons: a scarecrow dream is good in the nine months of spring to autumn, best in mid-autumn, not good in the three winter months (ymk:p30); a colt dreamed in spring brings a good year, in summer fire is to be feared, in autumn or winter half-good (ymk:p52). Time of night: a dream before midnight shows its outcome far off, one after midnight near (ezs:p31, read on the image). Time to fulfilment: the real dream shows within three days (ymk:p63). Handling: a good dream is told to no one and honoured with offerings counted by the months, twelve, thirteen in a leap year (ymk:p59); a bad one is washed away with a verse by the fourth hour of the morning. Neither text ties a dream to a 庚申 night or a day of the cycle (the 庚申 article of the 永代大雑書 is about quarrels and intercourse). Edo prints in the OCR's rough text: machine reading, the quoted lines partly checked on the images.
