# Schneider's edition of 'Deceiving Death' gives the black girl's verse in Sanskrit with kālarātrī, a Yama's-wife gloss, yamamaṇḍala for «gshin rje'i gnas», and pañcatva as the idiom behind «lnga yi gong du» (RD382)

RD382, inv-ds (2026-11-14). Johannes Schneider's edition of Vāgīśvarakīrti's Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśa (Vienna 2010, Österreichische Akademie der Wissenschaften; the Grundtext pp. 73-122, translation, notes pp. 169-196 and the introduction pp. 35-50 are open access on verlag.oeaw.ac.at) gives the Sanskrit and Tibetan of 'Deceiving Death' chapter 1 with the parallels to the Saṃvarodaya (Sut) chapter 19. The facts, in the pool's own words (the book is a modern work: facts only).

The black girl's verse. Strophe 1.127 of the Sanskrit reads «kālī kumārī saṃkruddhā yā tu badhnāti bandhanaiḥ / kālarātrī tu sā jñeyā saptāhān mṛtyukārikā», against the Tibetan «bu mo nag mo rab khros nas / gang zhig bcings pas 'chi ba ni / de ni dus mtshan yin zhes bya / zhag bdun nas ni 'chi byed yin». So the Sanskrit of 'Deceiving Death' has the verse, and «dus mtshan» is kālarātrī there too; «zhag bdun» is saptāha. Schneider's note to 127c gives the parallel as Sut 19.23c and holds, against Tsuda's kālarātris, that kālarātrī is the original reading of the Saṃvarodaya as well (four of eight witnesses CDEP); he says the word is a personification of the end of the world, but can name Durgā or Yamī (Apte), and that the Tibetan gloss to 127 (the 4th of the verse) reads it as «gshin rje'i chung ma», the wife of the death god. Strophe 128: «kṛṣṇavastrā tu yā nārī kālī kāmayate naram … yāty asau yamamaṇḍalam» with the Tibetan «de ni gshin rje'i gnas su 'gro»: the end of that line has «yamamaṇḍala», the place of Yama, not yamadarśana, and Schneider gives the Saṃvarodaya 19.23 as its parallel and calls the syntax of both broken, with the readings uncertain.

The first signs. Strophe 1.36 (parallel Sut 19.2, «trayadivasaparād ūrdhvaṃ pañcatvaṃ gacchate tadā») has in the Tibetan of the Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra ch. 23 (Cmt, Schneider cites it as TT 70, 328a6) «rkang mthil lte bar 'bugs byed na / zhag gsum nas ni 'chi bar 'gyur» («when it pricks the soles and the navel, after three days he dies»), and in the Ḍākārṇava (D.mt, TT 19, 32b5) a span of two days. Neither has «five». The Sanskrit idiom «pañcatvaṃ gacchati» means to die (to go to the state of the five elements), so the Sdom 'byung's «lnga yi gong du 'gro bar 'gyur» renders «parād ūrdhvam pañcatvam» word by word: «beyond (pha rol) three days, afterwards (gong du) to fiveness (lnga yi) he goes»; the Tibetan «lnga» is not a count of days.
