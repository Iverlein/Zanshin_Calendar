# ba ra is cattle and goats, so «བ་རའི་འོ་མ» is herd milk; the lexicon only

RD393 (2026-11-11). The keyed Great Tibetan-Chinese Dictionary (tmz:box1) gives ba ra two senses: "cattle and goats" (ba phyugs dang ra) and "the byre where cattle graze"; Ives Waldo gives the same two. In «བ་རའི་འོ་མ» ("the milk of ba ra"), the form in which the Four Tantras list it among the foods that bring cold phlegm and in which the Blue Beryl takes it into the remedy for a spirit that leads in dream (st:p493b), the first sense is the one that can take «འོ་མ»: "milk of cattle and goats". So the milk is the ordinary herd-milk, mixed with dung and rubbed on the soles; the byre sense does not fit a milk. This is the lexicon's gloss; no commentary read (RD304) says what the milk is, and the dictionary does not say why a dream of being led away calls for it. The be'u bum's dream set does not use the phrase.
