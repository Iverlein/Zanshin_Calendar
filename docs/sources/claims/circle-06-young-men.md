# The circles: the sixth, the striped iron tiger, for young men

The sixth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the striped iron tiger, is read for young men in their prime. Its starting point: the tiger of the enemy element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: five starting points drawn from the tiger of the enemy element of the one reckoned: whether the life-arrow is broken at the joints; whether the tiger-skin with its three wheels is lost; whether the spear of the prime and the bow are broken; whether parted from the *gyi ling* horse; whether the hero's fighting ground is empty.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the life-arrow not broken at the waist: a good man first, the striped Chinese tiger's pattern on him, brilliant and radiant; life reaches its end: good. | the three-jointed arrow broken at the waist: the *dar sri* has risen; to himself, or father and son, or brothers, an obstacle to life by weapons. | ransom the life, recitation, the long-life rite, turning the weapon aside with a stag's horn, receiving the long-life empowerment and pressing down the *dar sri*. |
| Body | tiger-skin and leopard-quiver not loosened at the waist, like tiger dress adorning the body; four sons born: good. | loosened: arrow-quarrels; avoid going abroad; though flourishing until now, obstacles, illness and epidemics later. | fasten the wheel that guards against epidemics and holds the root of the generations, turn back, set *tsatsa* to the number of his years. |
| Power | spear and bow unbroken, food and wealth together; the paternal clan flourishes, all needs come; the sons' house on the right, the daughters' on the left; a white silk on the spear: good. | broken: great rise and decline and loss; gold, turquoise, food, wealth, cattle, horses, sheep, lands and houses slip away. | fasten the wheel of raising the power on the body, set up torma images for the male god and war god, press down the sri («གོང»), summon fortune. |
| Luck | not parted from the *gyi ling* horse, people speak kindly; he drinks a full measure of beer in his father's land; no strife or war; wishes fulfilled: good. | parted from the horse: talk and gossip strike; quarrels in the brothers' and sisters' places; the family wander abroad and die. | rites, restoring the luck, pressing down the enemy sri, setting up the dhāraṇī against gossip. |
| Mewa | the hero's fighting ground not empty, courage great; the line grows and all rely on him; he leads the ranks of the brave. | empty: courage small; a big boast but little reach in the end. | ransom the la and receive the fierce empowerment. |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
