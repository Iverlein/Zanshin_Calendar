# The thirty's count of years: the notes' wording and the manuals' count

How the thirty's count of years is worded, and what the manuals' count of years is (question 301):

- WB (vol. 1, p. 453) gives no number: from the male year count down, from the female year up, "where it falls is like the progressed year" (ལོག་མེན, log men). The notes (vol. 2, p. 532, read again on the Gser thang reprint, I3CN5029 img. 538, 2026-10-25) give it: «བརྩི་ལོ་གྲངས་དུ་ཡིན་པར», count "as many as the years of the one reckoned". The word is «ཡིན» (to be), not «ལོན» (to have lived) as in the notes on the circles (p. 533: «ལོ་གྲངས་དུ་ལོན་པར»); the two chapters are worded alike but for that verb.
- UT (ut:p4) gives the manuals' way to find that number: for the one reckoned, the years run one in the year of birth, thirteen at its next return, twenty-five, thirty-seven … ninety-seven; from the nearest of these one counts on with one's own years to the present year. So the "number of the years" (ལོ་གྲངས, lo grangs) of a person is the nominal age, one in the birth year, and UT uses the same word for the turning starting points it counts up and down. That the notes' «ལོ་གྲངས» is this number is an inference from the identical word; the text does not say it.
- Through the sixty or the twelve: UT names the starting years by element (for a wood male rat, the fire tiger and the water monkey), and the *Moonbeams* names every pair by element (dz:i029-034). The pebbles compare the aspects of the year reached (life force, body, power, luck) with the person's own, and those depend on the year's element as well as its animal; so the count runs through the sixty. Inference; no text states it.
- Not settled: whether the pair's own year counts as the first place or the count starts from the next.
