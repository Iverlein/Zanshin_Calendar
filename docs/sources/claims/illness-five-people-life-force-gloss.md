# The five people the sick need: the manuals' gloss, the life force's mother, son, enemy and friend

WB vol. 1, p. 445 names five people the sick need: the nurse (ནད་གཡོག, nad g.yog) by the mother, the physician (ལྷ་རྗེ, lha rje) by the son, the messenger (ཕོ་ཉ, pho nya) by the friend, the pillow-guard (སྔས་བསྲུང་པ, sngas bsrung pa), and then «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམ་ན».

Two twentieth-century Tibetan medical manuals quote the verse "from the same" (WB, called there the White Beryl) and add one sentence after it: «ཞེས་ནད་པའི་སྲོག་གི་མ་བུ་དགྲ་གྲོགས་ལ་བྱའོ», "this is done with the mother, son, enemy and friend of the sick person's life force". Both are BDRC etexts found by full-text search on 2026-11-01: Khyentse Wangpo's and Takla Norbu's medical manuals (MW1KG8532, etext I1KG8565, printed p. 445-446) and the same lines in another etext of the manual (I1KG21872, etext p. 239); a third copy, Takla Norbu's *Zla shel nor bu'i sgron me* (MW20065, 313 pp., Lhasa 1996), gives the same sentence in a search snippet. All three quote the verse as «ཕོ་ཉ་གྲོགས་ལ་སྔས་བསྲུང་བ། མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམས་ན། ཞུད་དང་རྐྱེན་གྱིས་འཆི་མི་འགྱུར།» (one copy «ལྔས་བསྲུང་བ»).

What this settles and what not. The relations of the five people are those of the sick person's life force (the notes of WB and the regent's reply 135 say the same), and the gloss lists four: mother, son, enemy, friend. The verse gives three of them to the nurse, physician and messenger, so the fourth, the enemy, falls to the pillow-guard by count; this is an inference, since the gloss does not pair them and lists them in another order. The last line «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ» is not glossed by the manuals; a reading as "the three in harmony (mother, son, friend), oneself and the fifth (the enemy)" fits the count of five but is not stated. The manuals also read «ཞུད» for the decline (as WB's etext), not a different word.
