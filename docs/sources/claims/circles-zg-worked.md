# The circles: the count and the five pebbles, worked by Zhechen Gyaltsab

How the circles are counted and fought, by Zhechen Gyaltsab's *Garland of White Utpalas* (zg:p303-304), LT (lt:p65-67) and the notes (wb.2:p533b):

1. The starting animal is the circle's own (claim circles-count), in the element related to the reckoned person's power: for a wood-monkey king the first circle starts from the iron bird.
2. From it count upward as many years as the one reckoned has lived (དུ་ལོན, du lon); man or woman makes no difference (Zhechen Gyaltsab: «འདི་ལ་ཕོ་མོ་སོགས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་མེད»). LT and the notes say the same: upward, by the years lived.
3. The year reached: its life force, body, power, luck and mewa, each fought by the reckoned person's life force, body, power, luck and body mewa. The worked meeting: wood monkey against iron bird, the life force meets itself (iron and iron), the body is the son, the power the enemy.
4. The colour as in ch. 27 (claim circles-colour-rule).

The example sets the person against the starting year itself and names its body and power, so the count runs through the sixty years with their elements, not through the twelve animals only. Whether "as many years as lived" counts the year of birth as one is not said in the *Garland*; UT counts the years of the one reckoned inclusively, one in the year of birth (ut:p4), and the example's meeting with the starting year itself is where an inclusive count of one would fall: an inference.

The tenth circle, finely: for a servant born tiger, horse or dog, from the friend's tiger; rat, dragon or monkey, the mother's dragon; pig, sheep or hare, the pig of the power's element; bird, ox or snake, the son's bird; "applied to the master's year", as the notes observe (Zhechen Gyaltsab); roughly, from the mother's bird (Zhechen Gyaltsab, LT).
