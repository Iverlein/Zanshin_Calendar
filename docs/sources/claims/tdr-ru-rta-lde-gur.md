# ru rta is a medicinal herb of the decoction class and lde gu a paste, so «ru rta'i lde gur» is a paste of ru rta; the plant's name is not given

RD393 (2026-11-11). In the be'u bum the dream rites use «ru rta» in two ways: the Śramaṇā recitation is said 108 times over water of ru rta and the water is used to wash ([p339]: «ཉལ་ཁང་དུ་ ... རྟའི་ཆུ་ལ་ལན་བརྒྱ་རྩ་བརྒྱད་མངོན་པར་བཟླས་པས་ཁྲུས་བྱས», the etext's «སྟའི» corrected to «རྟའི»), and a second rite is said 108 times over ru rta at the place of lying and the face and both feet are washed ([p340], the etext's «རུ་སྐ» corrected to «རྟ»). The Great Tibetan-Chinese Dictionary (tmz:box1) defines ru rta as a pleasant-tasting medicinal herb of the class of the decoction medicines (thang sman), pungent-hot, sweet and bitter in taste, even after digestion, helpful in disorders of wind and blood, lung disease, brown phlegm and other illnesses, with the synonyms "the pervader", "the one marked by a staff", "the ripe one" and "the blazing one"; its second sense is a horse of the old district troops, not meant here. The dictionaries searched give no botanical name; "rue" is not said by any of them and is not to be written.
Mipham's version (mph.128:i189-192) says «ru rta'i lde gur» is recited over twenty-one times and rubbed on hands and feet. «lde gu» is, in the same dictionary, a soft dough or paste of flour or a thick juice or a fragrant-water infusion, and in medicine a powder stirred into honey-water and the like (a «lde gu'i sbyor ba»); Ives Waldo adds mixture, syrup, ointment. With the terminative ra, «ru rta'i lde gur» is therefore "(made) into a paste of ru rta": the same herb as the be'u bum's water, in the form of a paste for the hands and feet where the be'u bum washes with its water. This answers the word; whether the plant is the costus the Tibetan physicians mean by ru rta is not said by the sources read here and remains for a reader of the medical texts.
