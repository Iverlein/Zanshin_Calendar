# The Bṛhadyātrā ties the dream's humour to the part of the night and to the rains, autumn and spring (RD265)

Varāhamihira's *Bṛhadyātrā* ch. 16 (the *Svapnādhyāya*, bya:p41-46) is the one Indian dream text read in RD265 that ties the dream's *kind* to a part of the night and to seasons. Verse 10 says that in the last, the middle and the first part of the night, and in the seasons called Prāvṛṣ (the rains), Śarad (autumn) and Mādhava (spring), the fruit of a dream from the disorder of wind, bile and phlegm is "common" (*sādhāraṇa*); verses 8–9 give the images of the bilious (gold, jewels, garlands, the sun, fire) and of the phlegmatic (the moon, flowers, rivers, lakes, the sea). The three lists run in one order, and so, if paired by position, the last part of the night goes with the rains and wind, the middle with autumn and bile, the first with spring and phlegm; the verse does not state the pairs in words (the page image, read by RD269, settles the order of the lists and nothing more).

This is the Indian form of what Negelein reads into the Atharva appendix (neg:p17-19's neighbour, his Introduction p. XXII): three groups of the nine ruling stars (Rāhu, Ketu and Saturn; Jupiter, Mercury and the Moon; Venus, Mars and the Sun) govern the three seasons, the three humours and the three kinds of dream. The appendix itself (apv:p445's chapter, 68.1.4–8) gives the humours from the planets; RD263 found no season name in the chapter, so the seasons are Negelein's gloss and, here, the *Bṛhadyātrā*'s own word. The *Garuḍapurāṇa* has the humours' dreams without a season (gpu:p131).

It is a tie of the dream to the season and the part of the night by the dreamer's temperament; it gives no span to the fruit and no lunar day, weekday or month.

Doubt: verse 10 is read on the page image of Pingree's print (image n28, p. 42; the editor's apparatus gives "prāvṛḍ-śara°" in Viṣṇuśarman and "prāvicaraṃ" in M for the season names, "phalasannipātaḥ" in Viṣṇuśarman against "khalu sannipāte" in M); the pairing by position is ours.

RD295 (2026-11-10): the pairing by position is the physician's own order: *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* Sūtrasthāna 1.8 puts wind, bile, phlegm at the end, middle and beginning of the night, and 12.24–25 makes the rains, autumn and spring the seasons of the aggravation of wind, bile and phlegm; leaf n28 read again at full size, v. 10 as above, *saṃjñite*. The pairs are still the reader's, not stated in the verse (skd, section RD295).
