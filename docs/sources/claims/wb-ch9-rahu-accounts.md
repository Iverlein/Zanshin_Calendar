# WB ch. 9 gives the accounts of Rāhu's seizing in sūtra, tantra, the Vedas and medicine, and holds only the Kālacakra's as definitive

In its opening (machine reading of pp. 56–57) the author sets out ten kinds of telling and takes the straight meaning first: from the Close Placement of Mindfulness, the lioness's son Rāhu hides the sun and moon with his hands (the sun's rays hurt his eyes; the moon, because he wished to touch the goddess); from the Sūtra of the Sun and Moon, the sun and moon seized by the asura Rāhu take refuge, the Buddha and Indra speak a verse and Rāhu lets go; from the Nirvāṇa sūtra, Rāhu's hand covers them, and asuras cannot hurt sun and moon, as they cannot draw blood from a Tathāgata; from the Uttaratantra and the Kriyā tantras, the sun and moon are freed from his mouth. Then the Vedic tale: at the churning of the milk ocean Rāhu, in a god's form, stole the nectar, the sun and Viṣṇu told on him, the wheel cut off his head, and his body died while his head lived; the medical Eight Branches adds that the churned nectar gave a fiery man and thence 'poison', and garlic from a drop at his cut neck. The author calls the outsiders' tellings not to be held as true (refuted in the Pramāṇavārttika and elsewhere), and the telling of Rāhu's beheading 'only a way of telling'.
