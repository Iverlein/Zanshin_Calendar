# Ransom closing «བསྐྱུར»/«བསྒྱུར» of each of the 28 mansion verses, by print and etext

# The closing «བསྐྱུར» or «བསྒྱུར» of each ransom of the 28 mansion verses, witness by witness

WB vol. 2, ch. 33, pp. 313–328. Each verse ends its ransom (the figure, «རྗེན», filled and set over the patient, who is circled with it) with a closing word, «བསྐྱུར» (cast away) or «བསྒྱུར» (turned). Counted 2026-11-24 (inv-q) from the BDRC etexts, aligned verse by verse: the 1996 edition (I1KG12907, pp. 321–336), the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029, pp. 319–334, the same typesetting) and the Zhol print's machine etext (I1KG1710, pp. 1067–1084, noisy). The pool's machine passage (Yigdzin-1 and MITRA on the reprint) is the last column. G = «བསྒྱུར», K = «བསྐྱུར», – = no closing word in that text.

Result. The two etexts agree at 15 verses (K at 3: Kṛttikā, Pūrvāṣāḍhā; G at 13 incl. Bharaṇī and Revatī), split at 9 (1, 4, 6, 8, 12, 13, 17, 19, 24), one is blank at 27, and 15, 23, 26 have no closing word. The reprint is the same typesetting as the 1996 edition, so their agreement is one print read twice by two machines and their splits are the machines' noise, not two readings of the text; the Zhol etext is a third, noisier machine over a different block print and has only 9 verses legible. So no verse has more than a machine witness for its letter; nothing was told on a print (the stack is the filled block recorded at q645).

The rule from WB's own other uses: «བཞི་མདོར་བསྒྱུར» (cast at the crossroads, with the sense of turning the illness out) stands in Bharaṇī and Revatī in every text that has them (1996, reprint, Zhol for Revatī) and «བཞི་མདོར་བསྐྱུར» nowhere; so these two verses take «བསྒྱུར». Correction to the whole-etext count of inv-k: «བསྐྱུར་བས་གྲོལ» is not 0 in the 1996 etext, it stands in Aśvinī's ransom (p. 321, and BDRC's phrase search gives that line twice), so that formula does not bind the other verses to G. Where the two etexts and the Zhol machine agree on G (Punarvasu, Viśākhā, Jyeṣṭhā, Uttarāṣāḍhā: all of the legible witnesses) a text may take «བསྒྱུར» with the limit that it is machine agreement; Kṛttikā and Pūrvāṣāḍhā (K, K in both etexts, Zhol absent) take «བསྐྱུར» on the same limit. For the other verses a text names no letter, or follows the majority of the legible witnesses and says so.
