# «དུར» is one of the four slayer animals, ox, sheep, dog, dragon; the count for the light-clearer's months is not said

In WB's reckoning for the dead and in the notes, «དུར» (grave) beside an animal is one member of the four slayer animals, the ox, sheep, dog and dragon (WB p. 99: «སྤྱི་གཤེད་གླང་ལུག་ཁྱི་འབྲུག», "the general slayers: ox, sheep, dog, dragon"; «སྒོས་གཤེད་དུར་གྱི་ལོ», the special slayer on the grave's year). Wangkhyi's four animals on p. 210 (ox, sheep, dragon, dog) are the same four, so his «དུར་ཉི» ("the grave day") is the day of such a grave animal, and the notes use «གཤེད་དུར་ཉི་མ» for exactly a day of the slayer's grave. Which of the four is a person's or a month's grave depends on the power or element: the notes' example for tiger and hare is the south-west sheep (p. 515), and for an earth-pig bride the own grave is the earth dragon (great) and earth dog (small), the slayer's the wood dragon and wood dog (p. 530); a modern dictionary of Steinert's collection (no. 62, «bod_yig_tshig_gter_rgya_mtsho», edition not identified) gives the same rule as the definition of «དུར་མིག»: wood grave the sheep, fire grave the dog, iron grave the ox, earth and water the dragon («shing dur lug, me dur khyi, lcags dur glang, sa chu 'brug»), and for «རང་དུར» the grave year of the power that is the enemy of the own power. For the light-clearer's «དུར་གྱི་སྟེང» in the four first months (tiger, snake, monkey, pig) this gives two candidate counts, neither stated by ch. 31: by the element of the month's animal, wood tiger the sheep, fire snake the dog, iron monkey the ox, water pig the dragon; or by the triads of ch. 23 (vol. 1, p. 373), the dog, ox, dragon and sheep in that order. The four animals are the same either way; their order against the months is not settled.
