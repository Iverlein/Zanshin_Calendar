# Four Penn copies of the Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya give a month for the fourth watch; none has three fortnights or half a month

# Four Penn copies of the Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya give a month for the fourth watch; none has three fortnights or half a month (RD357)

Read on Penn's full IIIF images (Colenda), 2026-11-10; the earlier readings of RD277 were on a partly delivered image.

- **Item 1871** (pn1871:i2b, read at 2×, the whole of the first text leaf): verse 2 "dvitīye cāṣṭabhir māsais tribhir māsais triyāmake" (eight months, three months), verse 3 "caturthe prahare svapno māsaikena vipacyate | aruṇodayavelāyāṃ daśāhena phalaṃ bhavet", verse 4 "govisarjanavelāyāṃ sadyaḥ svapno vidhīyate". The colophon (pn1871:i4, f. 4r) reads "iti bṛhaspatyukta-svapnādhyāyaḥ samāptaḥ", as the catalogue says; it names no scribe and no date.
- **Item 2730** (pn2730:i0): "caturthe prahare dṛṣṭaṃ māsena phalaṃ bhavet", the same eight and three months, the same dawn and cows' release lines; an own first verse ("nānā-svapna-samuccayam").
- **Item 687** (pn687:i1): a different wording and a different second span: "svapnās tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ | ṣaḍbhir māsair dvitīye tu tribhir māsais tṛtīyake" (**six** months for the second watch, as the Matsya and the Agni have), and the fourth watch is missing in the line and written back in the margin above the page as "yāme caturthe māsaikaṃ …" (one month; the last words not read). So a copyist's omission was mended by a corrector with a month.
- **Item 673** (dated saṃvat 1881 = 1824 by its colophon on f. 6v, read on the image, pn673:i6): the first leaf, read by RD277, has a month; its chapter has 69 verses.

So all four of Penn's copies of the Svapnādhyāya that carry the span verse read **a month** for the fourth watch (q871 for the Penn copies), and none reads Negelein's i "tripakṣake" for the third (q868: the Penn Item 1871 reads "three months" in the third watch, "triyāmake", not "three fortnights", and a month, not "half a month"; the question's alternative "eight months, three months, half a month" is the Brahmavaivarta's and is not the Penn reading either).

Which wording is older is not decided: the Brahmavaivarta has half a month in the two prints read (bvp:p662-665; bvg:p3, where the Nepali gloss says fifteen days), the Svapnādhyāya copies a month in every witness, the Atharva-pariśiṣṭa a month at the cows' release (apv:p445) and no fourth span at all. The Penn Svapnādhyāya copies are all of the 18th–19th century (1824 the only dated one); the one scribal event seen, Item 687's marginal repair, restores "a month", which makes "a month" the wording the correctors of this family expected, not a proof of its age. That "ardhamāsena" is a correction of "māsena" (q871) or the reverse: no manuscript older than the 18th century has been read; open.
