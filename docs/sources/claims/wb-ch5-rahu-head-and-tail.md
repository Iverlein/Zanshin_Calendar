# WB ch. 5: Rāhu found by the pure month, head and tail, hour of rising, the sight

WB's chapter 5 finds Rāhu (སྒྲ་གཅན, sgra gcan), called "the slow one of those with merit" (བསོད་ནམས་ལྡན་པའི་བུལ་བ), by the pure month: for the pure month give 209, multiply by 30, mix in the date-count, set five places with 0, 0, 14 (མ་ནུ), 0 and the parts, multiply by 12, and with the further 23, 6, 60, 60 throw away the wheel of 27; the remainder, taken from the wheel, gives the head's mansion and enjoyment (pp. 34). The tail (མཇུག) is half a wheel from it (the half-wheel is printed «ཕྱེད ༥», which is 13.5 only if the half-point is dropped by the machines). A second way gives 429 and 460; the house's place by 135 and 575 and the days passed in it by the remainder (p. 34). On p. 35 the head is also found by taking the sun, 12, from Rāhu (adding 27 if not risen), multiplying by 60, dividing by the moon's 135, and counting to the left from the "nor" place; a further way divides by 405 and sets the head in one of eight directions with its hour of rising: 1, early morning half; 2, day half; 3, dusk half; 4, the dark junction; 5, evening half; 6, midnight; 7, dawn. The head to the left, the tail to the right, oneself in the belly, the direction looked at; twelve places of four stars are seen forward and behind, and the "time fire" sight, before, is to be avoided; the actions named for which this serves are battle, entering, cutting, showing skill, ornaments, life-rites and ransom-throwing (མདོས་གཏོར) (p. 35).
