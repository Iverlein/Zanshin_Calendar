# WB ch. 35 pp. 451–453: who may be taught reckoning, the pledges and the answer of the Fifth Dalai Lama

Pages 451–453 close the section on reckoning with the ethics of the field: the Fifth Dalai Lama's answer, as quoted, says that reckoning of the dead can turn a birth to a good place and that the old and the new mantra traditions each have means to stop what is bad; reckoning begun with refuge and the awakening mind becomes a cause of omniscience; it is to be taught only to a fit vessel (much learning, wisdom, quick mind, firm practice, effort, faith, little sleep, no ale) and kept from the unfit; the fourteen root downfalls and the faults of the wicked, the proud, the greedy and the deceitful are listed; the five fields help one another and the reckoner must know the others; reckoners who foretell without knowing even the way are blamed. The page names the White Beryl's own earlier title (Phug lugs rtsis kyi legs bshad mkhas pa'i mgul rgyan bai dUrya dkar po'i do shal dpyod ldan snying nor) for the pledges. Machine reading.
