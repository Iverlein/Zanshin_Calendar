# The Kanjur's Showing of Dreams sūtra reads a bodhisattva's dreams by stage and gives remedies of days, months and years, never a part of the night (RD381)

The Kanjur sūtra "the Showing of Dreams" (Ratnakūṭa ch. 4, Toh 48, nine hundred ślokas, three fascicles, translated by Prajñāvarma and Ye shes sde and others) reads a bodhisattva's dreams of the Tathāgata as signs of the stage he stands on or of an obstruction of karma or a work of Māra, and gives for each the remedy and its length, never a part of the night: for a dream of a clay Tathāgata a lamp on the head at the stūpa or in the Dharma library; for a dream of the Tathāgata's nirvāṇa honouring the Three Jewels and giving robes; for a beginner seven days of speaking the Dharma or holding a lamp all night with confession three times by day and by night; for a falling dream three months of giving, three times by day and by night. Years are named for the higher stages (four, three, two, one for the stages four to seven; the verb is not understood). Read in the etext only.
