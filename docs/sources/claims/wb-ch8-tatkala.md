# The Sanskrit word tatkāla in ch. 8 and its spelling in the print

The Sanskrit word of ch. 8 for the instantaneous union of times is *tatkāla*, Sanskrit *tat-kāla*, 'that time, the time in question', with the adjective *tātkālika*, 'happening at that time, immediately, instantly appearing'. Monier-Williams: «tat-kāla m. that time...; the time referred to» (Varāhamihira's Bṛhat Saṃhitā, Laghujātaka), «tat-kālam ind. at that time, immediately», and *tātkālika*, «happening at that time, Sūryasiddhānta vii, 12; Golādhyāya vii, 27... instantly appearing» (the instantaneous, true motion of a planet in Indian astronomy). The Gser thang print spells it «ཏཏྐལ» (ta-tka-la, the long vowel not written) in every place of pp. 52, 54 and 55 (RD206); the machine's «ཏནླལ», «ཏརྐྵ», «ཏཏྐཱལ», «ཏ་ཏཱལ» and «ཏནྐཱལ» are misreadings of it. So the term is the Sanskrit *tatkāla*, and the reading «tātkāla» of the question differs from the print's spelling only by the vowel length.
