# No reader ranks the birth reckoning against the year of age for one year

Searched 2026-11-01, answered as far as the sources go. WB says only that the luck's rise and decline go with the luck, «སྔ་མ་མི་འགྱུར་ཕྱི་མ་འགྱུར», the birth luck unchanging, the year's changing, both applied to the present month, day and hour, then "gathered well, tell the verdict as it is" (vol. 1, p. 335); Mipham glosses the pair and nothing more (vol. 2, p. 484: «སྔ་མ་མི་འགྱུར་བ་བཙས་ཀླུང་དང་ཕྱི་མ་འགྱུར་བ་ཀེག་རྩིས་དར་གུད»). The close of the birth reckoning gives the one weighing rule WB has: not one or two results, but the force and number of the results above and below, the good greater and more good, the bad bad, even middling, and by the rites of the moment the bad can turn good and by passing conditions the good go bad (wb.1:p349). No text in WB, the notes, the G.ya' sel, UT (whose overall examination assigns the birth years by age: to twenty-five the mother's pebbles chiefly, to forty-nine one's own rise and decline, after that the father's, ut:p153-155) or the Bhutan collection says which stands when the two disagree for a year. So: the birth gives the ground, the year of age the change over it, and where they differ the verdict is the weighing of p. 349 over both, not a rule of precedence.
