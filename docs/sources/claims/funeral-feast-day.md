# The funeral feast's council, omen beer, day and seats

The day and the ground of the funeral feast (WB vol. 2, pp. 126–127; Mipham's notes, pp. 520–521).

- **The council beer** is brewed on Aśvinī (དབྱུ་གུ, dbyu gu), Punarvasu (ནབས་སོ, nabs so) or Puṣya (རྒྱལ, rgyal); the council is held on a good planet and mansion, on the one-white: the notes lay the sky's mewa with its centre and take the direction where the one-white then stands (fire-tiger, eight-white in the centre: the west).
- **The omen beer** (ལྟས་ཆང, ltas chang) is set on Bharaṇī (བྲ་ཉེ, bra nye) or Maghā (མཆུ, mchu), on the two-black (the notes: the north-east that year); six omens: rotten, dried, worms, risen, ferment set, and the like. If it is good the feast's work begins.
- **The feast's day**: month, date, planet and mansion good, no harmful earth lord moving that day (the notes: twelve identifying for the month, the dead's clan element's own, mother, son, friend and enemy months and the grave month, the main living person's own obstacle, seventh, the two slayers up and down, the grave month and the black month all to be avoided, as the fourth examination says; for the day the planets, mansions, earth lords, fangs and faces and the rest as before, the weekday's planet's aspects counted).
- **The direction**: by one's own and the patron's year (the notes: not the direction of the reckoner's, the patron's or the head of the row's year, of the twelve and of the sixty, and not the patron's clan element's enemy direction, e.g. west for wood); the year's, month's, day's and hour's earth lords not offended; no assembly on the two-black or nine-red.
- **The workers**: the patron's clan element's mother year kneads the dough (for wood, a mouse or pig year), the son year cuts the butter, the friend year hands out salt and barley.
- **The seats**: the head lama's throne faces a white mewa, not its back to the east; not toward Tese, the hal dog, the seat-spreader, the earth kings of year, month, day and hour, the five-yellow, the ngams shing, the girl and the empty vase; no row on the five-yellow.
