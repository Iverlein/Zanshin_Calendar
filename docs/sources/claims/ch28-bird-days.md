# The sky-bird's days after the winter solstice

WB vol. 2, pp. 29–30: the sky-bird's days (བྱ་ཞག, bya zhag), counted from the winter solstice, from its 24th day: the tail [?] 6 days (clear sky: early frost), the wing's corner 6 (wind and snow: heavy late-summer rain), the shoulders 3 (no snow: drought), the neck 7 (snow: no grain [?]), the face 3 (snow: no drought), the wing's tip 5 (clear, windy, cold: no frost or hail, even rain): thirty days. Snow that evening: a good year for Tibet. Then 10 days to the chick's coming (warmth needed until the 32nd [day?]; cold, a spring freeze); then *khrums* 12 days (cold: cold until weeding); *shol po* half a month (snow and cold: the six measures before were wrong); *smyung mo* 9 days (snow or rain: early-summer drought); *she mo* 7 days (snow or rain: midsummer drought).

Also on the first day of the eleventh month: beings chased from a direction, that direction's rites prevail; none chased, no strife.

The days are calculable from the winter solstice; the weather on them is observed. The days before and after the winter solstice are matched with those of the summer solstice, examined from a month before the winter solstice to 27 days after.
