# རྟ་བླ in ch. 81's commentary: a many-legged worm, the centipede

The creature «རྟ་བླ» (rta bla) in ch. 81's commentary and in Sde srid's verse (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, a medical dictionary on BDRC, no scan opened): the centipede, a many-legged worm.

**The dictionaries.** རྟ་བླ (rta bla) as a headword: a worm with many legs and a long, thin body (Tshig mdzod chen mo, no. 25, marked as a regional word; Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho, the same; Ives Waldo); the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs: a long worm with many legs and with striped lines on its back, usable as a medicine; the 84000 glossary renders it "centipede" (not used for the sense). The Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo (BDRC I00EGS1017132, an etext read only as OCR) has the entry: a worm of reddish-black colour with ridges on its back, also called "the hundred-footed, the hundred-handed".

**In the lists.** Kongtrul's treasury lists the small and water creatures with «རྟ་བླ» between the scorpion and spider (སྡིག་པ་དང་སྡོམ, sdig pa dang sdom) and the "gron bu", and another list has «རྟ་བླ་བྱ་ཕོ་ནག་པོ» (the rta bla, the black male bird) beside the tadpole, the scorpion and the spider. In ch. 81 Khro ru's gloss and Sde srid's verse have it with the creatures of the dream list (`ktr:p329-332`); the Four Tantras' «འབུ་སྦྲང» (insects, bee-grubs) for which the Supplement puts «རྟ་བླ» is a creature of the same sort, so the variant of q845 is a different word for a creeping creature, not a correction of sense.

**A consequence for the Svapnohana.** The Svapnohana's «རྟ་བླ་མོ» (rta bla mo; q800, q814, q825) may be this word with the feminine ending, "a centipede", before «ལྟོ་འཕྱེ» (belly-walker, the snake): the creatures of a list, not "a horse and a bla mo". It is only a lead; the passage there is not read here.
