# The five people the sick need: the pillow-guard's relation and what «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ» counts

WB vol. 1, p. 445 lists five people the sick need: the nurse (ནད་གཡོག, nad g.yog) by the mother, the physician (ལྷ་རྗེ, lha rje) by the son, the messenger by the friend, the pillow-guard (སྔས་བསྲུང་པ, sngas bsrung pa) and then «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམ་ན», whose gathering means no death from decline or conditions. The pool's sources name no relation for the pillow-guard, and give the last line no gloss; what they do say:

- Mipham's notes (wb.2:p497-499, machine): the five, nurse and the rest, are the mother, son and so on of the *life force* of the one reckoned; each must be free of the seven-corner (བདུན་ཟུར, bdun zur), the tomb-slayer and the like. "And so on" is all that is said of the other two.
- The regent's reply 135 (ys:i492-493, machine): the five are reckoned by the life force's element as the chief, "the mother and friend and the rest in that order"; the nurse is the attendant of the protecting mother and the physician the son, "and so on". The reply's source is Duhar Nagpo's plank-tree instruction in the first translation of the *Speckled Elements*, of which it quotes the line «དགོས་པའི་མི་ལྔའི་ལོ་འདི་ནི» ("the year of the five people needed"); it adds nothing on the pillow-guard.
- «མཐུན་གསུམ», the three in harmony, is a term elsewhere: the three year animals of one triad (tiger, horse, dog; pig, sheep, hare; and so on), called "the year's three in harmony" in the manual of the *Dpal bsdus* (etext, p. 43) and used in the same sense in WB's notes (wb.2, etext p. 538, "the three in harmony each in their own", for the bride's year). So the verse's «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམ་ན» may count the triad of the sick person's own animal, oneself, and the five; or the three named relations in harmony, oneself and the five. The texts do not choose.

So the answer to the question: the pillow-guard is chosen, like the others, by a relation of the life force's element, but which relation no text found says; and «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ» is glossed nowhere. The earlier claim [[illness-death-verdict]] lists the pillow-guard without a relation, as the verse does.
