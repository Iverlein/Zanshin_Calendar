# Householders take the one-day vow on the full moon, the new moon and the eighth (Lo chen Dharma shrI)

Lo chen Dharma shrI's commentary on Mnga' ris paN chen's *Ascertainment of the Three Vows* (a Nyingma work of Smin grol gling) says in its definition of the one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas) that householders (ཁྱིམ་པ, khyim pa) take and keep it at the times of the full moon, the new moon and the eighth day, «ཉ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད» (dpn:i55, read on the scan), and speaks of a person who has pledged to keep it on those days of every month, «ཟླ་བ་བྱུང་ངོ་ཅོག་གི་ཉ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད་ལ» (dpn:i58, read on the scan).

- Days: the full moon, the new moon, the eighth. The text does not say whether "the eighth" is the eighth of the waxing half alone or of each half (the 8th and the 23rd), and it names no 14th.
- Practice: the one-day vow of eight branches, for one day and night.
- For whom: householders; a lay devotee (དགེ་བསྙེན, dge bsnyen) who takes it restores his own trainings by it.
- Benefit named here: none beyond the name, "restoring and cleansing" (གསོ་སྦྱོང, gso sbyong): it restores the trainings and cleanses what is to be given up.
- Tradition: stated by a Nyingma author; the rule itself rests on the common Abhidharma, where the commentary on the *Treasury* names the pledge to keep the vow "always on the eighth" (akb:i364-365, etext).
