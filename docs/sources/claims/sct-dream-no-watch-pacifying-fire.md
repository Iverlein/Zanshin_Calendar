# The Common Tantra: the dawn dream, many good signs, no watch; the pacifying fire offering for the bad (RD373)

The Common Tantra has the dawn dream and the lying down on kusha with the head to the east, as the Sngags rim chen mo says, and for a bad dream it prescribes the pacifying fire offering (ཞི་བའི་སྦྱིན་སྲེག, zhi ba'i sbyin sreg): with the pacifying secret mantra, cow's butter, one hundred and eight times, by the rite (sct:p295-297). It names no watch of the night, no day of the month and no season: the pupil is asked at dawn what was seen, whenever in the night, and the answer is sorted by what the dream shows, in a long list of good signs (a Buddha, a stupa, the Sangha, kings, gold, victory, climbing a mountain, wonders, and so on); non-virtuous signs are to be avoided, and the virtuous are said to be accomplished. The signs of the virtuous are rated best, middle and lowest, as the three accomplishments of secret mantra are.

The Sngags rim chen mo quotes two lines of it, «ཀུ་ཤ་བཏིང་བའི་ས་གཞི་ལ། ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སྔས་བལྟས་ཉལ་དུ་གཞུག», which in the Derge etext's second line reads «སྔས་ལྟས»; the sense is the same (the pillow side to the east). Which spelling the scan has was not read (red print too faint at the size served). The remedy that Tsong kha pa takes from it, the pacifying fire offering, is the Common Tantra's own; Tsong kha pa's praise of it is his.
