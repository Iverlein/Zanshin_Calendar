# WB ch. 14 ends: the day's length in Tibet grows and shrinks by two srang a day, to about thirty-three chu tshod and thirty srang

The close of ch. 14 (machine reading of p. 179) says that in Tibet the day grows and shrinks by two srang-breaths (སྲང་དབུགས་གཉིས) a day, told at length in the Khams chapter, and that in direct observation the longest day does not come to more than thirty-three chu tshod and thirty srang, the longest end given as thirty-six. The colophon names the chapter-list's three forms (extended, middle, abridged), the 'unpassable threshold' (ཐེམ་མི་འདའ) beside the outer and inner, and the lines of Chos-rje Bde-legs-nyi-ma, Bsod-nams-rgya-mtsho and the Dumpo hand-tradition. The figure thirty-six and the reading of 'snowy mountain' are unsettled.
