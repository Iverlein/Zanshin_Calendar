# The night of the first dream: from 節分 to 2 January

The date of the first dream (初夢) moved. The earliest written case is Saigyō's spring poem in the 山家集 (Kamakura period), headed "composed on the morning of the beginning of spring", in which the first dream is the one dreamt on the night of 節分 into 立春 (this reading by Japanese Wikipedia and a reading blog; the poem's critical text was not seen). Then, by the 日本大百科全書, the calendar's arrangement moved it to New Year's Eve and New Year's night, and it settled, it seems, on the night of 2 January, "the beginning of work". Japanese Wikipedia adds that in the Edo period three nights were held at once (31 December to 1 January, 1 to 2 January, 2 to 3 January), the second tied to the custom of staying awake on New Year's Eve,. The 日本大百科全書 says 正月2日 without saying by which calendar the day is counted; whether the night is the second of the Gregorian January or of the lunar first month in the Edo sources is not settled here.
