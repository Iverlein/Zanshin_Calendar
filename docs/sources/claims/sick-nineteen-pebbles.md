# The nineteen pebbles serve the sick and the newborn, over year, month, day and hour

The *Moonbeams* (dz:i051-052) lays nineteen pebbles for one tormented by illness and demons or for a child just born: the reckoned's life force fought against the life force of the sky year, the month, the day and the hour, and its rise and decline laid on each of the four (eight); the body the same way (eight); the lunar date by the eight goddesses of the half-month, the weekday's planet and the mansion, good or bad (three). LT gives the same nineteen twice: once as the infant's obstacle reckoning of WB's fifth section, counted day by day for a boy or a girl until the child is a month old (lt:p20), and once under WB's own heading, "the reckoning of the day and the *za ma*, told for the sick and infants" (lt:p43-44).

So the two manuals state that the nineteen serve both the sick, whoever they are, and the newborn; neither limits them to infants. WB's p. 445 (wb.1:p445) heads them the same way.
