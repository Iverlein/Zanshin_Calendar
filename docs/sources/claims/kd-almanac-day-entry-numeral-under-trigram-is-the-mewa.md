# The numeral under the trigram letter in a day's cell is the day's mewa

A Lhasa almanac's day-entry, as Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan (ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་རྒྱལ་མཚན, tshul khrims rgyal mtshan) of Nalendra explains it for the 1st of the 3rd month of a wood pig year, has these items in order: the date's number; the weekday (ཉི); the mansion that accompanies the weekday (ཐ་སྐར, the moon's mansion on that date, with its time of change); the planet's and the mansion's element concord; the great concord (དུས་དབྱིག); the combination (སེལ་བ, of the 27); the dependent-origination link (ཚོར་བ, of the twelve); the effect (བྱེད་པ, of the eleven) where fierce, with its times; the date's trigram (ལི, one of the eight spar kha); and, beneath the trigram letter, a numeral that is **the date's mewa (སྨེ་བ), the nine mewa turning one to each day**; here 4 green (ལྗང་). Then the date's earth lords, the strikes and turnings and the sky door.

So the small numeral under the trigram letter in a day's cell is the day's mewa, not a count of the month's or the year's. This answers q730 (the 7 under li on the 1st of the model almanac's 11th month is that day's mewa, and has no need to agree with the mewa a season's first month begins on) and q1159 (the numerals follow the nine-fold daily turning, so a cell is checked by the rule once the first day's mewa and the turn's direction are known; the direction and the month's first mewa are WB's chapter on the mewa and were not rechecked here). The author explains a printed Lhasa almanac of 1995; the almanac's entries are, in his words, written as the *Vaiḍūrya dkar po* sets them (kd.1:p495), so the identity of the numeral is taken from the explanation of a printed descendant of WB's model almanac, not from WB's own text.
