# The nineteen pebbles of the sick

The nineteen pebbles (WB pp. 445–447; the notes, p. 498), told for the sick and for infants: the one reckoned's life force fought against the life force of the sky year (གནམ་ལོ, gnam lo), the month (the minister), the day (the soldier) and the hour (the weapon), and the life force's rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud) carried onto each: eight; the same for the body: eight; and one pebble each for the date, the weekday and the mansion of falling ill: nineteen. Good and smooth is white, rough and bad black, and the verdict is laid by the count of white and black. Each of the sixteen has its own reading white and its rite black (a table in wb.1:p445-447); the weekday and mansion are taken as they come, not fought, and the date is read by the eight goddesses (the notes).
