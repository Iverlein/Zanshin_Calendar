# Mipham: the three nya of a month are rightly the 8th, 14th and 15th; his notes on the White Beryl gloss them as full moon, new moon and eighth

Mipham (rank 2) rules on the term in a note of his own in his collection of mantras (mgz.33:p180, read on the scan): the three nya (ཉ་གསུམ, nya gsum) of a month are rightly taken as the eighth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth. He records the other count, the full moon, the new moon and the eighth, as that of the Recitations of Pad gling, says that various identifications are seen, and sets them aside.

In his notes on the White Beryl he glosses the same term as the full moon, the new moon and the eighth (wb.2:p504-512, p. 509, read on the scan), there explaining the White Beryl's usage in the reckoning of the dead, where a death on one of the three counts as a factor. The two statements are both his; the ruling "it is right" is the one in the mantra collection.

The same three days recur in what he tells practitioners: offerings in particular on the 8th, 14th and 15th for one who practises a mantra of action or conduct tantra (mgz.23:p7-8), and the Four Great Kings' servants, sons and the Kings themselves going about the world on the 8th, 14th and 15th of the half-month (mgz.11:p146).

- Days: the 8th, 14th and 15th (Mipham's ruling); the 15th, 30th and 8th (the count he sets aside, and the White Beryl's as he glosses it).
- Practice in the passage itself: a mantra for clear dreams recited on those three days; the ruling is on the term, not on a vow.
- For whom: whoever uses the term; no lay rule is stated.
