# «གཞོབ་སེལ», «གུང་ཕུར», «ཤ་རུ», «ཤ་རི་བྷྱོ», «མ་ཏྲམ»: what the dictionaries and BDRC give

Dictionaries and BDRC full-text search, 2026-11-01. «གཞོབ» (gzhob) is the singe, the smell of burning hair, skin or bone (Tshig mdzod no. 55 quoting the commentary on the Four Tantras; Dag tshig no. 37), so «གཞོབ་སེལ» is the removing of that taint. As a rite it stands in the same Bon list as WB's: «བོན་ནི་བོང་ར་གཞོབ་སེལ་བཙན། མདེ་ཐུང་གནམ་ཐེལ་བཅོས་པར་བྱ» (MW26041, MW30023, MW28845) and «བོན་དུ་མདེ་ཐུང་གཞོབ་སེལ་གནམ་ཐེ་བཅོས» (MW3CN2232): the short arrow and the gzhob sel as the Bon rites of a standard trigram-remedy set, the very pair of WB p. 105; Mipham's note (p. 517) calls the left sha ru a Bon rite. «ཤ་རུ» (sha ru) is the stag's antler (nos. 8, 25, 62); BDRC gives 1417 hits for it, too many to sift. «གུང་ཕུར» (gung phur): the White Beryl's own outline names «གུང་ཕུར་སྡེ་དང་སྐར་མཆུ་གནོན» and the Gser thang etext has «ས་བདག་གི་འཁྲུགས་གཏོ་གུང་ཕུར་བཏང་བ» (sending the earth lords' disturbance rite, the gung phur): a named class of rite against the earth lords' disturbance; the dictionaries do not list it. «ཤ་རི་བྷྱོ» (sha ri bhyo) is the tail of a wrathful mantra («ཨོཾ་མ་ཧཱ་ཀཱ་ལ་ཤ་རི་བྷྱོ» in MW1KG14, MW23681), not a rite. «མ་ཏྲམ» (ma tram) is a term of Nyingma wrathful rites («མ་ཏྲམ་རུ་ཏྲ», «མ་ཏྲམ་བསྒྲལ་མནན»: liberating and pressing down) that the dictionaries do not give. The ur mnan's seed syllables on the spokes were not searched. Bon by WB's and Mipham's own words: gzhob sel and the left sha ru; the rest not decided.
