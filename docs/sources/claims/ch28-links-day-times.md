# The twelve links on the times of the day, with their readings

WB vol. 2, pp. 37–38: the twelve links in a daytime setting, each with its time and its reading for works and for the sick: ignorance at sunrise on the high peak (good for all works; the sick harmed from the mother's kin, no death); formation when the sun lights the valleys (trade and journeys good; rites needed); consciousness at the first warmth (consecrations and beginnings good); name and form at the great warmth (works fail; earth lords harm the sick); the six sense fields at half-warmth (good: sons and wealth found); contact in the great morning (enemies rise; put off great works); feeling at noon (very good); craving as noon passes (nothing bears fruit); grasping when the shadows are small and lengthening (good); becoming when the great shadow reaches the plain (obstacles; long illness); birth when the sun is a spear's length high (good, wealth); aging and death at the red line, as dusk comes (bad; grave spirits threaten the sick).

Unlike p. 356 (ignorance at midnight), this setting starts at sunrise; birth's "spear's length" stands out of the run of the times.
