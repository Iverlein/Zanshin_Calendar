# The Mara days' images are the day's persons: the mansions come from the pairs the Moonbeams' supplement names

The Mara days (བདུད་ཀྱི་ཉི་མ, bdud kyi nyi ma) of WB vol. 2, p. 336 name each day by an image and a person; the mansions are not told there. The Moonbeams' supplement in KD (kd.3:p732) gives the whole list as planet with mansion, and the tables of pp. 341–342 agree with it cell by cell:

- Sun with the Pleiades (སྨིན་དྲུག, smin drug, 2): the day for asking the gods (the letter Tri on a stone under a stūpa);
- Moon with Uttaraphalgunī (དབོ, dbo, 11): the nun's day (the Moon rides the iron speckled bird);
- Mars with Āśleṣā (སྐག, skag, 8): the general's day (Mars holds an axe; the goat-heads are what is pressed down in the rite);
- Mercury with Pūrvāṣāḍhā (ཆུ་སྟོད, chu stod, 19): the barley-measurer's day (Mercury holds an empty measure);
- Jupiter with the Head (མགོ, mgo, Mṛgaśiras, 4): the monk's and the Bon-man's day (Jupiter sits on the cushion of the head);
- Venus with Puṣya (རྒྱལ, rgyal, 7): the day of the great and mighty one;
- Saturn with Śatabhiṣaj (མོན་གྲུ, mon gru, 23): the horse-dealer's day (Saturn is caught in a golden chain).

So the image ties to the day's person and attribute, and the mansion comes from the pair. Two images carry the mansion's own name: Jupiter's «མགོ» is Mṛgaśiras's name, and Saturn's chain is WB's own other name for Śatabhiṣaj, «སྒྲོག» (the Joining days of the same page set Jupiter on «སྒྲོག», 23). The other images do not come from the mansions' shapes: WB's mansion verses give Uttaraphalgunī two stars in the shape of a throne and Āśleṣā six stars in the shape of a hooded cobra (གདེངས་ཀ, gdengs ka), nothing of a bird or goat-heads. No commentary gives a further reading of the images.
