# Rtsa gsum gling pa calls his night and doze visions a hazy experience-appearance, overlapping the dream (RD389)

Rtsa gsum gling pa's own verse life does not name a state between sleep and waking by a phrase. Four times (images 98, 103, 406, 473 of vol. 30) he calls what he saw at night or in a doze an experience-appearance (ཉམས་སྣང, nyams snang) and adds "hazy" (ཟ་ཟི, za zi; once "hazy in between", ཟ་ཟི་བར་མ, za zi bar ma). Twice he pairs it with the dream: "a dream or an experience-appearance" (image 98), "an experience-appearance dream" (image 103), so that for him the two words overlap and the haze marks the quality of the sight, not a separate state. The one sight that follows a brief daytime doze (image 103) ends when he "caught memory" (དྲན་པ་ཟིན་པ, dran pa zin pa): he rises and prays, and the guru who appeared answers him. Of these four none is read as an omen by a rule; the episodes are visions of the guru, ḍākinīs and treasure-guardians that lead on to prophecy and the finding of treasure in the story, which is a reading of the vision as guidance, not of its time in the night; image 406 places one on the night of the fourteenth of the lunar month, and none ties the state to an hour.
