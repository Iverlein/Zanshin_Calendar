# RST: the night in three watches, and only the dawn third is examined

The ACIP method (RST, the dream part, etext only) divides the night into three thirds (ཐུན, thun, "watch") and gives each a nature: the first third, the dusk (སྲོད, srod), is earlier habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags): the last three days' deeds, places, companions, food and talk, or deeds of former lives, and nothing certain can be taken from it. The middle third is the tricks of demons and spirits (འདྲེ, 'dre; སྲིན་པོ, srin po; ལྷ་མིན, lha min; བདུད, bdud), good or bad, with nothing certain either. The dawn third (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) makes clear what is to come: only there do good and bad bear fruit, so only there is examination worth making. The text gives no span after which the fruit falls, only the rule of which watch counts.

Dreams are not examined at all when the dreamer is ill, grieving or in a disturbed mind. The verses name the watches by the night's thirds, not by hours.
