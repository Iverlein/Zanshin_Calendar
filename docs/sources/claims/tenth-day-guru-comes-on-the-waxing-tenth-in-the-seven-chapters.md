# The Seven Chapters Prayer: at the end of the age the Guru comes in person on the waxing tenth

The seventh prayer of the Seven Chapters (the one to the king of Gung thang, refrain «bsam pa lhun gyis 'grub par byin gyis rlobs») says that at the end of the degenerate age the Guru comes to Tibet's affairs every morning and evening and «ཡར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུའི་དུས་སུ་དངོས་སུ་བྱོན», at the time of the waxing tenth comes in person (lbm:p29). Only the waxing half is named, with the word «in person» (དངོས་སུ), the other days being the coming of emanations. This is the text recited daily in the liturgies of the Sa skya, Bo dong, 'Bri gung and Mdo chen collections (BDRC phrase search, seven etexts, 2026-11-21), so it is the best known line for the day.

Si tu Padma don yod (1774-1853) states the promise in his own words, as the Guru's pledge made when he went to the Rākṣasa land and now stays as a knowledge-holder until saṃsāra is empty: he «made the promise to come in person at the time of the waxing tenth» (sdd:p20), and names the Padma bka' thang, the Gser gling and the Bka' bsdus as the texts that prophesy the later treasure-revealers (not as the source of the pledge).

Provenance: the Byang gter version of the Rin chen gter mdzod, revealed by Sprul sku Bzang po grags pa and given to Rig 'dzin Rgod kyi ldem 'phru can (colophon, lbm:p33-34), a Nyingma treasure. Whether the waxing half only is meant against the Padma bka' thang's unqualified «every tenth» is not settled by the lines; Zhe chen rgyal tshab keeps both halves (tenth-day-waxing-half-is-the-gurus-both-halves-kept). The Seven Chapters name no twenty-fifth and no day of the dākinīs in any of the seven prayers (the only «tshes» in the text is this one). State: etext of the Rin chen gter mdzod.
