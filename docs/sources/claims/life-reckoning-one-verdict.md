# Reply 111's life reckoning is ch. 22, its one pebble the verdict on the whole birth

The regent's reply 111 (ys:i467-468) bars joining "two bad ones told in the life reckoning (ཚེ་རྩིས, tshe rtsis), the *ma klungs* and the rest", and reads it as not joining two "utterly bad" lives, each life reckoning gathered into one verdict pebble (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar) by its white and black pebbles.

**Which reckoning.** The questioner calls WB's ch. 22 "the life reckoning": question 102 opens «ཚེ་རྩིས་ཀྱི་ཐོག་མར། སྐྱེས་ཆེན་འགྲོ་བའི་དོན་ཕེབས་དང་།», the first words of ch. 22 (ys:i090, on the scan; WB vol. 1, p. 259). And the bar he asks about is WB's line in the match's second point (wb.1:p355, on the scan): «གཞན་ཡང་མ་ཀླུངས་ནས་བཤད་པའི། །ངན་པའི་འབྲས་བུ་གཅིག་པ་དང་། །གསུམ་འདོམ་གཟའ་སྐྱེས་ནང་མི་བསྡེབས།», those sharing one bad result told in the mother's luck (མ་ཀླུངས, ma klungs) are not joined. Mipham's notes gloss the same line «མ་ཀླུང་ནས་བཤད་པའི་ངན་འབྲས་གཅིག་པ་ནི་ལས་རྩིས་བྱས་པས་གཉིས་ཀ་ངན་པ», the same bad result is both being bad when the karma reckoning (ལས་རྩིས, las rtsis) is made (wb.2:p524, on the scan). So the regent's "life reckoning" and Mipham's "karma reckoning" are one: each spouse's birth reckoning of ch. 22, and both readers turn WB's line into a test of each spouse's whole reckoning.

**Which verdict.** The regent gathers "the places of the life reckoning" into one pebble. WB's own rule for gathering ch. 22 as a whole is its close (wb.1:p349, on the scan): one or two results do not decide; the force and number of the results are weighed above and below, the good greater and more good, the bad greater and more bad, even middling, and then the verdict is stated. Of the two verdicts over the hundred (pp. 329–331), the general verdict by where the pebbles lie gives shapes and parts of life, not one colour, so it does not by itself make the single pebble. As a reading, then, the pebble of reply 111 is p. 349's verdict on the whole birth reckoning, black when the bad results are greater and more, and a couple is barred when both spouses' pebbles are black. That the regent means p. 349, and whether a middling verdict bars, neither text says.
