# The reason for using the last watch (evening mixed with the day, midnight too deep, dawn clear) is in Kun dga' rin chen and later manuals, not in the root tantra (RD372)

The reason that the first three watches are not certain and the fourth is used (Tsong kha pa, snr:p370) is not in the root tantra, and it is not Tsong kha pa's own invention either: a Sakya explanation of the same verse, found by exact-phrase search in BDRC, gives it in so many words. In the Rgyud sde kun btus (Rdzong gsar print, etext VEIE0OPIBF2693C2_I2PD14800, pp. 460-461, in an initiation manual that begins at p. 358; the text quotes first the Common Tantra, then the root tantra's six lines) it reads: «དེ་ཡང་ཐུན་དང་པོ་སྲོད་ཀྱི་ཆ་ཉིན་བར་གྱི་སྣང་བའི་བག་ཆགས་དང་འདྲེས་ཅུང་ཟད་མི་གསལ། ཐུན་གཉིས་པ་དང་གསུམ་པ་ནམ་གུང་གི་ཆ་གཉིད་འཐུག་པོ་ཞེས་རྨི་ལམ་མི་འབྱུང་། ཐུན་ཐ་མ་ཐོ་རང་གི་ཆར་རྨི་ལམ་ཤིན་དུ་གསལ་བས་དེ་ལ་བརྟག་དགོས་ཏེ།» ("As for it, the first watch, the evening part, is mingled with the habits of the day's appearances and a little unclear; the second and third watches, the midnight part, are 'deep sleep', so no dream comes; the last watch, the dawn part, the dream is very clear, so that one is to examine"). BDRC's snippet of Kun dga' rin chen's (Sakya Trizin, 1517-1584) collected works (MW1KG4326, vol. 2; the etext answers 403 to the API, so only the snippet, in Wylie, was read) has the same words: «srod kyi cha nyin bar gyi snang ba'i bag chags dang 'dres pas cung mi gsal zhing/ nam gung gnyid mthug po zhes rmi lam mi 'byung/ de nas tho ...», so the explanation is at least his, and the Rgyud sde kun btus copies it or its source (a doubt; the compiler of that collection is Jamyang Loter Wangpo, 1847-1914). Shes rab 'od zer (1518-1584, Trengpo Terton) says that the verse is "cut into four equal parts of the night, and the last, the dawn part, is to be examined" (snippet, Wylie «mtshan mo'i cha mthun bzhir bcad pa'i tha ma tho rengs kyi cha la brtag par bya zhing»); Chos kyi grags pa (Drikung Chungtsang, 1595-1659): in etext VE22082_008, p. 32, he quotes the verse and says only «ཞེས་པས་བརྟག» ("he examines by this"), and BDRC's snippet of his volume VE22082_011 reads «zhes gsungs pas/ rmi lam ni tho rangs kyi thun tha mar gang byung ...» ("as said, the dream that comes in the last watch at dawn ..."; snippet only, the page not opened). So the reasons are: evening mixed with the day, midnight too deep a sleep, dawn clear; this is the Tibetan commentators' reason, from the 16th century on, not an Indian word, and the hits opened are all from the 16th century on; the exact-phrase searches found the verse in 28 etext places, and only the first four to eight of each result list can be read through the tool, so an earlier place may be among the rest. No commentary of the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa itself (Bu ston, Mkhas grub rje, Rong zom, Sa skya) was found for the verse: the phrases searched were the verse's lines and Tsong kha pa's wording «ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད» (six hits, all the Sngags rim chen mo or books that quote it: Tsong kha pa's collected works, the Sngags rim chen mo etext itself, a Sngags rim blo sbyong volume, Padma rdo rje's book, and two more not opened).
