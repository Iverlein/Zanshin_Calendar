# The householder's one-day vow is asked of a master; only the tantric restoring may be taken before a support (Karma chags med)

Karma chags med's Ri chos sets the householder's one-day vow against the conduct tantra's restoring of eight branches (rcz:p150-151, etext only, a poor OCR): the two are kept alike and differ in meaning.

- The one-day vow (བསྙེན་གནས, bsnyen gnas) of eight branches taught in the Vinaya is the householders' vow. It must be asked of a master and may not be taken by oneself before a support (རྟེན, rten). The ordained may not keep it: it is said to harm their own vow.
- The restoring of eight branches (གསོ་སྦྱོང་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པ, gso sbyong yan lag brgyad pa) of the action and conduct tantras is a Mahāyāna vow of one day and night, best taken from a master and otherwise each day before a support; a lay devotee who keeps it is cleansed of his small faults, and monks and novices who do the silent fasting practice (སྨྱུང་གནས, smyung gnas) have the faults of their vows restored.

This differs from Lo chen Dharma shrI's account (dpn:i58, scan; claim one-day-vow-from-whom-and-until-when), where one who has pledged the days of every month beforehand may, for the Sautrāntikas, take the vow himself before a support. Karma chags med allows taking before a support only for the tantric restoring. Both are rank 3; neither is weighed here. The verse names no lunar day.
