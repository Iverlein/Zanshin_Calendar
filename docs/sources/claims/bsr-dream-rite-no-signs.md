# Bu ston's examination of dreams is a rite for a dream of one's birth and death and gives no list of dream signs

The two folios attributed to Bu ston and headed "the examination of dreams" (BDRC) are not a list of dream signs. They are a rite for asking to be shown, in a dream, one's own birth and death (the next birth): a quiet place, a Buddha image on a mandala, the Śākyamuni mantra recited from the first of the waxing moon, sleep in the lion's posture. The only signs they name are of the six kinds of beings (sounds, signs, marks, and lights: white gods, blue animals, red hungry ghosts, black hells, with green and yellow for humans and demi-gods in an order the machine reading does not settle). No good or bad dream image, no hour of the night and no named source for signs is given, so no item of White Beryl ch. 30's list (G1-G16, B1-B16) has a parallel here and the concordance gains no row. The lineage written after the rite (a paṇḍita Vikhyātadeva as spelt, Śākyaśrī, Dhanaśrī, Jam gsar, a lo tsā ba, the adept Ze dmar ba, Bu ston) belongs to the rite. Read by machine on a cursive scan; the points that rest on single syllables are the colours' order, the title's «རྟག»/«བརྟག» and the lineage's names.
