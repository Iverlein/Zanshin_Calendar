# The eighteen funeral shares and who may not be buried

The eighteen funeral shares of the fourth examination, "the pure way of Khyung" (ཁྱུང, khyung), and those who may not be buried (wb.2:p68-70; the notes call them clear in the root text, wb.2:p503-504).

| Kind | For whom | What is done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Great 1, the faithful's | a spiritual teacher | burnt on a throne, a reliquary for the bones; rites in the Dharma's way, a hundred lamps and teachings, scripture read, a feast, thirteen god-cakes, gifts to the ordained |
| Great 2, the canopy | a lord, earth lord, king, one of high line | his goods given away, a canopy of five silks, a tiger skin and jewels; 7 or 9 food shares |
| Great 3, the wise one's | a minister of the council | food to all the paternal kin, a temple restored, images made; four spears with silk; 7 food shares |
| Great 4, the rich man's | one with food and wealth | his share of goods and 11 food shares |
| Great 5, the hero's tiger funeral | a leader who subdued enemies | armour, tiger cloth, leopard sheath, sword and spear; 5 food shares |
| Great 6, the father's | a father of seven or more sons | each son gives a good; food shares by the number of sons |
| Small 1, the herdsman's | a kin manager who dies without son or goods | the paternal kin together, with the fire-washing |
| Small 2, the eating woman's | a woman without son or boy | her daughter buries her and offers the food |
| Small 3, the separated one's | one of 20–30 without household or siblings | the la sent elsewhere; the corpse taken to another valley with gold, turquoise and a ladle of food |
| Small 4, the bondwoman's | a woman with a daughter and no husband | the corpse to a cut bank out of the village's sight; nine cleansings |
| Small 5, the little child's | under fourteen | no right to the burial ground: hidden at its own place without turning its direction |
| Small 6, the servant's | one with none but the masters to bury him | the master and mistress do it; not kept above the house |
| Disordered 1, the king's commoner funeral | one owed a great share the living cannot afford | beer bought and people lent, "just not to shame him" |
| Disordered 2, the heirless royal | one without sons who has 5 or 7 brothers | food shares by the number of brothers |
| Disordered 3, the *gre mo sman* | a son of 14–15 killed by a blade | the corpse to the border of slate and meadow |
| Disordered 4, old and infant | when people and herds are few | sent to the grave by a medicinal means |
| Disordered 5, not even with great merit | monk, mantra practitioner or Bon priest dying in his row, one dying in his own rite | no funeral |
| Disordered 6, not even with great power | a leper | no funeral: wrapped in hide and cast into water |

The thirteen ways of funeral name who buries whom (lord and subject, husband and wife, maternal uncle and nephew, father and son, teacher and pupil, each both ways). Not allowed the seven-day rites: one with great obstacles to all the living, one who never sent out sons and sisters; parents and elder brothers may not bury [them]. Food may not be offered to "the thirteen", of whom WB names only one who choked on food; the notes gloss the phrase without naming the rest.
