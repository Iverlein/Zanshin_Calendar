# MDP's dream part (pp. 134-137) is a transmitted rite with a lineage ending in Bu ston, signed as a monk's notes

MDP's dream part, printed pp. 134–137, is made of three pieces. (1) Pp. 134 to the top of 135: a rite in lines of nine syllables for seeing the place of one's next birth in a dream (the mandala of nine heaps, the Buddha's image, the five offerings, the seven-limb offering, the torma, the mantra «ཨོ་བུདྡྷ་ཙུ་རུ་མ་ཙུ་རུ་ཀེ་སྭཱཧཱ» as the approach, lying in the lion's posture); the verses end with «རྫོགས་སོ», "it is finished", and three strokes. (2) Then the line of transmission «ཆོས་འདིའི་བརྒྱུད་པ» of this teaching: the paṇḍita Vikhyātadeva, the great paṇḍita Śākyaśrī, Dānaśrī, the teacher 'Jam gsar, the lama Rga lo, the great adept Zed dmar ba Sangs rgyas ye shes, the great abbot Bu ston Rin po che; the order of the names makes Bu ston the last holder named. (3) Then prose instructions for the same practice (the clean place, the offerings, the torma, the approach of 110,000 recitations, the sound, signs and appearances of the six kinds of beings, the colours white god, green human, yellow asura, blue animal, red hungry ghost, black hell), which end at the top of p. 137 with the words «དགེ་སློང་སྒྲ་ཚད་པའི་ཟིན་བྲིས་སོ», "notes taken down by the monk Sgra tshad pa", and a dedication («འཁོར་བ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་གྱི་རྒྱ་མཚོ་ཆེན་པོ་སྐེམ་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག», may the great ocean of saṃsāra's suffering be dried up). So the part is not the collectors' own composition and has no author's name in the sense of a composer: it is a transmitted practice written down, signed as notes by a monk, with a lineage that ends with Bu ston. It has no list of dream images. The front matter of the volume (title page, imprint, contents, the first text's heading, images 4–19 of the preview scans) holds no preface or collectors' note on the sources of the texts. Who Sgra tshad pa is, and whether the lineage's Rga lo is the translator Rga lo tsā ba, are not settled by the pages.
