# The notes' general verdict is one pebble laid over a counted run; Sprugton's 75 has none for the limbs

# The notes' «ཁ་དམར་སྤྱི» is one pebble laid over a counted run; Sprugtön's sum of 75 has none for the limbs

For question 564. In the notes on the reckoning of the dead (WB vol. 2, p. 503) the general verdict is named a pebble of its own: «ཉི་ཤུ་པོ་དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པའི་སྤྱིའི་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་གཅིག་སྟེ་བཏང་གཅོད་རྡེའུ་ཉེར་གཅིག» (wb.2:p502-503), "twenty counted white and black, one general verdict pebble: twenty-one deciding pebbles". So «ཁ་དམར་སྤྱི» is a verdict laid once over a run that has been counted, and it is counted in the total. Read so, Sprugtön's closing «ཁ་དམར་སྤྱི་ལྟར་གདགས་པར་བྱ» (wb.2:p22-23) would lay one verdict pebble over the sixteen limbs by the general rule. Against it stands the sum: 50 roots, 16 limbs, 5 verdict pebbles and 4 cherished make the colophon's 75 and leave no seventy-sixth (ch27-sprugton-limbs-beside-the-five); the notes of pp. 533-538 are silent on this reckoning. Nothing found settles whether the colophon's sum leaves the limbs' verdict uncounted or the closing line only says "read as in general".
