# The dreams that mark the harmful nāga spirits' illness (ch. 81)

Image group: the dreamer ill. Read by 1 text: Four Tantras (for a sick dreamer).
Offered only when the dreamer is ill: every row is a reading for a sick dreamer (the Four Tantras above WB ch. 25).
The creature names of the list were settled by RD227 (fish and snakes, tadpoles and frogs, a scorpion and a spider, insects and bees, a meadow); the image is no longer listed apart in dream-unsettled-words.

RD274 (2026-11-09) after RD255, RD264 and RD271: the list is not the nāga's illness alone. Khro ru and the Drikung sort it by the spirit it marks (st-dream-naga-illness): water, trees and the green meadow, falling within the aim of a harm sent at one (གཏད་དུ་ཚུད་པ, not "a pledge": st-gtad-du-tshud-pa-under-a-sent-harm); the creatures, the general sign of the flesh-eaters; things sticking to the body, sitting in a crevice and beasts chasing, signs of a spirit; narrow doors, passes and the pit, being pressed down; the sun setting, dust, hail, arrows, an egg and the moving statues, the sign of a male spirit (ཕོ་གདོན); women drawing out the essence, a female spirit (མོ་གདོན). The words once left open are read: ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ is the clay statues of the temple moving (ལྡེར་སོ of the Bhutan and Drathang prints is the same word spelt otherwise; st-lder-bzo-is-the-temples-clay-statues), and ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ is "the time felt by the god", against "the time felt by oneself" (st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god; the scans of three prints all read ལྷས and དུས, the ངུས of two etexts is OCR, RD264). The picker may show a single row, the sign of being struck by a spirit, or the commentaries' sort; the text of the chapter itself gives only the first.
