# Saraha's verse of the dawn stands in his Buddhakapāla rite; two Guhyasamāja rites with the same stretch (RD373)

The verse of Saraha that the Sngags rim chen mo gives at snr:p369 («ས་ར་ཧས་ཀུ་ཤའི་སྟན་ལ་ཁྱོད་ཉོལ་ལ། ནང་པར་བཞིན་བཟང་རྣམས་ལོངས་ཤིག ... དཔལ་ལྡན་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐོད་པ་ཡི། དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ཆེན་པོ་མཐོང་ཞེས་བརྗོད») stands in Saraha's rite of the glorious Buddhakapāla mandala (Derge Bstan 'gyur etext VE23703_1342, pp. 462-488, [p470]), in the Derge etext as «ཀུ་ཤའི་སྟན་ལ་ཁྱོད་ཉོལ་ལ། ནང་པར་བཞིན་བཟངས་རྣམས་ལོངས་ཤིག། སང་གི་ནང་པར་ཁྱོད་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། དཔལ་ལྡན་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐོད་པ་ཡི། གྲོང་ཁྱེར་ཆེན་པོ་མཐོང་ཞེས་བརྗོད» (sbk:p470): where the Sngags rim chen mo has "the great mandala" the etext has "the great city", and the skull (ཐོད་པ) stands in the deity's name. The same passage asks the dream "at the proper times of dawn" and says that for a bad sign one turns back the obstructors; it names no watch and no day.

Two more rites of the Guhyasamāja that the Sngags rim chen mo does not name in these pages have the same stretch: the rite attributed to Klu sgrub (Nāgārjuna, Derge etext VE23703_1351, pp. 32-71, [p43]) has "the dream sign, tell me at dawn" and the destroying of a non-virtuous dream by the mantra of all rites (ngm:p43), and Kṛṣṇa's (Nag po pa's) rite in the same volume ([p496]-[p518], at [p506]-[p507]) has the same verse of joy, the question at dawn and the Ender of Obstructors said over the tooth-sticks; Kṛṣṇa's rite was read in the etext and not put down as a passage.
