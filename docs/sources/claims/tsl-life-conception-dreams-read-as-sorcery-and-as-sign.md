# The parents' dreams at Rtsa gsum gling pa's conception: the people read sorcery, he reads good dependent arising; no hour or day is given

The same pair of dreams at Rtsa gsum gling pa's conception was read in two ways. The parents dreamed (the mother, an Indian ācārya bathing the child and dissolving into light at the crown; the father, a Heruka giving a sword to be put in the son's hand; i36-37); "the people" said it was a curse of sorcery (བྱད་ཁ, byad kha), mantra-holders were set to repel it, and later, when the mother's body showed the child, the dream was held a harm (སུན་ཀ, «sun ka», sense not settled) because "many children had died before" after such dreams (i38). The son's own song to his mother (i49-50) reads the same dream as good dependent arising (རྟེན་འབྲེལ་བཟང་པོའི་རྨི་ལམ), the first of three marks (the dream at conception; the gladness of gods, spirits and men at his birth; the prophecy tested), and says to the father: "what use is a good dream to your great doubt?". Rtsa gsum gling pa's reading is his, given after the event; a reading by the people is not a rule of the texts. Dependence on the hour or day is not stated for this dream; it ties the dream to a stage of the pregnancy («མངལ་དུ་ཞུགས་པའི་ཚེ», when entering the womb) and to who dreamed it.
