# Yu thog: kings, planets and nāgas are met by Hayagrīva, Vajrapāṇi and Nāgarakṣa; the animal chain stands in «the dream of spirits»; two other teachings put it elsewhere and give other antidotes (RD403, q1118)

RD403 (q1118). The Yu thog sentence is «to tame spirits (གདོན་འདུལ་བ་ལ): for the kings Hayagrīva, for the planets Vajrapāṇi, for the nāgas Nāgarakṣa and the like, one meditates each one's own antidote and subdues them» (dds.47:p293-295). So kings (རྒྱལ་པོ), planets (གཟའ) and nāgas (ཀླུ) are named, in the sentence on taming gdon, as classes with an antidote deity each; the text does not call them «classes of gdon» in a separate sentence, and no commentary on the Yu thog text was found, but the sentence has no other subject. The next sentence is «and in the dream of spirits (གདོན་གྱི་རྨི་ལམ) also: a dog by a wolf, a wolf by a tiger, a tiger by a lion and so on, and for any other animal one sees, one meditates whatever antidote goes and subdues it». So in the dream of spirits the animal seen is met with the next stronger one; whether the spirit itself shows as the animal the sentence does not say («whichever animal is seen»).

Two other teachings of dream training (dds.52:p19-20, p245-246) have the same lists in a different order: there the animal chain (mouse by cat, dog by wolf, wolf by tiger, tiger by lion, man by flesh-eater) belongs to tame «what is to be tamed of the world» and is not tied to a dream of spirits, and the spirits are the second half with their own antidotes: nāgas by the garuḍa, kings (and a «dpe dkar» type) by Hayagrīva, klu bdud and klu srin by Avalokiteśvara (p20) or Mañjuśrī (p246), harmful spirits and flesh-eaters by Vajrapāṇi, the nāgas and other gdon named at the end as «that spirit». They agree with the Yu thog text that kings are met by Hayagrīva and disagree on the nāgas (garuḍa against Nāgarakṣa) and on the planets (not named there). This is a conflict of the lists, not of one teaching: none of the three says that a spirit shows as an animal, and the Yu thog text alone calls the animal dream «the dream of spirits».
