# The thirty: whose year each starting point reckons

Whose year a starting point reckons (Mipham's notes, WB vol. 2, pp. 532–533):

| Point | Whose year |
| --- | --- |
| 1–9 | the person's own ("up to the ninth it is easy") |
| 10, subjects and servants | if it concerns subjects, the lord's who asks |
| 11, horses | the horse owner's |
| son and bride («བུ་བག», presumably 14, the bride's going) | the son's and the bride's |
| 15, house and household | the house owner's |
| 17, the copper vessel | the year of the asker who buys the bronze vessel |
| 21, the old | for an old person who has died, his year and the year of death, with the age |
| 23–24, the dead | the dead's year and age |
| 25, the grave site | the dead's year, or applied to the asker as well |
| the rest | each one's own year |

The year so named is the one counted from in claim thirty-count. The notes do not say which year serves for points 12–13, 16, 18–20, 22 and 26–30 beyond "each one's own".
