# The «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག» of the Mchog dga' occur 24 times on 22 pages, about half in verse lines, the dream line among them; the Nag rtsis dream lines do not quote them

In the Mchog dga' (Phug pa Lhun grub rgya mtsho's commentary on the Dbyangs 'char, in the Kun 'dus chen mo vol. 3) the words «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག» (some of the Chinese and Nepalese) occur 24 times, on 22 printed pages between 71 and 402 (etext pp. 91-422), always as one voice among several: beside «སྔ་མ་ཁ་ཅིག» (some of the earlier ones), «འགྲེལ་པ་བྱེད་པ་དག» (the commentators) and «བོད་འགའ་ཞིག» (some Tibetans, p. 119). About half quote verse lines in seven-syllable couplets, introduced «ཞེས་བཀོད» or «ཞེས་འཆད»: the planets' directions (p. 87), the houses' colours (p. 133), the benefic and malefic planets (pp. 143, 144, 146), the twelve results (p. 160), the table of victory and defeat (p. 164), the thirty parts for the thief (p. 174), Saturn's dream (p. 178, the lines of the question q1074), the houses' elements (p. 181), the mansions' castes (p. 260); the rest say how they set a thing, often with the Mchog dga's judgement that it fits the root text or does not.

So the Saturn lines on p. 178 («སྤེན་པས་མི་ངན་རྣམས་དང་འགྲོགས། རི་དང་བྲག་ལ་འཛེག་པ་རྨི», Saturn: keeping company with bad people, one dreams of climbing hills and cliffs) are one of a series of verse lines the Mchog dga' takes from a source it calls only «some of the Chinese and Nepalese», of which the dream rule is one place among two dozen, and not a dream-specific quotation. Nothing in the Nag rtsis dream lines of the same volume (the claim on the seven lines) is that source or quotes it: none of them speaks of a planet. BDRC's exact phrase search for the Saturn line finds it only in the Kun 'dus and in the *Bzo rtsis zlos gar* vol. 2 (MW1PD95727, search only, the copy of the same Mchog dga'); likewise for the lines of the tenth and eleventh place and Saturn with Ketu (kd.3:p187, kd.3:p315: «སྤེན་པ་ལ་གྲགས་པ་ངན་པ་འབྱུང», «མཁྲིས་ནད་ཚད་ནད་གྲང་ནད་འོང», «རྨི་ལམ་ངན་དང་མྱ་ངན་འོང»: the same two books, 2 hits each; «ནོར་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཐོབ་རྨི་ལམ་རྣོ་བ» and «རྨི་ལམ་རྣོ་བ་དང་དགྲ་ཉམས»: none, the etext's spacing may differ). Who the Chinese and Nepalese are, and whether their verses are a book of their own reckoning, is not told in the Mchog dga'.
