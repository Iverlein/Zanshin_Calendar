# Bhaṭṭotpala's gloss pairs the Bṛhadyātrā's parts of the night and seasons with the humours: last third, rains, wind; middle, autumn, bile; first, spring, phlegm (RD312)

Bhaṭṭotpala's *Vivaraṇa* on the verse that the Bṛhadyātrā numbers 16.10 (the Aśvamedhīyayātrā recension numbers it 17.11, printed p. 179; the gloss is on p. 180) states what the verse leaves to its reader, and states it in the verse's own order (*yathākramam*): the last third of the night is the aggravation of wind, the middle third of bile, the first third of phlegm; and in the rains wind, in autumn bile, in the spring called Mādhava phlegm; in the other seasons the humour only accumulates (*saṃcaya*). A dream is then seen with the signs of the humour that is at its height in the dreamer's hour and season: one of a windy nature sees the signs of wind in the last third of the night and in the rains, one of bile those of bile in the middle third, and so on; the "common" (*sādhāraṇa*) dream, the mixed one (*sannipāta*), is made of all the signs.

So the three pairs read by position in RD295 (last part of the night, rains, wind; middle, autumn, bile; first, spring, phlegm) are the commentator's own, not only the reader's: a commentary of the 10th century says them in words. It is still a pairing of the dream's *kind* with the dreamer's humour and the hour and season; it gives no span from the dream to its fruit, no lunar day, weekday or month. The recension of the verse in this print has "prāvṛṭśare" where Pingree's text has the variants of its manuscripts for the season names; the sense is not changed.

Doubt: the gloss is read on a modern print (Kathmandu, V.S. 2026) at full size, every line legible; the print's own manuscripts are not named in the pages read.
