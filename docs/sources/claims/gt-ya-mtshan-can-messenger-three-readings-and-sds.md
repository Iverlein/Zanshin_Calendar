# The good messenger «ya mtshan can» in the Four Tantras: Byang pa's outsider in a vow, Rin spungs pa's monk or Brahmin, Khro ru's man of qualities; the Siddhasāra's bad non-Buddhist ascetic

The Four Tantras' line on the good messenger, «ཕོ་ཉ་ཡ་མཚན་ཅན་དང་དགེ་སྦྱོང་དང་། རིགས་མཐུན་པ་ནི་ནད་པ་སོས་པའི་རྟགས།» (gt:i41), is read three ways, and the Siddhasāra has a bad messenger who looks like one of them.

- Byang pa (etext pp. 204-205), glossing from the Moonbeams on the Heart of Medicine, Body ch. 6: ya mtshan can (ཡ་མཚན་ཅན) is an outsider (ཕྱི་རོལ་པ, phyi rol pa) who keeps a particular vow (བརྟུལ་ཞུགས, brtul zhugs); a monk (དགེ་སྦྱོང, dge sbyong) is one who has gone forth and lives chastely; «of his kind» (རིགས་མཐུན་པ) is one of like family. Such a messenger means recovery.
- Rin spungs pa (etext p. 227): ya mtshan can is a monk (དགེ་སློང, dge slong), an ascetic, or one of a Brahmin family of clean habits, one who has completed the qualities of abandoning and realising and works for beings; «of his kind» is one who has practised the physician's lore or holds some other branch of learning.
- Khro ru (etext p. 297): ya mtshan can is one who has qualities that raise him above others, or one who has gone forth with a virtuous mind; «of his kind» may be one who is not that but is dignified in dress, or a layman who is handsome, in his youth, with sound senses, a good name and clean habits; the opposite is a eunuch, a man with a black birthmark, one with a ruined limb or sense.
- The Siddhasāra (sds:p75-77) lists «a non-Buddhist ascetic» (མུ་སྟེགས་ཅན་གྱི་རབ་ཏུ་བྱུང་བ, mu stegs can gyi rab tu byung ba) among the unwelcome messengers. Byang pa's «outsider in a vow» would be the same man as the Siddhasāra's, but good in the Four Tantras' line and bad in the Siddhasāra's list; Rin spungs pa's and Khro ru's readings leave no outsider in the Tantra's line, so there the two do not clash.
