# The Lord Lama's replies on the star and Chinese reckonings, and the iron bird and monkey

Two replies of the regent cite one work by its title, «རྩིས་སྐར་ནག་གི་དྲི་ལན», "the replies on the star and the Chinese (black) reckonings": reply 137 (ys:i494-496, on the scan) as the replies of «༧རྗེ་བླ་མ», the Lord Lama, and reply 156 (ys.2:i016, machine) as the replies of «༧རྣམ་འདེན་མཆོག», the Supreme Guide. Both stand under the mark ༧ of honour and both name the work by its title only: the pool does not find the author's name, and does not know whether the two are one lama under two epithets. The identification with a named teacher is not made here.

What reply 137 takes from the work: it "gave", first, the thirteen Chinese circles of the *Dpyad lding*, set out as the five-fold circles in the common way then known to the people, and, below, as the instruction, the thirty circles of China and Tibet, "turned from upon the iron bird and monkey" (ལྕགས་བྱ་སྤྲེལ་སྟེང་ནས་བསྐོར). The regent reads this as the pointer to the source of the way this treatise reckons: the eight-fold and five-fold circles, little known among the people, are turned from the iron bird and monkey and finely divided by the male or female of the year of the person reckoned for. So the iron bird and monkey are given as the common point from which the circles turn; the male or female of the reckoned person's year is what the treatise adds to it. The reply names no other pair and does not call the iron bird and monkey an example.
