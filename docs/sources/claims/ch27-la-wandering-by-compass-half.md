# Ch. 27, p. 16: the la's wandering goes to the opposite half of the compass from the animal's own place

On p. 16 the verse of whose la wanders (ཕོ་མོའི་བླ་ཅིག་འཁྱམས་མ་འཁྱམས, "does the la of man or woman wander?"; Yigdzin-1 on the 1996 print, img. 24, ll. 19-27 of the page; the same words on the other prints by the claim [ch27-la-wandering-directions](ch27-la-wandering-directions.md)) names four years, each under the sky year (གནམ་ལོ, gnam lo) of one trine, and gives the la a side: the rat year's south, the snake year's north, the horse year's north, the pig year's south.

Our own count, not a word of the text: the four years stand, on the circle of the twelve animals as the Phugpa lay them round the compass (rat at north, hare east, horse south, bird west, the others between), as the rat and the pig in the northern half, the snake and the horse in the southern half. Each la then goes to the opposite half of the compass from the animal's own: the rat and the pig south, the snake and the horse north. In the order of the cycle the four directions run rat south, snake north, horse north, pig south, the two northern animals sharing one side and the two southern the other, as the verse prints them.

This is a second way of counting from RD51's, which went by the trine (the sky year's side) and found the snake and the pig the two that do not go opposite; by the animal's own place on the compass none is an exception, so a misprint of east or west for north or south is not needed to explain the pairs. The verse does not state any such rule, no text read gives one, and it names only four of the twelve years, so the count is a likely reason for the printed directions, not a proof; the rat's «ལྷོར», read «ལྟོར» by Yigdzin-1 on the Taikhang, is south by this count too, and by the pig's «ལྷོ་ཡི» beside it.
