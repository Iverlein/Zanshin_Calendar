# «ཡོ་ནས» in the dictionaries: crooked or aslant, no place; «འདྲེན» serves or leads

No dictionary of Steinert's 64 has «ཡོ་ནས», and none has «ཡོ» as a place or a thing of the house. The senses: «ཡོ་བ» (yo ba) "crooked, aslant, awry" and «ཁ་ཡོ» "mouth crooked" (Valby); an old «ཡོ» "all"; «ཁ་ཡོག» is "false charge", not a lid. WB's one other «ཡོ་ནས» (vol. 2, printed p. 249, the omens of hills) is the verb with «ནས». The nearest gloss of the rat day's pair is the jar's mouth askew, from which the beer is served or drawn («འདྲེན» is "serve" in «ཆང་འདྲེན» of the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs*, "lead" and "draw" elsewhere); that is a guess, and whose «འདྲེན» it is (the beer's, or a corpse's, since «འདྲེན་པ» is also the one who leads) is not settled.
