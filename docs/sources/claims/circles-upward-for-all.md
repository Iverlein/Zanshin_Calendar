# The circles are counted upward for everyone, by the years lived

Reply 139 (ys:i496-497): the thirteen circles are drawn upward (གྱེན་འདྲེན, gyen 'dren) for every person, man or woman. Counting male down and female up, like the progressed year (ལོག་མེན, log men), as some Tibetan scholars and new compositions do, has no scripture behind it and is a mixture by analogy with the thirty. The proof for upward: the starting points handed down from Darchen Thigle (དར་ཆེན་ཐིག་ལེ, dar chen thig le) annotate the first "draw upward by the years lived" (དུ་ལོན, du lon), as all the old texts do; and if the count turned by sex, the king's circle would serve for the queen and the seventh circle, for women, would be needless.

Stated: upward for all, by "the years lived". Inferred: "the years lived" (དུ་ལོན) is the same count as Zhechen Gyaltsab's (zg:p303-304) and the notes' "by the years lived" (wb.2:p533b); whether the birth year counts as one (question 301) the reply does not say.
