# The thirty: the ninth point, the adorned parrot, for young women and girls

The ninth of the thirty starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go), from the mouth of the adorned parrot (སྤྲ་མཁན་ནེ་ཙོ, spra mkhan ne tso), is read for young women and girls (དམན་ཆུང་བུ་མོ, dman chung bu mo). Its eight pebbles are laid as for every point (claims thirty-count and thirty-eight-fighters), each white or black, and read so:

| Pebble | Asks | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whether the wool on the spindle (འཕང་ར, 'phang ra) is used up | She comes to the fore (མགོ་ཐོན, mgo thon), good | The wool is used up: unwished sorrow in the household and among its old women | — |
| 2 | Whether the lustre of the neck turquoise has faded | Not faded; her own kindness is greater than her parents' | The small turquoise has lost its lustre: her head is weak, illness and chronic disease come | Strive at rites such as raising glory (དཔལ, dpal) |
| 3 | Whether the *ha lo* flower (ཧ་ལོ, ha lo; not identified) has been carried off by frost | Not carried off, good | Carried off by frost: gossip or obstacles come | Strive at service rites (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) |
| 4 | Whether the white silver mirror is broken | Its back is not broken | The white silver mirror is broken, bad | — |
| 5 | Whether the lustre of the silk clothes has faded | Good in the father's line's place | The silk's lustre has faded: bad for the father's line | Strive at service rites |
| 6 | Whether the gold and turquoise head ornament has parted from her | Not cast off, good | Parted: a short time with both parents, and in the end bad in the lifelong companion's place | The nine turnings back of sorrow (མྱ་ངན་དགུ་བཟློག, mya ngan dgu bzlog) and the black remedy rite (གཏོ, gto) help |
| 7 | Whether the string of the conch-shell rosary is cut | Not cut, good | The strings of the iron and conch rosaries are cut: bad for her life | — |
| 8 | Whether the eyelet of the waist ornament (རྐེད་རྒྱན་འཕྲུལ་མིག, rked rgyan 'phrul mig) is worn | The eyelet is not worn; belt, short knife and neck ornaments good | The belt's eyelet is worn: bad for the household's old women | Strive at service rites that turn these back |

Whose year is reckoned: the person's own (the notes, vol. 2, p. 533: «གཞན་རྣམས་སོ་སོའི་ལོ་རྟ»).

Which of the eight fighting pebbles is which check is not stated; the order of p. 453 is the likely one (claim thirty-board-groups; question 259).
