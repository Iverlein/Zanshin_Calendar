# Both tenths are kept, the waxing one is the Guru's: Zhe chen rgyal tshab

Zhe chen rgyal tshab's text on the tenth day (zgp.13:p536-537, machine reading; p. 535, where it begins, not read) rules on the two tenths of the month: the masters of India and Tibet, of the new and the old schools, kept the offering of the time (དུས་མཆོད, dus mchod) on the tenth of both the waxing and the waning half without a break, since "the night of the waning half and the day of the waxing half" are occasions on which accomplishment is quick; but of the two the occasion of Padmasambhava's deeds, and the one on which he thinks of his followers, is the tenth of the waxing half (ཡར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུ, yar ngo'i tshes bcu). Three statements are quoted for it: the Northern Treasure (an emanation of Padma comes when the waxing tenth dawns), Ratna gling pa's treasure, here called Tshes bcu'i bskul byang (the oath to come on the tenth of every month), and a verse that he comes every morning and evening and in person on the waxing tenth, the same promise as in Rtsa gsum gling pa's treasure prayer (sgc:i207-208).

For whom and what: the text speaks of "each person of faith" (དད་ལྡན་མི་རེ) before whom an emanation comes if the offering of the monkey month's tenth is kept, and of "whoever has faith" for the other months (zgp.13:p537-540); the act named throughout is keeping the offering of the time, without a vow as a condition. The benefits it lists are the worldly and the final ones of each month (claim tenth-day-benefit-of-each-animal-month-padma-lingpa). It sets the monkey month's tenth, and the monkey year's above all, as chief, and fixes that month as the fifth Hor month (zgp.13:p541, scan; claim monkey-month-is-the-fifth-hor-month-snron). From p. 541 on the text is a customary of a monastery's own tenth-day festival (dress, chant, cymbals and drums), which concerns its monks and was not recorded.

This adds to the first pass's finding that the promise texts do not name the waning tenth (claim tenth-day-waning-half-not-named): here both tenths are kept, the waning one for its night, and the waxing one is the Guru's.
