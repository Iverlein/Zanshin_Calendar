# Ch. 27: Sprugtön's sixteen limbs are sixteen pebbles beside the five verdict pebbles (75 = 50 + 16 + 5 + 4)

Sprugtön's obstacle reckoning (WB vol. 2, pp. 22-23, called "of seventy-five pebbles" in the colophon, p. 25) adds up if each limb is one pebble: ten root pebbles of five aspects each, fifty; the sixteen limbs, sixteen; the five verdict pebbles (ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་ལྔ), five; the four cherished pebbles (གཅེས་རྡེལ་བཞི), four: seventy-five. The sixteen are, by kind: the own obstacle, the seventh, the three in accord, the fourth executioner and the year's mansion (five kinds, the three in accord counted as one); the nine-eye, the grave-eye and the bone-eye (three), together eight; the mother-luck, the entering of the nest and the rough element enemy (three); the grave obstacle, the Chinese arrow, the country obstacle, the bed obstacle and the house obstacle with the king's gate blocked (five). Each is laid white if it does not fall and black if it does; none is tied to one of the five aspects in the text.

The five verdict pebbles are tied to the aspects: "to each of the five, the life force, body, power, luck and la, one verdict of the counted white and black", counted over the ten roots' fifty. The limbs end "where these fall, a black pebble, where not, a white; lay the verdict as in general (ཁ་དམར་སྤྱི་ལྟར་གདགས)". The arithmetic says the limbs are sixteen pebbles beside the five verdict pebbles, not counted into them; "as in general" then means the verdict is laid by the general rule of the book for a run of white and black pebbles, no verdict pebble being named for the limbs apart from the five. This rests on the sum and the order, not on a sentence that says it: the count of the limbs is the reader's (the print's «རྩུབ», "rough", may be a word of its own or a sixteenth item). The question stays open on the notes' silence: none of the notes (vol. 2, pp. 533-538) touches Sprugtön's reckoning.
