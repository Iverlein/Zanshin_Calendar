# Other Indian omens are tied to the lunar day, the nakṣatra and the season; dreams are not (RD265)

The dream texts of RD263 and RD265 never tie the fruit of a dream, or the dream itself, to a *tithi*, a weekday, a nakṣatra or a month. The omen literature that stands round them does tie other omens to those things, and that is the frame in which a Tibetan reader of the *Svapnohana* might expect such a tie:

- **The lunar day.** A halo round five bodies does its harm by the day of the half-month: the fifth and the two after, the towns and the treasury; the eighth, the crown prince and the generals; the ninth to the eleventh, the princes; the twelfth, a siege; the thirteenth, mutiny; the fourteenth, the queen; the fifteenth, the king (Atharva-Veda *Pariśiṣṭa* 63, in Kohlbrugge, kho:p72-74). A whirlwind or *nirghāta* on the first, eighth and fourteenth lunar day (*pratipad, aṣṭamī, caturdaśī*) is mild, on the others severe (Parāśara, adb:p307-309; kho:p58-61).
- **The nakṣatra.** A whirlwind's effect on the rain depends on the nakṣatra (Parāśara, adb:p307-309; kho:p58-61); meteors, planets and nakṣatras decide which classes are struck (kho:p48).
- **The time of day and night.** Omens harm different classes by the watch of the day and of the night; the twilights are critical times (kho:p48, kho:p58-61, neg:p17-19).
- **The season.** Wonders of nature are lucky in their own season and terrible out of it (kho:p140-141; neg:p17-19).
- **The time of fruit.** An omen ripens in at most a year; a heavenly omen a year, an omen of the air and the earth six months; the comet's fruit in as many years as it was seen months, and nothing in its first three fortnights (neg:p17-19).

For dreams the same compilation, the *Adbhutasāgara*, which collects all omens and gives the lunar day and the nakṣatra of a *nirghāta*, has only the watch of the night and the hour of dawn or of the cows' release (adb:p513-515).

The one place where the lunar day, the nakṣatra and the weekday meet a dream is the *Yogayātrā*, 2.23–24 (yyt:p28-29): the king, on the morning of the march, hears the *tithi*, the *nakṣatra* and the weekday of the day read, and the hearing destroys his bad dreams, among its other fruits. The verse does not date a dream; Negelein's gloss (neg:p35) reads it as the astrologer fixing the date, star and weekday of the dream, which the verse does not say.

Not found in any text read: a dream whose fruit depends on the lunar day, the weekday, the nakṣatra or the month on which it was seen. Wayman's report that the *Aṅgavijjā* asks "the part of the phase" (RD263) is answered at [angavijja-dream-time-fortnight-season](angavijja-dream-time-fortnight-season.md): the *Aṅgavijjā* asks the fortnight and the season of the dream as facts to find out.
