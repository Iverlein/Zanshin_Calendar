# In Rtsa gsum gling pa's life the dreams come at named hours of the night: nub mo, gung thun, srod, tho rangs; a bad dream's marks are gone at sunrise

In Rtsa gsum gling pa's own life story the dreams that matter come at named hours of the night, and each hour is given in the line that tells the dream: «ནུབ་མོ་» (nub mo), the night in general, in most episodes (conception, a bad dream, the leper); «གུང་ཐུན» (gung thun, the midday or middle session of the day's four sessions, used here for a dream "that night": «དེ་ནུབ་ཉལ་བའི་གུང་ཐུན་རྨི་ལམ་ལ», i138); «ནུབ་མོ་སྲོད་ལ» / «སྲོད་འཕྲོས» (srod, the evening, for the dream of the god Lha btsan 'bar ba, i169, and the evening visitors, i322); and «ཐོ་རངས» (tho rangs, dawn), once for a second dream of the same night as the gung thun one (i139: «ཡང་གཅིག་ཐོ་རངས་རྨི་ལམ»), once for waking from the leper dream (i322) and once for waking from the tested dream (i170). A bad dream's marks are gone «ནམ་ལངས་ཉི་ཤར་དུས» (nam langs nyi shar dus, at sunrise, i57). So the life gives, for the second dream of a night, the dawn; it does not say, in these pages, that one hour of the night yields a good or a bad dream. The hour is told as part of the narrative, and what is set beside it is what followed: the dream is checked on waking (the valley, the body), or by an event days or months later.
