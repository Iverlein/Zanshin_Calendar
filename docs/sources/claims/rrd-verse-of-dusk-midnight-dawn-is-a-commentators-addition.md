# The verse «at dusk phlegm, at midnight bile, dawn the time of wind» is not in the Zab mo nang don's root verses; the commentators bring it in «from the tantra» (RD384)

The Zab mo nang don's own root verses on the dream do not name the parts of the night; the verse «at dusk phlegm is mostly strong; at midnight bile is said; dawn is the very time of wind» is brought in by the commentators to explain the line «by the condition of the constituents», and they give it as «from the tantra(s)», one of them as the Mañjuśrī root tantra's (RD384, 2026-11-10, etext only).

The root verses (as Dbang phyug dpal bzang quotes them, rrd.13:p286-289; the root print BDRC I1701 gives the same, but its OCR is poor and was not used for the words) give the appearances by the place the dream-consciousness goes (above, below, between; the four directions; broad and narrow) and by the constituent (ཁམས, khams): for wind blue and black things, flying, riding; for bile fire, gold, red and yellow; for phlegm water, snow, pearls, white clothes, a woman, an elephant, silver; and close with «the Jina told it in the Mañjuśrī root tantra» (རྒྱལ་བས་འཇམ་དཔལ་རྩ་རྒྱུད་གསུངས). No «ནམ་ཕྱེད», «ཐོ་རངས» or «སྲོད་ལ» stands in them.

The verse of the three parts is in the commentaries at the line «by the condition of the constituents»: Dbang phyug dpal bzang po «from the tantras» (རྒྱུད་རྣམས་ལས), Karma Phrin las pa «from the tantra» (རྒྱུད་ལས, rrd.14:p232-234), Kong sprul «from the tantra» (rrd.15:p235-236), Zhwa dmar IV Chos grags ye shes «the Mañjuśrī root tantra says» (འཇམ་དཔལ་རྩ་རྒྱུད་ལས, rrd.16:p220); the medical commentators Zur mkhar ba and, copying him, the Blue Beryl, give it as «because it is said at length in the Tantra» (རྒྱུད་ནས་མང་དུ་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར) after quoting «the omniscient Rang byung rdo rje» (rrd.17:p352-355); Dar mo sman rams pa's Gser mchan puts the same dream section into verse, with the verse as «Moreover, in the state of the dream...» and no source named for it (rrd.18:p285-286). The sense is the same in all: dusk, phlegm; midnight, bile; dawn, wind; and the dream's objects then take the colour of that constituent. It says what appears, not whether the dream is true; and it has three parts, where the Mañjuśrī root tantra's own verse has four watches (claim rrd-the-humour-verse-is-not-in-the-derge-manjushri-root-tantra).

The Karmapa's own verses (rrd.5:p57-59) do not use it, and none of the commentaries read quotes Marpa's saying or the Karmapa's verses (BDRC's exact-string search finds the saying only in vol. 5).
