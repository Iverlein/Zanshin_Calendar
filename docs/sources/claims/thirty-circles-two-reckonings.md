# The thirty and the circles are two reckonings

The regent answers question 137 of *Removing the Tarnish* (ys:i494-496): the thirty Chinese and Tibetan starting points (སྐོར་འགོ, skor 'go) of ch. 26, in their eight-pebble and five-pebble ways, and the thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding) of ch. 27 are each to be known apart, in WB's order; the common reckoners who count the years from the iron bird for "the crystal garuḍa" and so make the thirty the same as the circles are wrong. For the thirty, he reads an older reply of his teacher as the source for turning the pairs from the bird and monkey "distinguished in detail by the male or female year of the one reckoned", which is WB's count down for a male year and up for a female (wb.1:p453-454).

What the reply states: the two are not one; the thirty's pairs are counted by the sex of the reckoned person's year. What is inferred: nothing further; which element picks the pairs is not said here (question 259 stays open for ch. 26).
