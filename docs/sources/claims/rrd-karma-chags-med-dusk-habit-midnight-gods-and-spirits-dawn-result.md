# Karma chags med: the dream of the evening is habit, of midnight the display of gods and spirits, both uncertain; of dawn it has a result (RD405)

Karma chags med of Gnas mdo (Karma Kagyu) sets the three parts of the night in his verses on the signs of practice in dreams, in his own words and not as Marpa's saying: the dream of the evening (ནམ་སྲོད, nam srod) is habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) and nothing in it is certain; the dream of midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) is the magical display (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ, cho 'phrul) of gods and spirits (ལྷ་འདྲེ, lha 'dre) and nothing in it is certain; the dream of dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) has a result. For the dawn dream the sleeper lies squatting or in the lion's posture, since the left side stirs the channels and wind; and a dream forgotten on waking is not certain however good or bad. A dream of fright and terror is read as a sign of the generation stage held coarsely, and if the spirits and the rest are then fiercely subdued, as a sign that a little power has come.

So the midnight dream is named, in a Karma Kagyu instruction, the work of gods and spirits, uncertain, where Marpa's song (rrd.23:p102-104) has the emanations of Māra (བདུད, bdud) to be not trusted and the Karmapa's verses (rrd.5:p57-59) the power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang). The line of Karma chags med is nearest the Karmapa's in kind (spirits, not Māra) and adds gods; it does not say that it quotes Marpa. The remedy it gives for the fierce dream is the subduing of the spirits in the dream itself; no rule is given for the midnight dream beyond that it is not to be relied on. The same four lines stand in three other prints of the text (BDRC I2PD18241, I2496, I3CN4737) with only slips of the copyist; the page here is the etext's, not the print's.
