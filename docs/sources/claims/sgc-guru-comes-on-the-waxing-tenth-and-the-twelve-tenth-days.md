# Sgo chen's liturgies: Rtsa gsum gling pa's treasure prayer has the Guru come in person on the tenth of the waxing half; the prayer of the twelve tenth days joins an offering to each

The liturgy collection of Sgo chen dgon (etext only) holds two prayers that fix the tenth:

1. The prayer Bsam pa lhun grub, whose colophon says it was invited by O rgyan Rtsa gsum gling pa from Bsam yas Mchims phu (his treasure, rank 2): when the fortunate pray, the Guru comes every morning and evening for Tibet's sake, and "at the time of the tenth of the waxing half (ཡར་ངོ་ཚེས་བཅུ, yar ngo tshes bcu) he comes in person"; at the great places on the good occasions he heads the dākinīs' gathering (sgc:i207-208). The tenth meant is that of the waxing half; the twenty-fifth is not named.
2. The prayer of the life story of Guru Rinpoche's tenth days, Drop of Blessing, by Padma'i rig 'dzin 'Chi med bstan gnyis g.yung drung gling pa (rank 3, a later author): the tenth of the waxing half of each of the twelve months is a great occasion (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) with a deed of the Guru; on every tenth one offers and prays for that deed's blessing, so that what is unwanted is calmed and good gathers, remembering the Guru's promise to come to Tibet on each tenth; its colophon calls the offering of the tenth something the people of the Snowy Land must not leave undone, and names its sources, the History of the Guru's Four Bodies and the Benefits of the Tenth from the treasure of Lho brag Mgon po rin chen (sgc:i214-215).

For whom: the first, whoever prays; the second, everyone in Tibet, lay people included. The practice: offering and prayer to the Guru on the waxing tenth. The benefit: meeting the Guru, obstacles calmed, wishes accomplished. Both read in the etext only; the twelve stanzas of the months (etext pp. 208-214) are not recorded here.
