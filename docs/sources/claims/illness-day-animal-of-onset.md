# The day animals of WB pp. 428–429 are those of the day of falling ill

The twelve animals of WB vol. 1, pp. 428–429, headed «ཉི་མའི་ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང», "the day's illness and spirit identified", are the day animals of the time the sick person *fell ill*, not the birth animal. No sentence says so in so many words; it rests on four things that agree.

1. WB sets the whole identification inside one reckoning of the time of falling ill. Its outline (wb.1:p450) says: first examine the time the illness struck (the year, month, day and hour, by the eight trigrams), in the middle identify the illness and the spirit, at the end heal and lay the verdict. The identification's three readings that follow the progressed year are of the moment: the trigram (pp. 427–428), the day animal (pp. 428–429) and the mewa, which p. 431 says outright is the one "under which one fell ill" (wb.1:p431-433).
2. The regent's reply 133 (ys:i491, read on the scan) says the four times of the five pebbles, year, month, day and hour, are those «on whichever» one fell ill («གང་གི་ཐོག་ཏུ་ན་བ»); the day is one of them.
3. Mipham's notes (wb.2:p497-499) lay the nineteen pebbles' last three as the date, weekday and mansion of the day of falling ill («ན་ཉིན་གྱི་ཚེས་གཟའ་སྐར་མ»), so the day of falling ill is the day WB's illness reckoning keeps.
4. The *Garland of Stars* (kd.3:p693, etext only) names the four things the identification is examined by: the progressed year, the trigram, the mewa and the day (ཉི་མ, nyi ma), and sets the day's section (kd.3:p701) directly after the mewa's.

Nothing in the pool reads these animals by the birth year; the birth year is read in WB's other rows (the progressed year on the sick person's own animal, pp. 426–427, and the ransoms by the year animal, pp. 429–431, where the text says the year of the sick person). RD12's outline taking the animal as the birth animal is not supported by any passage. The rows' «ཕོ་ཡང་མོ་ལྕི» ("male light, female heavy") mark which sex is hit harder on that day's animal; they do not name the person's sex from a year.

What stays uncertain: where the day turns when one fell ill at night; no text speaks to it.
