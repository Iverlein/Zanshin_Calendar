# Negi: thun gsum is the Sanskrit triyāmā, the night (RD322, q941)

Negi's Tibetan–Sanskrit dictionary (Steinert no. 50) gives ཐུན་གསུམ (thun gsum) and ཐུན་གསུམ་པ (thun gsum pa) a second sense besides the third watch: the night, triyāmā, rātriḥ, and for ཐུན་གསུམ the Amarakośa's synonym verse of the night (a.ko. 1.4.5, "atha śarvarī niśā niśīthinī rātristriyāmā …") with the gloss trayo yāmā yasyāḥ sā triyāmā, "the one that has three watches" (a.vi. 1.4.5). So the answer to q941 is yes: ཐུན་གསུམ in WB's list of the night's names is the Tibetan of the Sanskrit triyāmā, as the Amarakośa's synonyms for the night are rendered in the lexica; Tshig mdzod chen mo and Tshig gter rgya mtsho mark "thun gsum pa" as a name of the night in the register of synonyms (mngon), and Ives–Waldo and Valby list it among the night's names. Negi gives for it the references ṅa.ko. 69 and rā.ko. 2.661, which are other Sanskrit lexica (not identified here). The Tibetan Amarakośa itself (a translation with this rendering) was not reached; the equivalence rests on the lexica.
