# The Four Tantras' dream rule beside the three-thirds rule of LGD and RST and the Svapnohana's watches

The Four Tantras' rule of the dream by night (st-dream-night-first-part-dawn) is a two-way rule, not a count of watches: the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod) gives no fruit because it is forgotten, the dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) gives fruit when the dream is clear. It names no middle part and no fourth watch, and gives no span after which the fruit falls.

Against the three-thirds rule (LGD, RST). LGD (lgd-dream-times-of-night) names dusk (སྲོད, srod) for habit, midnight (གུང, gung) for the afflictions and early dawn for omen; RST (rst-three-watches) gives three thirds of which only the last, the dawn third, is examined. The Four Tantras agree with both in what counts: only the dawn dream is taken as a sign. They are narrower in what they name: «མཚན་སྟོད», the first part, covers all that is not dawn. Khro ru reads it as dusk with midnight alike (so the dusk and the middle thirds of LGD and RST both fall into it, each without fruit), while Byang pa and 'Bum nag gsal sgron read it as the half of the night up to midnight and leave the stretch from midnight to dawn without a name; the three-thirds rule fills that stretch with the third in the middle (tricks of spirits, afflictions), and the Four Tantras' own lines do not. The reason they give is different too: forgetting (the sleeper does not keep it), where LGD and RST give the nature of the dream (habit, afflictions, spirits).

Against the Svapnohana (svo-watches-and-spans). The Svapnohana's four watches with fruit within a year, eight months, three months and half a month, and a fifth term, daybreak (སྐྱ་རེངས, skya rengs), within ten days, give an earlier dream a longer, not an empty, fruit; for the Four Tantras the early dream has none. Both rules make the dawn the surest time, but the Svapnohana counts spans and the Four Tantras do not; the Four Tantras cannot be read as the first-watch rule of the Svapnohana, since they never say that the first part's dream falls in a year. The only line of the Four Tantras' commentaries that gives a measure is the *Moonbeams* quoted in Byang pa and Rin spungs pa: the dream of the first part of the night has its fruit «far» and what is seen «small», and a dream at daybreak, sleep not taken again, is a great fruit that very day: a gradation, not a count. Modern Tibetan books (svo-modern-tibetan-quotations) put the Svapnohana's watches, the dawn's «last third» line and these two lines side by side in one passage, which is their own joining, not the Four Tantras'.

For a calendar of watches the Four Tantras give a rank on medicine's ground: dawn is the time of the dream that counts, and the earlier the night the less it counts; the bounds of «མཚན་སྟོད» are not fixed by the text, and the commentators differ (midnight, or dusk with midnight).
