# The Svapnādhyāya of the Penn manuscripts, the Srinagar and Ramtek manuscripts and the Odia print: the series of spans, a month for the fourth watch (RD277)

Five Indian witnesses of a short *Svapnādhyāya* (a dream chapter, which one manuscript calls Bṛhaspati's), read on the images, with the spans of the watches as each gives them, set beside Negelein's letters (neg:p14-16) and the Brahmavaivarta's series (bvp:p662-665, which the Svapnohana shares, svo:p268):

| Witness | Date | First watch | Second | Third | Fourth | Dawn | Early morning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 1871, f. 1v (pn1871:i2), colophon "iti bṛhaspatyuktasvapnādhyāyaḥ" | 18th–19th century (catalogue: circa 1700–1850) | a year | **eight months** | three months | **a month** (*māsaikena*) | ten days | at once, at the cows' release |
| Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 673, f. 1 (pn673:i1), colophon of saṃvat 1881 | **1824 CE** | a year | eight months | three months | **a month** | ten days | not in the verse |
| Peerzada volume, Srinagar, "bṛhaspatir uvāca" (pzs:i102-103) | undated | a year | eight months | three months | **a month** | ten days | not in the first four verses |
| Ramtek M 856, "bṛhaspatir uvāca" (rmk:i2) | undated | a year | eight months | three months | **a month** | ten days | not in the lines read |
| Odia print, Cuttack 1929 (osv:i3) | 1929 | a year | **seven (?) months** | three months | **a month and a half** (*sārdhamāsa*), glossed in Odia "fifteen days" | ten days | at once, if woken |
| Negelein's manuscripts a, f, m, n, q (neg:p14-16) | a: 1845; the others undated | a year | eight (or six) months | three months | a month | ten days | at once (cows' release or milking) |
| Negelein's i, "Bṛhaspati's" (neg:p14-16, neg:p32) | undated | a year | eight months | **three fortnights** | a month | ten days | not given |
| Brahmavaivarta, with the Svapnohana (bvp:p662-665) | | a year | eight months | three months | **half a month** | ten days | at once |

What follows from it, in our words:

- **The Penn Bṛhaspati *Svapnādhyāya* does not read Negelein's i** (three fortnights, a month): its series is the one of Negelein's a and f, with "a month" for the fourth watch. In the verse the manuscript pn1871 shares the second and third half-lines with a (*dvitīye cāṣṭabhir māsais tribhir māsais triyāmake*), and the last two lines with f (*aruṇodaya-velāyāṃ daśāhena phalaṃ bhavet | go-visarjana-velāyāṃ sadyaḥ svapno vidhīyate*). It is not the five-span series of the Svapnohana either: the fourth span is a month, not half a month.
- **Four manuscripts agree** (pn1871, pn673, the Srinagar volume pzs and the Ramtek M 856 rmk; the last two name the speaker, "bṛhaspatir uvāca"): a text copied in 1824 has the same series, so the form "a year, eight months, three months, a month, ten days" (with Bṛhaspati as speaker) is a stable text of the *Svapnādhyāya*, not one scribe's slip (the Kālaprakāśikā's year, half a year, three months, a month, twelve days, kpd:p184-189, is a related but different form).
- **Only the Odia print has "a month and a half"**, whose paraphrase says fifteen days, the Brahmavaivarta's half-month; its Sanskrit and its gloss do not agree, so one cannot say which the compiler meant, and "seven months" there may be a slip for eight.
- **None of the three has Negelein's "three fortnights"** (the only witness: i, which he reads in a Leipzig manuscript with a verse of its own, "tripakṣake").
- **The Svapnohana's half-month for the fourth watch and its eight months for the second are the Brahmavaivarta's**; the Penn *Svapnādhyāya* texts give the same eight months for the second watch and ten days at dawn and differ only by "a month" for the fourth, so the Svapnohana's series stands one span away from this family as well as on the Brahmavaivarta's. Which of the two readings of the fourth span (a month, half a month) is the older cannot be said from these witnesses: the two Penn copies are of the 18th–19th century.
- **The Penn Item 2730** (the catalogue: 2 leaves, "iti vṛhasyatiproktaḥ svapnādhyāyaḥ", colophon f. 2v) has on its last leaf a verse that recommends reading the treatise in the morning as a remedy for bad dreams ("… prātar utthāya yaḥ paṭhet | duḥsvapnanāśanaṃ tasya …"), like the verses 86ff. of Negelein's i (neg:p32); its first leaf, where the span verse would stand, was not delivered by the server in the time of the reading.

Not found: any of these texts naming a tithi, a weekday or a month for a span; the only clock is the watch (neg:p17-19).
