# The three stages of the Lama Gongdü dream examination: approach, application, examination

The Lama Gongdü text (LGD) gives the examination of dreams as three stages (རིམ་པ, rim pa), each a step of the practice, and the stage of the examination proper is itself ordered into seven kinds. The first stage is the approach and accomplishment (བསྙེན་སྒྲུབ, bsnyen sgrub): in a clean, solitary place, the practitioner bathes, takes clean new clothes, gives up meat, beer, garlic and onion, takes refuge and arouses the mind, visualises himself as Padmasambhava with Amitābha at the crown, Avalokiteśvara at the throat and Śākyamuni at the heart, recites a mantra of Śākyamuni for three days without other speech, and prepares a collyrium of white and red sandalwood and costus ground in the milk of a clean woman who has borne one boy, kept secret from every other being. Its fruit is clear senses, a bright mind, dreams that do not fade, and signs of purified obscurations. The second stage is the application to the work (ལས་ལ་སྦྱར་བ, las la sbyar ba): in the waxing half again, the pillow to the north with the images and offerings at its head, the seven-limb offering, the hundred-syllable recitation of Vajrasattva, the body imagined as white crystal with a white letter A inside, a thousand recitations, a drop at each of the three places and at each sense door, the lion's posture, the wish said in mind and aloud three times, the late-day meal left out, no other thought let in; the dream is caught at waking. The third stage is the examination itself (བརྟག་པ་དངོས, brtag pa dngos): the seven kinds in order (lgd:p966-967 ff.). So a dream is not examined on any night's dream: the text has the dreamer prepare for it, ask for it and catch it.
