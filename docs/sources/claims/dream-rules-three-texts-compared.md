# Siddhasāra ch. 5, the Four Tantras' ch. 7 and Gtsang sman's thirteen examinations side by side: the messenger, the physician's setting out, whose dream, the rule to decline

Three texts set the physician's examination before he takes a case: the Siddhasāra's chapter 5 (SDS), the Four Tantras' seventh chapter of the Explanatory Tantra (the Drathang print gt:i41-43, with Byang pa, Rin spungs pa and Khro ru for the glosses) and Gtsang sman Ye shes bzang po's thirteen examinations (GSM). Side by side:

| | Siddhasāra ch. 5 (sds:p72-73, sds:p75-77) | Four Tantras, Explanatory Tantra ch. 7 (gt:i41, gt:i42-43) | Gtsang sman (gsm:p263-264) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Why the examination | so as not to work in vain on one already spent; the span of life is to be known from the messenger, the signs of death and the signs (p. 73) | the physician who does not know the signs of death cannot choose what to take up and give up and wins no name (st:p34) | the first of thirteen examinations, before the treating |
| Order | signs on the body, then the bad dreams, the good dreams, the messenger, the physician's setting out, then the sentence on the physician's good name | the messenger first (five points in Byang pa and Khro ru: who he is, what the physician is doing when he comes, the day of setting out, the signs on the road, the signs at the house), then the dream verses, then the changes of bearing | the physician's own dream at dawn on the night before the messenger is to come, then the lucky connection, the place and road, the body, and on |
| Whose dream | the dreams are a person's, not the physician's: the healthy one who sees the bad ones falls ill, the sick one dies; of the good ones, the healthy gains wealth, the sick recovers soon | the bad dreams are the sick one's: those who dream them constantly die; for the healthy one it is uncertain and a service rite frees him; of the night the dream of the first part is forgotten and the dawn dream, seen and clear, bears fruit | the physician's own dream |
| Messenger | bad: of low family, a limb missing, a non-Buddhist ascetic, a noose or staff in hand, red, black or old clothes, weeping, oil on head and body, cutting grass or wood, a woman or a eunuch, riding a donkey, a she-camel or a buffalo; the opposite is good | good: a ya mtshan can, a monk, one of the physician's own kind; bad: frightened, stiff, trembling, toying with stones and lumps of earth, calling from afar, doing evil, with unlucky ornaments and speech; Byang pa quotes at length the Moonbeams' longer list (the Moonbeams on the Heart of Medicine, Body ch. 6), which has the weapon or staff, the eunuch, the woman, the red garland and salve, sesame-oil mud, old clothes, one cloth, and riding a donkey, a camel or a buffalo | not treated; the messenger is the next step |
| The physician setting out | good: an elephant's cry, thunder or a drum heard; a jewel, a garland, fresh meat, a parasol, a full vase seen; birds with male names on the left and female on the right; a dog or jackal going to the right; bad always: a go dha (iguana), a snake, a nāga snake | bad: the sixth, fourth and ninth of the lunar day, an eclipse, a bad star, the night; a broken or burning thing, «kill», a cat, a monkey, a mongoose, a snake across the road; good: full vessels, a bell, a lamp, flowers, a god's image, white ornaments, one keeping the dharma, a banner, fire, a horse, young with their mothers, pleasing sounds, food and drink, on the road or at the house | the good dream is the road itself: new clothes, the sun rising, a well-saddled white, blue, grey or goose-coloured horse ridden to a mountain top or the north-east |
| Rule to decline | none stated; «unwelcome», «unfit» for the bad signs; the chapter closes that the physician who examines thus and begins the work with certainty gains a name that is beautiful like an old garland among many good ones | the Tantra's own lines say that with such a messenger the work does not succeed, that the messenger's coming at the wrong moment is a sign the sick one will die, and that setting out is bad on those days; the explicit commands «the physician is not to go», «that sick one is not to be treated», «the physician is to give him up» are verses of the Moonbeams that Byang pa (pp. 206-210) and Khro ru (p. 299) quote beside the Tantra, not lines of gt:i41 | stated: after a bad dream the sick one will not live and the physician must not go to him; if he must, he goes having granted that the sick one will not live |

The word for the messenger differs: the Siddhasāra writes «ཕྲིན་པ» (phrin pa), the Four Tantras and Gtsang sman «ཕོ་ཉ» (pho nya); in the Siddhasāra's volume «ཕྲིན་པ» stands in chapter 5 only (pp. 73 and 76).

What the three share: a messenger sorted by who he is and how he comes, and the physician's own setting out as part of the examination; the lists have common figures (a staff or weapon in hand, old and red clothes, oil on the body, a woman or eunuch, riding a donkey, a camel or a buffalo; the good lists have full vessels, flowers or garlands, a banner). What differs: only Gtsang sman puts the physician's own dream first, and only the Four Tantras tie the dream to the dawn.
