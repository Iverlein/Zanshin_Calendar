# The Garland is the Vajrāvalī; its remedy of a bad dream is the whirling mantra (RD373)

The Garland of the Sngags rim chen mo is the Vajrāvalī: the rite of the pupil's staying in the print's [p229]-[p231] runs in the order that Tsong kha pa follows, the verse that rouses joy, the explanation of "being born", the whirling mantra (འཁྱིལ་བ་སྔགས, 'khyil ba'i sngags) for guarding, the giving of it for recitation, "tell me the dream at dawn", the lion's posture, the teacher's own guarding in the mandala house, the torma at dawn, the question about dreams, and bad dreams marked by falling from a mountain guarded with the whirling mantra (vjv:p230-231). The sentence the Sngags rim chen mo gives as the Garland's (the Great Vehicle itself "greatly arising") stands there. So for the bad dream the Garland's remedy is the whirling mantra, the amṛta whirling mantra of the Sngags rim chen mo; the Garland names no pacifying fire offering and no watch, no day.

The example of a bad dream that the Sngags rim chen mo gives, falling from a mountain, is the Garland's (the OCR's «ཨ་རི་ལ་ལྷུང་བ» for «རི་ལས་ལྷུང་བ» is a doubt). The other points the Sngags rim chen mo credits to the Garland (the three ways of raising the god into the sky, snr:p369-370) are in a part of the rite not read in this reading. The text is an OCR etext with stray marks and an interlinear note mixed into the lines; the etext was read, not a scan. The Derge print of the Vajrāvalī is the etext VE23703_1391 (found 2026-11-13, q1062): it has the same sentence and reads «རི་ལ་ལྷུང་བ» at the doubt, without the ཨ (vjv:p230-231b).
