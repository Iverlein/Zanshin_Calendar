# The grave, the slayer and Wangkhyi's day-time animal are the same four animals: ox, sheep, dragon, dog

Wangkhyi's four day-time animals on p. 210 (ox for the last month of summer, sheep for winter, dragon for autumn, dog for spring) are the animals opposite the last months' own (sheep, ox, dog, dragon months) in the circle of twelve, the four «གཤེད» (gshed, slayer) animals: ox against sheep, dragon against dog. The light-clearer of p. 219 stands for the last months «ཡར་གྱི་གཤེད་ལ», on the upper slayer, which so is the same four animals; and the grave («དུར») of the first months (tiger, snake, monkey, pig) is, by either count the sources give, one of the same four: the triads of WB's ch. 23 (vol. 1, p. 373) give dog, ox, dragon, sheep for tiger, snake, monkey, pig; the element rule of Lha mo tshul khrims gives sheep, dog, ox, dragon. Both counts name the same set, so the four animals, ox, sheep, dragon and dog, serve as the grave, the slayer and Wangkhyi's day throughout, and only their assignment to the first months differs; ch. 31 does not say which. This is a reading from the circle, not a statement of any text; it narrows questions 28, 443 and 444 to the choice between the two assignments, and gives for question 29 the ox day as the day the animal of the last month's slayer falls on (Wangkhyi's «གནས་པའི་ཉི་དུས» the narrowest). Whether the ox's day and hour must coincide (445, 448) no text separates, as RD72 and RD76 found.
