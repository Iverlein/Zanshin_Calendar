# P. 99: the four slayers' directions, the corpse's facing and «རྨེ་བདུན»

On the Gser thang reprint (img. 105–106, pp. 99, ll. 8 and 25–26; 2026-11-01) the lines read as the 1996 setting does: «གཤེད་བཞི་ཤར་དུ་མི་གཏོང་ངོ» (l. 8), «སྤྲ་སྒོ་རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས་ནི» and «རྨེ་བདུན་ཤར་བའི་གདོང་ལ་འཛེམ» (ll. 25–26). Before the line stand the slayer directions by year: horse and snake north, bird and monkey south, tiger and hare west, mouse and pig the corner, so «the four slayers» are these four pairs' directions, and the unnamed side is the east; «ཤར་དུ» is then at best "to the east" as the direction left over, which this page does not settle. In ll. 25–26 the corpse's facing is given by the pair of trigrams («ལིར་ཤི་ཁེན་དང་ཁེན་ཤི་ལི», "died on li, khen; died on khen, li"), each set toward its own enemy; the next lines let the corpse face where the guest looks, and the page ends the sentence with «རྨེ་བདུན་ཤར་བའི་གདོང་ལ་འཛེམ» ("beware the face where the rme bdun rises"). «རྨེ» was read so on the reprint; whether it stands for «སྨེ» (the seven of the mewa) is likely but the page does not say. «སྤྲ་སྒོ» (l. 25) is read so on the page and found in no dictionary consulted; unexplained.
