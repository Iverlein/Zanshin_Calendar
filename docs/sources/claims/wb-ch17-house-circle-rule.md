# Ch. 17: the rule for the sun's house-circle and the tithi-circle

The rule for the sun's house-circle (wb.1:p190): the circle is 360 ("sky, season, fire"). Set it down in two places; multiply the lower by 2 and divide by 65, and join the quotient to the upper: that is the day (zhag po). Multiply the remainder by 60 and divide by 65: that is the hour (chu tshod). The hour, 60, 6 and 13 are multiplied in turn, each with its part put in, and the tithi-circle is made. The tithi-circle's place of days is multiplied by 60, the hours put in, and the chain 60, 6, 13 repeated; the sum is multiplied by 707, giving 73,668,268,800 (read on the scan against the divisibility of the number, 104,198,400 times 707). Of three places set down, the lower is divided by 707, the middle takes what 64 gains, and from the upper the remainder 72,515,574,000 (equal to the first number less 708 times 1,628,100) is subtracted; the rest, divided by 707 and 13 and then by 6 and 60, gives the breaths, the chu srang and the hours, and the wealth is the place of the day. By it the solar day's circle is made. This is the plain form of the rule; the variations and the planets' versions follow on pp. 191-196.
