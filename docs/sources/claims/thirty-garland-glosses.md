# The thirty's rare words in the Garland of Stars and the dictionaries

# The thirty's rare words in the *Garland of Stars* and the dictionaries

What the *Garland* (KD vol. 3, etext only) and the dictionaries (Steinert's 64) give for words that question 305 and 391 left unexplained; none is a full answer.

- **«བསྟན་མ»** (bstan ma, p. 462, the bride's direction): the twelve female mountain spirits of Tibet (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, Valby: "furies, female subterranean spirits"); the *Garland* says «བསྟན་མ་བུ་ཚའི་བཤན་པ་ཡིན» (p. 644), "the bstan ma is the sons' slayer (བཤན་པ, bshan pa, butcher)". So the black reading is that the bride's road runs into the nose of a bstan ma, and the harm is to her and her sons.
- **«རོལ་པོ་ཁ་ནག་གཉན»** (p. 461): the *Garland* has «རོལ་པོ་ཁ་ནག་དུས་གཉན་པས། ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་ལ་སོགས་བྱ་བ་གཅེས» (p. 537), "the rol po kha nag, the gnyan (གཉན, gnyan, a class of harming spirit) of the times: do the long-life rite", and lists it again among the gnyan of the times whose harm is a sheep's butting, to be avoided (p. 667, with an editor's «གསུམ་པ ༡༦»). So it is a time-gnyan, not a place.
- **«ཁ་ལན»** (p. 461): the *Garland* has it in the same point's fifth and sixth checks, «ཁ་ལན་མ་ཆགས་ས་རུ་ནི། དྲོ་སྟེར་མི་ཡོང་བཟང་བ་ཡིན» (p. 537); the dictionaries have «ཁ་ལེན» (kha len, acceptance of an invitation; cattle taking in) but not «ཁ་ལན»; the sense in the check is not clear.
- **«དམ་སྲི»** (dam sri): the samaya-breakers' demons; **«གྲེ་བོ»** (gre bo): a kind of male demon; **«རྒྱལ་བསེན»** (rgyal bsen): male and female noxious spirits; **«ནལ་མ»** (nal ma): the bra ma tree, or a woman who has lived with her brother; **«སླ་ང»** (sla nga): a roasting pan; **«ཉེ་འུ»** (nye'u): a small fish or a transgressor; **«མིང་སྟོན»** (ming ston): the feast of a child's naming; **«དམུ་ཡབ»** (dmu yab): one proud and ill-behaved in youth; all as the dictionaries give them, none checked in the point's own context.
- **«ཐེའུ་བསེན», «ཐེལ་འགོང», «རྒྱལ་བསེན», «གུང་ཕུར»** stand in the *Garland* in the same points as in WB (printed pp. 523, 529, 531) and are used as beings and rites known to its reader, without a gloss.
- **Not found** in the dictionaries: ja gshang, sngo thul, glong rgyas, yos ston, 'brang ma, da pa sri, thel 'gong, g.yo 'khrug, the'u bsen, gung phur, hal khyi (the *Garland* lists it as bad for all, p. 644).
