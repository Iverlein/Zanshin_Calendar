# The corpse's posture and the omens of mountain, fire and water

The small strikes read at the funeral (WB vol. 2, pp. 93–94; the notes, pp. 510–511, read on the scan, number thirteen postures): the corpse's posture, good stretched on its back with the hands extended, middling with the hands under the chest or crossed behind while sitting, bad with the head north, hands crossed behind, hands over the chest, the mouth open (wealth), an eye open (the right for men, the left for women), the eyelids open or shut, the knees stretched with the feet bent, the head to a corner; no bad posture, the deciding pebble white. The mountain ground (21 omens): square, round or crescent ground, white, yellow or green, a soft meadow good; ravines like sprouts or fingers, triangular or torn ground, hanging cliffs, blue-black, speckled or red earth, slate caves bad. The fire (33 omens): white, yellow, red or blue blaze and the number of roots and tips good; heavy smoke, one-sided or double blaze, black or blue bulges bad; the unburnt part shows what the dead clings to (skin the fields, flesh the house, bones wealth, heart the sons, lungs the elders, entrails the women, liver jewels, head the teacher, upper body guests, lower body the spouse), and a piece of its cloth is burnt. The water (12 omens): stopping in the shallows, a repetition; vanishing in a deep lake or reaching a circling lake, good; two waters, quarrels; three, good; tied to a stone, staying good; face up or looking back bad, face down middling, looking away good. These are observed, not reckoned.
