# p. 71: the dog day's puppy and the auspicious little song at death

Two stacks of p. 71 read on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 077) at 3× to 6×, with the etexts of the other prints beside. The dog day's former life, l. 16, is «ཁྱི་གུ», a puppy: the first letter is the same shape as the «ཁྱི» of the line's own «ཁྱི་ཡི་ཉི་མར»; the Gser thang etext has «ཚེ་སྔོན་ཁྱི་གུ་ཞིག་ཏུ་སྐྱེས» and BDRC's full-text search finds the same words in a second print of WB (MW2PD17386, search-only) and none outside WB. So the line is not a repeat of the bird day's: the former life differs (a goose for the bird, a puppy for the dog). The next-life line, «ཕྱི་མ་ནུབ་ཕྱོགས་བུ་སྤུན་ནི། །གསུམ་གྱི་བར་པ་ཞིག་ཏུ་སྐྱེ», is carved identically on both days, as seen on the scan (l. 15 and l. 16, the same words one under the other), so the repeat is the print's, not a misreading. The auspicious thing taken at death, l. 26, is «གླུ་ཆུང», a little song: a stack of ག with ལ and the vowel u, and BDRC's full-text search finds «བཀྲ་ཤིས་གླུ་ཆུང» in the 1996 etext, the Gser thang etext, the Zhol etext (I1KG1710), the Bhutan collection's volume (I1KG11905), a second WB etext (MW2PD17386, search-only) and a manual that quotes the passage (MW26041, «dkar rtsis dang nag rtsis kyi gzhung», search-only); none has another letter. So «[མུ?]» was «གླུ»: the dying take, sing, an auspicious little song, with mouth shut and eyes closed.
