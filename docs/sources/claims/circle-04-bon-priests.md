# The circles: the fourth, the turquoise dragon, for the Bon priests

The fourth of ch. 27's thirteen circles (དཔྱད་ལྡིང, dpyad lding), from the turquoise dragon, is read for the Bon priests. Its starting point: the dragon of the mother element of the one reckoned (by the notes, the element of his power, claim circles-count), counted upward by the years he has lived; the year reached has its life force, body, power, luck and mewa fought by his own five, one pebble each, white or black by the rule of colour (claim circles-colour-rule).

What the five ask, in the text's words as we render them: counting upward from the dragon of the mother element of the one reckoned: whether the meteoric-iron coral has been carried off by the *btsa'* (rust, or a blight); whether the god-arrow is broken at the waist; whether the drum and cymbal have slipped from the hand; whether the Bon priest of fortune (ཕྱྭ་བོན, phywa bon) has slid from his seat; whether the cord of the thread-cross (རྒྱང་བུ, rgyang bu) is cut.

| Pebble | White | Black | Remedy for black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life force | the coral not carried off: the Bon line not cut, life long; the forefathers' good Bon line is held now and fortune gathers. | carried off: the line good but declining now; a small child would be at risk of death; the Bon line parted from fortune, at risk of losing its holder. | restore fortune (ཕྱྭ་གཡང, phywa g.yang), fulfilment and confession, diligent offerings to the gods and protectors. |
| Body | the white feathers of the god-arrow not broken at the waist: the line of the Bon of fortune longer than a river, the god-arrow with its silk, the Bon of fortune with its luck; descendants and line-holders unbroken. | broken: the Bon line short and without fortune; no descendants, the line at risk; sons, grandsons, clan all waste away. | restore the god-castle (གསས་མཁར, gsas mkhar), raise the body, fasten the wheel that holds the root of the generations. |
| Power | drum and cymbal not slipped from hand and seat: the Bon of fortune can protect with power, the power small but existence wide: good. | slipped from hand and from the throne's edge: bad talk and wrong thoughts come; the root and branch pledges are polluted, so sons, wealth and ornaments are lost and horses and livestock die. | fulfilling what is broken, stirring the pit (དོང་དཀྲུགས, dong dkrugs), the long-life rite, ransoming death, reciting the long-life sūtra, fierce feast and confession. |
| Luck | the god-Bon (the print's «ལྷ་བོན») firm on his seat: fame and disciples spread in ten directions, activity equal to the bounds of the sky. | the Shenrab king slides from his seat: though he summons the patron's fortune, his own fortune declines; envy between the protecting gods and the destroying spirits harms greatly; blame without fault, hostile talk. | the fierce three-headed turning back, and above all meeting and turning back the grave sri (དུར་སྲི, dur sri). |
| Mewa | the thread-cross's cord not cut: very good; the god-ground white, life and fortune summoned, the Bon line long and flourishing: good. | the cord cut: spells and pollution and the fault of the skull-cup come; great rise and decline in the Bon line; fearing bad conditions, a great turning back in the four directions and the stag's-horn thread-cross (ཤ་བའི་རུ་མདོས, sha ba'i ru mdos). | — |

No verdict is laid over a circle as a whole, in the text or in the notes (vol. 2, p. 533).
