# Marpa's own song has the saying of the three watches: dusk habit, midnight Māra's emanations, dawn the gods' prophecy

The saying the third Karmapa quotes in the words «Marpa said» (dusk the habit, midnight the power of spirits, dawn true) does stand in Marpa's own collected works, in a song of the Lotsāba about the dream in which Maitrīpa taught him, and its middle line differs. There it reads: at the evening (ནམ་སྲོད, nam srod) the dream arises from habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) and there is nothing to put weight on in it; at midnight (ནམ་གུང, nam gung) it shows the emanations of Māra (བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ, bdud kyi sprul pa) and one must not trust it; at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) the gods' prophecy, if great, is a wonder. So the Karmapa's «midnight dream is the power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang)» is a paraphrase of Marpa's line: the figure at midnight is Māra's work, not the work of the spirit class (གདོན, gdon). The song is in the three Lives of Marpa of the first volume ([p103], [p168], [p297], the last the Mthong ba don yod that BDRC ascribes to Gtsang smyon) and in the twenty-five great songs of the fifth volume ([p31]); BDRC's phrase search finds it in eleven places, which are prints and copies of these Lives and songs. The rule does not say that dusk dreams are worthless in themselves, only that no weight is laid on them; and the dawn line is the one a dream can be true.
