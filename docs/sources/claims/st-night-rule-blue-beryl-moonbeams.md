# The Blue Beryl and the Moonbeams on «མཚན་སྟོད» in ch. 7: dusk, and up to midnight

Ch. 7's night rule «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད་ངས་འབྲས་བུ་མེད། ཐོ་རངས་མཐོང་དང་གསལ་ན་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན» (gt:i42; claim st-dream-night-first-part-dawn) in the Blue Beryl and the Moonbeams, BDRC etexts read 2026-11-10 (etext only).

The Blue Beryl (VEIE0OPICF5AF372_I1PD411, its part on the Explanatory Tantra, ch. 7; etext pp. 209 and 216, printed pp. 181 and 188). Two places. (1) Etext p. 209, on the Eight Limbs' verse «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་འབྲས་བུ་རིང»: མཚན་སྟོད, the first part of the night (mtshan stod), is glossed «up to midnight» (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད, nam phyed yan chad); a dream dreamt so early has its fruit far off, and what is seen is for the most part small and weak. It goes on with the dawn dream (seen when the cattle are let out to graze, with no sleep after it and no unfitting talk to cut it: great fruit that very day) and the giving and burnt offering that make a bad dream's fruit small. This is the Moonbeams' sentence, below, in the Blue Beryl's words. (2) Etext p. 216, its own comment on the Four Tantras' verse, after the six kinds of dream (seen, heard, felt, a prayer, a sign of accomplishment, born of illness): whichever of these is dreamt in the first part of the night, that is at dusk (མཚན་སྟོད་དེ་སྲོད་ལ, mtshan stod de srod la), sleep goes on unbroken after it and it is forgotten (the spelling བརྗེད་ངས, as on the prints), so no fruit comes, good or bad; midnight is of the same kind; a dream of any sort seen at the close of dawn, which sleep does not cut again and which stays clear in the mind, brings its fruit for certain. So for the Blue Beryl མཚན་སྟོད་ is the early part of the night, dusk, with midnight alike; in the Eight Limbs' verse the time up to midnight. It cites the Moonbeams by name on the causes of the sick man's dreams (p. 208) and on the good dreams (p. 217).

The Moonbeams (VE23703_1517, etext pp. 160–161): it comments on the Eight Limbs' verse «མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་འབྲས་བུ་རིང། མཐོང་བ་ཆུང་ངུ་བྱེད་པ་ཡིན» and says that a dream dreamt up to the half of the first part of the night (མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད, mtshan stod phyed pa yan chad) has its fruit far off, and what is seen is for the most part small and weak; this is the sentence Byang pa quotes (bjp:p183). The Four Tantras' verse does not stand in it, in either spelling, བརྗེད་ངས or བརྗེད་ངེས. Before the verse it says that the first five kinds of dream, a dream of one's own nature, the day's dream that is forgotten, and the very long and the very short have no fruit; after it, the dawn dream seen with no sleep and no unfitting talk between gives its great fruit that day, and giving and burnt offering make a bad dream's fruit small.

Which part of the night is མཚན་སྟོད་: the Moonbeams, for the Eight Limbs, the first half of the first part; the Blue Beryl, in the Eight Limbs' verse, the time up to midnight and, in the Four Tantras' verse, dusk with midnight alike. Neither gives an hour or a count of days.
