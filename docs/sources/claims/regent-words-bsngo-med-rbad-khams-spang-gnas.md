# «བསྔོ་མེད», «རྦད་ཁམ» and «སྤང་གནས» in the regent's replies 138, 140 and 141

Three words of the regent's replies that the pool had not read, with the context that glosses each.

«དཀར་ནག་བསྔོ་མེད» (reply 138, ys:i496, scan): the regent says that in the plank-tree (ཐང་ཤིང, thang shing) a speckled result is "white and black without apportioning, a bad result" (དཀར་ནག་བསྔོ་མེད་ངན་པའི་འབྲས་བུ), while in the obstacle reckoning a speckled pebble must be shown with a result like the son meeting. It is the short form of WB's «དཀར་ནག་བསྔོ་རུ་མ་བཏུབ» ([[plank-tree-bsngo-ru]]): white and black mixed so that neither can be assigned. The regent's own gloss is "bad".

«རྦད་ཁམ» (reply 140, ys:i497-498): "if one says, 'cleared so, it is understood without difficulty, so no «རྦད་ཁམ» is needed': although much pride of the strong may arise from this, the earlier [doubts] are many" and were cleared by the Tibetan scholars' treatises. The same word stands in the questioner's own request 94 (ys:i087, machine) with a final ས: «ང་མཁས་ང་བཙུན་གྱི་རྦད་ཁམས་སྒྲོག», "proclaim a swagger of 'I am learned, I am venerable'". So it is a boast or bluster, something one proclaims, and the reply says none is needed, since the question is answered. Not found in a dictionary (none opened).

«སྤང་གནས་ཀྱི་རྩིས་ལོག་མིན་པ» (reply 141, ys:i498-499): "since the sixty *khog pa* and the rest are not a wrong reckoning of what is to be rejected, why state them apart?" The reply goes on to say that they are not «སྤང་བྱ་དངོས», things to be given up in themselves, and are set apart so that the essentials of each reckoning stay unmixed. So «སྤང་གནས», a ground of rejection, is the same as «སྤང་བྱ», what is to be rejected: the clause says the small reckonings are not false ones.
