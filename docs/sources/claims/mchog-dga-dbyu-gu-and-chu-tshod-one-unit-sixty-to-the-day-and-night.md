# In the Mchog dga' the staff (dbyu gu) and the water-hour (chu tshod) are one unit, 60 to the day-and-night, and the third of the five places carries both names (q1044)

In the Mchog dga' (མཆོག་དགའ, Mchog dga') the staff (དབྱུ་གུ, dbyu gu) and the water-hour (ཆུ་ཚོད, chu tshod) are one unit under two names; the third of the five places is that unit. Evidence in the book itself:

- Ch. 1 (printed p. 32): six breaths (དབུགས) are a water-srang (ཆུ་སྲང), «sixty such water-srang are called a staff or one water-hour» (དབྱུ་གུའམ་ཆུ་ཚོད་གཅིག), and sixty such staffs one day-and-night; so 60 staffs = 60 water-hours to the day-and-night, five water-hours to each of the twelve junctions and seven and a half to a watch (kd.3:p32).
- Ch. 2 (printed pp. 120-122): the same chain of places is named by the staff on p. 120 (the instant, its part, the staff, the water-srang, the breath) and by the water-hour where it is worked: «what stands in the lower water-hours is put on top» and the divisor of 1800 is «the wheel of the water-hours» (kd.3:p121); the worked example has no «water-hour, water-srang and breaths» left after the parts (kd.3:p122), the three names the third, fourth and fifth places carry on p. 120 as «staff, water-srang, breath» (kd.3:p120). The third place is therefore both.
- The book also says that other counts of the day-and-night (twenty-four or sixteen junctions, four staffs, thirty-two water-hours, a hundred staffs) are found in the inner scriptures, and that this place follows the Kālacakra's sixty (kd.3:p32): a «staff» of four to the day-and-night is a different unit from this one, so the name «dbyu gu» is not always the water-hour; in the Mchog dga's own places it is.
