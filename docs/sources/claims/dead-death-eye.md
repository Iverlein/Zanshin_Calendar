# At whom the death's eye looks

The eleventh examination names whom the death's eye (ཤི་མིག, shi mig) looks at (WB vol. 2, p. 94; the notes, pp. 511–512, read on the scan). By aspect: the life force at the household lord and the monk, the body at the paternal kin, the power at the paternal aunt and the dough-maid, the luck at the maternal uncle, the clan element at the king's seat, the year at the *phywa* (ཕྱྭ) priest, the month at the counsellor, the day at the soldiers, the hour at the hunter, the planet at the household, the mansion at the villagers, the mewa at widows and orphans, the trigram at all. Its thirteen root pebbles fight the year's life force with the dead's life force, body, power, luck and clan, the four times' power, the planet's and mansion's element, the dead's trigram and mewa. The living it looks at: those of the dead's year animal, the dead's seventh, the four executioners upward, the triad downward, and those of the same trigram seat. The bird's eight places, by the trigram of death: *kham* the grandmother and elders, *khon* nephews, *li* fathers and uncles, *khen* daughters and middle women, *dwa* grandsons, *zin* young men, *gin* old women and daughters-in-law, *zon* sons-in-law and husbands; where the named relatives are not living, no harm. The joined hands (ཤི་གསོན་ལག་སྦྲེལ): a living person on the dead's trigram and mewa seat; rites cut it. The venomous year roots (iron-four, fire-eight, the six small whites, the four great executioners): the oldest living of such a year dying is bad for the young, the youngest dying bad for the old; these make the verdict black. The leftover pebble (རྡེལ་རོ, rdel ro) coming out is bad, with nine identifying pebbles for the relatives down and across; not coming out, good.
