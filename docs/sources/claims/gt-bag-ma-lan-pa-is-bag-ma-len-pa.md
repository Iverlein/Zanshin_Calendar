# བག་མ་ལན་པ in ch. 7's verse: taking a bride, lan pa a spelling of len pa

The words «བག་མ་ལན་པ» (bag ma lan pa) in the Drathang line of ch. 7 (RD271, 2026-11-09; dictionaries by Steinert's collection, BDRC etexts, no scan opened): the same as «བག་མ་ལེན་པ» (bag ma len pa), taking a bride; ལན་པ is a spelling of ལེན་པ.

**The dictionaries.** བག་མ (bag ma): a bride, a newly married wife (Rangjung Yeshe, Jim Valby, Ives Waldo, Dan Martin, Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs; the Dag tshig adds that the bridegroom's family's festival is the "bag ston"). The Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho gives the stock verbs of the bride: «བག་མ་གཏོང་བ» (to give a bride away) and «བག་མ་ལེན་པ» (to take a bride). ལེན་པ (len pa) is "to take, to receive" (all the dictionaries). ལན་པ (lan pa) is, in Ives Waldo and the Tshig mdzod chen mo, a cause, a fault, a reply, and does not fit; so the verb is len pa.

**In use.** The etexts also spell the verb ལན་པ in this very phrase: in the commentary on the Abhidharmakośa (BDRC, a text of the 'Phags pa'i gang zag series), «བག་མ་གཏོང་བ་དང་། བག་མ་ལན་པ་རྣམས་མེད་དེ» (there is no giving or taking of a bride in hell), where the printed page before it has «བག་མ་ལེན་པ»; and in the Kālacakra commentary, «བག་མ་ལན་པ་དང་ལག་པ་འཛིན་པ་ལ་སོགས་པའི་བྱ་བ» (the acts of taking a bride, taking the hand and the like). BDRC's search «བག་མ་ལན་པ» has these two hits, so the spelling is a known one for the same act. In ch. 7 the commentaries have ལེན་པ (Byang pa, Khro ru, the Drikung) and the Drathang's ལན་པ is that spelling; the machine's «ལན» need not be a misreading (`gt:i42-43`).

**The sense.** Taking a bride, as a dream of the dying: one of the images of the list.
