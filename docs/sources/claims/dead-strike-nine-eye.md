# The nine-eye strike by the dead's age

The nine-eye strike counts the dead's age in nines (WB vol. 2, pp. 87–88): ages 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63, 72 and 81 strike, a man's corpse repeating six times, a woman's three, or five. Each age has its trigrams, its count of further deaths, who leads the corpse and its rite: 9 on *li* and *kham*, four corpses (eight beyond), burn it and cast the fires south, give an old horse and ox; 18 dying on *khen* or *khon*, rough for seven, no feast for three years, the younger of an old one and a girl leads; 27 dying on *dwa*, nine black rites with a Bon priest and a monk; 36 on *khen* or *khon*, four or eight, an old woman leads, the corpse covered from the sky, buried upright or burnt facing east; 45 on *kham* and *li*, bad for three, five, eleven, no feast for three years, the corpse cast into the river; 54 on *gin* and *zon*, twelve die, the elder child leads, monks not Bon priests; 63 on *dwa* or *zin*, three or seven, a grown man leads, the corpse boxed, a mantra feast; 72 on *gin* or *zon*, rough for four, eight, twelve, a *mtshe ma* (མཚེ་མ) leads; 81 on *li* and *kham*, nine die, a harnessed horse and four sheep. The notes give nine identifying pebbles and the deciding pebble (p. 506, machine-read). Whether the age is the dead's year of age or full years is not said here.
