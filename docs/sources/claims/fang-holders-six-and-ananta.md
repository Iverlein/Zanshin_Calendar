# The six fang-holders are the birth and work places and four more places counted from the birth; Ananta stands apart

The six fang-holders (མཆེ་བ་དྲུག་འཛིན, mche ba drug 'dzin) of WB vol. 2, pp. 305–306 are named in a KD commentary on the *dbyangs 'char* (kd.3:p256-258; the page heading reads "Dbyangs 'char'i rgyus 'grel Mchog dga'", chapter 5): counting from the mansion of the person's name-star or birth-star as the birth place (སྐྱེ་ས, skye sa), the six are the birth place; the work place (ལས་ཀྱི་ས, las kyi sa), the tenth mansion from it; Rnam 'gyes, the sixteenth foot; Rgya can, the eighteenth foot, called the goddess; Pinaśaka, the twenty-third; Yid can, the twenty-fifth. The fierce planet's fang or face falling on any of them is the harm, and so the reckoning is called "the six fang-holders". Ananta, the nineteenth foot, is added "on top" and is not counted among the six; some say it is reckoned for a king alone, the commentator thinks it may serve others as well.

WB's remedies stand under seven heads in this order: the birth place, the work place, Rnam dgyes, Rgya chen, Ananta, Pinaśaka, Yid can: the six holders and Ananta between. The commentator rejects another view that took the six to be the two fangs of Rāhu's mouth and the two of the tail with their faces. The count mixes mansions and feet (a mansion has four feet on the wheel of eighty-one); which is meant at each place is as the commentary has it above.
