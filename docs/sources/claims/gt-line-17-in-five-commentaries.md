# Ch. 7's dream verse, line 17 in five commentaries: four spell it mi bzang ba, Rin spungs pa mi zad pa, none mi bzad pa

Five commentaries of the Four Tantras' tradition were opened as BDRC etexts (2026-11-10, RD302) to see how each takes up ch. 7's bad-dream verse at its line 17, the line the prints split on (claim gt-dream-verse-chag-po-ri-prints: «མི་བཟང་བ» on four prints, «མི་བཟད་པ» on four). The commentaries do not split the way the prints do: four spell it «མི་བཟང་བ» and one «མི་ཟད་པ»; none has «མི་བཟད་པ» as the Tantra's line. The page numbers are those of the etext.

**Count by commentary, kept apart from the count by print.** «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ», rmi lam mi bzang ba, bad dreams: Skyem pa Tshe dbang (Nyi ma, BDRC I4711, etext p. 189), Zur mkhar ba (Mes po'i zhal lung, I8LS19191, p. 355), the Blue Beryl (I1PD411, p. 215), the Drikung dka' mchan (BDK, I1KG24731, p. 80). «རྨི་ལམ་མི་ཟད་པ», rmi lam mi zad pa: Rin spungs pa's Dri med kun gsal (I4707, etext pp. 236-237). «མི་བཟད་པ»: nobody as the line. The Zur mkhar ba and the Blue Beryl are not two witnesses: the Blue Beryl's passage on this verse follows Zur mkhar ba's (the same sentences in the same order in the stretch read, pp. 355-356 and 215), so the four of «མི་བཟང་བ» are three commentators' texts, one of them copied.

**What each does with the line.**
- Skyem pa Tshe dbang, p. 189: heads the comment with the verse's two lines as a lemma, «དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ་ རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ། ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག་ནད་ཀྱི་བཀག་ཕྱིར་མཐོང་།», and glosses them: the bad dreams other than the ones listed above, which "etc." takes in, are seen because the passage of the mind's entry (yid 'jug bu ga) is blocked by illness. The lemma is the clearest quotation of the verse's line 17 in the five: «མི་བཟང་བ».
- Rin spungs pa, pp. 236-237: the lemma is «དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ་རྨི་ལམ་མི་ཟད་པ། ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག་ནད་ཀྱིས་བཀག་ཕྱིར་མཐོང་།», the page turns after «མི་ཟད་» and the next begins «པ།»; his gloss is "the bad dreams of that kind that go on without end (ཟད་མི་ཤེས་པ)". He adds a quotation from the Moonbeams (Zla ba'i 'od zer) on the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya's Body chapter (ch. 6) whose wording is «ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག་བཀག་པའི་ཕྱིར། མི་ཟད་རྨི་ལམ་མཐོང་གྱུར་།», so «མི་ཟད་» (mi zad) stands in that Indian text's commentary too: his spelling may come from there, not from a copy of the Tantra. The earlier claim that Rin spungs pa has «མི་ཟད་པ» (the Drathang passage's note) stands, and now stands for the lemma's own spelling.
- Zur mkhar ba, p. 355: no lemma of the verse's line. He glosses «དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ» as the bad dream kinds "of the sort that is «མི་བཟད་པ»" (མི་བཟད་པ, "unbearable": frightening and ill-omened), and two sentences on, in his own commentary, writes «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ་དེ་ལྟ་བུ» (such a bad dream) for the sick, with the sentence "and for the one without illness, to dream them is not auspicious". So in one passage he has «མི་བཟད་པ» as a gloss and «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ» as the echo of the line; the first is an adjective of his own for the dream, and the second is the Tantra's wording.
- The Blue Beryl, p. 215: the same two uses, in the same order («རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་རྣམ་པ་མི་བཟད་པ་དེ» and, after the cause of the sick man's dream, «རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ་ལྟ་བུ»; the etext has a space inside «མི་བཟང་ བ»).
- The Drikung dka' mchan, p. 80: the verse's lines are worked into the commentary, and «དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ་རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ་རྨི་བའི་རྒྱུ་མཚན་ནི་…» (the cause of seeing such bad dreams: the passages of the mind's entry in the heart blocked by illness) has «མི་བཟང་བ».

**What this adds to the prints' count.** The commentators' lemma fixes the sense only: «མི་བཟང་བ», "not good", reads as the plain "bad dreams"; «མི་ཟད་པ» as "unending". Rin spungs pa's spelling stands alone among the five, and has a source in the Moonbeams; with Skyem pa Tshe dbang's lemma and the Drikung, the majority of the commentaries agree with the Drathang's reading. No commentary read gives «མི་བཟད་པ» as the Tantra's line, so the four prints that read «མི་བཟད་པ» (I3CN1711, I3CN1696, I5910, I5907) do not find a witness in these five.

**Verse-line search by the animals.** The verse's lines of the animals riding (སྤྲེའུ་སྟག, the monkey, the tiger) stand in the verse's third part, not as a quoted line of the Tantra in Zur mkhar ba, the Blue Beryl or Skyem pa Tshe dbang (searched with spaces and tsheg stripped: not found in any of the three); they stand in the Drikung (p. 80) and in Rin spungs pa (p. 234). The Blue Beryl quotes the Moonbeams' verse on the same dreams («བོང་བུ་རྔ་མོ་བྱི་ལ་སྤྲེའུ། སེང་གེ་སྟག་དང་ཁྭ་དག་དང་», Blue Beryl p. 214), another verse than the Tantra's.
