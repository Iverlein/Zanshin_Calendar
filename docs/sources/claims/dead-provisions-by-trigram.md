# The corpse's provisions by the trigram of death

The provisions laid with a corpse (རོ་རྫོངས, ro rdzongs) are chosen by the trigram the death fell on (WB vol. 2, p. 75). Each trigram has its tree, its bird and its goods: *li* rose wood, magpie, rice, turquoise, hat, felt, head meat and butter broth; *khon* willow, the *thi ba* (བྱ་ཐི་བ) bird, sesame, a warming medicine; *dwa* the *tshar* (ཚར་ཤིང) wood, a feather of the *'jol mo* (འཇོལ་མོ), a donkey's cloth, blood, the left rib; *khen* *sbyar pa* (སྦྱར་པ) wood, crane, buckwheat, dog and marmot meat, the left leg, cloth boots; *kham* *spen* (སྤེན་ཤིང) wood, crow, peas, buckwheat, dried meat, goat-hair cloth, ox, pig and fish meat, entrails, a short coat; *gin* juniper, owl, brocade, wheat, the right leg; *zin* *stag* (སྟག་ཤིང) wood, parrot, the *skyur* rib (སྐྱུར་རྩིབ), a belt; *zon* spruce, hoopoe, *phug dar* (ཕུག་དར) silk, dzo meat. The dead's share of goods goes as provisions on whichever trigram, matched to the grave's account. Several items are not identified (the corpus passage gives them as printed).
