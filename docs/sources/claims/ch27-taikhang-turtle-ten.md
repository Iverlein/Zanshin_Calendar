# Ch. 27: the turtle's wood verse carves ten srin po on the Taikhang and the Zhol print

In the golden turtle's wood verse (WB vol. 2, p. 20, l. 14), the Taikhang reproduction (I5878 img. 36, block folio 26, line 4, crop enlarged 4 times) and the Zhol print (img. 654, line 4, Yigdzin-1) both carry «ཤིང་གླང་མི་ལོ་ཤིང་ལུག་པ། །ལྷག་མ་བཅུ་པོ་སྲིན་པོའི་ལོ»: the wood sheep's last syllable is པ, and the remainder is ten (བཅུ), not nine. The syllable is «བཅུ» in Yigdzin-1 on both prints, in agy on a crop (the prefix བ and ཅ with ུ), and by the eye a stack with ུ that is not «དགུ».

So the printed ten stands on the Taikhang and the Zhol print as on the 1996 print and the reprint; the sum of sixty that asks for nine (claim ch27-turtle-wood-sheep-and-element) rests on no print read. Whether the *srin po* of the wood verse are ten and the wood sheep a class year of its own, or the verse's count is a slip carried by every print, is left to a reader of the tradition (question 364).
