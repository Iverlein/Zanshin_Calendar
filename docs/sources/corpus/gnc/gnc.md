The text carries on from p. 558: the "new prophecy" of the examination of dreams, the wheel drawn at the pillow.

The wheel (continued). Round the lotus at its centre (the end of p. 558 gives its first ten spokes): a ring of ten spokes with the mantra «oṃ dha ha ri ha na ston cig svāhā», a second ring of twenty lotus petals with a mantra asking that the matter be shown clearly, and a ring of eight tongues of a mountain-fire with a third mantra; "so one draws" (the colours are not given by the text here). The substance: the flesh of a vulture's head, best if there is any, otherwise dry vulture flesh, is smeared on; the centre is blue, the spokes green, the spoke-marks yellow, the rim of the petals blue and the fire red. This wheel is laid as the pillow-head.

Beside the sleeper. On the right of the sleeping place a paper roll, a span high, with a bird's beak drawn on it as long as the roll, a mantra inside the beak (oṃ ve ta li ston ston ...), the beak made blue with powder of any bird's beak; the roll is set with the beak's tip toward the right. On the left a second roll, a finger's breadth across, with the mantra «oṃ sa pob se phob su su phob da phob cig» written inside, set to the left.

The wheel again, its centre. At its centre a vulture, white in the middle; round it eight spokes the eight things by direction: east a bird's head-tuft (khu byug), south a human hand, west a human head, north a bow and arrow, south-east a bird's wing-plate, south-west a bird's head, north-west a vase, north-east a ritual dagger; and in the spoke-marks: east a white oṃ, south a peacock's feather-eye, west a dot (thig le), north a mirror, south-east a gold ear-ring, south-west a parasol, north-west a jewel, north-east fire-water. On the rim the mantra against what harms, blurs or dims the examination of the dream: all harmful demons and obstructors are to be bound ("stambhaya"), none to stand up for a thousand aeons; ending in repeated "phaṭ". A black mountain-fire with a mantra-head turned inward is drawn; the substance is the powder of iron, "lir" and "gan", three kinds; many mats are laid on it. One washes the five limbs with one's own water; recites the mantra «oṃ vajra se lwa li ...» before sleep, whatever number comes, even ten thousand is not too many. Dry vulture-flesh powder with the mantra is rubbed a little on the five limbs, the mantra-water drunk and rubbed on the five limbs; then the mantra is recited until sleep. "Through this the dream becomes clear."

Colophon: prophesied by the dakini Ye shes mtsho rgyal on the fourteenth night of the Pleiades month (smin drug zla ba). Then "maṅgalaṃ", and a new text begins, a divination of clairvoyance from the Dpal rno bkra (mo mngon shes 'dra ma), starting with a mo divination done with a small counter or grains (machine reading, doubtful); not read further.

No lunar day: the text prescribes none for the rite, only the sleep after the washing and the recitation until sleep comes; the fourteenth night belongs to the colophon's date of the prophecy. As the two earlier colophons (pp. 557-558) likewise give dates of speaking, the dates are all of revelation.
