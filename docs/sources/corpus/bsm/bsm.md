## <a id="bsm-p8-12"></a>bsm:p8-12 Ch. 2: the dream as a skill of the astrologer; no chapter on dreams

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> (English only, N. Chidambaram Iyer's translation, 1884; the Sanskrit was not searched.)
> Ch. 2, on the astrologer: "He must be able to interpret natural gestures and dreams" (with Iyer's note that in war the king's minister, astrologer and priest were told to sleep in the temple and their dreams were read); and v. 23: "The evils of bad dreams, of sad thoughts, of ill omens and of evil deeds and the like will vanish immediately [when] one hears of the moon's motion among the stars."

Bṛhatsaṃhitā, ch. 2 (the astrologer's qualities), pp. 8 and 12 of Iyer's translation.

The whole text layer of the translation (about 980,000 characters) was searched for dream and svapna: the work has no chapter on dreams, and reading dreams appears only as a skill the king's astrologer must have (ch. 2), as an evil that the astrologer's reckoning of the moon's course removes (ch. 2 v. 23), and in the chapter Iyer numbers XLVII (the king's bath at Puṣya), where Varāhamihira says that the good or bad dreams dreamt on the night spent at the spot "are given in my work on Yoga Yatra" (v. 22; the page was not noted). Nothing in it ties a dream to a watch, a lunar day, a weekday, a month or a season.
