## <a id="yyt-p28-29"></a>yyt:p28-29 Yogayātrā 2.23–24: the king hears the tithi, nakṣatra and weekday, and bad dreams are destroyed

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> प्रणम्य देवान् स्वगुरूंश्च पूर्वं दत्त्वा च गां वत्सयुतां द्विजाय ।
> दृष्ट्वा मुखं सर्पिषि दर्पणे वा नक्षत्रमादौ शृणुयात् तिथिं च ॥ २३ ॥
> श्रुत्वा तिथिं भं ग्रहवासरं च प्राप्नोति धर्मार्थयशांसि सौख्यम् ।
> आरोग्यमायुर्विजयं सुतांश्च दुःस्वप्नघातं प्रियतां च लोके ॥ २४ ॥

*Yogayātrā* ch. 2 (*Ācārādhyāya*, the king's conduct), verses 23–24, pp. 28–29 of Dvivedi's edition (the running head of the page with v. 24 reads "ācārādhyāyaḥ 29"); the lines above are the words as Weber prints them in Roman transliteration in *Indische Studien* 10 (1868), p. 170 ("Die Yogayātrā 2, 18–26"), checked against the Devanagari layer of Dvivedi's edition, which reads the last word of v. 24 "duḥsvapna-jātaṃ" where Weber's manuscripts A and B give "-ghātaṃ" (OCR can take घा for जा; the commentary glosses the fruit as the removal of bad dreams).

In our words: on the morning of the march, after the cleaning of the teeth, the king bows to the gods and his teachers, gives a cow with her calf to a brahman, looks at his face in clarified butter or in a mirror, and first hears the *nakṣatra* and the *tithi* read; having heard the *tithi*, the *nakṣatra* and the weekday of the planet (*graha-vāsara*, so the three parts of the almanac with the day-lord) he gains right, wealth and fame, happiness, health, long life, victory, sons, the destruction of bad dreams (*duḥsvapna-ghāta*) and the love of the world. Dvivedi's commentator says the five limbs of the almanac are meant and that the hearing is the king's.

So the verse makes the reading of the *tithi*, nakṣatra and weekday itself a cause of the end of bad dreams; it does not name the day of a dream or let the *tithi* of a dream decide its fruit. Negelein's gloss (neg:p35), that the astrologer fixes the date, star and weekday "of the dreams", is not what the verse says. The other mentions of dreams in the work are those recorded at yyt:p188.

## <a id="yyt-p188"></a>yyt:p188 Ch. 13, the closing verse: the omens of departure hold in dreams

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> yān yātra maṅgalāmaṅgalāni nirgacchatāṃ pradiṣṭāni |
> svapneṣv apy etāni śubhāśubhāni [vidyāt …]
> (the end of the last verse is garbled in the text layer; the commentary's gloss begins "yāny atra prāsthānikādhyāye …")

Yogayātrā ch. 13 (Prāsthānikādhyāya, on the omens at departure), the closing verse, about p. 188 of Dvivedi's edition.

The verse says that the good and bad omens told in this chapter for those going out hold also in dreams. The Devanagari text layer of the whole edition (about 280,000 characters) was searched for svapna: the other hits are mentions of sleep or dream as a sign in the army and among the king's animals and the commentary's glosses. There is no chapter on dreams and no time of a dream's fruit; the Bṛhatsaṃhitā (ch. 47) points here for the reading of dreams, and what it points to is this one verse.
