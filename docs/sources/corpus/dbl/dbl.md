Homage to the king of the Śākyas. Of the many views on the years of his life, this one for now: on the fifteenth of the month of sa ga (ས་ག, sa ga) of the earth sheep year he entered the womb of Māyā; on the fifteenth of the waxing half of the month of mchu (མཆུ, mchu) of the iron monkey year, which had a leap month of chu stod (ཆུ་སྟོད, chu stod), he was born; twenty-nine years he stayed in the palace; on the eighth of sa ga of the earth dog year he left it and went forth; after six years of austerities, at dawn on the fifteenth of sa ga of the male wood horse year, he awoke fully; at the end of seven weeks, on the fourth of the waxing half of chu stod, he turned the wheel of Dharma; having worked for those to be tamed for forty-six years, at eighty-one, on the full moon of sa ga of the iron dragon year, he passed beyond sorrow.

So the great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) of his wonderful deeds are these: the waxing half of the second month (a doubt, as read), the great miracles; the fifteenth of the fourth, the full awakening; the fourth of the sixth, the turning of the wheel; the twenty-second of the ninth, the descent from the gods: the four great festivals known to all. The fifteenth of sa ga holds the entry into the womb, the awakening and the passing beyond sorrow, "the great occasion where the three meet". The fifteenth of the bright half of mchu is the time of the birth in the Lumbinī grove; the eighth of the waxing half of sa ga is the great occasion of the going forth.

On those good great occasions whatever virtue is done, led by trust and faith, is said to be multiplied a hundred koṭi-fold (བྱེ་བ་བརྒྱ, bye ba brgya). Shown by giving: whoever with a wholly pure intention gives but one lump of dough, since it becomes a hundred koṭi, a thousand million (ཐེར་འབུམ, ther 'bum) lumps of flour and butter arise; the fruit of giving a single coin is two million in all (so the line reads). And by the higher or lower field of the gift: given to an animal it is a hundredfold, to an ordinary person a thousandfold, to one of lax discipline ten thousandfold, to one of pure discipline a hundred thousandfold; and given to one's father and mother, to the sick, to one who speaks the Dharma and to a bodhisattva in his last birth, though they are not noble ones, the gift is beyond measure: so the second Omniscient One, Vasubandhu (དབྱིག་གཉེན, dbyig gnyen), explained.

"A solar eclipse a hundred thousand koṭi, a lunar eclipse seven koṭi-fold, the ordinary good days (དུས་བཟང་ཕལ, dus bzang phal) a hundredfold: so said the great paṇḍita Śākyaśrī (པཎ་ཆེན་ཤཱཀྱ་ཤྲཱི, paN chen shAkya shrI)." Since even a little virtue bears endless fruit, strive in the virtue of the three doors with trust and pure intention. The teaching is near its setting; may the virtue of striving here win a precious support in the next life, a qualified teacher's care and the end of the two stages' path.

Colophon: written by Dbyangs can dga' ba'i blo gros, after the words of the holy ones, as an exhortation to virtue for himself and others.
