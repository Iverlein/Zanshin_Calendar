## <a id="mnb-p267"></a>mnb:p267 Ch. 81, the dream lines in the Bhutan print of the Instruction Tantra: ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ and ལྷས་ཚོར་ངུས་ཏེ (OCR)

*Read: etext, 2026-11-09; the scan by eye, 2026-11-09 (RD264).*

> སྤྲུལ་ལྗོང་ཉ་སྤྲུལ་འབུ་སྦྲང་སྲེག་སྡོམ་སོགས། །… མདའ་དང་སྒོ་ང་ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ། །བུད་མེད་དག་གིས་ལུས་ཟུངས་འབྱིན་པ་རྨི། །ལྷས་ཚོར་ངུས་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས། །ལུས་ལ་བརྟག་པ་བཞིན་ལ་འགྱུར་ལྡོག་བྱེད། །

*Man ngag rgyud*, the Instruction Tantra, in a print from Bum thang chos 'khor rab brtan (Bhutan, 210 folios, in Gene Smith's collection, BDRC MW4CZ16923), the eighty-first chapter's dream lines (etext p. 267). The etext is a poor OCR: of the creatures' line it gives «སྤྲུལ་ལྗོང་ཉ་སྤྲུལ་འབུ་སྦྲང་སྲེག་སྡོམ་སོགས» (the Serthang print has སྦལ་ལྗོང་ཉ་སྦྲུལ་འབུ་སྦྲང་སྡིག་སྡོམ་སོགས; the differences are the kind of fault the OCR makes with stacked letters), and for the lines that matter: *…arrow and egg, a temple, «ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ»; women drawing out the body's essence; «ལྷས་ཚོར་ངུས་ཏེ» the spirit's sign; the body is examined likewise, and the marks change*. So this print reads སོ (so) for བཟོ in ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ, as the Drathang print (st:p350-351, machine reading) does, and ངུས་ for དུས་, as the 2005 print of the Supplement's etext (lth:p325) does; ལྷས་ stands, not ལུས་ (this is the etext's reading; on the scan the syllable is དུས, see below).

Read in the etext only, 2026-11-09 (RD255); a scan is served (open access) and was not opened, so the two readings are the OCR's and unconfirmed: a ས for a བཟ stack and a ང for a ད are both slips the OCR could make. Only the lines quoted are given; the omitted words are marked «…».

RD264 (2026-11-09), the scan (BDRC I1KG13565, open; the image number is the etext page, so ch. 81's dream lines are on img. 267 and the print is a long palm-leaf-shaped page of six rows, 1980 × 364 px, the letters about 25 px high; Yigdzin-1 over img. 266–269, then crops at 6–16× by eye). The third row of img. 267 reads, syllable by syllable: ལྷ་ཁང་ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ། བུད་མེད་དག་གིས་ལུས་ཟུངས་འབྱིན་པ་རྨི། ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས། (Yigdzin-1 gives the same). Three words settled or narrowed:

- **ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ** (crop x 565–700, y 128–178, 12×): ལྷས (ལྷ with ས), ཚོར (ཚ, ོ, ར), then དུས (a ད with ུ and the final ས) and ཏེ. The syllable is དུས, not ངུས: the OCR's ངུས་ is the etext's fault. ལྷས is ལྷ with ས, not ལུས.
- **ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ** (crop x 160–290, y 130–175, 12×, and x 190–270, 16× with the contrast stretched): ལྡེར (ལྡེ with ེ, then ར), a tsheg, and one glyph with ོ above its head, built as ས (the curl at the left, the right stem), with nothing of a separate བ at its left, then འགུལ. So the print reads **སོ** (so), not བཟོ (bzo); a ཟོ without the prefix cannot be ruled out at this size, but a བཟ stack as the Serthang scan has it (a separate boxed བ and ཟ) is not there. The etext's reading stands.
- **The sister clause** (the fourth row of img. 267, crop x 355–520, y 192–238, 11×, contrast stretched): ཚོར་དུས་ཡིན་གདོན་ with, before ཚོར, a ར (a head stroke with a right leg, no curl) and ང: **རང་ཚོར་དུས་ཡིན**, as the Serthang print, not the etext's ངང་ཚོར; the glyph after ཚོར་ is again དུས (ད, ུ, ས), moderate confidence for the first letter (the print's cursive joins ར to its ང).
- Ch. 91: see mnb:p418.

The page is a woodblock print in a cursive-leaning hand with the strokes joined; at this resolution only a stack as plain as བཟ or ཕྱྭ is told apart with confidence, and the two readings above are by eye, not machine-checked beyond Yigdzin-1.

## <a id="mnb-p418"></a>mnb:p418 Ch. 91, the dream of a spirit and its remedies in the Bhutan print: མོ་ཕྱྭ་རིམ་གྲོ

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD264).*

> དུག་བྱུང་ཕྱི་ནང་གསང་རྟགས་ངོས་བཟུང་ལ། །དེ་ཡི་གཉེན་པོས་བསད་ཅིང་ལུས་ཟུངས་བསྲུང་། །གདོན་ཡིན་རྨི་ལམ་བུད་མེད་འདྲེན་པར་འགྱུར། །མོ་ཕྱྭ་རིམ་གྲོ་རྟེན་འབྲེལ་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་གསོ། །

The end of the ninety-first chapter of the Instruction Tantra, making the flavour (རོ་ཙ་བར་བྱ་བ, ro tsa bar bya ba), in the Bhutan print, etext p. 418, img. 418, the fourth row of the scan at its right end (crop x 1490–1890, y 145–190, 4–10×). Yigdzin-1 reads the four lines as above, the last with «མོ་ཕྱྭ་རིམ་གྲོ་» at the row's end and «རྟེན་འབྲེལ་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་གསོ» on the next row. By eye: «གདོན་ཡིན་རྨི་ལམ་བུད་མེད་འདྲེན་པར་འགྱུར» clear (the whole row's close), then after the stroke and tsheg མོ་ (མ with ོ), then **ཕྱྭ**: ཕ with the subjoined ya at its right and under it the small triangle of the wa-zur (ྭ), not the hook of ུ (crop x 1690–1890, y 145–190, 10×); then རིམ་གྲོ. The Serthang scan has the same triangle (st:p493), the Drathang print's machine reading too: three prints for ཕྱྭ (phywa; its sense is not looked up here, see st:p493). The etext reads «མོ་ཕྱ་ཛམ» (OCR). Yigdzin-1 over img. 418 finds neither of the Serthang print's two bracketed remedies (the thread, the milk) in the Bhutan print, as in the Drathang print (whether the scan carries them as interlinear notes was not looked for): the verse runs straight on to «དགོས་པ་ནད་མེད་ལང་ཚོར་ལྡན་པ་ཡིས།» (Yigdzin-1 and the etext agree on these words; they were not read on the scan).
