## <a id="ahs-i259"></a>ahs:i259 Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha Śārīrasthāna 12: the dream of the cows' release, great and at once

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD312), leaf n259 of the item at full size; the page head's last digit blurred.*

> Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha, Śārīrasthāna ch. 12 (the head "अ० १२ ] शारीरस्थानम् [ २५२/२५३"), leaf n259, the passage on the kinds of dream, the middle of the page:
>
> स्वप्नस्तु स्वयमन्येन वा दृष्टः शुभाशुभहेतुः स सप्तविधो दृष्टः श्रुतोऽनुभूतः प्रार्थितः संकल्पितो भाविको दोषजश्च ॥ तत्राद्यः पंचविधोऽफलः तथा यथास्वप्रकृतिर्विस्मृतो दिवाऽतिह्रस्वोऽतिदीर्घो नातिसुप्तदृष्टश्च पूर्वरात्रेऽल्पं चिराच्च फलं करोति ॥ गोसर्गे महत्सद्यश्च ॥ निद्रया च पुनरनुपहतः प्रतीपैर्वा वचोभिर्दानयमनियमादिभिर्वा ॥

The elder Vāgbhaṭa's wording of the same passage, in prose: "A dream, seen by oneself or by another, is a cause of good or ill; it is of seven kinds: seen, heard, experienced, wished, imagined, foreseen, and from a humour. Of these the first five are without fruit [as in the Hṛdaya]; and so are one's own nature, forgotten, by day, too short, too long, seen in not deep sleep; [a dream] of the early night gives little fruit, and late. At the cows' release [it gives] a great [fruit] and at once. Provided sleep is not broken, nor contrary words spoken, nor [the evil is turned] by gifts, restraints, observances and the like."

The line reads "gosarge mahat sadyaś ca": the word is "gosarge", with no variant noted under it in this edition (the page carries no apparatus; the editor's preface says that the variant readings of other manuscripts are written under the text where he has them). The Saṅgraha has the dream of the cows' release "at once" (sadyaḥ), where the Hṛdaya has "that same day" (tadahaḥ).
