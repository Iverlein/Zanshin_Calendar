## <a id="kd-1-p493"></a>kd.1:p493 The month head: the month's earth lords and where works are not begun

*Read: etext, 2026-10-26.*

> ལྔ་ཤར། དགུ་ནུབ། ཟིན་འཕུང་ཉི་མ་རྩེ་ཤར་དུས་ནུབ་ནས་ཤར་དུ་རྒྱུ། ལུག་རྫོགས་ནས་ལྡོག །ཧལ་ཁྱི་བྱང་སྟོད། འབྲུག་རྗེ་ཁྱམས་དང་རང་འཐག་ལ་གནས། ཡས་ལམ་བྱང་ཤར་ཞེས་པ། ཟླ་བའི་ས་བདག་ལས་ལྷ་ལྔ་ཤར་དང་། ལྷ་དགུ་ནུབ་ཏུ་འཚོགས་པ་དང་། ཟིན་འཕུང་རྒྱུ་ཚེས་ཉིན་སོ་སོར་བཀོད་པའི་ཕྱོགས་དང་དུས་སྟོན། ཟླ་བའི་ཧལ་ཁྱི་བྱང་སྟོད་དང་། ནང་ལྟ་འབྲུག་རྗེ་ཁྱམས་དང་རང་འཐག་ལ་གནས་པས། དེ་དག་གནས་ཕྱོགས་དང་བུམ་སྟོང་ལ་བྱ་བ་གལ་ཆེའི་རིགས་ཀྱི་ལས་སྣེ་གཏོད་མི་རུང་། ཡས་ཞེས་པ་གླུད་ཡིན་པས་དེ་གཏོང་བའི་ལམ་ཟླ་བ་འདིར་བྱང་ཤར་ཡིན།

Etext p. 509 (printed p. 493), BDRC's etext only. Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan explains the month head of a Lhasa almanac (the 3rd month of a wood pig year), after its bum stong, line by line.

"'Five east, nine west; zin 'phung goes from west to east when the sun strikes the peaks, and turns back at the end of the sheep [hour]; hal khyi upper north; 'brug rje stays in the courtyard and the loom (?); the ransom's road north-east': among the month's earth lords, the five gods gather in the east and the nine in the west, and zin 'phung's course is given with the direction and time written on each date. As the month's hal khyi is in the upper north and, of the house, 'brug rje stays in the courtyard and at «རང་འཐག», no beginning of important works may be made in their places or in [the direction of] the empty vase. 'Yas' is the ransom (glud), and the road to send it this month is north-east."

What it shows for D4: an almanac reader of WB explains the month's earth lords as places in which important works are not begun; he sets no weight against the date's results, which he explains apart on the next pages.

## <a id="kd-1-p493b"></a>kd.1:p493b The month head: the skipped and doubled dates in the two reckonings; the planets' rise and decline; the verse on Īśvara in the sheep house

*Read: etext, 2026-11-14.*

> ཚེས་གྲངས་ཆད་ལྷག་ལ་ཕུག་ལུགས་གྲུབ་པའི་ཚེས་༦ལྷག ཚེས་༡༤ཚད། དཔལ་ལྡན་བྱེད་པའི་ཚེས་༢ ༡ཆད། ལྷག་མེད། བྱེད་གྲུབ་གང་ཡང་ཚེས་ལ་ ། གཟའ་ཞིབ་པ་དར། གུད། གྱོད། ཆད་ལྷག་མེད་པ་ཞག་སུམ་ཅུ་ཚང་ན་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཟླ་བ་ཡིན ཞུད། ཅེས་པ་ནི་ཟླ་བ་དེར་གཟའ་གང་དང་གང་དར་གུད་སྟོན་པའོ། །བལ་ལྡན་ཁྱིམ་དུ་ལྷ་ ཆེན་དང་། །རི་སྐྱེས་མ་གཉིས་བཞེངས་མོལ་བས། །སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ནི་བདེ་བར་གནས། ། ཆར་ཆུ་དུས་འབེབས་ལོ་ཏོག་བཟང་། །ཞེས་པ་ནི་བལ་ལྡན་ནི་ལུག ལྷ་ཆེན་ནི་དབང་ཕྱུག རི་ སྐྱེས་མ་ནི་ཨུ་མ་སྟེ་ལུག་ཁྱིམ་དུ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཡབ་ཡུམ་བཞེངས་ཏེ་མཇལ་བའི་བཟང་འབྲས་ བརྗོད་པ་སྟེ། གཞན་དུ་བཞུགས་དང་། གཟིམས་མཇལ་ལ་ནི་འབྲས་བུ་ངན་པའོ། །དེ་ནས་ ཁྱད་པར་དུ་ཆར་རྩིས་འགོད་པ་ལ། འོད་འགྲོ་ཟླ་སྐྱེས་དུས་མེ་ལུག་ཁྱིམ་རོལ། །འདེགས་བྱེད་ ཁབ་ཏུ་བསིལ་ལྡན་རཱ་ཧུས་སྤྱོད། །ཚངས་སྐྱེས་ལུས་དམར་ཀརྦ་བུམ་པར་གནས། །ཉ་གཞུའི་ གྲོང་དུ་འཚོ་བྱེད་དཀར་པོས་འཁྱུད། །ཅེས་པ་ཁྱིམ་དེ་དག་ཏུ་གཟའ་གང་དང་གང་གནས་པ་ མིང་གི་མངོན་བརྗོད་ཀྱིས་སྤྲས་ཏེ་སྟོན་པའོ།

Etext p. 509 (printed p. 493), the rest of the page after kd.1:p493, BDRC's etext only; the etext's lines are out of order here (the sentence on the dates is broken by the next line, and the closing clause on whole months stands before «ཞུད»).

On the dates skipped and doubled (ཚེས་གྲངས་ཆད་ལྷག): in the Phugpa accomplished reckoning (ཕུག་ལུགས་གྲུབ་པ) the 6th is doubled (ལྷག) and the 14th skipped (the etext prints ཚད for ཆད); in the Dpal ldan effect reckoning (དཔལ་ལྡན་བྱེད་པ) the figures stand as «༢ ༡» with «ཆད» (skipped) and nothing doubled (ལྷག་མེད). The figures with a space may be the 21st alone or the 2nd and the 1st; the etext does not decide. When neither the effect nor the accomplished reckoning skips or doubles any date and all thirty days are whole, it is an auspicious month (བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཟླ་བ). The detailed planets (གཟའ་ཞིབ་པ) are shown as «rise» (དར), «decline» (གུད), «dispute» (གྱོད) and «removed» (ཞུད), which tell which planets rise or decline in the month.

Then the almanac's verse «བལ་ལྡན་ཁྱིམ་དུ་ལྷ་ཆེན་དང་། །རི་སྐྱེས་མ་གཉིས་བཞེངས་མོལ་བས། །སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ནི་བདེ་བར་གནས། །ཆར་ཆུ་དུས་འབེབས་ལོ་ཏོག་བཟང་།» is explained: Bal ldan is the sheep (Aries, ལུག), the great god (ལྷ་ཆེན) is Īśvara (དབང་ཕྱུག), the mountain-born (རི་སྐྱེས་མ) is Umā (ཨུ་མ): father and mother Īśvara stand and meet in the sheep house, which tells a good result; when they sit, or lie down to meet, elsewhere, the result is bad. For the rain reckoning in particular there is a second verse, «འོད་འགྲོ་ཟླ་སྐྱེས་དུས་མེ་ལུག་ཁྱིམ་རོལ། …», which names by epithets which planet stands in which house.

Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan goes on with the same almanac (the 3rd month of a wood pig year): the forecast, the closing items of the month head, then the day's entry, taking the 1st of the month as the example.

The forecast: the month's elements are chiefly earth and wind, so although the clouds are large many are scattered by wind; from the 1st to the earth month's full moon (the etext's «ས་ཟླའི་ཉ») the planet «friend of the gods» moves among ordinary mansions and the «sangs mdun» (a planet in front) rises in the east, so the upper north has wind, storms and dust; from the 22nd to the 8th of the next month the «sangs» ends its course and dark clouds gather in the north-east with many light showers; from the 16th Jupiter (ཕུར་བུ, phur bu) turns back in its mansion and dust-wind rises. «The numbers 1 to 30, eleven in a row» are the dates the general entry holds. Then the month head carries, laid out well: the *dhruva* (the fixed number) of the sun by accomplished and by effect reckoning; the new-moon and its remaining time; the whole parts; Rāhu's root, face and tail, the three; the planets' allotment through the twelve houses; and under the table the corrected slow and quick of the five planets.

The day's entry, from the 1st of the 3rd month of a wood pig year, by item: the numeral 1 is the Tibetan date. «ཉི» (nyi) is the sun among the seven weekdays. «ཐ་སྐར» (tha skar) is the first of the 28 mansions, the mansion that accompanies the weekday, the one the moon is in on that date. «མེ་རླུང་» (fire, wind) is the element of the sun, fire, and of the mansion tha skar, wind: the elemental concord of planet and mansion, of the ten small concords. «སྤྲེལ་གྱི་གཉིས་ནས་བྲ་ཉེ» (from the second of the monkey [hour]'s five water-hours, bra nye) shows that in the evening monkey watch, from the second water-hour, the mansion tha skar passes and bra nye begins to arrive. «མེ་གཉིས» is the element concord of that. «དུས་དབྱིག» (dus dbyig) is of the 28 great concords. Among the great concords of that moment the text sets: the seven accomplishing combinations and the seven death-combinations of planet and mansion meeting, and the fourteen burning dates of planet and date meeting, which are three concords not shared with the other tradition, [those] of the tantra-commentary school; and others: the seven pairs (ཟུང་སྦྱོར), seven king-of-demons (བདུད་རྒྱལ), seven accomplishing-sun (གྲུབ་ཉི), seven auspicious-sun (བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཉི་མ), seven increase-sun (འཕེལ་ཉི), seven awakening-sun (ཆུབ་ཉི), seven discordant-sun (མི་མཐུན་ཉི་མ), seven combination-sun (སྦྱོར་ཉི), seven demon-sun (བདུད་ཉི), seven non-concord-sun (མི་འཕྲོད་ཉི་མ) and seven destroying-sun (འཇིག་ཉི), which he says are the concords told in the Black reckoning (ནག་རྩིས) and others; these can come single or stacked. The paragraph that follows the six sets, read for D5 beside weighing.md (KP's prose and WB's verse): the author asks what to do when the results of the many things to be examined in one day are mixed good and bad, and answers with the order of strength.

When many things are reckoned, many unequal goods and bads will come, but the strength of each good and bad must be reckoned, namely: the date has one quality, the planet four times, the mansion eight times, the combination period (དུས་སྦྱོར) ten million [times]. Again: if planet and mansion differ in good and bad, the planet leads; if mansion and date differ, the mansion leads; if combination (སྦྱོར་བ) and effect (བྱེད་པ) differ, the effect, being the fiercer, leads; if the day (ཉི་མ) and the time (དུས་ཚོད) differ, the time leads; if none are equal, the concord (འཕྲོད) itself leads; where there is no special case (དམིགས་བསལ) the general grouping (ཕྱོགས་བསྡེབས) is held to lead; where good and bad are not equal in anything, the difference of strength of good and bad is reckoned and the stronger leads. «Rāhu, the weekday, the mansion, the date, the effect, the combination, the day: each earlier one is stronger than the later.» Because the power of all of these is complete in the one instant combination period (ཏཏྟལ་དུས་སྦྱོར), the combination period must be held the chief of all, it is said. Likewise, from two verses: «The Jupiter [day] works when the time rises; the life-planet's day is virtuous, [but] flee the killer's cruelty (གཤེད་གཟར)» and «the life-power [mansions] of bla, srog and dbang are good for anything; the keg, bdud and gshed mansions are bad», shown by these, whatever the general, it is important to hold as chief what must be reckoned as special for the particular case; as is said: «a treatise's general rule being fixed, the particular becomes strong».

So it is hard for everything to be good together; but «though one knows the many and the diffuse, the way of the wise is to grasp the chief point», and, from the *Clear Mirror of Reckoning Texts* (རྩིས་གཞུང་རབ་གསལ་མེ་ལོང), «what is taken as the basis of examination is looked into by reasoning; bring the diffuse to the few and gather their red mouths [the vital points] into the crux». Therefore, when the chief ones are good, protect against some of the bad kinds, and apply, as the occasion allows, the general and the special remedies for obscuration and for decline; there is no need for doubt: if the harm is true, the turning-back is true, and if the loss is true the confession is true, since for those of equal start there is no denying the reasoning, and both are results of the reckoning.

The author closes with a verse of his own, «On the heart of the reckoning's meaning…», which urges study, and gives his name: written by Tshul khrims.
