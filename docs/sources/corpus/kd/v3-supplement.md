# KD vol. 3, Kun bzang blo gros's supplement to the Moonbeams: the illness reckoning's identification

Kun bzang blo gros's (ཀུན་བཟང་བློ་གྲོས, kun bzang blo gros) *The Garland of Stars* (རྒྱུ་སྐར་ཕྲེང་བ, rgyu skar phreng ba), a supplement to Dharmaśrī's *Moonbeams* (KD vol. 3, from p. 513 by the list of contents), restates WB's illness reckoning. ## <a id="kd-3-p693"></a>kd.3:p693 The identification of illness and spirit: the progressed year, trigram, mewa and day

*Read: etext, 2026-10-25.*

> ས་བཅད་གསུམ་པ། ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང་བ།
> ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་གཟུང་པ་ནི། །ལོག་མེན་སྤར་ཁ་སྨེ་བ་དང་། །ཉི་མ་རྣམས་ལ་བརྟག་བྱ་སྟེ། །

KD vol. 3, p. 693 (etext VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4717 p. 713, printed page = etext page − 20; read from BDRC's etext only, the scan not seen), the start of the third section of Kun bzang blo gros's supplement (ཀུན་བཟང་བློ་གྲོས, kun bzang blo gros) to the *Moonbeams*, *The Garland of Stars* (རྒྱུ་སྐར་ཕྲེང་བ, rgyu skar phreng ba), after the sixteen heads and the plank-trees have been given: "Third section: identifying the illness and the spirit. The identifying of illness and spirit is to be examined by the progressed year (ལོག་མེན, log men), the trigram, the mewa, and the day (ཉི་མ, nyi ma)." The four are set side by side as the inputs of the identification; the first is the sick person's progressed year, the other three are the time's. The text goes on with the progressed year, then (p. 695) the trigram's, (p. 697) the mewa's and (p. 701) the day's illness and spirit, in that order, the day last; WB's own order is the progressed year (pp. 426–427), the trigram (pp. 427–428), the day (pp. 428–429) and the mewa (pp. 431–433).

## <a id="kd-3-p701"></a>kd.3:p701 The day's illness and spirit identified

*Read: etext, 2026-10-25.*

> ཉི་མའི་ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང་བའི་སྐོར། ཉི་མའི་ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་གཟུང་། །བྱི་བ་ཕོ་ཡང་མོ་ལྕི་སྟེ། །ཚགས་ཚིགས་ན་བའམ་འབར་འབུར་སྐྲངས། །གདོན་དུ་རྣལ་འདྲེ་གཡོས་པའམ། །

KD vol. 3, p. 701 (etext p. 721; etext only), the heading «ཉི་མའི་ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང་བའི་སྐོར», "the section on identifying the day's illness and spirit", set directly after the mewa's rites and the times to send them (the three whites at sunrise, black and blue at sunset, the greens at «མྱུར་སྨད», the yellow and the reds at noon). Under it the supplement copies WB's twelve animals verse by verse (WB vol. 1, pp. 428–429: «the rat: male light, female heavy; the joints ache, swellings; the spirit, the *nal* demon stirred»); the quotation here is its first lines, with the etext's noise left out. The supplement adds no gloss saying whose animal it is.
