> lde gu | 1) {bag zan sla ba dang/ phye ma bskol ba'i skyo ma/} 2) {khu ba gar po/} 3) {dri bzang sbangs pa'i chu/}
> lde gu'i sbyor ba | {sbrang chu bcad pa dang/ mar gyi wu snyigs phyung ba/ bu ram chur bsdus pa bcas kyi steng du nad gang sel bar byed pa'i sman phye bsrel te dkrug de'i steng ka ra zhib btags btab nas skyo ma gar po bsten dgos pa'i rnying zhing zhen pa'i nad zhi bar byed pa'i zhi byed kyi sde tshan zhig}
> ba ra | 1) {ba phyugs dang ra/} 2) {ba phyugs 'jug sa'i ra ba/}
> ba ru | {shing tog cig gi ming ste/} {bod kyi sman gzhung du a ru ba ru skyu ru gsum la sman gyi 'bras bu gsum zer/}   (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho)
> Ives Waldo: ru rta | 1) thang sman ... 2) ...; lde gu | 1) thick kneaded dough &, boiled flour dough/ paste/ porridge; 2) thick juice; 3) water steeped in good smelling thing; 4) mixture; 5) syrup; 6) ointment; ba ra | 1) cattle/ cows and goats; 2) cattle corral/ shed; ba ru | beleric myrobalan

Look-ups of 2026-11-11 in the keyed Great Tibetan-Chinese Dictionary (Steinert's set no. 25), with the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho (no. 62) and Ives Waldo (no. 08) where named. Each headword in Wylie as the set keys it; the Tibetan above is the dictionary's own definition.
ru rta: (1) a pleasant-tasting medicinal herb of the class of the "decoction medicines" (thang sman), its taste (ro) pungent-hot (tsha), sweet and bitter; after digestion even (snyoms); by its power it helps the illnesses of disordered wind and blood, the moving of the stomach (?), lung disease, "gag pa", dead-flesh (sha ro), water-sri (chu sri), the winding of the "moon-sign" (zla mtshan 'khyil ba), brown phlegm (bad kan smug po), swelling of the glang thabs; its synonym names are the pervader (khyab byed), the one marked by a staff (dbyug pas mtshan), the ripe one (smin ldan), the blazing one ('od 'bar ma); (2) formerly, a horse of one colour ridden by the troop of each Tibetan district (ru dmag). Ives Waldo gives only "thang sman" for the first sense. The dictionaries do not name the plant in a Western botanical name; "rue" is not given.
lde gu: (1) soft dough of flour, and the gruel (skyo ma) of boiled flour; (2) thick juice; (3) water steeped with fragrant matter. Ives Waldo: a thick kneaded dough, paste, porridge; thick juice; water steeped in a good-smelling thing; a mixture; syrup; ointment. The dictionary's «lde gu'i sbyor ba» is a medicine powder stirred into honey-water, clarified butter-dregs and molasses water, with finely ground sugar on top: a class of pacifying compounds. So «lde gur» (with the terminative ra) is "into / as a paste, mixture or electuary".
ba ra: (1) cattle and goats (ba phyugs dang ra); (2) the byre where cattle graze (ba phyugs 'jug sa'i ra ba). Ives Waldo: cattle, cows and goats; a cattle corral.
ba ru: (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho) the name of a fruit tree; in the Tibetan medical texts the three, a ru, ba ru and skyu ru, are called the three fruits of medicine. Ives Waldo: beleric myrobalan.

## <a id="tmz-box2"></a>tmz:box2 The entries sa sros and sa srod in the keyed Tibetan dictionaries (q1116)

*Read: etext, 2026-11-13.*

> sa sros | {nyi ma nub nas yun ring ma lon pa'i dus kyi ming /}   (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs; the same in Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho)
> sa srod | {sa rub rjes nam phyed ma zin gong gi dus tshod/}   (Tshig mdzod chen mo); {nyi ma bzhud rjes mtshan thog mar 'char bzhin pa'i dus cha/} (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho)
> Ives Waldo: sa srod | after dusk until midnight; sa sros | at dusk, towards evening, night
> Valby: sa sros | toward evening, at dusk time, after dusk, 1 of the 12 dus tshod, dusk; sa sros nas | when it grew dark
> Rangjung Yeshe: sa sros | at dusk, towards evening

sa sros: "the name of the time not long after the sun has set" (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs and Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho); Ives Waldo and Rangjung Yeshe: at dusk, towards evening; Valby: dusk, one of the twelve times of the day-and-night, and «sa sros nas», "when it grew dark". sa srod: the time between sunset and before midnight ("after the earth's darkening, before midnight is reached", Tshig mdzod chen mo; "the part of the time when the night is just rising after the sun has gone", Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho); Ives Waldo, "after dusk until midnight". So «sa sros nas nam phyed bar» is "from dusk to midnight"; the same two words stand in WB's list of the night's hours (wb.1:p17b: sunset, dusk (ས་སྲོས), the evening round, midnight, past midnight, dawn). > za zi | {'du long ste las dang skad cha mang po'i brel zing/ mi mang 'du 'dzoms za zi'i khrod du'ang mno bsam gtong thub pa/ smig rgyu za zis mgo rmongs te shar dang nub la sogs pa'i phyogs kyang mtshon par mi nus/}   (Tshig mdzod chen mo)
> za zi | 1. {za ri zi ri'am mi gsal ba'i don/} 2. {zang zing dang brel zing gi don/}   (Sgom sde tshig mdzod chen mo)
> za ri zi ri | 1) {mi gsal ba'i tshul/ nam za ri zi ri'i dus/ dran pa za ri zi ri/ chang gis bzi na sems rnal du mi gnas par za ri zi ri byed yong/} 2) {don med du g.yeng ba'i tshul/}   (Tshig mdzod chen mo)
> Barron: za zi | nebulous; indistinct; hazy. Rangjung Yeshe: za zi | ex {za zi phra phro g.yo 'gul can}. Dan Martin: za zi | = zang zing, chaotic, disordered, tumultuous (Klong chen pa). Negi: za zi | {mun mdangs she mun za zi dang} (the Amarakosa's synonym for darkness).

za zi: "the sense of what is not clear and what is out of order, as in «a za zi dream» (rmi lam za zi), «a za zi work», «to make the head za zi»" (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs); the bustle of much work and talk, the confusion of a crowd, the mirage that confuses the head so that east and west cannot be told (Tshig mdzod chen mo); «unclear» and «chaotic» (Sgom sde); «nebulous, indistinct, hazy» (Barron); in Negi a word for dimness of the dark. The related za ri zi ri: «the manner of being unclear; the time of dusk and dimness; a hazy memory; the mind that does not stay still when drunk». No entry gives za zi the sense «half asleep» or names a state of sleep; it says that the thing seen is indistinct or disordered, and the dictionary's own example is a dream. rab tshes: "the name of the fifteenth rab byung" (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Tshig gter rgya mtsho); Ives Waldo "15th year of rabjung". zla tshes: (1) "the abbreviation of «zla grangs tshes grangs»", that is month-number and date-number (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Tshig gter rgya mtsho), (2) the crescent, (3) the crescent in the sign of a HUM or a stupa's top; Ives Waldo "month and date, date of the month" beside "crescent moon". So the colophon «rab tshes me rta zla tshes» reads "the fifteenth rab byung, fire-horse, month and date": the cycle and the year named, the month and the day left unnumbered as a stock phrase. The twelve double-hours: in the Tshig mdzod chen mo's own list (also Tshig gter rgya mtsho and Ives Waldo) the order after the pig (srod 'khor) is nam phyed, phyed yol, tho rangs; Valby pairs nam phyed with the rat (byi ba) and phyed yol with the ox (glang); Dung dkar gives nam gung = rat (23-1 h) and nam phyed yol = ox (1-3 h), and nam gung is a synonym of mtshan phyed. The Tshig mdzod chen mo's entry nam phyed names the rat; the Tshig gter rgya mtsho's entry names the ox but its own list of the twelve has the same order as the Tshig mdzod chen mo's, in which nam phyed is followed by phyed yol. rmi ltas: "the sign of a good or bad dream" (Tshig mdzod chen mo); "signs in dreams, omens in dreams" (Ives Waldo). do ra: "a dancing ground, a stage" (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho; Ives Waldo "dancing ground, stage"), in the Sgom sde dictionary "the dancer's enclosure, the ground or place where dancing is done". gar mkhan: dancer, actor (synonyms gar pa, zlos gar mkhan, rol sgeg ma; and in the mngon-brjod language "peacock"). 'dren byed: in the mngon-brjod language "eye" and "he who leads" (Tshig mdzod chen mo); Ives Waldo adds "conveying, transporting"; in the Tshig mdzod chen mo's synonyms of the horse (rta) 'dren byed is one name, and Negi's Sanskrit equivalent of 'dren byed is ghoṭaka, "horse". rta ljang: "a wind-horse of the sun's good house, the leader" (Tshig mdzod chen mo), simply "the sun" (Ives Waldo, Rangjung Yeshe); rta ljang can: Indra or the sun (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Negi haridaśva).

bskal li: "a li tho (almanac) that serves for many years". bskal li'i re'u mig: "the name of the table of the enjoyment of the three hundred and one lunar days, to be added to the general wealth of the six houses". Ives Waldo: "table showing 371 coursing six houses khyim drug spyi nor to be added".

kha dmar: "a clear prophecy of the good or bad fruit of a reckoning (rtsis pa'i babs 'bras), an oracle about what will come" (Tshig mdzod chen mo; the Sangs rgyas chos gzhung dictionary); Valby "affirmative prediction (good or bad)", kha dmar phog "the realization of an affirmative prediction", kha dmar tog "the non-realization"; Ives Waldo adds "(2) accept responsibility for a task". The "red mouth" of the literal reading is the sign of this prediction, not a being.

blo ldan: in the mngon-brjod language "the planet Jupiter" (Tshig mdzod chen mo) and among the names of Jupiter (phur bu); nyi ma'i bu "son of the sun": Saturn (spen pa); bkra shis is a name of Mars (mig dmar); zla ba'i bu "son of the moon": Mercury (lhag pa); snyan ngag mkhan "the poet" (the pa sangs list spells it snyan dngags mkhan) and khu ba dkar po "white semen": Venus (pa sangs).

zla nag, the black month: (1) the full thirty-day month that takes the sign of the tenth year-sign counted backwards from the (honorific) year's own sign, for the rat year the hare month, for the ox year the dragon month; (2) the month that becomes the four-slayer (bzhi gshed) of the year, "the fourth counted in the year's own forward (mas) and backward (yas) direction, as the rat's forward four-slayer is the hare and its backward the bird"; of it "it is said: the upper black of the four corner signs (ra ba bzhi), the middle black of the four middle signs ('bring bzhi), the lower black of the four last signs (tha chung bzhi); for instance in a rat year the hare month and the bird month are called black, and especially 'the middle black'".

The five kha yan years (kha yan lo lnga), a term of the elemental reckoning ('byung rtsis): kha yan (the year-sign's life (srog) and power-element (dbang thang) have one element), khong nang (the power-element is the mother of the life), se zhig (the power-element is the son of the life), kha ral and mdun khur (the dictionary gives both as "the friend of the life", which cannot be two different relations; one of the two is perhaps meant as the enemy). Beside them the four lo pa of the life-element and the bone-element (rus khams): ma lo, bu lo, grogs lo, dgra lo.

bse: (4) rhinoceros, (2) dyed leather. bse rag: "a spirit that eats the essence of food and wealth, the ghost of poverty". gcong: (1) an illness hard to cure, a chronic one (consumption); (2) a cliff, a ravine; glo gcong: one of the eight lung illnesses (constant cough, wasted flesh, a rattle from the throat, heavy movement); dmu chu: dropsy, named after the dmu spirit, "one of the six chronic diseases (gcong chen nad drug)" in Valby. kha smras: "a man's talk, gossip". ljan ljin: "filth, dirt, sweepings; unclean", chu ljan ljin "filthy water". rbad: "boasting; all, wholly; abruptly"; rbad bcad "resolute", rbad de "completely".
