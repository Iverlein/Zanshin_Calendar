> 310༄༅།འབྲོང་རྩེ་བེའུ་བུམ་ཆེན་མོ། བཻཌཱུརྱའི་ས་གཞི་ཁྱབ་ལཁ་རྒྱན་དགོས་འདོད་འབྱུང་བའི་དཔག་བསམ་ཤིང་ གི་སྡོང་། ཡལ་གལོ་མ། མེ་ཏོག འབྲུས་[ འབྲས་བུ] སྣ་ཚོགས་པས་ བརྒྱན་པགུར་སྟེང་གི་ནམ་མཁའི་སྔོན་པོར་བསྒོམརི་རབ་གྱི་སྟེང་དུ་ལྷ་མོ་ ཤྲ་མ་ཎ་སྐུ་མདོག་སྔོན་། ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས་མགཡས་པས་རྔ་ཡབ་མེ་ ལོང་། གཡོན་པས་དཔགས་བསམ་ཤིང་གི་ཡལ་ཀ་འཛིན་པཞབས་གཉིས་ བརྐྱང་ནས་རི་རབ་ལ་སྐྱེགས་[ཀྱག]འདུག་གི་ཚུལ་གྱིས་བཞུགས་། དཔག་ བསམ་ཤིང་གི་སྡོང་པོ་ལ་རྒྱབ་བརྟེན་པ༑ སྐེད་[རྐེད ]པ་ཕྲ་བ་ནུ་མ་འབུར་བ། ཞལ་འཛུམ་པ་དང་བཅས་པས་རང་ལ་གཟིགས་པདར་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་སྟོད་ གཡོགས་དང་། སྨད་གཡོགས་ཅནརཏྣ་དང་མེ་ཏོག་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་གྱིས་ སྤྲས་ཏེམཛེས་ཤིང་ཡིད་དུ་འོང་བདུས་གསུམ་གྱི་ཤེས་བྱ་མ་ལུས་པ་མཁྱེན་ པས་བདག་ལ་ལུང་ཐམས་ཅད་མངོན་སུམ་སྟོན་པར་སྒོམདེ་ནས་དར་སྐུད་ལ་ བརྒྱུས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་གམ་ནས་གཙང་མ་འབྲུ་བདུནཐལ་མོ་སྦྱར་བའི་བར་དུ་ བཟུང་ནསདད་གུས་རྩེ་གཅིག་པསi མཁྱེན་རབ་ཀྱི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ ཤྲ་ མ་ ཎ་ བདག་མིང་འདི་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་ ཚེ་ཕྱི་ མ་ གང་ དུ་སྐྱེ་བའི་ གནས་དེ་མི་བསླུ་བར་ གསལ་པོར་ད་ལྟ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྟན་དུ་ཞེས་པ་གསུམ་ནས་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བར་དུ་ གསོལ་བ་བཏབ་ནསལྷ་མོའི་ཕྱག་ཏུ་ཕུལ་བར་བསམས་ནསམགུལ་ཆིངས་ [འཆིངས]ཀྱི་བལ་དུ་གཏུམཁ་རྒྱན་ལ་བཏགས་ནས་འདོད་དོན་གང་ཡིན། དེ་གསོལ་བ་གདབ་པའི་ཡིད་རྩེ་གཅིག་པསཨོཾ་ཤྲ་མ་ཎ་པི་ཤ་ཙི་མུན་དྷེ་སྭཱཧཱ། ཞེས་པ་བཟླས་ཏེའདི་ཁྲི་ཚོ་བཅུ་གཉིས་བསྙེན་པ་བྱས་ས་འོག་གཏེར་ཡང་ དངོས་སུ་མཐོང་བར་འགྱུར་རོ་གསུང་ངོ་།། ཐུན་མཚམས་སུ་གོང་བཞིན་གསོལ་ བ་བཏབ་ཅིང་མེ་ཏོག་གཏོར་རོ༎ དེ་ནས་སྲོད་ཀྱི་དུས་སུ་ལྷག་པར་འབད་ཅིང་། མགོ་བོ་བྱང་། རྐང་པ་། ཁ་ནུབརྒྱབ་ཤར་དུ་བསྟན་ཏེམགོ་ལྷ་མའི་ [མོའི] པང་དུ་བཞག་པར་མོས་ཤིང་། སེམས་གཞན་དུ་མ་ཡེངས་[ གཡེང ] པར་བརྟག་བྱ་འབའ་ཞིག་ཡིད་ལ་བྱེད་སྔགས་ཤུབ་བུས་བཟླས་ཤིང་ཉལ་བ་མན་

> 312༄།འབྲོང་རྩེ་བེའུ་བུམ་ཆེན་མོ། མ་བགོས་ ། དབེན་པར་ འདུག་ལ་ ཁྲི་ བཟླས་ པས་ འགྲུབ་ པར་ འགྱུར་ རོ དགོང་མོ་ ཉལ་ ཁ་ རུ་ སྐ་ [ རྟ ] ལ་ བརྒྱ་ རྩ་ བརྒྱད་ མངོན་ པར་ བཟླས་ ནས། གདོངས་[ གདོང] དང་སྐང་[རྐང] པ་གཉིས་བགྲུས་[ བཀྲུས] ནས་རྨི་ལམ་དུ་ དགེ་བ་དང་མི་དགེ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་སྟོན་པར་འགྱུར་རོཨོཾ་ན་མ་སརྦ་ནམ། ས་ མྱག་སཾ་བུདྷ་གྲོ་[ཀྲོ]ཏི་ནམཏདྱ་ཐཱ། ཨོཾ་ཙ་ལེ་ཙུ་ལེ་ཙུན་དེ་མཧཱ་བིདྱ་སདྱ་བཱ་ ད་བ་ར་དེ་ག་ཐ་ཡགཱ་ཐ་ཡ་སྭཱཧཱ། གོང་དུ་བཤད་པའི་ཆོ་གས་མེ་ལོང་ ངམཁྲིའ། མར་མེའམ་ཆུ་ སྣོད་ལ་ སྟོན་ པར་འགྱུར་རོལན་བརྒྱ་རྩ་ བརྒྱད་མངོན་པར་སྔགས་པའི་ཆུས་གདོང་བགྲུས་[བཀྲུས]ལ་མི་སྨྲ་པར་ཉལ་ན་ རྨི་ལམ་དུ་དགེ་མི་དགེ་སྟོན་པར་འགྱུར་རོཨོཾ་མུ་ཙི་ལི་སྭཱཧཱམོ་ཧ་ནི་སྭཱཧཱ། དང་ཏི་ལི་སྭཱཧཱ། ཐབས་ མཁས་ཐུགས་ རྗེ་ ཚད་ མེད་པའིའགྲོ་ལ་ཕན་ པ་ གསུང་མཚད་[ མཛད ] པ།། རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་བཏུད་ནས།། སྐྱེ་བ་ དྲག་[བརྟག]པའི་ཆོ་ག་བྲི།། དེ་ཡང་ཟླ་བ་ཡར་ངོས་སྐར་མ་རྒྱལ་སྐོང་[ སྟོང] ལྟ་བུ་ལགནས་ཁང་བྱི་དོར་བྱ་བླ་བྲེ་ལ་སོགས་བྲིས་ལ་མཆོད་པ་ཅི་འབྱོར་པ་ བཤམས་ལགང་ གི་ རྨི་ ལམ་ བརྟག་ པའི་ བཅོམ་ ལྡན་ འདས་ ཀྱི་ རྟེན་ འགྲོ [བཀྲམ]། _ ཕྱོགས་བཞིར་མཐོ་[ཐོ]བཞི་བཙུགས་ལ་སྔགས་ཆུ་བྱུགས་པར་བྱས་ ཏེ། དེའི་སྔགས་འདིའོན་མོ་རཏྣ་ཏྲ་ཡཱ་། ན་མ་[མོ]ཨརྱ་ཨ་པ་གི་ཏེ་ཤ་ ར་ཡ་བོ་དྷེ་[དྷེ]ས་ཏྭ་ཡམཧཱ་ག་རུ་ནི་ག་ཡཏརྱ་ཐཱཏི་ལི་བྷནྟམི་ཙི་ལི་ མི་ལི་པ་མིན་བྷན་དྷ་ལིཞེས་ཉི་ཤུ་རྩ་གཅིག་བཟླས་པར་བྱའོ༎ དེ་ནས་ཁྲུས་ བྱས་། སྟན་ལ་འདུག་ཅིང་། སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་བྱཚད་མེད་བཞི་ བསྒོམདེ་ནས་གཏོར་མ་གསུམ་བཤམསདོང་ [ དང་ པོ ] ཡི་དྭགས་དང་ འབྱུང་པོ་ལ་བཏང་། དེ་ནས་ཆོས་སྐྱོང་ལརྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་ལ་བར་ཆད་མི་ འབྱུང་ཞིང་། བགེགས་ཀྱི་དབང་དུ་མི་འགྲོ་བར་ཆོ་ག་འདི་འགྲུབ་པར་མཛད་ དུ་གསོལཞེས་གསོལ་བ་བཏབ་བོགཏོར་མ་གཅིག་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ལ་ འབུལ། བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱི་ཐུགས་ཀཪ་དྲི་བཟང་བོའི་ཐིག་ལེ་གསུམ་བྱའོ

I1PD407; etext pages [p337]-[p342] = printed pp. 309-314), the whole of the section "three" up to the end of the White Tārā rite; the scribe's corrections stand in square brackets. [p337] Three: the three examinations of dreams relying on the goddess Śramaṇā, on Śākyamuni and on Tārā. Verse: bowing at the feet of the lama, the captain who holds sway over the ocean-island of the instructions (མན་ངག, man ngag), I write the oral advice for examining, in reality or in dream, all the wishes of this life, the temporary and the final.
The examination of dreams through the goddess Śramaṇā (ཤྲ་མ་ཎ, shra ma Na) has two parts: first, clearing the opposing conditions and gathering the favourable; second, the sādhana.
First: at a good time in the waxing moon, in a solitary place made clean, on a square earth-mandala smeared with fragrant scent set a vase filled with its essence and clean water, its neck adorned with white wool and its mouth with clean branches of fresh juniper and the like, a canopy of blue cloth over it; clean offerings around; whatever images of the Three Jewels are at hand are set out. Cut the going out and in of discursive thought; make firm boundaries of body and speech; until the rite is complete keep the fast (སྙུང་གནས, snyung gnas), at the least no afternoon food, no meat, liquor or the three of the garlic kind; in particular avoid the (wrong hours of eating(?)) and food that carries defilement (གྲིབ, grib). Before the Three Jewels, at the three times, confess strongly through the four powers: this is the key point.
Second: the sādhana. Refuge, the arising of the mind of enlightenment, the four immeasurables first; purify into emptiness by the mantra svabhāva; from that, a vase made of the four precious substances on Mount Meru, and over it all [p338] the trunk of a wish-fulfilling tree filling the beryl ground, with branches, leaves, flowers and fruit of many kinds; the canopy above, meditated as the blue sky. On Meru the goddess Śramaṇā, blue, one face and two hands, the right holding a «རྔ་ཡབ» (rnga yab) and a mirror, the left a branch of the wish-fulfilling tree; the two feet stretched out, seated on Meru in a posture of ease, leaning her back on the tree's trunk, slender-waisted, full-breasted, smiling and looking at oneself; adorned with an upper and a lower garment of many silks and with jewels and flowers of many kinds, beautiful and pleasing; knowing all that is to be known of the three times, she shows me directly all prophecies: so one meditates.
Then, holding in joined palms flowers or seven clean grains threaded on silk, with faith and one-pointed mind: "Lady Śramaṇā, lord of knowing, I, named so-and-so, wherever I shall be born in the next life, show that place unmistakably and clearly, now!" This is said from three up to twenty-one times; one imagines it offered into the goddess's hand. The neck-thread of wool is then tied to the vase's neck adornment (?) and, with the wish to ask whatever one wishes and one-pointed mind, the mantra «oṃ śramaṇa piśaci muṇḍe svāhā» is recited. If one does twelve times ten thousand of it as the preliminary, one will see even the treasure under the earth directly, so it is said. At the session breaks the supplication is made as above and flowers are scattered. Then in the evening one strives especially: head to the north, feet [to the south: the word is missing in the etext], face to the west, back to the east; trust that the head lies in the goddess's lap; with mind undistracted, thinking only of the matter to be examined, reciting the mantra under the breath, one goes to sleep.
[p339] The oral instruction: for one night not more than one matter, so the first, one important matter only, is kept in mind and examined; when it is done, one turns the wish to others. By this one can examine, for oneself and others, the earlier and later births, long or short life, whether the meaning is good, what helps and what harms and the rest, and, so long as enlightenment is not attained, all that is plainly seen and the sure and the good. Of the ways it comes, the best is a direct prophecy or what rises as a meditation experience (ཉམས་སྣང, nyams snang); the middle a sound in the sky; what is not clear is cast aside and done again and again, and later it comes clear. By the power of the oral instruction the dream is fixed for a while as good or bad, but by the power of virtue and sin it can change: if good comes, one asks for better; if bad, one asks to be shown a means of turning it back, and then strives at that means. So practising, the best are accomplished in three days, the middle in (a longer time), the lowest in seven. If it does not come, it is a sign of much defilement and sin: guard against defilement, keep the boundaries, make the confession of sins as one is able, repeat three times: that it will not come is impossible, so he said. All that appears must not be told to people, for it is unclear to them: secrecy is important.
"Among the examinations of dreams there is none like this instruction of Śramaṇā, an emanation of Tārā; the lama says so, and it is established by experience." In the bedroom, one washes with water over which «ru rta» (རུ་རྟ; the etext's «སྟ» with the correction «རྟ») was recited 108 times with the mantra, keeps the matter in mind and lies down without speaking: in the dream the good and the not good are prophesied. Mantra «oṃ śramaṇa piśaci nimuṇḍe svā». Sitting at the foot of a «བ་རུ» (ba ru) tree or a banyan (ནྱ་གྲོ་དྷ, nyagrodha) tree, not speaking at all, one who recites a hundred thousand accomplishes it, and the conduct of the world will also be told. Or, having bathed well and put on clean clothes, [p340] sitting in solitude, one who recites ten thousand accomplishes it. In the evening, at the place of lying, «ru rta» (the etext's «རུ་སྐ», corrected «རྟ») is recited over 108 times, face and both feet are washed with it, and in the dream the good and the not good are shown. Mantra «oṃ namo sarvanam samyak sam buddha kroti nam tadyathā, oṃ cale cule cunde mahā vidyā sadya vāda vara de ga tha ya gā tha ya svāhā». By the rite told above it will also show in a mirror, a throne (?), a lamp or a vessel of water: with water over which 108 times the mantra was said one washes the face and lies silent, and in the dream the good and the not good are shown. Mantra «oṃ mucili svāhā, mohani svāhā, and tili svāhā».
The Śākyamuni rite begins: "Bowing to the Complete Buddha, skilled in means, of measureless compassion, who spoke for the good of beings, I write the rite for examining births (the etext's «དྲག» for «བརྟག», corrected in its brackets)." The time: in the waxing moon, under a constellation like Rgyal (the etext's «རྒྱལ་སྐོང» with the bracket «སྟོང»; not settled). The room is swept; a canopy and the like are drawn; the offerings at hand are set out; the image of the Blessed One who examines dreams is spread out; at the four directions four stakes (?) are set and anointed with mantra water. Its mantra «oṃ mo ratna traya ... ārya apagite śaraya bodhe ...» is recited twenty-one times. Then one bathes, sits on the seat, makes refuge, the mind of enlightenment and the four immeasurables, then sets out three tormas: the first is given to the pretas and the spirits; then to the Dharma protector: "may no obstacle come to the examination of dreams, may I not fall under the power of the demons, may this rite be accomplished!" so the supplication is made; one torma is offered to the Blessed One; at the Bhagavān's heart three drops of fragrant scent are made. [p341] Offering, praise and the seven limbs are made and the supplication is made again and again. Then, through bathing and a conduct that stands in perfect order, speech is cut off, and as the preliminary recitation, though it is said that ten thousand are to be done, five hundred thousand (?) are done («འབུམ་ཕྲག་ལྔའི་བདུན་བྱ» in the etext, the number not settled). When reciting, one is generated as one's own deity; later, when the rite is applied to the work, the image and the offerings are set out as before and the seven-limb prayer is made many times. With the truth-words (?) and having bathed at the place of lying with mantra water, with one mind keeping in thought the matter, the body to be taken in the next life, the deed to be done and the like, one lies down not further than an arm-span from the Bhagavān's body, the head turned to the east or the north, in the posture of the Bhagavān's passing into nirvāṇa. The Bhagavān then gives the prophecy, and his body is also seen. Speech is cut off and nothing else is said. If it does not come in the (first) night, it is repeated up to the third and beyond. Of this the best is a meditation experience (ཉམས་སྣང); the middle, another shows it; the lowest dreams it through a sign and a hint. Mantra «oṃ buddha curu mācuru ke svā». This is the examination through the Venerable Śākyamuni.
White Tārā: (after the mark ༈) the instruction for the examination of dreams through White Tārā, by which dependent origination arises as clairvoyance: first one meditates one's own deity and does the full self-generation once; from that state one generates oneself as the Venerable White Tārā; in the heart a lotus opens, in it a white tāṃ, blazing with light, so that the inside of the body is empty and filled with fire (?). Four silver mirrors are imagined in the four directions of the tāṃ, with the letters A, NU, TA, [RA] on the east, south, west and north mirrors, their images rising one after another. [p342] Light goes out from the tāṃ, draws Tārā from Akaniṣṭha, she is absorbed, and the supplication that dependent origination arise as clairvoyance is made. Then whatever one wishes is imagined as two white tāṃ, from which light goes out and does the work of the realm («བྱི [ཕྱིའི] འགྲོ་དོན», the outer good of beings), in particular accomplishing one's own aim; a light like a star-arrow comes from the tāṃ and strikes the point between the eyebrows; with the wish for accomplishment the tāṃ is dissolved without remainder into the tāṃ at the heart, and one imagines that whatever one wishes will be told in a dream without doubt. One goes to sleep in the lion's lying posture (སེང་གེའི་ཉལ་སྟབས). The A on the east mirror: all the appearances marked by A are accomplished. The south, with NU: all the omens are accomplished. The west, with TA: all the appearances of omens that are true come true; what is not true does not come true. The north, with RA: the appearance of living beings (སྲོག་ཆགས, srog chags) is accomplished; no other is.

End of the Śākyamuni rite (the frame it shares). After the accomplishment of the recitation, 500,000 or 700,000 (it is said 10 million is taught), the rite is applied in the work: the shrine and offerings are set out as before, the seven-limb prayer is made seven times, and before lying down one washes with water charged by mantra and holds in mind the matter to be asked, the form of the body, what is to be done and the like, with one pointed intent. One lies down not further than an arm's length from the Buddha's body, the head turned east or north, in the posture of the Buddha's passing into nirvāṇa. The Buddha then gives a prophecy and the body is seen. It is cut off by deciding the doubt; no other doubt remains. If it does not come on the first night, the rite is repeated up to the third night and further. The best sign is a meditation appearance (nyams snang); the middle, another one showing it; the lowest, a sign and a hint. Mantra «oṃ buddha curu mācuru ki svāhā».

The White Tārā rite (title: the examination of dreams through White Tārā; the instruction by which dependent origination arises as clairvoyance). First one meditates one's own deity and does the full self-generation once. From that state one generates the Venerable White Tārā; in the heart, a lotus opened and a white tāṃ blazing with light that fills the inside of the body. Light goes out from the tāṃ and draws Tārā from Akaniṣṭha with her retinue; she is honoured and asked that dependent origination arise as clairvoyance. One imagines four silver mirrors in the four directions of the tāṃ, with the letters A, NU, TA, RA on the east, south, west and north mirrors, the images appearing one after another. Whatever one wishes is imagined as two white tāṃ, from which light goes out to do the work of the realm, in particular one's own aim; a light like a star-arrow from the tāṃ strikes the point between one's eyebrows; with the wish for accomplishment the tāṃ is dissolved without remainder into the tāṃ at the heart, and one imagines that whatever one wants will be told in a dream without doubt. One lies down in the lion posture.

Reading of the mirrors. The east mirror, bearing A: appearances marked by A are accomplished. The south, with NU: all omens (mtshan ma) are accomplished. The west, with TA: all appearances of omens come true, and what does not come true does not accomplish. The north, with RA: the appearances of living beings (srog chags; the Delhi machine reading had «སྤོག་ཆཊ», RD240) are accomplished, and no others.
