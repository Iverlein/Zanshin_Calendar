The seventh chapter of the Explanatory Tantra (the signs of the body's destruction, ལུས་ཀྱི་འཇིག་ལྟས, lus kyi 'jig ltas) in the Drathang woodblock print (BDRC I4CZ327131, 2622 px, one folio side an image), the opening on img. 41 and the verse of the bad dreams at the head of img. 42. The chapter opens by naming four kinds of sign, distant, near, uncertain and certain, and the distant ones as the messenger (ཕོ་ཉ, pho nya), the dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and the change of bearing (རྣམ་འགྱུར, rnam 'gyur). Img. 42 holds the verse that names the bad dreams of the sick (riding a corpse, a buffalo, a horse, a pig or a donkey naked toward the south; a tree growing from the heart; falling into a pit; lying in a charnel ground; the head cut off; entering the mother's womb again; carried off by water; sinking in mud; drinking and dancing with the dead) as the signs that the lord of death has the sick; then the lines that give the cause (the passages of the mind's entry blocked by the illness), who dies of them (the sick who dream them constantly), the healthy (uncertain; the rite of service, རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro, brings release) and the six kinds of dream. The words of the verse are as Yigdzin-1 reads them; the line **དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ་རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ** was read by eye (crops x 150–560 and 385–500, y 250–312, 5–14×, the row second from the foot of the page): after མི the syllable has a boxed བ as its first letter and then a small ཟ and ང (the root is small and overlaid by an interlinear note's strokes), and the last syllable is the boxed བ; **མི་བཟང་བ**, not མི་ཟད་པ, which has no prefix བ.

The seventh chapter of the Explanatory Tantra (བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud), the signs of the body's destruction, the distant sign that is the dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam), in the Drathang print: the verse lines of img. 42 from the end of its first line to img. 43, line 2. The English follows the verse in order, a sentence to two or four verse lines; the Tibetan is set one verse line to a line (a few lines run two verse lines together, as Yigdzin-1 reads them with its shads).

- Dreams: a cat (བྱི་ལ, byi la), a monkey, a tiger, a fox; riding a corpse (རོ, ro): the sign that the lord of death (འཆི་བདག, 'chi bdag) has seized the sick.
- A buffalo, a horse, a pig, a donkey, the rnga mo (རྔ་མོ, rnga mo; Khro ru's gloss takes it for the female dzo, ཇོ་མོ, jo mo): ridden naked and going toward the south: he dies.
- From the crown a shoot (ལྕུག་མ, lcug ma) grows, with a bird's nest on it.
- A palm (ཏཱ་ལ, tā la) or a thorny tree grows in the heart.
- A lotus (པདྨ, padma) drawn out; falling into a precipice; lying in a charnel ground (དུར་ཁྲོད, dur khrod).
- The head broken (མགོ་ཆག, mgo chag; the commentaries of Byang pa, Rin spungs pa and Khro ru: cut off); ringed round by crows, hungry ghosts (ཡི་དགས, yi dags) and the «rme can» (རྨེ་ཅན, left untranslated).
- The skin of the legs falling off; entering the mother's womb; carried off by water; sinking in mud; devoured by fish.
- Finding iron and gold; trading; losing in a fight.
- Asking the tax of the bro pa (བྲོ་པ, bro pa, left untranslated; Khro ru: the portion of food and goods); taking a bride.
- Sitting naked; the hair and beard shaved off.
- Drinking beer (ཆང, chang) with the dead (གཤིན, gshin) and being dragged along by them.
- Adorned with red clothes and a red garland; dancing together with the dead (གཤིན་པོ, gshin po).
- These are the signs that the sick has gone into the power of the lord of death.
- These and the like are bad dreams; the passages of the mind's entry (ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག, yid 'jug bu ga) are blocked by the illness, and so they are seen.
- The sick who dream them constantly die; for the one without illness it is not certain, and the rite of service (རིམ་གྲོ, rim gro) frees him.
- Six kinds: what was seen, heard and felt; a dream after a prayer; one that came true; and one that arose from illness.
- A dream of the early night is forgotten and has no fruit; one seen at dawn and clear gives its fruit.
- The good dreams, signs of long life and wealth: a god, the bull of the herd (ཁྱུ་མཆོག, khyu mchog), a man of renown; a blazing fire, a lake, the body smeared with blood or with filth; white clothes worn, a pennant, a parasol, a tree, grain found; climbing a mountain, a fine house or a tree with fruit; riding a lion, an elephant, a horse or a cow; crossing a great river or lake, going north-east; escaping distress, defeating an enemy; offerings made to oneself by the gods and by father and mother. Whoever dreams any of these has long life, no illness, and finds wealth.
