**The rite.** In a quiet place, at a good time, in the evening, bathed and clean, one sets out a painted or sculpted image of the Noble Lord of the World (འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག, 'jig rten dbang phyug; Avalokiteśvara) and sits before it, or only visualises it; blesses the offerings with Oṃ āḥ hūṃ three times; sees Avalokiteśvara with his circle of buddhas and bodhisattvas in the sky before one; makes offerings with the mantra Oṃ ārya lokeśvara saparivāra argham … (to the end, pratīccha ye svāhā); praises (Praised by all the buddhas, collection of every excellence, called Spyan ras gzigs, I bow, ever loving); prays: "you of great compassion, think of me with love, and show me clearly in tonight's dream, without confusion, whatever matter I wish to examine", and prays so long with fierce one-pointed devotion; then, never losing the image of the Noble One and his retinue, lies with the head to the south and the face to the east on a bed spread with kuśa grass over clean ground, in clean clothes, recites the three mantras (Oṃ mucili svāhā; Oṃ mohani svāhā; Oṃ dantali svāhā) twenty-one times each, blesses the left palm and breathes on the mouth twenty-one times, thinks "let him show me clearly what I wish", and, mindful and with the perception of the appearance unbroken, turns onto the right side in the lion's posture and sleeps, to examine the dream.

**The time.** «དེ་ཡང་མཚན་མོ་ལ་ཆ་བཞིར་བགོས་པའི་ཐུན་རྗེས་མ་ལ་བརྟག་དགོས་ཤིང་།»: the night is divided into four parts, and the examination must be of the last watch (ཐུན, thun); the signs, as they came, must not be forgotten, and the way of judging them is then known. «འདི་ནི་ཁུངས་ཁྱད་པར་ཅན་དང་ལྡན་པའི་གདམས་པ་ཟབ་མོ་ཡིད་ཆེས་ཅན་ཡིན་ནོ།»: this is a profound instruction with a special source, to be trusted (the photostat reads «གདུངས་ཀྱང་བར་ཅན», a slip). Colophon: written by Dam tshig rdo rje as a seeker of the meaning wished.
