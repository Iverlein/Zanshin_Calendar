Four pages (the title and the text in three images) of a short ritual attributed to Bu ston, headed "the examination of dreams" in BDRC's catalogue; the title line on the scan is read by the machine as «རྨི་ལམ་རྟག་པ་བཞུགས་སོ», with «རྟག» for the expected «བརྟག» (doubt, below). The text is not a list of dream signs. It is a rite for a person who wants to examine birth and death (the next birth), done in a very quiet place, from the first day of the waxing moon.

The preparation: a clean body and mind, meat, beer, garlic and the like and the afternoon meal given up; a clean room with a mandala, nine heaps of fragrant flowers on a good stand, a Buddha image or relic-holding support set on it, five offerings in front, the seven-limbed offering, a prayer that no obstacle arise, and a cake offering (gtor ma) to the spirits. The practitioner imagines the Buddha Śākyamuni with his retinue filling the sky in front, and recites his mantra (as the machine reads it: oṃ buddha tsu ru ma tsu ru ke svāhā): ten thousand times as the approach, a hundred and eight times at bedtime, the same in the afternoons; at bedtime the wish is asked three times, strongly, one wish at a time, and a request is never more than one at a time. A second account (p. 827 of the print) repeats the rite with the Buddha's heart-mantra garland, the approach of ten myriad recitations (the machine reads «འབུམ་ཕྲག་བཅུ་གཅིག», eleven hundred thousand), then from the first of each waxing moon ten thousand in the early part, whatever is fitting in the afternoon, a hundred and one at bedtime, and water blessed with it for washing.

The sleep: with the request "show me in a dream how the Blessed One takes his birth", the dreamer lies in the lion's posture (right side) and sleeps. The signs: sounds, signs and marks of the six kinds of beings, which if they do not come once are tried again with the rite. When the signs of the bad migrations (ངན་འགྲོ, ngan 'gro) appear they will not harm the body or life, and virtue is to be done. The colours of the light seen: white, the gods; the machine reads next "green: human; yellow: demi-god (lha min)", then blue, animals; red, hungry ghosts (yi dwags); black, the hells; all shown as seen, heard or touched forms. The order green-human and yellow-demi-god is the machine's and is not settled on the scan; it may be reversed.

The lineage given after the rite (p. 827), as read: the paṇḍita Vikhyātadeva (as the machine spells «བི་ཁྱད་དད་དེ་པ»), the great paṇḍita Śākyaśrī, Dhanaśrī, the teacher Jam gsar (Mañjuśrī the new), the teacher Rwa/Rgwa lo (the machine reads «རྒ་ལོ»), the great adept Ze dmar ba Sangs rgyas ye shes, the abbot Bu ston. The colophon on p. 828 reads, by the machine, as the note of a monk Sgra tshad pa, and ends with the wish that the ocean of suffering of cyclic existence be dried up. The page after (img. 837) is the print's imprint: the Palbrtsegs Tibetan manuscripts institute, set in 2008, volume 28.

Nothing in the text names a good or bad dream image, a time of the night, or a source for them, apart from "the tantra" for how the recitation goes ("as the tantra says").
