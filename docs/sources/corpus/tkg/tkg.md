> ལྟས་ངན་པའི་སྐབས་གཞན་དུའང་བྱས་ན་ཤིན་ཏུ་ལེགས་ལ་ཟློག་ཐབས་ཀྱང་རྒྱས་པར་སྣང་ངོ། །གསུམ་པ་ནི། རས་བྲིས་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་དབང་བསྐུར་བ་སྔར་བཤད་པའི་སློབ་དཔོན་གསུམ་ལས། ངག་དབང་གྲགས་པས་སྟ་གོན་གང་ཡང་མ་བཤད་པར་

Four images of a mandala-consecration rite (vol. 3), the part where the disciples dream before the consecration and the teacher asks them what they saw. BDRC's title for this division names good and bad dream signs; the section's own heading is not on these images, and img. 388 names the work the rite belongs to as «རྡོ་རྗེ་འཆང་ཆེན་པོའི་ལམ་གྱི་རིམ་པ་གསང་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་གནད་རྣམ་པར་ཕྱེ་བ» (the opening of the key points of all secrets, the stages of the path of great Vajradhara), from which the rite's steps are drawn. The scan's own stamp reads one less than BDRC's image number (00384 on img. 386).

Img. 385: the disciples are protected by the winding (འཁྱིལ་བ) mantra and given the mantra to recite; each is told to look into his dream and tell it to the teacher in the morning, and is laid down in the lion's posture, on a ground spread with kusha grass, facing east (the General Tantra, སྤྱི་རྒྱུད, is quoted for this); a verse of Saraha's is quoted for the same laying down.

Img. 386: at dawn the teacher gives a cake offering and asks the disciples their dreams. If they saw the Three Jewels, friends and the like, he makes them glad; if they dreamt of falling from a mountain and the like, they are protected. The night is divided in four parts and the dreams of the first three have no certainty, so the fourth is taken. A verse from the Mañjuśrī root tantra (as the text names it) gives each part its cause: the first part's dream arises from phlegm (བད་ཀན, bad kan); the second's is bile (མཁྲིས་པ, mkhris pa), what is blamed in the world; the third's is wind (རླུང, rlung); the fourth's arises truly. For a bad dream the Garland (ཕྲེང་བ) says to protect with the nectar-winding mantra, and Lamp-maker the Good (མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོ) is quoted next (continued on img. 387: the obstacles are destroyed by Vighnāntaka).

Img. 387: Nāgabodhi (Klu byang) says to meditate on selflessness; the General Tantra, a peaceful fire offering; Dārika-pa says that a bad dream sign of a disciple is overcome by all the rites, and gives the teacher many ways of reversing it in a verse: a cake offering to all the field-protecting ḍākinīs, the hundred-syllable mantra, the protection wheel, recitation, armour deities, supplication, divine pride in the six ornaments and the fire offering. These are the seven means of reversal, set down by the text for the Cakrasamvara (Bde mchog) practice and said to hold for the others; done also on other occasions of bad dream signs they are very good. Then the consecration on a painted cloth begins, with the three teachers already named; Ngag dbang grags pa gives no preliminaries.

Img. 388 is on preliminaries of the vase and the disciple, the meditation mandala and the body mandala, with the author's verse on the preliminaries and the headings of the main rite (the thread, the powders, the vases); it has no dream words.

No dream image is judged good or bad in the section except the two on img. 386: the Three Jewels and friends (glad), a fall from a mountain (to be guarded against). The sources named are tantric: the General Tantra, the Mañjuśrī root tantra, the Garland, Saraha, Nāgabodhi, Dārika-pa, Lamp-maker the Good, Ngag dbang grags pa.
