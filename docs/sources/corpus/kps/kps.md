## <a id="kps-p232-236"></a>kps:p232-236 Chapter XLII, the interpretation of dreams, and its rule of the quarters of the night

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> (English only: the Devanagari of the original, which Iyer prints above his translation, is missing from the text layers of the four scans of the edition read; the Sanskrit verse was not reached.)
> 
> "The Effect of Dreams during the 1st quarter of the night will be realised in a year; that of dreams of the 2nd quarter, in six months; the influence of dreams in the 3rd quarter will be evident in a month; dreams before dawn will be realised in twelve days; dreams before sunrise announce their effects in a day. If there be two dreams, the effects of the latter alone will prevail. It is well to go to sleep after an evil dream; this should be divulged. Good dreams, however, should always be kept secret."

Kālaprakāśikā ch. XLII, the end of the chapter on dreams (Iyer's translation, 1917; printed p. 236 of the 1982 reprint; the chapter runs pp. 232–236).

Five spans, by the part of the night: the first quarter of the night, a year; the second, six months; the third, a month; "before dawn", twelve days; "before sunrise", a day. Of two dreams the later one prevails. After a bad dream one goes back to sleep and tells it; a good dream is kept secret.

The chapter before this sentence lists good dreams (the sun and moon, a virgin with a lotus, milk, a head cut off in blood, the Vedas heard, and others, with their gains and losses) and bad dreams (death, disease, loss); none of the images carries a day of the lunar month, a weekday, a month or a season. The next chapter (XLIII, Śrījayantī) is the birthday of Kṛṣṇa on the eighth of the dark half of Śrāvaṇa and has nothing to do with dreams.
