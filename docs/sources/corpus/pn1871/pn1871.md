The table on f. 1r (the lower half of the first image, canvas 1; the upper half of the sheet is blank): a ruled table in two halves, each row beginning with an abbreviation of one lunar mansion (nakṣatra) in the left-hand column of its half, then four columns of numbers, and at the right the gift for it. The gifts read are: coral (pravāla-dāna), a cow (go-dāna), food (anna-dāna), cloth (ambara-dāna, vastra-dāna), a yellow cloth (pīta-dāna), gold (suvarṇa-dāna), copper (tāmra-dāna), bronze (kāṃsya-dāna), a red cloth (ārakta-dāna), the sacrifice of the nine planets (navagraha-makha), a gift of ... (gopya-dāna? doubtful), pearls (muktāphala-dāna) and a buffalo cow (mahiṣī-dāna). The headings read "nakṣatra" and "... dāna" (the rest of the left heading, "caraṇa…", is doubtful), and one cell reads "kṛṣṇājinavast…"/"kṛṣṇājina" (a black antelope skin).
It is a table of gifts by mansion, set before the text: no dream, watch, month or fortnight is named in it. The numbers (5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and others) were not read for their meaning, and the mansion abbreviations were not each identified.

## <a id="pn1871-i2"></a>pn1871:i2 The first text leaf of the Svapnādhyāya: the spans of the watches (lines 1–4)

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD277), lines 1–9 of the leaf at 2× on the partly delivered image (1632 × 1800 px, the lower part of the sheet not yet delivered when read); the catalogue's colophon and date from the TEI file.*

> ॥ श्रीगणेशाय नमः ॥ स्वप्नाध्यायं प्रवक्ष्यामि यथोक्तं गुरुभाषितं ।
> येन विज्ञायते सर्व(?)नराणां वस्तु शुभाशुभं ॥
> स्वप्नस्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरफलं भवेत् ।
> द्वितीये चाष्टभिर्मासैः त्रिभिर्मासैस्त्रियामके ॥
> चतुर्थे प्रहरे स्वप्नो मासैकेन विपच्यते ।
> अरुणोदयवेलायां दशाहेन फलं भवेत् ॥
> गोविसर्जनवेलायां सद्यः स्वप्नो विधीयते ।
> वातिकं पैत्तिकं चैव चिंतितं च विवर्जितं ॥ आरो[हणं …]

Image 2 (3014_0002) of the OPenn item, labelled in the TEI "1v [=2v]-2r [=3r]": the first leaf of text, the sheet 13 × 24 cm with a double red margin line at the sides, ten lines a leaf, the sacred syllables and the verse ends touched with red and yellow. The page opens, after the salutation to Gaṇeśa, with the promise of "the chapter of dreams as it was spoken, as the teacher said it". Our Roman reading of the verses: *svapnas tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-phalaṃ bhavet | dvitīye cāṣṭabhir māsais tribhir māsais triyāmake || caturthe prahare svapno māsaikena vipacyate | aruṇodaya-velāyāṃ daśāhena phalaṃ bhavet || go-visarjana-velāyāṃ sadyaḥ svapno vidhīyate | vātikaṃ paittikaṃ caiva cintitaṃ ca vivarjitaṃ ||*

What the passage says, in our words:

- **The series**: a dream of the first watch (*yāma*) has its fruit in a year; of the second, in eight months (*aṣṭabhir māsaiḥ*); of the third, in three months; a dream of the fourth watch (*caturthe prahare*) ripens in **one month** (*māsaikena*); at the dawn's red (*aruṇodaya*) in ten days; at the hour of the cows' release, at once.
- The dream of wind and of bile and the dream of what was thought of are set aside (line 4), as in the other texts of this family (the Svapnohana's "what was in the mind by day", svo:p268).
- The lines 5 to 9 begin the list of good dreams ("ārohaṇaṃ go-vṛṣa-kuñjarāṇāṃ prāsāda-śailāgra-vanaspatīnāṃ …", climbing a cow, bull, elephant, palace, mountain peak, tree, as in the printed Svapnādhyāya of osv:i3) and were read for the match only.
- **What the TEI file says**: the title *Svapnādhyāya* (alternative title Svapnādhyāyastotra, given by the cataloguer), the colophon of the first work "iti bṛhaspatyuktasvapnādhyāyaḥ samāptah" (f. 4r: "the dream chapter spoken by Bṛhaspati is finished"), a table on f. 1r, a hymn to the nine planets (Navagrahastotra, f. 4v) attributed in the catalogue to Kālidāsa, the date "circa 1700–1850, 18th–19th century", no scribe's name, and the Pariśiṣṭa label of the catalogue's summary. The colophon was not seen on an image (see Doubts).

Doubts: line 1's "sarva(?)" before "narāṇāṃ" is not clear to the eye. "aṣṭabhir" in line 2 is clear (the conjunct ṣṭa and the bhi are plain at 2×); "māsaikena" in line 3 is clear; the same wording as manuscript a of Negelein's list for the second half-line (neg:p14-16). Not read: lines 5–9 beyond their start, the rest of the sheet and f. 2–5 (the download had delivered only their upper lines).

## <a id="pn1871-i2b"></a>pn1871:i2b The first text leaf of the Svapnādhyāya read again on Penn's full images: verses 1–5, the spans of the watches in v. 2–3

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), on the full-size page image.*

> ॥ श्रीगणेशाय नमः ॥ स्वप्नाध्यायं प्रवक्ष्यामि यथोक्तं गुरुभाषितं ।
> येन विज्ञायते सर्वं नराणां वस्तु शुभाशुभं ॥ १ ॥
> स्वप्नस्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरफलं भवेत् ।
> द्वितीये चाष्टभिर्मासैस्त्रिभिर्मासैस्त्रियामके ॥ २ ॥
> चतुर्थे प्रहरे स्वप्नो मासैकेन विपच्यते ।
> अरुणोदयवेलायां दशाहेन फलं भवेत् ॥ ३ ॥
> गोविसर्जनवेलायां सद्यः स्वप्नो विधीयते ।
> वातिकं पैत्तिकं चैव चिंतितं च विवर्जितं ॥ ४ ॥
> आरोहणं गोवृषकुंजराणां प्रासादशैलाग्रवनस्पतीनां ।
> विष्ठानुलेपोपरुदितं च स्वप्ने सुगम्यागमनं च धन्यं ॥ ५ ॥

v. 1: Homage to Gaṇeśa. "I shall tell the chapter on dreams as it was spoken, as the teacher said it, by which the good and the bad of men's affairs is known."
v. 2: "A dream of the first watch bears its fruit in a year; of the second, in eight months; of the third watch, in three months."
v. 3: "A dream in the fourth watch ripens in one month (māsaikena); at the dawn's red, the fruit comes in ten days."
v. 4: "At the hour of the cows' release a dream is held to bear fruit at once; the dream of wind and of bile, and what was thought of, are to be set aside."
v. 5: "To climb a cow, a bull, an elephant, a palace, a mountain peak, a tree; to be smeared with dung, and to weep; and going to a woman of whom it is right to go: these are blessed in a dream."

The verse numbers 1 to 5 are the scribe's, in red-touched double strokes at the end of each pair of half-lines; the page has ten lines and the fifth verse ends in line 7. The English is ours.

## <a id="pn1871-i4"></a>pn1871:i4 The end of the Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya and its colophon on f. 4r, with the start of the Navagrahastotra

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), on the full-size page image.*

> … नागवल्लीं (?) … प्रातरुत्थाय यः पठेत् (?) ।
> दुःस्वप्ननाशनं तस्य सुस्वप्नं च भविष्यति ॥
> इति बृहस्पत्युक्तस्वप्नाध्यायः समाप्तः ॥ ॥
> अथ नवग्रहस्तोत्रं लिख्यते ॥ भास्वान् काश्यपगोत्रजोऽरुणरुचिः सिंहस्य राज्येश्वरः …

The last verses of the Svapnādhyāya and its colophon, on the lower page of the fourth leaf (f. 4r, third line down): the one who reads the chapter after rising in the morning will have his bad dream destroyed and a good dream will come to him (the middle words of the line before it, "nāgavallīṃ …", are not read); then the colophon "iti bṛhaspatyukta-svapnādhyāyaḥ samāptaḥ", "the dream chapter spoken by Bṛhaspati is finished"; then, in the next line, "now the hymn to the nine planets is written", and the first verse of it, which begins with the sun ("bhāsvān kāśyapagotrajo ..."), the nine planets in the order of the week with their lords and signs.
The colophon names no scribe and no date. The numbered verses of the page above it run to 52 ("... tasya śrīḥ sarvatomukhī ॥ 52 ॥", the white sandal and white flower, in a line that stands at the head of the lower page).
