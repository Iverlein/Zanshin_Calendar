UT, ch. 7, the marriage reckoning, its short practice (ལག་ལེན་མདོར་བསྡུས, lag len mdor bsdus) and the bride's going: printed pp. 240–243 (etext VEIE0OPI6328F058_I4767, pp. 262–265; printed page = etext page − 22), from BDRC's etext only; no scan of this edition is on BDRC. A modern edition: quoted no further than its facts need.

UT opens the short practice by saying that the ways of reckoning a bride's going are without end, and that all of them agreeing good is hard to meet in any match but for a few of great merit such as a universal monarch, so that this practice is enough to tell the good from the bad as it is (p. 240). It then names what to avoid by the year, the trigram, the mewa and the road (pp. 240–241), and adds: these must be avoided, or else mended (p. 242, «འདི་དག་འཛེམ་དགོས་ཡང་ན་བཅོས»). Under the planet and mansion it quotes A rā ga (ཨ་རཱ་ག, a rA ga): "a very good mansion is not [as good as] a *grub sbyor*; a very bad mansion is not [as bad as] a *rkyen skyon*" (p. 242; the sense is not clear). Under the directions, "the devil, the executioner and the bane" (ཕྱོགས་བདུད་གཤེད་རྐྱེན, phyogs bdud gshed rkyen): for the direction, avoiding the bad earth lords that go about by year, month, day and hour, and in particular the faces of the sky dog, the *the khyim*, the earth goddess, *ki kang* and the *hal khyi*, is the crux (གནད, gnad); "since the day's and the month's sky dog are the chief, give [her] the belly and the like in a good way" (p. 242); and of the three, the *the khyim* as the bride's devil, the black sky dog as her executioner and the earth goddess as her bane, "these three must be avoided above all" (pp. 242–243).

What it gives: within the bride's going the earth lords of the direction are «the crux», and among them the day's and month's sky dog «the chief»; neither is set against the planet, mansion or date, which UT takes under their own head before it.
