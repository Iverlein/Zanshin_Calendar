# UT, the third part's fourth chapter: the golden rosary of rise and decline, the karma reckoning

*The Wondrous Examination of the Element Reckoning, a Garland of Blue
Utpalas* (see [ch1-results.md](ch1-results.md) for the work and its editor):
its third part's fourth chapter, "the examination of the golden rosary of
rise and decline" (དར་རྒུད་གསེར་འཕྲེང་གི་དཔྱད་དོན, dar rgud gser 'phreng gi dpyad
don; contents, etext p. 13), the birth's hundred with its rise and decline,
which its colophon (p. 175) calls the karma reckoning (ལས་རྩིས, las rtsis). A
modern edition, quoted for its facts. Read 2026-10-25 for questions 355 and
388 in BDRC's etext (VEIE0OPI6328F058_I4767; printed page = etext page − 22,
the printed number at each page's foot); sources.yaml names no scan of the
edition, so every passage here is etext only. Its sections, by the contents:
the single examination (p. 149), the general (p. 153), the overall (སྤོམ་དཔྱད,
spom dpyad, p. 154), the examination of the verdicts (ཁ་དམར་དཔྱད་པ, kha dmar
dpyad pa) and the side verdicts (ཟུར་གྱི་ཁ་དམར, zur gyi kha dmar, p. 155), the
karma reckoning's sheet (ལས་རྩིས་ཁྲ་མ, las rtsis khra ma, p. 159), the trigram's
karma reckoning (p. 165), and to the day's karma reckoning and the virtue at
the time of death (pp. 173–175).

UT, the close of the birth's hundred in its karma reckoning (etext pp. 175–177, printed pp. 153–155), after each pebble's own reading (pp. 149–153, where every aspect's own, mother, son, friend and enemy pebble has a reading white and black, as in WB vol. 1, pp. 329–331). Etext only; «[…]» marks words the OCR garbles; «…» marks the remedies left out.

**The overall examination.** From birth to twenty-five, happiness and sorrow rest chiefly on the mother; from twenty-five to forty-nine, on one's own rise and decline; from forty-nine on, on the father's; examine the enemy and friend starting points as shown above.

**The examination of the verdicts** (ཁ་དམར་དཔྱད་པ, kha dmar dpyad pa), by kind of pebble across the four aspects. The own pebbles white: the life's sun rises in the sky, ninety-five years of life; black: the life's sun goes to the western mountain, great obstacles to life (remedies: ransoming death, practices for long life, changing the name). The mother pebbles white: a child of a flourishing father, the maternal uncles a mountain at one's back, high standing, no illness; black: wealth lost, illness, or hated and despised by all. The son pebbles white: surrounded by six or seven sons, the father's house strong, a good line; black: children destroyed by enemies, or few descendants and suffering. The friend pebbles white: great wealth and plenty, the luck of yaks and sheep; black: wealth lost, livestock bad, poor (དབུལ, dbul). The enemy pebbles white: rich in sons and wealth in old age, no harm from enemies and thieves; black: one who has wealth goes as a beggar, or what one has falls to enemies.

**The side verdicts** (ཟུར་གྱི་ཁ་དམར, zur gyi kha dmar), one for each aspect. The life force's pebbles white: long life; black: strive in practices for long life and lamps. The body's white: the glory of sons; black: though there are many sons and brothers, at last the line is cut and the glory of sons is empty, or enjoyments become poor. The power's white: rich in enjoyments, food and wealth plentiful, horses and livestock glorious; turned black: enemies carry it off, harmful spirits hurt, losses come. The luck's white: loved by all, great fame, good for the horses; black: the reverse (remedies: raising glory, great prayer flags, fumigation).

So in this manual's karma reckoning each aspect is given one verdict, white or black, and its black verdict reads as a short life for the life force, a cut line or poverty for the body, losses for the power, and the loss of love and fame for the luck. Unlike WB (vol. 1, p. 331), it reads the enemy pebbles white as good.
