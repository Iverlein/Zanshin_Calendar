Section seven, the general reckoning (སྤྱི་རྩིས, spyi rtsis), has four parts: the thirty Chinese and Tibetan starting points, the circles of Balmo (བལ་མོ་ལྡིང་སྐོར, bal mo lding skor), the earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag), and the reckoning of results.

The first: the starting points of the king and the others are turned from the rat and ox and the rest whose element agrees with the year-horse (ལོ་རྟ, lo rta) of whoever is reckoned. If the one reckoned is of a male year, count down («སྦུབ») from the male year, the rat or another, as many places as the years of the one reckoned; if of a female year, count up («བཀེན»); and examine as the text says, the life force (སྲོག, srog) of the fourth upward from there being fought by the life force of the one reckoned, and so on. Up to the ninth this is easy. For the tenth, if it concerns subjects, the year-horse of the lord who asks; for the eleventh, the horse owner's year-horse and the like; for son and bride («བུ་བག»), the son's and the bride's year-horses; likewise the house owner; for the copper vessel (ཟངས་ཐེར, zangs ther: a water copper, and *ther bo*, a name of bronze), when a bronze vessel is bought, the asker's year-horse and so on. If one who has reached old age (the *bkra*, བཀྲ) dies, examine by that old person's year and the year of death, with the age; the examination after death by the dead's year and age; whatever the grave, the same; the grave site is applied to the dead's year or to the asker as well; the rest by each one's own year-horse.

And, without regard to whether the person is a man or a woman, by whichever the year is, male or female, count down or up, and the life force and the rest of where it falls fight the life force and the rest of the one reckoned, each its own kind. The results are clear in the text. In Khyung's (ཁྱུང, khyung) oral tradition there is also a way that draws a garuḍa in the middle of a nine-square board and sets the trigrams (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) round it clockwise from *kham*; but by the unbroken oral tradition of old, the first, third and fifth are the place of the forebears and the first part of life, called the upper three; the second, fourth and sixth the lower three; the seventh and eighth are reckoned as the middle. And where the first three come out two white and one black, for the speckled the white share is greater, so it is good, and so on. A way of fighting these without turning the progressed year (ལོག་མིན, log min), as if it went into the reckoning of karma (ལས་རྩིས, las rtsis), is found elsewhere too.

The rest of the page and p. 538, the 360 starting points worked for a person of the water-rat year, were not read on the scan; the etext's last sentence on them, «སུམ་བརྒྱ་དྲུག་ཅུའི་སྐོར་མགོ་ཞེས་པ་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཀླུང་བླ་རང་དུར་གཤེད་དུར་བཅས … ལོ་དྲུག་ཅུས་བསྒོས་པས་སུམ་བརྒྱ་དྲུག་ཅུ་འོང་བ་ཡིན» (p. 538, etext only), makes the 360 the six aspects (life force, body, power, luck or la, one's own grave, the executioner's grave) times the sixty years.

Then the omens of the time (དུས་ལྟས, dus ltas). The treatises of signs hold countless stages of the time, and very many systems that show the time from the sky's omens (rain, the clouds' course, rainbows, shooting stars), the wind in the space between, and the earth's trees and rivers, with very great accuracy; here only what WB's chapter 28 shows in part.

The examination of the finger joints: the first mansion-house on the 27th of the first spring month, the eleventh Hor month; likewise the 25th of the *rgyal* month (རྒྱལ་ཟླ, rgyal zla) and the 23rd of the Miracle month (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ, cho 'phrul): roughly, the days the moon's mansion *lha mtshams* (ལྷ་མཚམས, lha mtshams; Anurādhā) rises; precisely, the date in each of the three months on which the moon reaches *lha mtshams* by the reckoning of its course must be taken first. All three mansion-houses are examined at dawn in the same way; where the first and the others fall is clear from the figure. Its early, middle and later belong to the first mansion-house's result for the early sowing, and so on; some apply the three to the upper, lower and middle valley.

«སེ་ཧུད» is a yellow cloud's tip. «གཟའ་ཡིས་བཙོན་བཟུང» is when on that day a fierce planet harms the sun's mansion. «ས་འབིར» is an earthquake. The winter's examination joined with the summer: examine before the winter solstice and match it with up to a month before the summer solstice. "The first three rounds, the second two" and so on is explained by some as the first nine days tripled and so on, but the sound reading is the first doubled, and only the result of the harvest [?] comes. The bird's days (བྱ་ཞག, bya zhag) too are counted as solar days (ཉིན་ཞག, nyin zhag). The rest is clear. «ཟོར་ཐང་འཇོམས» is the day of the first sowing of the fields. The other small omens, the results of the twelve links, the twelve to be avoided (སྤང་བྱ་བཅུ་གཉིས, spang bya bcu gnyis: the black small feet), the reckoning for those who head works, the reckoning for ordination and the letters' enemies and friends are clear from the text; the reckoning for opening manured land and for building houses are easy to understand.
