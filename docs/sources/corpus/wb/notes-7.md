Section seven, the general reckoning (སྤྱི་རྩིས, spyi rtsis), has four parts: the thirty Chinese and Tibetan starting points, the circles of Balmo (བལ་མོ་ལྡིང་སྐོར, bal mo lding skor), the earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag), and the reckoning of results.

The first: the starting points of the king and the others are turned from the rat and ox and the rest whose element agrees with the year-horse (ལོ་རྟ, lo rta) of whoever is reckoned. If the one reckoned is of a male year, count down («སྦུབ») from the male year, the rat or another, as many places as the years of the one reckoned; if of a female year, count up («བཀེན»); and examine as the text says, the life force (སྲོག, srog) of the fourth upward from there being fought by the life force of the one reckoned, and so on. Up to the ninth this is easy. For the tenth, if it concerns subjects, the year-horse of the lord who asks; for the eleventh, the horse owner's year-horse and the like; for son and bride («བུ་བག»), the son's and the bride's year-horses; likewise the house owner; for the copper vessel (ཟངས་ཐེར, zangs ther: a water copper, and *ther bo*, a name of bronze), when a bronze vessel is bought, the asker's year-horse and so on. If one who has reached old age (the *bkra*, བཀྲ) dies, examine by that old person's year and the year of death, with the age; the examination after death by the dead's year and age; whatever the grave, the same; the grave site is applied to the dead's year or to the asker as well; the rest by each one's own year-horse.

And, without regard to whether the person is a man or a woman, by whichever the year is, male or female, count down or up, and the life force and the rest of where it falls fight the life force and the rest of the one reckoned, each its own kind. The results are clear in the text. In Khyung's (ཁྱུང, khyung) oral tradition there is also a way that draws a garuḍa in the middle of a nine-square board and sets the trigrams (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) round it clockwise from *kham*; but by the unbroken oral tradition of old, the first, third and fifth are the place of the forebears and the first part of life, called the upper three; the second, fourth and sixth the lower three; the seventh and eighth are reckoned as the middle. And where the first three come out two white and one black, for the speckled the white share is greater, so it is good, and so on. A way of fighting these without turning the progressed year (ལོག་མིན, log min), as if it went into the reckoning of karma (ལས་རྩིས, las rtsis), is found elsewhere too.
