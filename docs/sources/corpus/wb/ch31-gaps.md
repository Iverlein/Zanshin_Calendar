[p. 175] The time gods, fifteen of them, and the black times; the sky dogs, male and female; the Silma (སིལ་སེ, sil se; the gods of the pebbles of Silma) and the Dud (bdud); the Gogku (འགོག་ཀུ, 'gog ku) with its number (5), and the twelve Gabpa (གབ་པ, gab pa); further what the Khol makes into the heap of earth; with these the time gods come to thirty-seven. Rāhu (རཱ་ཧུ, rā hu), the chief of all the planets, belongs by rights to the Khol's gods, but is set apart here and counted as one (1). The comings of the gods of year, month, day and time are in number four hundred and four [the sense of «དོན་བཞི» is not settled]; of these, with their manner of going and their manner of staying, at the time of reckoning the human body's own special points, the count of pebbles: the root holes and the nāgas (89) are fixed.

The inner examination of this: its nature. The "earth lord Kharbu" (ཁར་བུ), or "the twelve cruel ones of the sun", or "the sky's wheel" and the rest are one in essence, as follows. On the day of the rat it dwells in the beer jar: if the dead's beer is poured there, it is bad for young children; the grass bundle is not the barley offering [the sense is not settled]; the offering is cast away; the step is not placed [the corpse is not laid on it], but drawn out by the barley-bag [uncertain]. On the ox day it dwells in the granary and the storehouses: bad for those who take the dead's barley out and for the measurer; scatter the barley husks from the mouth of the granary to the four directions and, if the granary's hole is dug through, it is good to take the barley out. On the tiger day it dwells at the door: the corpse is not carried out; if the corpse meets the door, the householder is harmed; carry it over the roof or through a hole; or set the door's livestock on the right. On the hare day it dwells in the yak-yoke: if the corpse-carrier is hooked on the yoke, it is bad for sons and daughters; do not touch the yoke, or lift the yoke away and carry. On the dragon day it dwells in the loom: if the corpse's tie is hung on the loom, the flour-maker and the in-law die; open the loom's mouth and scatter flour from the middle. On the snake day it dwells in the corpse rope: drawn by the rope, the corpse-bearer dies; another rope is cut in three, joined again, and carried; cut again with a knife at the path's mouth; set on a raft, the fault is calmed. On the horse day it dwells in the camp: before the sound is loud [the corpse is carried silently]; carrying the corpse silently is bad for the horse; make flesh-bindings and give the bearer the offering, but if no noise is made, going to the burial place is bad. On the sheep day it dwells in the trough: dough kneaded in the trough is bad for the dough-butter; knead the dough on copper or a stone slab; on the day's place set a dough figure of a person. On the monkey day it dwells in the knife and the axe: what cuts the meat, its cutter dies; burn the axe and knives in fire. On the bird day it dwells in plates: when the dead's food is brought on a plate, it is bad for the head of the row, or sudden fright comes; make a stone slab, not a plate, or make the dough on a spread of felt. On the dog day it dwells in the step (ཡ་ཐེམ) and the non-step: on the step, a corpse laid there is bad for the father who carries it, or the bearer himself dies; wrap the step in cloth and it will hold; sorrow-beer is not drunk. On the pig day it dwells in the pit (འཇོར): if the grave is dug with a pick, the one who opens the soil, the elder, dies; open the soil with flesh and bone. "From the fort, the earth is likewise," it is said; "for the time-hours also likewise"; it is told in the "Knowing how to throw out what is to be shunned" [title not settled].

If it is violated, the Eight Appearances are recited; for covering, hang up a skull-offering, or as in the golden treasure, or cover with a human skeleton holding a spear [the last lines as printed].

> འདུག་པས་དོར་ཏེ་བགྱིས་པའི་ལེའུ་སྟེ་སོ་གཅིག་པའོ།། །།

[p. 240] [The closing note begins with «རིགས» after the last word of p. 237, «ནོར»; whether «ནོར་རིགས» is one phrase is not settled.] Those of a doubtful kind: those that rest in the sources of the tantras, such as the Sky, Earth and Eight Appearances and the Vajra Crown-Protuberance, and so on; and the "Precious Elements, All Gathered, Appearance-Clear" and the others, filled in from other sources where the translation is missing; and the practice of the Monlam-going Paṇa (the great one), the Crown-Protuberance Precious Treasury Lamp's commentary, "Light Rays Pervading All": for these, the visualizations from the sources that stand are set down. As for the Nine Black Women's circle, the Thesé and others' patched-in ones, and the visualizations of the earth lords of the lunar date's course: they are not set down here. They are only contrivances of unsourced thought; the "repair-of-nature" and the like and all of them, as there is no virtue and great harm in them, were thrown away and not set down. This is the thirty-first chapter.
