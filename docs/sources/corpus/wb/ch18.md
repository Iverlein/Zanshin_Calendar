## The sixty years' results (WB ch. 18) (RD184)

Chapter 18 of WB vol. 1 (pp. 198–212) is a verse chapter, almost without a
line of prose, on **what each year, each planet and each mansion brings**,
"from the *Mirror of the Times' Teaching* (དུས་བསྟན་མེ་ལོང, *dus bstan me long*)
and other texts". Its parts, by page:

| Pages | What |
| --- | --- |
| 198 | The title and the invocation of the Gentle Protector with five topknots (Mañjuśrī); then, from the *Mirror of the Times' Teaching*, the results of the sixty years of the cycle, two or four lines each (Excellent Arising to Peaceful on this page) |
| 199 | The same list to its end (Common to Exhauster); the results by the planet that is lord of the year (the short list); by the mansion that is lord of the year |
| 200 | The mansions' list to its end; a second, longer list of what each planet brings as lord of the year; the planets' straight and crooked courses through the mansions |
| 201 | The courses to their end; the seven kinds of conduct (སྤྱོད་པ་བདུན, *spyod pa bdun*) of the sage's text; conjunctions of Jupiter, Mars, Saturn, Venus and Mercury |
| 202 | Saturn in each of the mansions, and which peoples, trades and countries it harms; Rāhu in the east; the beginning of a second list of the sixty years, told by the great sage, the lama |
| 203–205 | That list: each year in four to ten lines, with the crops, the animals, the diseases, the kings and the neighbouring peoples (Excellent Arising to Wrathful, masculine) |
| 206–210 | A further list of the sixty years from the *Sūtra of the Secret* (མདོ་སྡེ་གསང་བ, *mdo sde gsang ba*), by the year's name with its element, sex and animal, in a terse notation; the results of the twelve animal years by where the earth lord (ས་བདག, *sa bdag*) dwells; the key to the notation of the sūtra *Tiger-Ear* (མདོ་སྟེ་སྟག་རྣ, *mdo ste stag rna*), on p. 210 (corrected by RD190) |

**The notation of the terse lists** (decoded on p. 209): *gro* wheat, *nas* barley,
*bra* buckwheat, *sran* peas, *'bras* rice, *til* sesame; "the black" (གནག, *gnag*) and
"the white" (དཀར་མོ, *dkar mo*) are crops named by their colour; *khre*, *zar ma*,
*so ba*, *ju tse*, *nyung ma* are the grains of China; "good" (ལེགས, *legs*),
"middling" ('bring), "flourishes" (rgyas) and "scarce" (dkon) grade a crop, and
"harmed" (nongs), "wolf" (spyang), "locked up" (btson) and "damp" (rlon) are the
marks of its faults. *Skya rgyal* (སྐྱ་རྒྱལ) is explained on p. 209: the minister
contending against the king is *skya rgyal*, the king against the minister
*skya* defeated; the 1996 etext reads *sku rgyal* (སྐུ་རྒྱལ) in these places.

[Peaceful, continuing:] all the kings have a virtuous mind and are loving and helpful to their retinue and subjects.
Common: at all times the sky is filled with clouds and rain falls at once; though very heavy rain falls, the crops are good and the grain plentiful; yet the king punishes the people and the like heavily.
Contradictor: wind and rain come to the land in the time of spring-summer (*sos ka*); no rain on mountains and in forests; heavy rain in late summer and autumn; if rain does fall, it falls in scattered drops in the regions; in the lands of *a bhi tsitta* (not settled) great drought comes; in *ka na tshu la tsa* too great drought harms the crops.
Fully Holding: people suffer in bad times with bad minds; the days hot and tormenting, the nights with wind and rain very cold.
Heedless: cattle and other livestock all die and are lost; singers, brahmins and other great people come to die; rain in spring and early summer, great drought in winter and summer; the crops unfit and the grains bad; for cows, female yaks and other milk-animals no milk, curd or butter comes, scarce; copper, iron and precious things few; the fields have good green stalks, but in autumn no fruit comes; great famine, so wealth, supports (*rten*) and the like are sold and lost.
All-joyful: barley, rice and all the great grains are lost; very scarce, great famine; buckwheat, peas, sesame and the like, the lesser grains, are plentiful; death from hunger is common.
Ogre: barley, rice, small peas, sesame and the like are spoiled and lost; the kinds of medicine become scarce; people without love steal and rob.
Fire: great famine; cities burnt by fire; summer drought, the grain does not grow; what little grew is eaten by hail and the like and the crops become unfit.
Red-yellow: in the towns of the country the king is destroyed by the army of another; by disease, famine and the like people are driven to affliction.
Messenger of Time: in spring and early summer a little rain; in summer and autumn very heavy rain; yet a good year, grain plentiful; long-lasting diseases of the lungs and the like make the people very ill.
Aim-accomplished: kings are very proud and arrogant, without compassion toward the people; clouds hold water and the rain falls quickly and often.
Fierce: no rain; by drought the crops do not grow; what little grew is eaten by hail and insects.
Bad-minded: rain falls only as much as needed; famine comes a little, so goods are much traded; people do not repay debts to one another.
Great Drum: though there is nothing to desire, great clamour; people leave their own lands; comfort, and no harm to one another; cattle and other livestock comfortable.
Blood-vomiting: grain and food at first scarce and lacking; people prepare grain for beer and afterwards grain and food are lacking; people hurry to profit in trade; the kings are foolish and dull; the people deceive the king; the kings fight and quarrel.
Red-eyed: epidemic disease with *shing byi* (wood and mouse? not settled); great famine, the grain scarce; many people come to die; people hold unvirtuous minds and rob, destroy and harm one another.

Lines 1–4: the end of the sage lama's list (p. 205's last year, Exhauster, in his version): "people die before their time and fight; famine afflicts this world. Exhauster: fire-sparks in the world; the earths shake and beings that are not human come; ogres, jackals and the like [do something to] people (ma shi tha bar byed, not settled); the pig-faced destroy the cities; people are terrified by famine and die; the border country of Gora (གོ་ར) and the kings of the south, of Konkan (ཀོང་ཁ), die and are made unfit; in Siṅgala (སིངྒལ), in Tibet and in the countries Koli and Tsa (ཀོ་ལི་རྩ་ཡུལ, the reading not settled) two out of three people die."

Line 5: "Then, from the *Sūtra of the Secret* (མདོ་སྡེ་གསང་བ, *mdo sde gsang ba*)" the list of the sixty years begins, each year a name with its element, sex and animal and then the results in the notation of the key on p. 210. It starts with the first year of the element-animal cycle, wood male mouse, which carries the name Red-eyed, and goes on in the order of the cycle to water female pig, Blood-vomiting (p. 209). Entries are numbered here by their place in the cycle (1 = wood mouse, 60 = water pig). Wording of the notation: *good* (ལེགས), *very good* (བཟང), *middling* ('bring), *flourishes* (rgyas), *wins* (rgyal), *scarce* (dkon), *harmed* (nongs), *wolf* (spyang), *locked up* (btson), *damp* (rlon); *the black* (གནག) and *the white* (དཀར་མོ) are crops named by colour (p. 210). Terms left in Wylie because the words are not settled: *sad* (སད), *khre'ur* (ཁྲེའུར), *zhod* (ཞོད), *gshongs* (གཤོངས), *skya rgyal* (སྐྱ་རྒྱལ), *nam stod* and *nam smad* (ནམ་སྟོད, ནམ་སྨད).

1. Red-eyed (མིག་དམར), wood male mouse: the black [crop] middling; rice and the border lands good; in the middle summer month grain and food are scarce; *sad* comes upon the crops; robbers and thieves are rowdy.
2. Wrathful, masculine (ཁྲོ་བོ), wood ox: *so* and *zar* good; unrest rises and thieves are rowdy; at the end of spring discomfort of many kinds comes; goods and livestock are cheap; epidemics come *khre'ur*.
3. Exhauster (ཟད་པ), fire tiger: disease of the white; the sky-army is raised; rice and the rest good; in the north many people lose face; early drought and hail in spring come.
4. Excellent Arising (རབ་བྱུང), fire female hare: buckwheat and peas good, the white locked up; epidemics come *khre'ur*; little rain; unrest of king and minister and the like; war and unrest; the outer sea dries.
5. Distinct Arising (རྣམ་བྱུང), earth dragon: *so* and *zar* and the black middling (the one mark stands over the three, as the lines run); lord and minister stir; people leave their lands, move and flee (འཕོ་སྐྱས, not settled); many kinds of epidemic.
6. White (དཀར་པོ), earth snake: unrest turns back; "many, and the rule good" (མང་ལ་སྲིད་བཟང, not settled); the minister contends against the king; *nam stod* dry, *nam smad zhod*; buckwheat, peas and the like do not ripen; the white good.
7. Greatly Intoxicated (རབ་མྱོས), iron male horse: *khre* harmed; the sky-army is raised from the sky; heavy rain, *skya rgyal*; the fruit of the trees *g.yer* (not settled); *sad*, and thieves are rowdy; *so ba* and *zar ma* locked up.
8. Lord of Beings (སྐྱེས་བདག), iron sheep: wheat and barley locked up; *gshongs* and disease of the eyes come; many epidemics; sesame and rice good; heavy rain, the year good of itself.
9. Aṅgiras (ཨདྒིར, so printed for the name a ngi ra), water male monkey: *ju tse* wins; rice and *so* locked up; *khre* and the black good; unrest comes in the west; *nam stod* dry; famine in the border lands and *nam smad zhod*; "through merit all that is done is good".
10. Glorious Face (དཔལ་གདོང), water female bird: wheat and barley die; the minister contends against the king; in the middle of summer *skya rgyal* and much bad conduct; famine and the like come; illness and epidemics come suddenly (*khol bur*).
11. Thing (དངོས་པོ), wood dog: rice and *so* good; unrest of king and minister, strong hail in spring; crops sown early good; *nam smad zhod* and swellings increase.
12. Possessing Age (ན་ཚོད་ལྡན), wood female pig: famine comes and unrest in the west and north; the black good, the white middling, *khre* harmed; epidemics come suddenly; the minister contends against the king.
13. Holder (འཛིན་བྱེད), fire mouse: rice and *so* locked up; wheat and barley are both made middling; enemies, thieves and epidemics, much to fear; in early autumn drought comes; in gardens mice do harm.
14. Mighty One (དབང་ཕྱུག), fire female ox: buckwheat and peas damp, the white *wolf*; "as to weapons, water and earth-wind are strong" (མཚོན་དུ་ཆུ་དང་ས་རླུང་ཆེ, the sense not settled); unrest comes suddenly; *ju tse* flourishes, *khre* harmed.

44. Monkey (སྤྲེའུ), fire sheep: *ju tse* flourishes; *khre* middling, epidemics, and *sad* comes too; the mountain field good, *gshongs* and bad; rain and water even.

50. Heedless (བག་མེད), water ox: the white bad; *so ba* and *zar ma* good; the black middling; "wa sa wa rtse brdol" (not settled); on the *lcog* the frost-water sets in; *sad* rises and epidemics come suddenly.

54. Red-yellow (དམར་སེར), fire snake: the later sowing good; the black good; bad omens, the planets rise; *sad* and hail come and earth-wind is strong; wheat, the white and *ju tse* flourish; the sky-army in the east, much unrest and quarrel.
