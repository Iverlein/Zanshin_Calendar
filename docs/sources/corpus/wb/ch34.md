[p. 378] Chapter thirty-four: the results of each of the important works told separately.

Opening verses. A verse of homage to the one whose deep-red lotus eye's corner-arrow is shot to gather the glory of becoming and peace and the wealth of worldly good; the "Ornament of Marvellous Causes"; the "Summary Form": the teacher of what must be taken up and what cast off, as in a mirror's image, the translator-pandits' company; their kindness, told as "the results of what is needed, in elaboration", unlike a sheep's way: this is the holy ones' kindness; "The Example of Things": good and bad, virtue and non-virtue, the things to be known, in their unmixed state without veil, the words that show them unerring and clear: the silver mirror of good explanation, a marvel; and a pair that stands at the start of the second and third [verses] [the ornament's name is as the print gives it].

Then the separate works. For the ease of entry of dull minds, they are set out apart, unmixed. The list of the sixty-one works, numbered as the text numbers them: (1) birth; (2) naming; (3) weaving cloth; (4) putting on [new clothes]; (5) worshipping the gods; (6) building forts and towns; (7) taking up a household; (8) the hearth, and its building; (9) breaking the ground, and taming an elephant; (10) spectacles; (11) feasts, youthful play; (12) setting out on a road, and seeking a purpose; (13) trade; (14) goods and wealth, giving and taking; (15) planting trees, and sowing flowers; (16) gathering grain; (17) hanging the ... [«སྦ་ཕྱར», not settled]; (18) building a treasury; (19) raising a banner; (20) hunting; (21) examining thieves, robbers and prisoners; (22) ponds, lakes, wells, channels; (23) the reckoning for sowing seed in the field; (24) [«སྐྱས», not settled], (25) horse racing, horse, donkey and mule; (26) healing; (27) striking a sound; (28) taming horses; (29) lending and debts; (30) keeping a dog; (31) learning writing and arithmetic; (32) compounding medicine; (33) the lancet, the cautery and the stick, the healing; (34) bathing; (35) washing the hair and the divination for it; (36) entering the king's place; (37) making weapons; (38) serving a retinue; (39) taking a bride; (40) putting on ornaments; (41) the work for the dead, examined; (42) raising the supports of the three secrets (body, speech, mind); (43) examining sickness; (44) turning back, and the order of rites; (45) the burnt offering; (46) pressing down the sri; (47) ordination, teaching and hearing, drawing the mandala; (48) sādhana and offering, empowerment, consecration; (49) building [«ཤྭ་རགས»]; (50) craft, sound and [«སེན»]; (51) cutting, and war; and the arts of life; (52) poetry, language, teaching, composing treatises; (53) ransom-offering, fort; and throwing; (54) giving the power of magic; (55) bringing rain; (56) the dead; (57) holding an assembly's council; (58) the king's works, passing sentence and so on; (59) the works of the living, virtue and steadiness begun; (60) auspiciousness; (61) the accomplishing of life and wealth; and similar special famous ones. The chief works are for the most part told. The small works, of power (62), fierce (63), pacifying (64) and enriching (65): those not gathered into the rest are gathered into the fold of the four. So sixty-five earth examinations are shown. [In the text the number runs «ས་དཔྱད་དྲུག་ཅུ་རྩ་ལྔ», sixty-five; the first sixty-one are the list above.]

[p. 379] (1) Birth: first, the examination of birth. Hear the wheel of the examination of birth explained. Draw a handsome boy; the star that the sun embraces is drawn on the top of his head; at the mouth three, and two at the shoulders, two at the wrists and two at the hands, five at the heart, one at the navel, one at the secret place, two at the knees; eight are set at the feet: so birth is examined. Born in the feet: becomes tall or short; born in the knees: wanders and roams; born in the secret place: ruined by sex; born at the navel: becomes poor; born in the heart: has enjoyment; born in the hands: becomes a thief; born in the wrists: suffering; born at the shoulders: carries burdens; born in the mouth: has [authority of] the date; born in the star of the crown: becomes a king, without doubt.

The birth-time's moon and star, as the oral instruction says: from the pure hour that was sought, the wheel of the lifespan, long or short, and of good and bad, is to be told. From the hour of the moon-star of one's own birth, hours one to fifteen: born in the first part, the life measure is a full hundred years; from sixteen to thirty, the second part, eighty; from thirty-one to forty-five, the third part, born with a measure of a full sixty; from forty-six to the Dakini's end (mkha' ro), the fourth part, a full forty. Knowing this: from hour one of the moon-star to hour sixty, in the time of birth, whatever the hours that have passed are, multiplied by a hundred with twenty over [120], divided by ninety; the quotient is years; the remainders in order multiplied by sun (12) and the sky-fire (30); what comes from the earlier divisor is the month and day. The rest, hours, breaths and so on, are multiplied and divided in order. By this, from each one's own life measure, in order, deceptions-of-death are subtracted and added: to the first part nothing is added; the rest are the rough lifespan of a person. In fine detail, by the nine planets, how much the enjoyment and the deceptions of death, the additions and subtractions, of that person's life, the decline and the loss of enjoyment, and the way things appear, become clear; yet beings in whom the bad dawns are dulled in mind; so, before, the profound essential counsels were hidden in the three commentaries (an unclear passage), here also in the same way a little is set apart and hidden, shown separately. They are examined from the moon's stars.

Then the birth-results by the day-planets (the weekday of birth). Sun (1): if the child born on it lives seven days or seven months, he is long-lived and becomes a great, loving man; a girl is rich and brings land to the house. Moon (2): a boy born is rich and strong; a girl, beautiful, charming, pleasing. Mars (3), the red-eye: sinful in conduct, joyless, short-lived, bad; sows division, proud, very angry; a boy is hard to rear, a girl dies. Mercury (4), Lhagpa (ལྷག་པ; the print «ཉག་པར»): born sick and with ailing children; a boy bad, rough to his mother; if a girl, the parents become poor; as for her, great power over food and wealth. Jupiter (5): bad as the one before, yet clear-minded, learned, and does the dharma; a girl is long-lived with good friends. Venus (6), Pasang: becomes a [blacksmith or the like; «དམེ་དང་མགར་བ»]; a boy long-lived, keen of sense, rich; a girl falls sick, yet great in glory. Saturn (7): handsome, little merit; a middling son; a daughter short-lived.

[p. 380] Now by the star of birth (the mansion), the lunar mansions. Born in Dbyugpa (དབྱུག་པ, Anurādhā? the print «དབྱུག་པ་༤»; the number is as printed), a life of sixty to eighty years, a pleasant tongue, handsome, devout; a doctor or of the physician class; skilled in song, dance, music and sound; restless in mind and body. Born in the (1) [«བྲ་ཉེར»]: steady-minded, free of illness, truthful, little compassion; a life of thirty-six years, or if long, ninety-five; skilled in handwork, energetic; for want of compassion, he is born in hell afterwards. Smindrug (སྨིན་དྲུག) (2): a boy born has growing enjoyment, fame and great radiance; learned and very brave; even if bad in the first half of life, in the second he changes and goes well; a long life; if a girl is born, it is bad. Snarma (སྣར་མ) (2 [as printed]): the man born has a long life; handsome, gentle of speech, truthful; rich, follows the dharma, loves to give; honest, learned, beautiful in every way; brave, wholly victorious; always destroys enemies. Born at the Head (མགོ, 4): lifespan twenty-eight to two [«རེ་གཉིས»], if long, ninety is also said; dharma-holding, restrained, diligent, truthful; brave, clear-minded, has enjoyment; a girl born is said to be sharp-mouthed. Born in the Hand (ལག, 5): life thirty-five or sixty-five; lives by a robber's work; fierce, honest-less, no good with the hands; angry, poor, very foolish; does not do good deeds but is very eager in bad ones; proud, loves sin; above all, a boy is skilled; a girl is skilled in wool work. Born in the upper part of Gyal (རྒྱལ, 6): life fifty or sixty; a farmer with enjoyment, or one with the voice of dharma and discipline; his mind is very good, his nature simple; steady-minded, free from illness, content, and satisfied with a little. Born in the lower part of Gyal (7): life eighty; a man with the voice of dharma and discipline, peaceful, giving; a pleasant tongue, handsome, handy; restless of body and mind; learned, rich, of good fortune. Born in the La (ལ, 8): life fifty full years, if long sixty-three; foolish, few daughters; learned, face handsome, short time with his mother; angry, loves sin and division; great desire, yet stingy with giving; does no good, very eager in bad deeds. A man born in the Great Horse (རྟ་ཆེན, 9): if he does not die in nine years, he lives fifty-five by his kind; bad for a boy; if a girl, she herself dies; loves lying speech; [p. 381] has a large retinue and great enjoyment; a long time, good sons; skilled in craft, strives for virtue; noble, dharma-holding, rich. Born in the Little Horse (རྟ་ཆུང, 10): if he does not die within eight years, he lives fifty or fifty-five; a girl is sickly and bad; a boy's own child dies; until then a bad nature; deceitful, great lust; skilled in craft, very stingy; loves talk and clothes, jewels; fair of door and gentle of mouth, generous with food. Born in the Khra (ཁྲ, 11): lives a hundred years; rich, with enjoyment and discipline; no lies, truthful, loves giving; loves going and keeping the fast, becomes a little foolish; the cause of death is poison. The man born in the Byama (བྱ་མ, 18? the print gives «༡༡༨»): the mother's life is obstructed; lives full eighty years; pleasant tongue, handsome, skilful hands; good in courage and great in strength; restless in mind and body; pale, deceitful, very tricky; a lord, chief and wealthy; makes gifts and offerings of many kinds; dharma-holding, the seven limbs of awakening; tame-minded, straight, keeps all the laws; or a thief or thirsty, but faithful. Born in the Byeu (བྱེའུ, 12): lives twenty-eight years; skilled in song, dance and music; loves giving, loves pleasures, beautiful and dear; pleasant tongue, sharp mind, famous; a handsome face, but much displeasure comes; no lies, rough speech, great lust; eyes, limbs and senses good; wears many clothes and carries garlands. Born in the Sari (ས་རི, 14): lives twenty-six [or] sixty; reckoning, wealth, gains, becomes angry; deceit, sick at heart, with children; has enjoyment and handsome in form; kind above, hostile below, hostile in speech; jealous and very stingy; nature gentle and generous; loves journeys and beginnings; tame-minded, a trader; loving, truthful, practises the dharma; both sons and daughters strong. Born in the Saga (ས་ག, 15): seventy years; radiant and glorious; learned, wealthy, gathers women; brave, subdues enemies, great wisdom; bad at first, but good in later life; jealous, stingy, great radiance. Born in the Lagsor (ལག་སོར, 17): lives twenty-eight; a trader, subdued of sense, practises the dharma, has great wits; lives by trade or by the dharma; pleasant tongue, handsome, with discipline; excellent with cattle and wealth, with the wandering; loves wandering, and wandering suits him; other people love and gather round him. Born in the Ldeur (ལྡེའུར, 17): lives thirty-five; discipline broken, no compassion; makes offerings and the like grow; mostly ugly and short-lived; poor, content, of few wants; does no good, but very eager in bad deeds; if he practises the dharma, quickly breaks his discipline. Born in the Sogpa (སོག་པ, 18): thirty-five, or if long sixty-three; of fortunate male form; holds a good house and is a farmer; lord of wealth and grain; the world's work does not bear sons; if born, hard to rear; proud and haughty; very eager in bad deeds. Born in the Bre (བྲེ, 19): lives eighty; has the dharma and much learning; skilled in craft and all work; [the page's last lines, lost in the machine reading and supplied from MITRA and read on the scan:] practises the dharma and giving; with a retinue, faithful to all; if a little proud, by discipline attains the higher realms.

(43) Examining the sick [the machine reading has «༤༢» at the head; the list on p. 378 and white-beryl-ch34.md give 43]. From the moon-star of the hearing and the asking begin: draw a nāga figure: the mouth, the chest, the belly, the thigh, the tail, the neck, the shoulder, the navel, the secret place; three are asked. If the birth-name star of the sick has gone to the neck: death. In the belly and the thigh: a violent illness comes. In the tail and the chest: long and bad. In the mouth, the navel and the shoulder: victory over the illness, good well-being. Dwelling in the secret place: bad.

The Sun (1): when sick, palpitation of the heart and confusion; an epidemic illness, short. The Moon (2): discomfort and piercing pain in the lower body; a cold illness, short; harm from the outer "yugs", the nāga-offering, the dead's spirit. Mars (3): a blood, bile, heat illness; eye illness, head illness, disturbance of the mind; though not dying, it is soon healed; the red scum, the hearth's fire, and the red follower, the tsen and the gre-bo, bring harm. Mercury (4), Lhagpa: water illness, cold illness; the bowels, kidney or waist pain; a sudden illness, soon healed; Bon, the sorceress or the child-and-sister, a following nāga brings harm. Jupiter (5): a wind and bile illness; trembling, a wandering mind and fright come; though not dying, soon healed; for night wandering, the nāga offering, and the king's harm is the illness. Venus (6), Pasang: a lung illness and a bone illness; swelling of the arms, short; a king and tsen that followed for trade bring harm. Saturn (7): a general gathering illness; stupor, heaviness and a spinning head; when the four joints and the liver are sick, it is long, and a danger of death; Yama, the Theu-brang, the earth lord and the mamo bring harm. The breaking of the time-rite, the taking up of a child's ... answered by the gsur offering.

In the Dbyugpa star (དབྱུག་པ): from three days after falling ill one is freed from the illness; it is repaid by wandering and by water, and by the clothes of a beast of prey. In the lower Gyal (རྒྱལ་སྨད): a child, water; Shesang (ཤེ་སང): repaid by a woman's death; these two, in three days freed from the illness. Smindrug (སྨིན་དྲུག): made unclean by the oath-making; Khra (ཁྲ): repaid by a child's [sacrifice]; these two, in ten days freed. Bi-rdzi (བི་རྫི, 3): the making of a hearth-pit; Upper Gyal: repaid by the entry of a guest; these two, in five days freed. Head (མགོ): enmity and "yugs"; Wa (ཝ, 8): repaid by gathering and bad food; these two, in eight days freed. Hand (ལག): repaid by going to the centre of a large retinue; Byama (བྱ་མ, 12): repaid by fright and shock; Byaze (བྱ་ཟེ, 20): repaid by going to a large retinue; when these three have laid illness on, after a long time freed from the illness. The Great Horse (9): repaid by digging a rock-ledge; in a month freed from the illness. The Little Horse (10): repaid by cutting wood; in one day freed. Saga (15): repaid by night wandering; twelve days, freed from the illness. Lagsor (16): [p. 416] repaid by joining a fight; in three or five, freed. Ldeu (17): repaid by weapons and the noise of a council; after a long time he lives. Sogpa (18): repaid by unclean red meat; in twenty days freed from the illness. Bre (19): repaid by drowning and "yugs"; Sgrog (22): repaid by food of the yugs-place; these two, in twelve days freed. Phul (20): repaid by the soil of ploughing the field; in one month freed. Byagzhug (22): repaid by wandering and sprinkling; in nine days, it is said, he is relieved. Sari (14): repaid by breaking a ... ; in three days freed from the illness. Byaso (21): repaid by going to a large retinue; Bra-nye (17): repaid by going to a place of sorrow; Byiu (13): repaid by an eclipse of food; Upper Khrums (24): repaid by food of the yugs; Lower Khrums (25): repaid by drinking cold beer. Likewise the illness of the "da" [«ད་བཏབ»] is a danger of death. Lug-taṭṭala or the near house, Sra and the pair: a great danger of death. Khrigpa (འཁྲིག་པ, the "joining"), Lion, Aquarius, Ga-cu [«ག༷ཅུ»]: soon freed from the illness. Kaṭa, Sdigpa, Capricorn: from the seventh he lives. Fish, ox and girl: soon freed from the illness.

For the one who falls ill on ignorance of dependent arising: if he does not die within half a month, take care; he lives long. The compositional factors: if he does not die in seven days, he lives from the third day on. Consciousness: three or five, if not dead, seven or eight, he lives. Name-and-form: three, five or seven, he lives; at eight he does not recover [«བརྒྱད་ཐལ་སོས་པར་འགྱུར་བ་མིན»]. The sense-fields, black: three or four days, if not dead, from ten days he is freed. Contact: if not dead in three days, in eight he lives, long-lived. Feeling: if not dead in five, rising at nine, freed at ten. Craving: if not dead in eight, in ten he lives; grasping likewise. Becoming: if not dead on the third or ninth day, from the fifth he lives. Birth: if not dead in five days, in fifteen he lives. Old age and death: if not dead on the third or seventh day, he is freed from the illness.
