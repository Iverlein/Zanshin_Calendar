Chapter 2. The preliminary: a condensed statement of the good and necessary parts of the star reckoning (སྐར་རྩིས, skar rtsis) chiefly, the black reckoning (ནག་རྩིས, nag rtsis), "song and rising" (དབྱངས་འཆར, dbyangs 'char) and the rest.
From the beautiful body well made by the two accumulations, with the light of knowledge and love blazing, you dispel the darkness of unknowing of all beings who look to you, though you are not seen by others: to the Lord of the Sages, called "Giver of the unseen", I bow in faith.
The fourth of the rejections of denial, the denial of place (the reprint reads སྙོན; the 1996 scan སྟོན): The intent of the Abridged Tantra, which realizes the outer, inner and other three, settled without confusion — the Phugpa's system; the star reckoning that makes the outside clear, the moon [reading ཟླ in the 1996 scan, བླ in the reprint] put first; from the rising, the whole of what is to be known of the future.
The form with parts: A string of the hard points of the Lo-wo translator (གློ་བོ་ལོ་ཙཱ), the essence of the good found in the Spor-thang (སྤོར་ཐང) calculations, all gathered, the good exposition of this treatise, the "Golden Drop": I delight in setting the jewel key as its introduction.
The form of all: Now, on the kinds of year: it is not that of animals, of the unfixed, or of other beings [the text is not settled here]; the in-and-out breath is three hundred and sixty, and it fills one chu tshod (ཆུ་ཚོད, chu tshod); in a day and night there are twenty-one thousand six hundred; going so, thirty days make the month, twelve months are held to be the year: this is the coarse arrangement. Taken fine, the asterism in motion has a thousand and six hundred and twenty chu tshod (the wording is not settled), in all it turns, one year by the days; if measured by lunar days (ཚེས་ཞག), the days are "three hundred and one" (the wording is not settled)...; with the pulse-weight (སྲང), the fire-time and the water measure, the breath, the arrow ... by the Sage's measure [the line is not settled]; the civil days (ཉིན་ཞག) are three hundred and sixty-five, the chu tshod ... [the next lines name the units: breath, month, ... the form, the sun]; the days of the house (ཁྱིམ་ཞག) go round in three hundred and sixty; and this is the same as one human year. As to its names, the garland of months, the "thrower", the seasons, the counted year going and coming: these are the parts of time.
On how to take the head of the year: the sūtras, tantras and abhidharma terms and the rest have many views; in the Vinaya there are three ways of taking it, earlier and later differing; medicine has four seasons and five elements; the way of pairing the moon with the asterism of the full moon is the "Hor system", as it is known, in agreement [with some]; all of these, each with its proofs, have sought reasons and sources from scripture; to establish each as right, since they are the sayings of the company of the learned, doubt and dispute are not fitting.

Subtraction and counting (the first word, འདྲི in one scan and འདྲ༷ in the other, is not settled) begin from the right; multiplication and division from the left. The dots (zeros) of the multiplier are set at the end of the row of the multiplicand. When the multiplicand is empty there is nothing to multiply; when the multiplier is empty, erase the base. What "multiplying" means is to increase what is multiplied in number. Multiplying by one gives neither more nor less; multiplying by two and the rest increases upward. A dot at the end or in the middle of the divisor cannot divide, since it holds its own place; where nothing is obtained, give a dot as the share. Division has the single and the joined: in the single, take whatever is obtained; in the joined, the lower figure governs. What "dividing" means is the procedure of gathering into the number of the divisor. One added above becomes ten; the hundred and the rest can be known by reason. When emptied, bring the ten and the rest down in order, and set the remainder in its own place. A dot cannot come at the edge of a row. However many immeasurable things to be known are reckoned, all multiplication and division go in this way.
