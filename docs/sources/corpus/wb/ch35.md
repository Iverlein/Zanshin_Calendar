Gist: the reckonings of the living and the dead, begun only with refuge and the arising of the awakening mind, become a cause of omniscience (even a mouthful of food given in the way of awakening is a cause of buddhahood), and so are without doubt. Then a dedication prayer in the voice of the true-word hero («Kye! may my Clear Appearance reasoning, by the love of the awakening mind, be like the king of medicines or a wish-granting jewel for beings seized by illness, by the eighty-one sudden and the three hundred and sixty constant (ཡེ་འདྲོག, ye 'drog) ills, harmed by evil spirits, fallen into the abyss of cycle, sunk in the mire of the five poisons, and may they rise from sickness and death; all dedicated to beings»). Then who may be taught: a fit vessel with the full conditions of the instructions, not one wicked or dull or with broken pledges; to be kept secret from those who are not (like a white lioness's milk that only a vessel of gems holds); the marks of such a vessel (much learning, great wisdom, a sharp mind, firm practice, effort, faith, little sleep, no ale) and the pledges (དམ་ཚིག, dam tshig) of this star and black reckoning, with the downfalls named by their number (the first, vexing the teacher, then the vajra-siblings, love abandoned, the awakening mind lost, the siddha reviled, secrets given to the unripe, the aggregates despised, pure Dharma doubted, to the fourteen root downfalls) and the faults of the wicked, the proud, the greedy and the deceitful, who are not to be given it.

Gist: the vessels who seek only fame and wealth in this life are not to be given the secret; one who has no understanding and follows a pupil's whim muddles the work and falls to a bad rebirth; yet it is not to be hidden from one who has finished his own work. Some reckoners who do not know the Kālacakra's meaning object to the Īśvara-taught "vowel-rising" (དབྱངས་འཆར, dbyangs 'char) system; but this Lord of Ashes is the Noble Avalokiteśvara's emanation, taught as a treatise to show the future; the learned and the siddhas of the snow land accept it, and above all the supreme protector (the Fifth Dalai Lama) held it as his dearest practice. Reckoners who foretell without knowing even the way are like one who turns a road into poison; if it goes well it is a chance, if it goes badly their own greed brings them to ruin. Then, in prose, the author's own words from his Phug lugs reckoning work (Phug lugs rtsis kyi legs bshad mkhas pa'i mgul rgyan bai dUrya dkar po'i do shal dpyod ldan snying nor, the White Beryl's full title): the pledges of black reckoning as most reckoners have them, the test of who is a vessel to hold star and black reckoning, and a remark on how the five fields are taken from reckoning, which is only touched here because the opinions on their order are many and the quotations cannot be put into verse.
