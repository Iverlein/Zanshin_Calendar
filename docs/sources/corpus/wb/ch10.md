Chapter ten: on the new extension of the tables (*rē'u mig*, "little squares") and the working of the old ones.

Homage, in verses. By the one whose matchless discernment (the bracketed gloss "all-formed", *mthā' dag gzugs can*, and "the beautiful-formed" mark the lines) gives the wise a mind-ravishing knowledge, who grants boundless intelligence to expounding and composing, the omniscient Rin chen mtshan: the place of homage ("the adorner of the whole earth" in brackets). Through the broad road of the faultless reckoning treatises, the lotus-friend of the Phug school's tables, drawn out by the learned and diligent Rta ldang (or Rta ljang; the reading is not certain), these rays dispel the darkness of the wise; the faulty arisings are driven off.

The white lotus of the tables of the house (sign) and the lunar day is hard: though it has broad, deep petals, it closed in the middle; the many dull people shut it at the three sessions; this sun of a newly made good explanation opens it fully (bracketed gloss: "the beautiful-formed").

Though much has been said already on the expanded and abridged working of the tables for the five planets, I set down here, as my own memorandum and for the benefit of others, the widely used, faultless ones that have been extended, and some newly made.

First, the table of the *bskal li* (the reading of the term is not settled here) rests on one base for knowing the movement of lunar days, days, ages and planets: the hour (*chu tshod*), the "hole" (*bug*), the arrow (*mda'*), the water-weight (*chu srang*), fire, breath, sea and the part (*cha shas*), the "lord of men" (*mi bdag*). The hour, the veda (4), the moon-eye, the weight, the breath, power, fire, the sea and the parts are the sun's lunar-day enjoyment (*tshes longs*), the lunar-day circle of three hundred and one: (301). Multiplied by each one's own number it is called the sun's "halves". For the planets' and the sun's several enjoyments the lunar-day circle's count is applied; that is the "middle" planet and sun; with the purifying of the house of birth and the true-moving hour and so on, done as in the five-summaries, it is each table's root working.

To grasp it quickly: take the sun's lunar-day enjoyment, break up the weights, breaths, parts and the rest into their own parts and put each into its own place; divide the arrow-and-moon (the sum) by 135; the quotient, by the sun's three feet (*rkang*) is multiplied each separately.
