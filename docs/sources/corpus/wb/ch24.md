## <a id="wb-1-p380c"></a>wb.1:p380c The son meets: «དཀར་ན་གཟུང» in three prints

*Read: scan, 2026-11-07.*

> བུ་ཕྲད་དཀར་ན་གཟུང་དུ་བོར། །

"If the son meets, [with] the white [pebble] he is to be taken up and thrown (?)": the line on what the pebble's meeting a son means, read «དཀར་ན་གཟུང་དུ་བོར» in three independent prints: the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5028 img. 375, line 25; typeset, read by eye on a 3.5× line crop and by Yigdzin-1), the Taikhang (I5877 img. 515, line 6) and the 1996 etext. Only Yigdzin-1's reading of the Zhol print (img. 498) gives «དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་དུ་བོར», and its crop does not decide; the wording «དཀར་ན་གཟུང་» stands in the three others.

## <a id="wb-1-p381b"></a>wb.1:p381b The count by place: the manner of laying the pebbles by account

*Read: scan, 2026-11-01.*

> དེ་ལ་ཁ་སྨྲང་རྡེལ་འགྲེམ་ལུགས། །མི་མཐུན་མང་ཡང་དཀར་ནག་གི། །གསུམ་གཉིས་ལ་སོགས་མི་འདྲེན་པར། །ངོ་ཐེག་དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པ་ཡི། །གང་མང་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་བོར་བ།

WB vol. 1, p. 381, l. 2–4 (the 1996 etext, img. 391; the Gser thang reprint, I3CN5028 img. 376, read on a 4–7× crop for «ངོ་ཐེག་དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པ་ཡི»; the stack after ཁ་ in «ཁ་སྨྲང» is a solid blot at that size and is read from the etext):

"On that, the manner of laying out the pebbles by account (ཁ་སྨྲང, kha smrang): though the unlike are many, white and black are not to be drawn in threes and twos and the like; whichever is the more of the white and black counted by place (ངོ་ཐེག, ngo theg), the verdict pebble is laid that."

## <a id="wb-1-p385b"></a>wb.1:p385b Other verdicts as each fits, and the gathering into the four

*Read: machine, 2026-11-01.*

> ཐམས་ཅད་ནག་ན་ཤིན་ཏུ་ངན། །མིང་སྤོ་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་ཚེ་དབང་ཞུ། །འཁོར་གསུམ་ཡོངས་སྤང་སྤོང་དག་བྱ། །གཞན་ཡང་ཁ་དམར་ཅི་རིགས་གདགས། །སྨེ་སྤར་ལ་སོགས་ཀེག་མང་ཡང་། །མདོར་བསྡུས་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཐང་དང་། །ཀླུང་རྟ་བཞི་ཡིན་དེ་བས་ན། །ཁྱད་པར་སྡེ་བཞིའི་ཁ་དམར་བསྡུས།

WB vol. 1, p. 385, l. 15–19 (the 1996 etext, img. 395; the same verse in KD vol. 3, the *Rgyu skar phreng ba*, printed p. 679, etext only):

"If all are black, very bad: change the name, do the long-life rite, ask for the empowerment; give up the three circles entirely and purify. Further, lay the verdicts as each fits (ཅི་རིགས་གདགས, ci rigs gdags). Though the obstacles are many, the mewa (སྨེ་བ, sme ba), the trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) and the rest, in brief they are the four, life force, body, power and luck; therefore the special verdicts (ཁྱད་པར་, khyad par) of the four classes are gathered."

## <a id="wb-1-p385-387"></a>wb.1:p385-387 The household round: the Chinese pebble round of a household

*Read: scan, 2026-10-23.*

> །གཞན་ཡང་ཁྱིམ་གཅིག་བཟའ་མི་གྲངས། །མང་ཚོགས་ཁ་དམར་གཅེས་པ་ཡི། །རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར་ཞེས་བྱ་བ། །བཟའ་ཚང་ཁྱིམ་གཅིག་མི་དུ་ཡོད། །སོ་སོའི་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཀླུང་གི། །བཏང་གཅོད་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་རེ་དང་། །སྨེ་བ་ཁམས་རྡེལ་ལྔ་བོར་ལ། །ཕོ་མོ་རིམ་པ་རྒན་རིམ་བཞིན། །མ་ནོར་རྡེལ་རྣམས་ལེགས་པར་བཀྲམ། །གོང་མའི་ལུས་དང་འོག་མ་ཡི། །ཁམས་ནས་རིམ་པས་བླང་བར་བྱ། །ལྷག་མ་མ་རྫོགས་བར་དུ་ལེན། །རྡེལ་ཀུན་གསེགས་རྡར་རིས་སུ་བླང་། །དེ་ལ་རྐྱང་པའི་རྡེལ་སྐོར་ཟེར། །ལྷ་ས་རབ་འབྲིང་ཐ་གསུམ་མོ།། ལྷ་སར་རྡེལ་ནག་བབས་གྱུར་ན། །འཁོན་དང་མི་གཙང་བ་ལ་འཛེམ། །བསངས་དང་ཁྲུས་ཀྱི་ཆོ་ག་བྱ། །རྡེལ་དམར་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །ཐབ་གཞོབ་མི་གཙང་བ་ལ་འཛེམ། །སྔོ་དམར་གཉིས་པོ་བྱུང་གྱུར་ན། །ཀླུ་བཙན་རུ་བསྡུམ་བྱ་བ་ཡིན། །རྡེལ་ལྗང་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །འཐབ་དང་འཁོན་ལ་འཛེམ་པར་བྱ། །ཀླུ་བཙན་མཚོ་སྨན་ཆོ་གས་བཅོས། །རྡེལ་སེར་བྱུང་ན་ནད་ཡམས་ངོ། །གཙུག་ཏོར་སྟོང་བཟློག་རྒྱལ་མདོས་བྱ། །རྡེལ་དཀར་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །བཟང་ཞིང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་རྡེལ་ཡིན་ནོ། །གཏང་རག་ལྷ་གསོལ་ལྷ་ཆོག་བྱ། །རྡེལ་མཇུག་ལྷ་སར་བོར་བར་བྱ།། དེ་འོག་བར་པ་ཐ་མ་གསུམ། །མི་ས་རབ་འབྲིང་ཐ་གསུམ་ཡིན། །དེ་རུ་རྡེལ་དཀར་བྱུང་བ་ན། །བསམ་འགྲུབ་གཡུང་དྲུང་བདེ་ཞིང་སྐྱིད། །མི་རྣམས་ཀུན་ལ་ཤི་ཆད་མེད། །རྡེལ་ནག་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །གློ་བུར་ཡེ་འདྲོག་འོང་བའི་[p. 386, img. 396] དུས། །ཚེ་གཟུངས་ཆོ་ག་བྱེད་པར་བཤད། །རྡེལ་དེ་སྔོན་པོ་བྱུང་བ་ན། །འབྲས་དང་ཤུ་ཐོར་འོང་བའི་ངོ། །ས་གཉན་རྡོ་གཉན་བརྐོ་མི་ཉན། །ས་བདག་ཀླུ་ཡི་ཆོ་ག་བྱེད། །རྡེལ་དེ་ལྗང་གུ་བྱུང་བ་ན། །ཀླུ་བརྒྱད་ཀླུ་བསེན་དགུ་མདོས་བྱ། །རྡེལ་དམར་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །དམར་ངོ་གྲི་ཁ་མཚོན་བསྒྱུར་དང་། །ནག་པོ་མགོ་གསུམ་དགྲ་བྲུབ་གྱིས། །རྡེལ་སེར་བྱུང་ན་རིམས་ནད་ངོ། །བདུད་མདོས་མ་ཊཾ[?]་ཁྲག་མདོས་ཐོང་།། སྔ་མ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་རྡེལ་མཇུག་ལ། །དཀར་པོ་བཟང་རབ་གཏམ་སྙན་ཐོས། །ནག་པོ་ནད་ཡམས་ངོ་ཡང་ཡིན། །སེར་པོ་བྱུང་ན་འགོ་ནད་འོང་། །རྡེལ་སྔོན་བྱུང་བར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །ཀླུ་ཡི་ནད་དང་གཉན་ནད་འོང་། །དམར་བྱུང་གྲི་ངོ་མཚོན་ངོ་ཡོད།། རྡེལ་མཇུག་འགོ་མའི་གོང་མའི་འོག །ཁྱིམ་ས་རབ་འབྲིང་ཐ་གསུམ་ཟེར། །དེ་ལ་རྡེལ་དཀར་བབས་པ་ན། །བཀྲ་ཤིས་རྡེལ་ནི་ཡིན་པས་བཟང་། །ནག་ན་ཁྱིམ་སར་ན་ཆགས་ཆེ། །བདུད་ཀྱི་བླ་ཁྲོམ་འཕྲུལ་གསུམ་བྱ། །ཁྱིམ་ནང་ས་དཀར་ཕྱོགས་བཞིར་གཏོར། །ཁང་པའི་ཕྱོགས་བཞིར་གཡུང་དྲུང་བྲི། །སྐྱེད་ཕུར[?]་ཁུ་དཀར་གསོལ་བ་དང་། །སྔོ་ལྗང་རྡེལ་དེ་བབས་པ་ན། །ཀླུ་བརྒྱད་ས་བདག་གཏོ་བསྒྱུར་དང་། །ཀླུ་འབུམ་ཀླུ་ཡི་སྤང་སྐོང་འདོན། །རྡེལ་སེར་རིམས་ནད་ངོ་རུ་ཤར། །བློ[?]་བཟློག་མ་མོའི་ཆོ་ག་བྱ། Further, the precious verdicts (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar) where the many members (བཟའ་མི, bza' mi) of one house gather, called the Chinese pebble round (རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར, rgya nag rdel skor). However many people one household (བཟའ་ཚང, bza' tshang) holds, lay for each one deciding verdict pebble (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod) of each of the life force (སྲོག, srog), body (ལུས, lus), power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang) and luck (ཀླུང་རྟ, klung rta), and a pebble of the mewa's (སྨེ་བ, sme ba) element, five; lay the pebbles out well, without error, men and women in order, each by age. Take them in turn, the upper one's body and the lower one's element; take until the remainder is complete; take all the pebbles in a zigzag (གསེགས་རྡར་རིས, gsegs rdar ris). That is called the plain pebble round (རྐྱང་པ, rkyang pa): the god's place (ལྷ་ས, lha sa), best, middle and last.

If a black pebble falls in the god's place, keep from grudges and from what is impure; do the rites of purifying smoke and washing. If a red pebble comes, keep from impurity of the hearth. If blue and red both come, do a rite to bring the nāgas (ཀླུ, klu) and the *btsan* (བཙན, btsan) together. If a green pebble comes, keep from fights and grudges; remedy with the rites of the nāgas, *btsan* and lake goddesses (མཚོ་སྨན, mtsho sman). If a yellow pebble comes, it is the sign of an epidemic: the Uṣṇīṣa (གཙུག་ཏོར, gtsug tor) repelling rite of the thousand and the king's thread-cross (རྒྱལ་མདོས, rgyal mdos). If a white pebble comes, it is a good, auspicious pebble: do thanksgiving, offering to the gods and the gods' rite. Lay the tail pebble (རྡེལ་མཇུག, rdel mjug) in the god's place.

Below it the middle and the last, three: the people's place (མི་ས, mi sa), best, middle and last. If a white pebble comes there: wishes fulfilled, lasting, happy and well; no death or loss among all the people. If a black pebble comes: a time of sudden misfortune; longevity-dhāraṇī rites are prescribed. If that pebble is blue: the sign of swellings and dropsy; do not dig fierce earth or fierce stone; do the rites of the earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag) and nāgas. If it is green: the thread-cross of the eight nāgas and the nine *klu bsen*. If red: the sign of blades and weapons turned back, and the destruction of enemies by the black three-headed one. If yellow: the sign of contagious illness; send the māra thread-cross, the *ma ṭaṃ*[?] and the blood thread-cross.

On the tail of the first two: white, best and good, good news heard; black, the sign of sickness and epidemics; yellow, contagion comes; blue, illness of the nāgas and of the *gnyan* (གཉན, gnyan) comes; red, the sign of blades and weapons.

The tail pebble, under the upper of the first: the house's place (ཁྱིམ་ས, khyim sa), best, middle and last. If a white pebble falls there, it is an auspicious pebble and good. If black, much illness in the house: do the māras' *bla khrom* and the three magic rites, scatter white earth in the four quarters inside the house, draw swastikas on the four sides of the house, offer white liquid at the *skyed phur*[?]. If a blue or green pebble falls: the eight nāgas' and earth lords' turning rite, and recite the *Klu 'bum* and the nāgas' purifying and restoring. A yellow pebble shows the sign of contagion: do the *blo*[?] repelling rite of the *ma mo*. Red, the sign of red at the hearth: the red *gtor ma*, the wrathful play and the *zor* thread-cross.

The verdict of the house's place's leftover pebble (རྡེལ་རོ, rdel ro): two white, good fortune won; a white and a black, the sign of a mixed lot; one black, the sign of illness; two black, the sign of death and loss: a corpse will surely come in the end. The household's trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) rites and the like, and for the four, body, life force, power and luck, the rites that restore and raise them are offered; the small year's course is reckoned closely. If white, black, three come: a bone comes out of the middle of the butter; the king of teeth, the fang, breaks. If blue, red and yellow come: water seeps up from under the hearth-stone; what was thought in the heart goes to the lungs. If green comes, the sign of gossip. These are the leftover's verdicts: a leftover, it is said, is bad.

The first, plain pebble round is called the god's place; then the second turn is called the people's place; then the third turn is called the house's place. The wealth's place (ནོར་ས, nor sa), best, middle and last: one each in the god's and people's places, two each in the house's places; lay out the directions and take them. Below the house's place, the enemy's place (དགྲ་ས, dgra sa): if a white pebble rises there, the enemy's head is high: do the turning back, pressing down and destroying of enemies; if black, red, green, yellow or blue come, the enemy's head is brought low, and that is good.

Further results are told thus: a white pebble first in the god's place, the folded pebbles' remainder also white and the refolded remainder white: the sign that a gods' illness comes. Or the god's place first black, the folded pebble white and the refolded black: a māra has come down on the guardian gods, very bad: plant prayer flags on the hill-top, make offering to the local god and praise. If a white pebble comes in the people's place, the people at home are well; in the house's place, a white pebble: no loss to the house, grain and wealth increase; at the top of the enemy's place, a white pebble is the sign of lawsuits.

Now, for the general verdict: when in one house many have the mewa (སྨེ་བ, sme ba) of the year on the **one white**, it is a time when a corpse comes though no one is due to die; harm of the *chags 'dre* of the sky's *the'u rang*, the earth's and the trees' brings gods' illness and epidemics: beware of impurity. Many on the **three whites**: the time when the male god goes up to the sky: do the rites of flight and climbing, purifying washing and clearing to the gods. Many **black**: the *ya bdud* above stirs; illness and dirt come, bad for servants, horses, dogs and cattle; loss of courage and separation come: keep the man's country and the woman's apart; move the house and the hearth; scatter white earth in the house and set *tsha tsha*; recite the extensive, middle and short (the *Hundred Thousand*, the *Twenty Thousand*, the *Eight Thousand*), give teaching (to the saṅgha) and alms (to the poor), the hundred and four, which help. Many on the **three blue**: the earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag) are offended; the hearth falls, heads break, enemies rise, and grief threatens: bad; recite the Uṣṇīṣa white and black and the *Gzungs bsdus*; the black three-headed one helps. Many on the **four green**: offence with the earth lords and the nāgas (ཀླུ, klu) brings nāga illnesses, *dmu chu*, tumours and growths; as fire's enemy and water's enemy meet, make nāga offerings and the lake goddesses' thread-cross. Many on the **five yellow**: harm of the *rgyal 'gong*, loss, bile illness, harm of the *rbad 'dre*: do the king's and the blood thread-cross, and do not take Bon practitioners as enemies. Many on the **seven red**: enemies and *sri* rise; much sign of wounds and blood, bad for (one's own) horses: the *btsan*'s release, the hurling of the *zor*, the red repelling. Many on the **nine red**: life and fortune decline: bind the fortune rites, summoning of life, the fortune door and the fortune peg. In the house many with the enemy of the life force rising, the power's enemy rising and the mewa red: bad for livestock. Many black with one white: the wolf rises from the high hill-side. Many white with one black: a bone comes out of the middle of the butter. Raise each one's own mewa rites.

Further, in one house many on the three whites: the time when a god comes from the place of the gods' punishment: gods' illness and epidemics: the gods' rite, the poison washing, offering to the gods, fulfilment and confession. Many on the two black and the dark blue: a corpse is wanted; the *ya bdud* stirs, illness and grief and suffering come: the māra thread-cross and the ninefold repelling of grief. Many green: offence with the earth lords and the *gnyan*: remedy with the nāga rite, the lake goddesses and the *ma mo*. Many yellow: the *rgyal 'gong* enters the hearth: loss, bile and epidemics, harm of the *rbad 'dre*: send the king's and the blood thread-cross and the hundred *bse*. Many red: the sign of red, of blood: the *btsan* rite and the life's substitute. Many with the power's enemy rising, and the body red with the mouth upward: called *sha bred*, bad for livestock.

Again, each one's own "two black" is shown: one white is the two black of small boys (of children); two black is everyone's, the crux for men in their prime; three blue for children (the *shar po*'s two black); four green for widows (the two black of the *yugs sa*); five yellow, *brje*; six white, Bon (the reading of this pair not settled); seven red, the two black of women; eight white, of ministers and monks; nine red is the crux of the *btsad po* (the elders). To fall there is bad: raise the rites.
