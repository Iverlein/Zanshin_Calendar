The close of the quick tables' last table and a rule for the rough reckoning of lunar dates.

*The table (continued from p. 85).* A row of fourteen cells and a closing cell (the figures run 1 to 14 and 0) is headed by the "foundation" (*bya ra ba*, བྱ་ར་བ) taken from "the remainder of the division by the mind" (*yid kyis bgos lhag*; the mind is 14, as the bracket in the verse below also gives it). Under it seven rows are labelled by ranges of a number counted from 1 to 100: from 101 to 125; from 32 to 41; from 44 to 50; 63 by itself; from 76 to 83; from 85 to 94; from 96 to 100. Each row holds fourteen numerals; the numerals were not read cell by cell.

*The verse that follows* is headed "Another, a rough-reckoning (*rags rtsis*) method for the dates". For the pure *dhruva* (ཌྷྲུ་བ, the constant of the day's planet), the planet's station is given its hours (*chu tshod*) by the date counted; the figures of the rule are in brackets (4 weights, 22 for the hand-eye count, then 11 for "the fierce", 10, 8, 6, 12 and others), and they set out how, for Aries, Taurus, Virgo and the other houses, hours are given or taken off in sixes and elevens ("give to Aries, take from Taurus"), how Cancer's hours stay in place, how Leo, Virgo, Libra and Scorpio are treated, and how the date's count is mixed whole and divided by the mind (14), the remainder taken as the general parts. The verse closes: "from the hours (*chu tshod*) to the fourth or fifth only, rough mansions are known; into the abridged table of the chapters the result goes, and whichever drawing is wanted appears." It is the author's quick way of making the date reckoning for the rough (not exact) day.
