"Further, the ways of examining the earth lord Lto-'phye (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ, lto 'phye) in the tantra classes (the *Legs grub* of the Kriyā class, the *Rdor phreng*, the *Gsang ba spyi rgyud* and the rest, as the line names them) differ from one another, and though many, are shown in the book [of their own]. The practice, in brief, in the essence: in the general manner of the great lines of the eight, the extent equals the figure's own extent, and since even the smallest, as little as a bre-measure's footing, is wished to rest within the lines, draw a square as large as is fitting. In it, from east, south, west and north, [the plot] is divided into ten great parts [«ཆ་ཆེན»] each way, and within them nine small parts [«ཆ་ཕྲན»] are laid in each: in each direction ninety squares, equal to the days of three zodiac houses; the squares round the rim, all together, equal the 360 days of the houses. In this the earth lord Lto-'phye is a man, upper body with the measuring stake [«གདེངས་ཀ»]: the left hand covers the left ear, the right holds a jewel; the lower body is covered by a cloth, in the form of Lto-'phye. In the length of the body, ten *mtho* (མཐོ, *mtho*); in the width, the measure of its own face [«རང་ཞལ་དོ»]. Of these, the stake, face, heart, navel and secret place are one *mtho* each, five; likewise the tail is held to be five *mtho*. On the body the eight nāgas as told below. [He is set] from winter's first month, the girl's house: starting from fire, clockwise; each house a small part; he is moved, and the head is south in the three winter months, west in spring, north in summer, east in autumn; the days of the four middle [months], at the fifteenth: the Brahmā line of the direction is the place of the stretch. The way of taking the earth's aim and the accomplishment: for example, on the middle autumn month in the east middle, the head east and the tail's end west; from the east, to the three great parts: for the stake, face and heart, small parts, twenty-seven; leaving aside [the stretch] below, from the Brahmā line at the north back, small parts, half and fourteen, before the part let go; the earth's aim is east-west a half and nine, south-north a half and five: these are the special places to take accomplishment. Further, small differences are told in the chapter on results."

> གཡམ་པ་རྡོ་གས་བྱུང་གྱུར་ན། །བུད་མེད་བུས་པ་རྣམས་ལ་ངན། །གོས་ཧྲུལ་ཤིང་ཧྲུལ་བྱུང་གྱུར་ན། །ཕ་ཚན་ཤི་ཁ་བྱེ་ཉེན་ཡོད། (p. 283, l. 7–8)

"If a split stone slab comes up, it is bad for the women and the small children; if rotten cloth or rotten wood comes up, there is a danger of death for the father's kin."

> ཁྱི་སྤྱང་བྲང་ཤུལ་ས་བླངས་ལ། །སྒོ་ཡི་ཡ་ཐེམ་སྤྲས[སྦས?]་པ་ཡིས། །གོད་ཁ་བར་ཆད་མི་འབྱུང་ཐུབ། (p. 294, l. 7–8)

"Earth is taken from the old lair of the dog and wolf, and by adorning (or: hiding it in) the door's lintel, loss and hindrance can be kept from arising."

- **The tail's «མ་ཧེ» (q463).** The last syllable of «མཇུག་མ་གླང་ཆེན་རྟ་མ་ཧེ» is ha with the vowel e, not nya as Yigdzin-1 reads on both prints. Set beside the page's own letters at 6×, it has the second, inner stroke and the tail swung to the lower left of the «ཧ» of «མོ་ཧ» on p. 284 (img. 290, l. 18), unlike the plain «ད» of «དེ་ནས» on the same page (l. 23) and unlike the looped «ཉ» of «ཉམས» on the same line; the 1996 etext has «ཧེ» too, and the 1996 scan the same outline at 12×. «རྟ» has the shape of «རྟ» in «གླང་པོ་རྟ་མཆོག» (l. 24). The seven syllables are then elephant (གླང་ཆེན, glang chen), horse (རྟ, rta) and water buffalo (མ་ཧེ, ma he; so in Hopkins, the Rangjung Yeshe, the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* and the Mahāvyutpatti, Skt. *mahiṣa*, in Steinert's dictionaries); WB pairs «བ་ལང་མ་ཧེ» and «རྟ་བོང་མ་ཧེ» elsewhere (etext, vol. 1 img. 213; vol. 2 img. 109). So digging at the tail kills the large beasts and the cattle, and strength declines.
- **The harm by part (q463).** The three lines are as the machine read them, on both prints: the part where Lto-'phye lies kills the household (parents, sons and brothers, wife, friends), the back kills the digger or moves him and loses goods, the tail kills the beasts. The stack of the name in l. 17 is a blot on both scans (ལྟ against ལྷ not told), the etext and p. 281 have «ལྟོ་འཕྱེ». The words «དགུ་ཆའི་སར» stand as printed; whether they mean the ninth part or the nine small parts that make one great part of p. 281's square, these lines do not say.
- **«བུད་མེད་བུས་པ» (q464).** Read so on both scans: «བུད་» ends l. 7, «མེད་བུས་པ་རྣམས་ལ་ངན» opens l. 8, the letters clear at 4× on the reprint and 7× on the 1996 scan. «བུས་པ» is a small child in the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* («བྱིས་པ་ཆུང་ཆུང», a little child), the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* (a child not long born) and Dan Martin (Old Tibetan for «བྱིས་པ»), and WB uses it so: «བུས་པ་སྐྱེས» (a child born) and «བུད་མེད་ཤི» side by side (vol. 2 etext img. 72), «བུས་པ་བཙས་རྩིས» (the reckoning of a child's birth, vol. 1 etext img. 272). The line is: a split stone slab (གཡམ་པ, g.yam pa; a natural stone slab in the *Tshig mdzod chen mo*) found in the dug ground is bad for the women and the small children.
- **«ཁྱི་སྤྱང་བྲང་ཤུལ» (q464).** Read so on both scans, every letter clear on the reprint at 4×: dog (ཁྱི, khyi), wolf (སྤྱང, spyang, short for spyang ki), the dwelling (བྲང, brang; a lodging, dwelling place in Ives Waldo and the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs*) and its trace or former site (ཤུལ, shul; as «ཁང་ཤུལ», a house's former site, «ཉལ་ཤུལ», where one lay, in the *Tshig mdzod chen mo*). The *Tshig mdzod chen mo* also has «ཁྱི་སྤྱང» as one word, a kind of dog like a wolf, fierce. So the earth is taken from the old lair of dogs and wolves, or of that wolf-like dog; the lines do not tell the two apart. The earth goes to the door's lintel (ཡ་ཐེམ, ya them), against loss (གོད་ཁ, god kha; loss of property, of cattle, in the Rangjung Yeshe and Dan Martin) and hindrance; the verb's stack after «ཡ་ཐེམ» is a blot on the reprint at 7× and small on the 1996 scan at 12×: Yigdzin-1 reads «སྤྲས» (adorned) on the reprint and «སྤས» on the 1996 scan, the etext «སྦས» (hidden); not settled.
