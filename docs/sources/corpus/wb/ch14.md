… [when the figure is] drawn like the middle [version]: Rāhu, the fire, the smoke and the three "soot" (the second and third words are not settled), and the planets' five "stings" [of the teeth] not having occurred, it falls [short]; for the "maker" (བྱེད) and the "established" (གྲུབ) reckonings, both subtract; or, whichever kind of established reckoning, with the two places, set the "analysis" check-marks (དཔྱོད་ཁྲ) as in the middle one.
Thus the chapter-list from the "Neck Ornament of the Learned, the White Beryl Necklace, the Heart-jewel of the Analytic", the good exposition of the Phugpa reckoning, [begins:] in the time of the learned who face the Phugpa, though it appears that there are three, outer, inner and the "unpassable threshold" (ཐེམ་མི་འདའ), the "unpassable threshold" seems not to have been much spread among disciples and the like; and the hand-sheets offered in the time of the all-knowing Bsod-nams-rgya-mtsho by the pupil Chos-rje Bde-legs-nyi-ma, and the hand-tradition of Dumpo Don-dbang, differ somewhat; and although [writers] made proofs that the inner root was each one's own system, in substance it seems that this is not Chos-rje Bde-legs-nyi-ma's inner [root]. When this great Sakya protector formerly did not give hand-copies, he was also stuck for [other] good parts, and showed much kindness; and since the important time must now be reckoned and the rest, [the author], adorned with the many good parts that his own mind found, [made] the enlarged chapter-list; the hand-tradition of Dumpo, the unmixed middle one, no longer succeeds today either; so that many persons who need the chapter-list and the like may benefit, [he made] the abridgement, dividing it into three, extended, middle and abridged. In Tibet the day grows and shrinks by two srang-breaths [a day]: this is told at length from the first and second of the Chapter on the Elements (ཁམས་ལེའུ) and elsewhere; in direct observation, in the union of times and everywhere, the longest day does not come to more than thirty-three [chu tshod] and thirty srang; the longest end at thirty-six, [by] the unbroken increase and decrease of the Snowy Mountain (རི་བོ་གངས་ལྡན, the "snowy mountain", perhaps Tibet; unsettled) up to today. This is the fourteenth chapter, adorned with pure good tools, made so that the day-length of our Tibetans agrees with the direct union of times.
