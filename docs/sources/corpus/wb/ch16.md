The first six lines continue the praise begun on p. 184 (the first of them stands in brackets in the print) and carry no rule: «the grace that surpasses ornaments», the long-tailed comet, the golden-pillar pig and the rest in the solstice, the clairvoyant youthful tree, the fruit that shows what is to come, and (in brackets) the concentration that sets out many teachings. The readings «རྗེན་སྟོན» and «ནོར་འཛིན་པང་པར» are given as printed; their sense is not settled.

Then the matter. The summer and winter solstices are taken in many ways. Joined to our own rough measure (*sgang tshad*), the earlier teaching holds that on the day after the turning the shadow changes; the views agree in a mistaken way, but for the fine reckoning: at the place where we live the measure of the winter solstice, at the mean sun (*nyi bar*), is 18 minutes of the mansion (*skar ma*) and the number-word figures «གཟུགས་མེ» (1, 3) and «མཁའ་ཡོན» (0, 3) with «སྲང» (the *srang*); when it rises thus there is no error. To make it understood there is the wheel: in the black month the measure of the year when the sun enters *stong* is the date 18 (*ma nu*), the desired part (*'dod cha*) 7 and the fifth part 4. From it, in order from the east: for the date the *drag po* (11), for the desired part *gzugs* (1). To take it: for the years of the rab byung elapsed give «མཁའ་ནོར་ཆུ་བོ» (480), divide by «མེ་གཟུགས» (13); the quotient shows the number of wheels elapsed, the remainder the cell of this year in that wheel. The upper of the cell's two figures gives India, the lower Tibet: the date of the winter solstice, its desired part and its fifth part. To the mean sun of that date give the desired part that has risen and the fifth part 4; the measure then appears without confusion. By the same force the summer sun can be understood too, it is said, but since the measure and the rest are not clear mistakes mostly arise; so the summer solstice's measure is taken from the *Lamp that Makes the Solstice Clear* (*Nyi ldog gsal ba'i sgron me*): from the Brahmā-line (*tshangs thig*) of the eastern continent (the text writes Jambudvīpa's east) to the place where we live there are seven house-days (*khyim zhag*), and from the middle Brahmā-line to our place twenty-three; as that text says, the difference of the seven days is given to «རིག་བྱེད་མཁའ་མེ» (a number word: 4, 0, 3); or by the difference of twenty-three days subtract «སྐར་དྲུག་ཞེ་ལྔ» (6 minutes 45?) twice; the two come to the same, and the sun reaches «སྐར་དབང་དབྱུག་གཟུགས་མཁའ་མེ» at the mean sun: then the summer solstice of this place turns without error.

By the fourth method of working (*sgrub tshul*): take the remainder of the tenth's last arrow (*mda' ro*), multiply by «རོ» (6), divide by the desired part (18); the quotient and the remainder give, in the middle of the south-east, the month, the days and the desired part of the summer and winter solstices. For this place: add seven days of date to the days; for the desired part two, for the fifth part four: this gives the date, desired part and fifth part of our place. Mixed together, the measures of both solstices are known without error. Of whichever solstice the date and desired part are known: add six to the date and take the desired part from it (the printed «ཕྲི»): that gives the other solstice. For example, if the winter solstice's measure is added (the printed «སྟེར་དབྲི») the summer's is known; so, by adding or subtracting the house-days, the solstice of the following year is known. These things, the *Lamp that Makes the Solstice Clear* and the rest, are the hidden meaning of Nor bzang rgya mtsho's «Nyi ldog»; then the page breaks off in the reasons for it («དགོས་»).

Continued from p. 187: «… [they fixed it] by the making only; for the proven, the measure has no mansion-minute by nature; the faults of the making and the like confuse the time. In the time of the wise who are strict in measure, the pig day reckoned by the making agrees with the error-free pure proven reckoning, and at the mean sun without confusion, «མདའ་དང་དུས་མཚོ་མཁའ་མེ་སྲང» (5, 6; 4, 3; 0, 3 *srang*), when the pig's seven days rise, the net of doubt in leading them is cut.»

The table that follows lists, for each of the 65 years of the cycle (*bya ra ba*, the row head over the year numbers), the sage's date (*ri ti'i tshes grangs*) with its desired part (*'dod cha*) and fifth part (*lnga cha*), then the pig's date (*phag zhag tshes grangs*) with the same two parts. It is printed in four blocks of 15, 19, 18 and 13 years; this page holds the first three. The figures are Tibetan numerals, one cell each. The sage's fifth part is 1 in every cell and the pig's is 4.

Then the rule for the sage and the pig days. Next, to show the measure of the sage and pig days when they come: to the years of the rab byung elapsed give «དུས་མིག» (26), divide by *mda' ro* (65); with the remainder take the cell of the *bya ra* (the table's column). The upper is the sage, the lower the pig's days; the first of what comes is the date, the middle the desired part, the lower the fifth part. To the pure proven mean sun of the date so taken give the desired part and the fifth part by their numbers; each one's measure then appears without confusion. As the desired part and fifth part rise, check which date it falls on, the earlier or the later.
