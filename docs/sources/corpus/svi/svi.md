The Co ne print of Advayavajra's examination of dreams (rKTs 4282, Co ne item C2052, vol. 49 «rgyud tshi» 130a1-130b7, images 263-264 of I1GS66080; the place found by RD273). Two places read, by Yigdzin-1 on the frame at 1.5x and at 2x (unsharp mask) and by MITRA on the 1.5x frame and a half-line strip; letters about 25 px, bitonal, dotted.

The three parts of the night (img. 263, line 3): the first part disturbs the habit-traces (bag chags), in the second the hungry ghosts (yi dwags) go about, in the last one sleeps having examined the dream. Both Yigdzin-1 readings give «ཐ་མར་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟགས་ལ་ཉལ», as the Derge etext; MITRA's frame gives «མཐར་…ཞལ» and its strip «ཐ་མར་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟགས་པ་ལ» (the extra «པ» of the Peking, svi:p309-312, as a doubt); not read letter by letter by eye (the line is too dotted at 4x), so the Co ne agrees with Derge on the two Yigdzin-1 readings and with Peking's extra syllable on one MITRA strip.

The families' line (img. 264): after Vairocana and Ratnasambhava, Amitabha (འོད་དཔག་མེད, 'od dpag med), then Amoghasiddhi («དོན་ཡོད་གྲུབ་པ», don yod grub pa), Akshobhya (མི་བསྐྱོད་པ, mi bskyod pa) and the six families' Vajrasattva (རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས, rdo rje sems): the Amoghasiddhi syllables are the same in all four runs; the line ends «རིགས་དྲུག» in both Yigdzin-1 readings and «རིགས་བྱུང་» in MITRA's, as the Derge etext's «རིགས་དྲུག»; by eye not read.

Img. 263 opens with the preceding work's colophon (མཛད་པའོ) and the Sanskrit title, which each run garbles (Yigdzin-1 «སྭ་པ་ན་མི་ཀྵ», MITRA «སྤང་ཐ་མི་ཥྛ»). Img. 264 ends with the colophon: the work is by the Indian pandit Advayavajra (དཔལ་ལྡན་གཉིས་སུ་མེད་པ), translated by a yogin (the translator's name garbled: «ཛྙཱ་ན» and «ཤྲཱི» are read).

Title: Sanskrit *Svapnaīkṣā* (the etext's «སྭ་པ་ན་ཨི་ཀྵ»); Tibetan the examination of dreams (རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ, rmi lam brtag pa). Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, the wisdom being (འཇམ་དཔལ་ཡེ་ཤེས་སེམས་དཔའ, 'jam dpal ye shes sems dpa'). With body, speech and mind ever devoted, bowing to Mañjuśrī-jñānasattva, "for the sake of what comes of the jewel-like seal of the family (རིགས, rigs), I shall tell of dreams."

Preparation: sitting on a seat of kuśa grass, with fragrant grass laid as a pillow, a garland of jātaka bound, mantra-garland recited and blown over a young girl's milk, with other (substances) smeared on the eyes. Then the three parts of the night (ཆ, cha): in the first part the habitual traces are stirred (བག་ཆགས་འཁྲུག, bag chags 'khrug); in the second the hungry ghosts (ཡི་དྭགས, yi dwags) walk about; in the last one lies down having examined the dream.

The signs by family, from the first: Vairocana's, as the verses go on: a mirror held in the hand; the moon of the first of the lunar day (ཚེས་གཅིག, tshes gcig) seen; riding a chariot wheel; wearing white clothes and white ornaments; subduing an elephant with a hook; becoming a caravan leader through compassion. Ratnasambhava's: finding gold; entering a bath that is natural; a vase set on the crown; mastery over all the other continents; riding a horse and going to the continent of jewels; being lifted by a crowd of people. Amitābha's: a flower, a lotus, taken up; a book held in the hand; blowing a conch and the like; a garland of flowers worn; a peacock seized in a snare; holding the lotus and the teaching with compassion. Amoghasiddhi's: weapon in hand, winning in battle; becoming the king's retinue; the army surrounding the field; being set to peaceful work; a bird flies (བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ, the words unclear) and is sent as messenger; a forest burnt by fire. Akṣobhya's (begun): swimming without sinking in water; going and soaring in the sky; wearing the sun and moon as ornaments; pressing down a lion throne; a white parasol raised and a banner hoisted: becoming worthy of everyone's worship. Vajrasattva's (begun): blood and (serum) flowing; driving a mass of fire without fear; eating snakes and sin; holding a vajra and bell; pressing down a mountain top; propping up a city with a finger. Then, "of the mind": worshipping the guru and the chosen deity (ཡི་དམ, yi dam); asking for a book and raising a stūpa; going naked and singing and dancing; keeping company with an assembly of monks; (continued on page 262).

Notes. «ཚེས་གཅིག་ཟླ་བ་མཐོང་བ», the moon of the first lunar day seen, is an image among the others; the text gives it no time of fulfilment. The passage's own order of the families is: Vairocana, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi, Akṣobhya, Vajrasattva; the verse on page 262 names them «རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་རིན་ཆེན་འབྱུང་ལྡན་དང་། འོད་དཔག་མེད་དང་དོན་ཡོད་གྲུབ་པ་དང་། མི་བསྐྱོད་པ་དང་རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས་རིགས་དྲུག», where the plain reading of the sixth, «རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས», is Vajrasattva; the sixth group's sign list starts at «ཁྲག་དང་ཆུ་སེར་ཟག་པ་དང་». The syllables are the etext's, not read one by one.

Continuing the "of the mind" signs: fine clothes worn and tree-fruit eaten; the Tathāgata's seal (ཕྱག་རྒྱ, phyag rgya); pronouncing blessings and giving prophecies; a great pond whirling; a high house built: "what comes in accord with the mind." Bad signs (continued from the same list): an enemy stripping one of ornaments; a young woman grieving; being put inside a dungeon; goods seized and put in irons; being driven by evil beings of the lower worlds; wild beasts hanging from the body; bound with thread and covered with filth; eating and drinking filth; fleeing at the sight of the guru, the master, friends and parents; a very deserted place without people, or a wilderness, wretched with food and drink; crying "alas" and a yak's sorrowful call; armour stripped and struck with weapons; having gone into the middle of a forest and being attacked by tigers, jackals and animals. "These that come as torment of mind are to be taken as signs of virtue and non-virtue." The six families: Vairocana, Ratnasambhava (རིན་ཆེན་འབྱུང་ལྡན), Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi, Akṣobhya and Vajrasattva, "in their own order and mixed."

Remedy: for a fortunate dream, begin the practice, according to the rite told for the Jewel Seal (རིན་ཆེན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ); for a bad sign, a sesame fire offering (ཏིལ་གྱི་སྦྱིན་སྲེག, til gyi sbyin sreg), purified a thousand times in that family. Then: habitual traces (བག་ཆགས, bag chags), the hungry ghosts, what pleases the mind: all things of saṃsāra are like a dream, the yogin should see them so, for the delusion is not real. "From realising the good, wisdom is born; what is made by the mind has no birth. So, in secret mantra, take the means that depend on dependent arising as the chief." The dreams of generating the mind of awakening and of empowerment are "gathered from Nāgārjuna's text"; "may the clear stages of good and bad lead to seeing the face of Mañjuśrī-jñānasattva." End of the examination of dreams by the Indian abbot Dpal ldan Gnyis su med pa (Advayavajra); made as his own translation by the yogin, almsman Prajñāśrījñānakīrti.
