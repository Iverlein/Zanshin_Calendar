## <a id="skd-p470-471"></a>skd:p470-471 *svapna*: the Brahmavaivarta's chapter of the good dream, with the series of spans (p. 470 col. 3, p. 471)

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD272), the opening dialogue and the first nine lines of the chapter on p. 470 enlarged, and the series again on a second copy (the Chowkhamba Sanskrit Series reprint, Internet Archive id sabdakalpadrumavol5vardaprasadvasuhariprasadvasuchowkambhasanskritseries_441_O, p. 470 = leaf n477, p. 471 = n478: the same plates, the same lines); the rest of pp. 470–471 read on the page images at reduced size.*

> नन्द उवाच ।
> "केन स्वप्नेन किं पुण्यं केन पुंसां भवेत् सुखम् ।
> कोऽपि कोऽपि च सुस्वप्नस्तत् सर्व्वं कथय प्रभो ॥"
> श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
> "वेदेषु सामवेदश्च प्रशस्तः सर्व्वकर्म्मसु ।
> तत्रैव काण्वशाखायां पुण्यकाण्डे मनोहरे ॥
> सुव्यक्तो यत्र सुस्वप्नः शश्वत् पुण्यफलप्रदः ।
> तत् सर्व्वं लिखितं तात कथयामि निशामय ॥
> स्वप्नाध्यायं प्रवक्ष्यामि बहुपुण्यफलप्रदम् । …"
>
> स्वप्नस्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरफलप्रदः ।
> द्वितीये चाष्टभिर्म्मासैस्त्रिभिर्म्मासैस्तृतीयके ॥
> चतुर्थे चार्द्धमासेन स्वप्नः स्यात्तु फलप्रदः ।
> दशाहे फलदः स्वप्नोऽप्यरुणोदयदर्शने ॥
> प्रातः स्वप्नश्च फलदस्तत्क्षणं यदि बोधितः ।
> दिने मनसि यद्दृष्टं तत् सर्व्वञ्च लभेद् ध्रुवम् ॥
> चिन्ताव्याधिसमायुक्तो नरः स्वप्नञ्च पश्यति ।
> तत् सर्व्वं निष्फलं तात प्रयात्येव न संशयः ॥

The article *svapna* in the *Śabdakalpadruma*, vol. 5 p. 470 (columns 2–3) to p. 471, which quotes the chapter of the *Brahmavaivartapurāṇa*, *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa*, in which Nanda asks and the Lord answers what dream brings merit and happiness. The article's line before the chapter says "susvapnā yathā" (the good dreams, as follows); the chapter's end on p. 471 reads "iti brahmavaivarte śrīkṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍe susvapnadarśanaṃ 77 adhyāyaḥ" (the chapter is numbered 77 in this printing; Jivananda's 1888 printing numbers the verses of the series 73.5–6, bvp:p662-665; Negelein's own references differ again, neg:p14-16). The verses of the series are read from the page image: the line "dine manasi yad dṛṣṭaṃ tat sarvvañ ca labhed dhruvam" has a blurred conjunct on the Nag copy and reads so on the Chowkhamba copy; the others agree with the layer.

What the passage says, in our words:

- **The Purāṇa traces the teaching to a Vedic place.** Before the chapter the Lord says that among the Vedas the Sāmaveda is best for every act and that in it, "in the Kāṇva branch, in the lovely book of merit" (*kāṇvaśākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare*), the good dream is plainly set out, always giving the fruit of merit; "I shall tell the chapter of dreams" follows. The branch named is the Kāṇva, which is not a Sāmaveda branch by the usual tradition (our remark; the print says what it says). No such book is named elsewhere in the pool.
- **The series**: a dream of the first watch (*yāma*) gives its fruit in a year; of the second in eight months; of the third in three months; of the fourth in half a month; a dream seen at the sight of the dawn in ten days; a morning dream at once, if one is woken. This is the series of the *Svapnohana* word for number (svo:p268; claim indian-dream-spans-by-watch-nine-texts), here on a second print of the Purāṇa.
- The next two lines are not in the other witnesses RD269 read (bvp:p662-665, neg:p14-16): what one has seen in the mind in the day, one gets that surely; the dream of a man afflicted with worry or illness is fruitless, "no doubt".
- The remainder of the chapter (pp. 470–471) is the catalogue of good dream-images (the fall of a good sight: curds and rice, lotus leaf, a fruiting tree, women in white, a Brahmin giving a blessing, a book received, and so on) and the fruit of each, ending with the Lord's "I have told you the good dream, Nanda, the cause of merit; do you want to hear more?" The chapter has no further span.

## <a id="skd-p471-472"></a>skd:p471-472 *svapna*: the dreams of Akrūra and Kaṃsa and the chapter of the bad dream, with its remedies

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD272), the headings and the ends of the chapters; the verses of the images read at reduced size, not word by word.*

> इति ब्रह्मवैवर्त्ते श्रीकृष्णजन्मखण्डे सुस्वप्नदर्शनं ७७ अध्यायः ॥
> अक्रूरदृष्टसुस्वप्ना यथा, — "अथाक्रूरः स्वभवनं गत्वा कंसेन प्रेरितः …"
> इति ब्रह्मवैवर्त्ते श्रीकृष्णजन्मखण्डे ७०।१–१८ ॥ दुःस्वप्ना यथा, — नन्द उवाच । "श्रुतं सर्व्वं महाभाग दुःस्वप्नं कथय प्रभो …"
> इति ब्रह्मवैवर्त्ते श्रीकृष्णजन्मखण्डे दुःस्वप्नदर्शनम् ८२ अध्यायः ॥ कंसदृष्टदुःस्वप्ना यथा, — … इति ब्रह्मवैवर्त्ते श्रीकृष्णजन्मखण्डे ८३ अध्यायः ॥

Pp. 471–473 of the article: after the chapter of the good dream the article gives Akrūra's good dreams (the dream the night before he goes to Vraja; a long list of sights), then the chapter of the bad dream, also Nanda's question and the Lord's answer ("he who in a dream laughs from joy, dances, sings, … dies"; the long catalogue of bad sights, among them a black woman, a cow's death, a snake bite, a naked or shaven man; the remedies after them: a fire offering of red sandalwood sticks smeared with ghee, and the eight names of Viṣṇu, Acyuta, Keśava, Viṣṇu, Hari, Satya, Janārdana, Haṃsa and Nārāyaṇa, recited by the pure man facing east, ten times, or a thousand times, "and the bad dream becomes a good one"), ending "iti … duḥsvapnadarśanaṃ 82 adhyāyaḥ", and Kaṃsa's bad dream (ch. 83 in this print): Kaṃsa says he saw it "at the end of the night" (*niśāśeṣe*), tells the sages and the priest, and knows that his end is near (the verses end "the time of destruction stood at hand"). The reference after the verses of Akrūra's dream ("70.1–18", read as printed) is a verse-range of the printing used by the article, whose chapter numbers differ from Jivananda's.

In these chapters there is **no** span of fruit by watch, no tithi, weekday, nakṣatra or month: the only time ties are those of p. 470's series and the remedy "to say after waking in the morning".

## <a id="skd-p473"></a>skd:p473 *svapna*: the list of other places, the Suśruta definition and the Vāgbhaṭa verses on dreams

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD272), the list of places and the Vāgbhaṭa verses on time, seven kinds and remedy; the rest of the Vāgbhaṭa verses and p. 472 at reduced size.*

> परशुरामदृष्टदुःस्वप्नाः ब्रह्मवैवर्त्ते गणेशखण्डे ३३ अध्याये, कार्त्तवीर्य्यार्जुनदृष्टदुःस्वप्नास्तत्रैव ३४ अध्याये, घोरासुरदृष्टदुःस्वप्ना देवीपुराणे २२ अध्याये, कालिकापुराणे पुष्याभिषेके ८७ अध्याये, मत्स्यपुराणे यात्रानिमित्तस्वप्नाध्यायकथने २४२ अध्याये च ते द्रष्टव्याः ॥
>
> दृष्टः श्रुतोऽनुभूतश्च प्रार्थितः कल्पितस्तथा ।
> भाविको दोषजश्चेति स्वप्नः सप्तविधो मतः ॥
> तेष्वाद्या निष्फलाः पञ्च यथा स्वप्नप्रकृतिर्दिवा ।
> विस्मृतो दीर्घह्रस्वोऽति पूर्व्वरात्रे [वि]पात् फलम् ॥
> दृष्टः करोति तुच्छञ्च गोमर्गे तदहर्म्महत् ॥
> निद्रया चानुपहतः प्रतीपैर्व्वचनैस्तथा ।
> याति पापोऽल्पफलतां दानहोमजपादिभिः ॥
> … इति वाग्भटे शारीरस्थाने षष्ठेऽध्याये ॥

The end of the article's long quotation and its pointers, p. 473. After the dream of Kaṃsa the article sends the reader to other places, named with chapter numbers: the bad dreams seen by Paraśurāma (*Brahmavaivarta*, *Gaṇeśakhaṇḍa* 33, read as printed: the leading digit is faint at the column's edge), by Kārtavīryārjuna (the same part, 34), by the demon Ghora (*Devīpurāṇa* 22), the *Kālikāpurāṇa* (*puṣyābhiṣeka*, ch. 87) and the *Matsyapurāṇa* ch. 242 ("the chapter of dreams as omens of the march"). Then comes a quotation in the heading "vaidyakokta" (said in medicine): the verses open with the last words of the messenger omens and turn to dreams ("ity uktaṃ dūtaśakunaṃ svapnān…", the last word blurred), then the list of bad dreams (drinking with the dead and dragged by a dog; a man who laughs carried off by a woman; ...), and the article closes the quotation with "iti vāgbhaṭe śārīrasthāne ṣaṣṭhe 'dhyāye" (in Vāgbhaṭa, *Śārīrasthāna*, chapter 6). The print names only Vāgbhaṭa; which work (the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* has a *Śārīrasthāna*) was not checked against an edition. Before it, the article's first definition of *svapna* quotes the Suśruta-saṃhitā, *Śārīrasthāna*, chapter 4, on the dream as the knowledge of one asleep ("the self of the elements, as it sleeps, takes good and bad things in the mind stirred by *rajas*, from the body it had before; when the organs fail in their action…", "iti suśrute śārīrasthāne caturthe 'dhyāye"), followed by "darśanam, iti medinī" and the quotation of Medinī's lexicon.

What the physician's verses say about time, in our words (the print's line 4 is the only place in the physician's passage to touch the time of the fruit):

- Seven kinds of dream: seen, heard, experienced, wished for, imagined, foreboding, and due to the humours; the first five bring no fruit (a dream is then as the dreamer's own nature by day).
- A dream forgotten, or very long or very short, **"in the early night … the fruit"** (the line reads "pūrvarātre [vi]pāt phalam" on the page; the middle syllable is blurred, the sense unsettled: it may be "a dream of the early night gives its fruit in a lapse/after a lapse of time"), and a dream **"seen at the cows' path"** (*go-marga*, the hour of the cows' going out: the dawn) "makes little and that same day great" (the line reads "dṛṣṭaḥ karoti tucchaṃ ca gomarge tadaharmahat"); a dream of one who is not disturbed by sleep, or by unfriendly words, or of a sinner, gives little fruit and is turned by gifts, burnt offerings and recitations.
- No span in years, months or days is given by the physician.

The physician's pages give the same pair of ideas as the Purāṇic series — the early night is far, the hour of the cows' release is at once — and a remedy of gift, offering and recitation (as the *Svapnohana*'s remedy, svo:p271), but no numbers.
