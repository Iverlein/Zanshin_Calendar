The article *svapna* in the *Śabdakalpadruma*, vol. 5 p. 470 (columns 2–3) to p. 471, which quotes the chapter of the *Brahmavaivartapurāṇa*, *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa*, in which Nanda asks and the Lord answers what dream brings merit and happiness. The article's line before the chapter says "susvapnā yathā" (the good dreams, as follows); the chapter's end on p. 471 reads "iti brahmavaivarte śrīkṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍe susvapnadarśanaṃ 77 adhyāyaḥ" (the chapter is numbered 77 in this printing; Jivananda's 1888 printing numbers the verses of the series 73.5–6, bvp:p662-665; Negelein's own references differ again, neg:p14-16). The verses of the series are read from the page image: the line "dine manasi yad dṛṣṭaṃ tat sarvvañ ca labhed dhruvam" has a blurred conjunct on the Nag copy and reads so on the Chowkhamba copy; the others agree with the layer.

What the passage says, in our words:

- **The Purāṇa traces the teaching to a Vedic place.** Before the chapter the Lord says that among the Vedas the Sāmaveda is best for every act and that in it, "in the Kāṇva branch, in the lovely book of merit" (*kāṇvaśākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare*), the good dream is plainly set out, always giving the fruit of merit; "I shall tell the chapter of dreams" follows. The branch named is the Kāṇva, which is not a Sāmaveda branch by the usual tradition (our remark; the print says what it says). No such book is named elsewhere in the pool.
- **The series**: a dream of the first watch (*yāma*) gives its fruit in a year; of the second in eight months; of the third in three months; of the fourth in half a month; a dream seen at the sight of the dawn in ten days; a morning dream at once, if one is woken. This is the series of the *Svapnohana* word for number (svo:p268; claim indian-dream-spans-by-watch-nine-texts), here on a second print of the Purāṇa.
- The next two lines are not in the other witnesses RD269 read (bvp:p662-665, neg:p14-16): what one has seen in the mind in the day, one gets that surely; the dream of a man afflicted with worry or illness is fruitless, "no doubt".
- The remainder of the chapter (pp. 470–471) is the catalogue of good dream-images (the fall of a good sight: curds and rice, lotus leaf, a fruiting tree, women in white, a Brahmin giving a blessing, a book received, and so on) and the fruit of each, ending with the Lord's "I have told you the good dream, Nanda, the cause of merit; do you want to hear more?" The chapter has no further span.

Before it, the article's first definition of *svapna* quotes the Suśruta-saṃhitā, *Śārīrasthāna*, chapter 4, on the dream as the knowledge of one asleep ("the self of the elements, as it sleeps, takes good and bad things in the mind stirred by *rajas*, from the body it had before; when the organs fail in their action…", "iti suśrute śārīrasthāne caturthe 'dhyāye"), followed by "darśanam, iti medinī" and the quotation of Medinī's lexicon.

What the physician's verses say about time, in our words (the print's line 4 is the only place in the physician's passage to touch the time of the fruit):

- Seven kinds of dream: seen, heard, experienced, wished for, imagined, foreboding, and due to the humours; the first five bring no fruit (a dream is then as the dreamer's own nature by day).
- A dream forgotten, or very long or very short, **"in the early night … the fruit"** (the line reads "pūrvarātre cirāt phalam", "in the early night, the fruit late"; RD272 had read the word as blurred, "[vi]pāt", and RD295 settled it on the same page image and in the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya, see the RD295 section below), and a dream **"seen at the cows' path"** (*go-marga*, the hour of the cows' going out: the dawn) "makes little and that same day great" (the line reads "dṛṣṭaḥ karoti tucchaṃ ca gomarge tadaharmahat"); a dream of one who is not disturbed by sleep, or by unfriendly words, or of a sinner, gives little fruit and is turned by gifts, burnt offerings and recitations.
- No span in years, months or days is given by the physician.

The physician's pages give the same pair of ideas as the Purāṇic series — the early night is far, the hour of the cows' release is at once — and a remedy of gift, offering and recitation (as the *Svapnohana*'s remedy, svo:p271), but no numbers.

### The verses of the physician: q867

The Śabdakalpadruma's "iti vāgbhaṭe śārīrasthāne ṣaṣṭhe 'dhyāye" is the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya*'s *Śārīrasthāna* ch. 6. Kunte's page 655 (n784, foot) gives, with the commentary after each half-verse (the numbers are the print's 61 to 64; the commentary's gloss is Aruṇadatta's):

> तेष्वाद्या निष्फलाः पञ्च यथास्वप्रकृतिर्दिवा ।
> विस्मृतो दीर्घह्रस्वोऽति पूर्वरात्रे चिरात्फलम् ॥ ६१–६२ ॥
> दृष्टः करोति तुच्छं च गोसर्गे तदहर्महत् ॥ ६३ ॥
> निद्रया चानुपहतः प्रतीपैर्वचनैस्तथा ।
> याति पापोऽल्पफलतां दानहोमजपादिभिः ॥ ६४ ॥

Beside it, the Śabdakalpadruma's lines (p. 473, middle column, the same four lines in the same order, read at full size): «पूर्व्वरात्रे चिरात् फलम्», «दृष्टः करोति तुच्छञ्च गोमर्गे तदहर्म्महत्». Differences: the dictionary has "svapna-prakṛtir" where Kunte has "yathā-sva-prakṛtir" (a dream as one's own nature by day); the third word of the cows' line is "gomarge" in the dictionary (the print's *ma* and *sa* are alike in this type) and "gosarge" in Kunte, whose commentary glosses it "at the time of the cows' release" (*gavāṃ mokṣakāle*), so the word is *go-sarga*, the same hour as the *Bṛhadyātrā*'s *go-visarga* and the *Kālaprakāśikā*'s *go-visarge*. The blurred middle syllable of RD272 is therefore not "vipāt" but **"cirāt"** in both prints: «पूर्वरात्रे चिरात् फलम्», "in the early night, the fruit late". Aruṇadatta takes it so ("a dream seen in the early night gives its fruit after a long time, little"), adds that some read "or" for "and" (the fruit is long delayed or small), and that "early night" means the whole first part of the night; for the cows' line: the dream seen at the time of the cows' release gives its great fruit on that same day. The commentary also reads the first line as saying that dreams of one's own nature, of the day, forgotten, very long or very short are, besides the first five of the seven kinds, fruitless.

So the physician's passage on time has two terms: early night, late and small; the hour of the cows' release, great and the same day. No span in years or months. The next verse (65, p. 656) says that after an inauspicious dream, one who sees an auspicious one in the same place has the auspicious fruit; this is the point of the *Bṛhadyātrā*'s v. 31 ("paścād dṛṣṭo yaḥ sa pākaṃ vidhatte"), the later dream decides.

For the Tibetan side beside it, the Narthang manuscript's watch verse (svo:p271h): «ཐུན་གཉིས་ཟླ་བ་བརྒྱད་པ་ལ། །ཐུན་གསུམ་ཟླ་བ་གསུམ་གྱི་བར། །ཐུན་བཞི་ཟླ་བ་ཕྱེད་བར་དུ། །མཐོང་བར་འགྱུར་བ་ཐེ་ཚོམ་མེད། །སྐྱ་རེངས་ཤར་བའི་དུས་ཉིད་ལ།» (the second watch, the eighth month; the third, within three months; the fourth, within half a month; it will be seen, no doubt; at the very time the dawn rises). The *Svapnohana* gives a graded number for each watch; the physician gives only two ends of the night, far and near.

### The Bṛhadyātrā's lists in v. 10: q855

Leaf n28 of Pingree's print at full size reads, v. 10: «जघन्यमध्यप्रथमे निशांशे प्रावृच्छरन्माधवसंज्ञिते च । / काले मरुत्पित्तकफप्रकोपात् साधारणः स्यात् फलसन्निपातः ॥ १० ॥» (the print's *saṃjñite*, as RD269 read it). The verse is one sentence with three lists and states no pairing. The order of the lists is the same as the order of the humours and the same as the physician's own order for the night and the year, so the pairing by position is the physician's: *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* Sūtrasthāna 1.8 says the three humours stand, in the order wind, bile, phlegm, at the end, the middle and the beginning of age, day, night and meal; and 12.24–25 gives for each humour three seasons of gathering, aggravation and calming: wind gathers in summer, is aggravated in the rains and calmed in autumn; bile gathers in the rains, is aggravated in autumn; phlegm gathers in the cool season and is aggravated in spring. So the season of aggravation is the rains for wind, autumn for bile, spring for phlegm. Hence (the pairing is by that order, ours) last part of the night, rains, wind; middle part, autumn, bile; first part, spring, phlegm. The word "common" (*sādhāraṇa*) recalls 13.33–35 of the same text, where the rains, autumn and spring are the three months "between" the extremes (*sādhāraṇa*), the seasons without excess of heat, rain or cold. It is our reading of the verse; the *Bṛhadyātrā* does not state the pairs. Viṣṇuśarman's reading (apparatus) changes only the names of the first season ("prāvṛḍ-śara°").

### The Aṅgavijjā's bright beginning: q856

Ch. 42 (agv:p190-191) has the bright beginning at forenoon and the bright ends at afternoon; ch. 41 (agv:p186, printed p. 185) has the afternoon for both. A third list of the same pattern is in ch. 53 (*Uppātaṇajjhāya*, p. 210, line 10, leaf n325, read at full size): «सुक्केसु आदिमूलीयेसु पुव्वण्हगतं उप्पायं बूया । सुक्केसु मज्झिमविगाढेसु अद्धरत्तगतं उप्पायं बूया । सुक्केसु अंतेसु अवरण्हगतं उप्पायं बूया» (the bright beginning, forenoon (*puvvaṇha*); the bright deep middle, midnight; the bright ends, afternoon), the dark ones continuing evening, midnight, dawn. The forenoon for the bright beginning stands in two of the three chapters (42 and 53), so ch. 41's afternoon for it is the one that differs; ch. 53's list has midnight where ch. 42 has midday for the bright middle (the three lists are not identical in the middle), so the lists are not copies of one another. The editor's apparatus gives no variant for the word in ch. 41; which wording is the original is settled by no manuscript read here. The text layer of the print was searched for the other uses of the two words: besides pp. 185, 190–191 and 210 they occur only in lists of the parts of the day (not read further).
