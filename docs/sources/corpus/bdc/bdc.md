## <a id="bdc-i76"></a>bdc:i76 The four great festivals multiply virtue a hundred koṭi-fold, by the scripture's minor section and the Nirvāṇa

*Read: etext, 2026-11-16.*

> ཁྱད་པར་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ། བྱང་ཆུབ་བརྙེས་པ། ཆོས་འཁོར་བསྐོར་བ། ལྷ་བབས་རྣམས་དུས་ཆེན་བཞི་ཞེས་དགེ་བ་གང་བྱས་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བརྒྱར་འགྱུར་བར་ལུང་སིལ་བུ་དང་མྱང་འདས་སོགས་ལས་གསུངས་པས་ཕན་ཡོན་ལྷག་པར་ཆེའོ།།

BDRC's etext of the Zhol print only (VEIE0OPI6CC9D5D5_I00KG06678, its page marker 76, at character 50 582; BDRC rates the etext mediocre). The sentence reads the same in BDRC's search snippets of four other etexts: the work in the author's Collected Works (MW1PD107937_12F37A, pp. 429–510 of its volume) and in the *Snga 'gyur bka' ma shin tu rgyas pa* (MW1PD100944_731CF1), and Ye shes rgya mtsho's two lives of the Buddha (MW29186_2A2196, MW29186_56B589), which quote it with «ཞེས་གསུངས་སོ». On the scan it was not found: the images numbered 76–78 of I00KG06678 hold the description of the Four Great Kings on the folio marked 37, so the etext's page marker is not the image number here. The lines before the sentence are too garbled in this etext to quote; they set out the dates of the Buddha's festivals "as the unmistaken Phug tradition has them" («འཁྲུལ་མེད་ཕུག་པའི་ལུགས་སྲོལ་བཞིན་བཀོད»), among them the descent from the gods on the twenty-second of the month of tha skar (ཐ་སྐར, tha skar) and the passing beyond sorrow on the fifteenth of the month of sa ga (ས་ག, sa ga) of the iron dragon year, and name the Kashmiri great paṇḍita and Khro phu lo tsā ba for the twenty-second.

The Fifth Dalai Lama's rite of the seven Sugatas (the Medicine Buddha's rite), on the time for doing it: "and in particular the miracles, the attaining of awakening, the turning of the wheel of Dharma and the descent from the gods are called the four great festivals (དུས་ཆེན་བཞི, dus chen bzhi), and since the scripture's minor section (ལུང་སིལ་བུ, lung sil bu), the Nirvāṇa (མྱང་འདས, myang 'das) and others say that whatever virtue is done then becomes a hundred koṭi-fold (བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བརྒྱ, bye ba phrag brgya), the benefit is especially great."
