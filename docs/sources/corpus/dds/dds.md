**What the hits are.** No hit is a rule about dreams and dates (claim dds-no-dream-verdict-by-date-in-bdrc-etext-search). The dated dreams are log entries of biographies, songs and treasure texts (dds-dated-dreams-are-log-entries: the tenth and fifteenth nights, the earth-dog month); a dream's date is set beside a death's date after the event (dds-dream-date-matched-to-the-day-of-a-death, dds.8:p259); the dream month is a month name (dds-dream-month-is-a-month-name, dds.10:p38); the midnight dream is the first outer death-sign (dds-midnight-dream-in-the-death-signs, dds.9:p173); one line, doubtful, sets the waning fortnight beside «dream» in a rite for a boy (dds-mar-ngo-rmi-lam-doubt, dds.12:p439). A modern verse autobiography says that dream-signs showed the year, month, day and time of some things that came (etexts I3CN5137 p. 341 and I3CN3786 p. 334: «རྨི་ལྟས་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ཀྱི། འབྱུང་འགྱུར་འགའ་ཞིག་ཐོག་བབས་དང་»), which is the date of the event foretold, not of the dream; the author is a modern one, not quoted further.

> འདི་ལ་གསུམ།ཕྱིའི་འཆི་ལྟས་དང་།ནང་གི་འཆི་ལྟས་དང་།འཆི་བ་བླུ་བའི་ཚུལ་ལོ།དང་པོ་ནི།སྡོམ་འབྱུང་ལས་རྐང་མཐིལ་དག་ཏུ་ཕུག་པ་དང་།།གང་ཚེ་ལྟེ་བར་ཕུག་སྙམ་གྱུར།།དེ་ཚེ་ཉི་མ་གསུམ་ཕ་རོལ།།ལྔ་ཡི་གོང་དུ་འགྲོ་བར་འགྱུར།།བཤང་ག་ཅི་ཟག་པའི་དུས་དག་ཏུ།།དུས་མཚུངས་སྤྲིད་པ་བྱུང་གྱུར་ན།།དེ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ནི་དུས་ཚོད་ལ།།ལོ་གཅིག་གིས་ནི་འཆི་བར་འགྱུར། … ཞེས་གསུངས་ལ།རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཀྱང་དེ་ཉིད་ལས།།གང་ཞིག་གི་ནི་རྨི་ལམ་མཐར།།བྱེ་མའམ་ཐལ་བའི་ཕུང་པོའམ།།ལྷ་ཁང་མཆོད་སྡོང་ལ་འཛེགས་ཉིད།།དེ་ནི་སྔ་མ་བཞིན་དུ་འཆི།།གལ་ཏེ་བོང་བུའམ་སྤྲེའུ་ཞོན།གྲོགས་མཁར་ཕྱག་དར་ཕུང་པོ་ལ།།མངོན་འཛེགས་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་འགྲོ་བ་ནི།།གང་གི་རྨི་ལམ་མཐར་མཐོང་དང་།།བུད་མེད་གང་ཞིག་གོས་ནག་ཅིང་།།མདོག་ནག་འདོད་པ་སྤྱོད་པའི་མི།།དེ་ནི་དུས་མཚན་མོར་ཤེས་བྱ།།གཤིན་རྗེ་རུ་ནི་འགྲོ་བར་བསྟན།།བྱ་རྒོད་ཅེ་སྤྱང་ཁྱི་དང་ཝ།།དྲེད་དང་ཡི་དྭགས་ཤ་ཟ་ཡིས།།ཟ་བར་རྨི་ལམ་མཐོང་ན་ནི།།ངེས་པར་ལོ་གཅིག་དག་གིས་སོ།།གོས་དམར་ལུས་ལ་རབ་གཡོགས་དང་།།ཕྲེང་བ་དམར་པོས་རྣམ་བརྒྱན་དང་།།གང་ཚེ་ཏིལ་མར་བཀུས་པ་རྨི།།དེ་ནི་ཟླ་དྲུག་ལས་མི་འཚོ།།ཞེས་གསུངས་ཏེ་དེ་རྣམས་ཕྱིའི་འཆི་ལྟས་སོ། … ནང་གི་རླུང་གི་འཆི་ལྟས་ངེས་པར་མ་བྱས་པར།ཕྱིའི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཙམ་གྱིས་འཆི་བའི་ངེས་པ་མེད་ལ།།ཕྱི་ནང་གཉིས་ཀ་ངེས་པར་བྱས་ན་གསལ་བ་ཡིན་ནོ།

«This has three: the outer death signs, the inner death signs and the way of deceiving death. The first: from the Sdom 'byung (the Saṃvarodaya): "When the soles feel pricked and then the navel, after three days, before five, he goes. If at the times of faeces and urine a simultaneous … occurs, at that span of time he dies within a year." …
and the dream death signs also, from the same (བདེ་མཆོག་སྡོམ་འབྱུང, the Sdom 'byung quoted just above): "Whoever at the end of the dream climbs a heap of sand or ashes, or a temple, or a stupa — he dies as before. If he rides a donkey or a monkey, openly climbs an ant-hill or a heap of rags (phyag dar, not understood), going to the south, whoever sees that at the end of the dream; a woman in black clothes, black-coloured, a man given to desire — know that as Time-Night (དུས་མཚན་མོ, dus mtshan mo); it is shown that she goes to Yama's. If one sees in a dream that vultures, jackals, dogs, foxes, bears and flesh-eating spirits eat one: certainly within one year. Wrapped in a red garment, adorned with a red garland, smeared with sesame oil: he does not live beyond six months." Thus it is said; these are the outer death signs. … Without making certain of the inner wind death signs, there is no certainty of dying from the outer death signs alone; when both outer and inner are made certain, it is clear.»

«As before» in «he dies as before» (སྔ་མ་བཞིན་དུ་འཆི, snga ma bzhin du 'chi) follows the verse just above, whose last words are «within six months» (the ACIP etext, [p13]); the Sdom 'byung's own verse before it was not seen. «Time-Night» (དུས་མཚན་མོ) is a name of the black woman, not a time of the night; Marpa's rite (dds.19:p54) and the medical commentary (dds.20:p216) say the same of her. The dream is not tied to a watch of the night here. Text: Tsong kha pa's «Explanation of the upward transference of consciousness, Opening the golden door», the third chapter of the eighth section on the King of Tantras «Kha sbyor» (colophon, etext [p56]: written by the monk Tsong kha pa Blo bzang grags pa at Brog Ri bo che Dga' ldan rnam par rgyal ba'i gling, the scribe Bsod nams blo gros); it names the Vajraḍāka, the Four Seats, the Sdom 'byung, the Zhal lung and the Kha sbyor, and Abhayākaragupta's explanation, as the works it rests on. The same words stand in the Beijing 2012 volume (BDRC VEIE0OPI9244B62A_I3PD276, folios 6b-7b).

## <a id="dds-7-p23"></a>dds.7:p23 Mgur chung mu tig phreng ba don gsal nyams kyi dbyangs chung, etext VEIE0OPI42515047_I4PD946 (BDRC MW4PD929, 'Khrul zhig Dbang drag rgya mtsho): the colophon of a supplication

*Read: etext, 2026-11-10.*

> ཞེས་པའི་གསོལ་འདེབས་གསེར་ཐིག་ཕྲེང་བ་འདི་ས་ཕོ་བྱི་བའི་ལ་སྟག་པ་ཟླ་བའི་ཡར་ཚེས་བཅུ་ལ་རྨི་ལམ་དག་སྣང་གསལ་བ་ཞིག་ཕྱུང་བ་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས། སྒྱུ་མའི་རྣལ་འབྱོར་བ་ལ་ཡིག་རང་གྲོལ་གར་དབོད་དབད་དྲུག་རྒྱ་མཚོས་གནས་མཆོག་སྐུ་བླ་དཀར་ཚིན་གྱི་ཞོལ་དུ་བརྗོད་པའོ།

«This supplication, the Golden Garland of Drops, was spoken by the illusory yogin … (the name of the author is garbled in the etext) in the hall of the supreme place Sku bla dkar chin, relying on a clear pure vision in a dream (རྨི་ལམ་དག་སྣང་གསལ་བ, rmi lam dag snang gsal ba) that came on the tenth of the waxing (ཡར་ཚེས་བཅུ, yar tshes bcu) of the Tiger month (སྟག་པ་ཟླ་བ) of the earth male rat year.» A colophon giving the date of the dream that prompted the text.

«Asked what is the deepest of the death signs, (the lord) said: "Here the glorious lord Phag mo gru pa said: if one dreams of riding a white monkey with a red nose and going to the east, one dies after three days (ཞག་གསུམ་ནས་འཆི་བ)." Asked whether it is a point of the channels and winds and what the reason is for dreaming the white, red-nosed monkey: "There is a deep dharma in this; what shall I do?" — when he was asked, praying, he said "It is deep; then do it. For one's body to come about, three conditions must come together: the father's white drop, the mother's red drop and the ground-of-all consciousness (ཀུན་གཞིའི་རྣམ་ཤེས, kun gzhi'i rnam shes). As long as these three do not leave their places the body does not perish. The father's white drop stays between the two eyebrows; the mother's red drop stays at the tip of the lotus-bhaga (པདྨ་བྷ་ག, the vajra's tip in the text's words). When the places are disturbed the body perishes: the white drop of the father coming out downward and the mother's red drop coming up meet in the wheel of emanation at the navel inside the central channel; that is the reason for dreaming the white monkey. At the time of death the father's seed comes out of the lotus-bhaga and the mother's from the two nostrils, and the body is destroyed. This is deep: it makes one understand one's own body." The two may also not meet; if they do not meet, one does not dream it. Asked for a method of deceiving death: "There is a method of lengthening (བསྲིང་བ); it is the instruction of hiding the bodhicitta in the cloud: before the dream's wheel of illusion breaks, meditate the supreme holy lama in the centre of the heart, very clear, like a thread passed through a needle's eye, without distraction. As an example, it is like a wild horse tied to a post: the two seeds are held in their place."»

The text is the questions of Dpal chen put to the lord 'Jig rten mgon po (1143-1217) on his own constant practice («the protector's own constant practice, the questions and answers of Dpal chen», སྐྱོབ་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་དུས་རྒྱུན་གྱི་ཉམས་ལེན་དཔལ་ཆེན་གྱི་ཞུས་ལན, in his collected works, the etext's local [p23]-[p24] of the text that begins at [p1]); the saying is attributed to Phag mo gru pa, the dream is the white red-nosed monkey to the east alone, with the span three days; the donkey is not named here.

«Of the bad (dreams): … to turn away from the deed, to give up: a temple's stupa, or climbing a heap of sand or ashes; riding a donkey and going to the south and not coming back; riding a monkey, going south, and waking while staying there; a long pole, a life-tree, a stupa, an ant-hill, a heap of foul rags — whoever's name climbs, he does not live beyond six months. A black girl, wildly agitated, bound with a binding: that is called Time-Night (དུས་མཚན, dus mtshan); death after seven days. A woman in black clothes, a black man given to desire, a garland of karavira seen: he goes to the place of Yama. Darkness in a dream, or a precipice, a pit; falling from a tree: he lies in the power of the lord of death. …trees, grass or wood growing on body and head, without fruit: he does not live beyond six months. A man who rides a monkey as his mount and goes to the east: if he wakes, 'that is his last night (མཚན་མོ་ཐ་མ, mtshan mo tha ma)'. Eaten by dogs, jackals, vultures, … a donkey, a sow, a buffalo carrying (him) off: certainly from one year he is shown to die. If one sees before the sleeper a man with an iron staff, in black clothes: death in three months. Smeared with red scent, adorned with a red garland, with sesame oil, shaved, in red clothes, riding a donkey fast going to the south — if one dreams oneself so: he dies from six months. Red scent, a garland or red clothes found in dreams: he dies in eight months.»

(Rendered where clear; the lines about trees on the body and the last-but-one verse were not fully understood.) The verses sit in the Hevajra rite as the bad signs of the empowerment's dream, with the means of turning them back after them (the water of the five nectars, taking all things as dream, a fierce fire-offering); the fuller list than the Sdom 'byung quotation of Tsong kha pa, and here the monkey going east is «his last night» (མཚན་མོ་ཐ་མ), not the dawn.

## <a id="dds-18-p57"></a>dds.18:p57 Rang byung rdo rje, the verses on dreams: the evening dream a habit-imprint, the midnight dream the power of spirits, the dawn dream true, 'so Marpa said' (RD374)

*Read: etext, 2026-11-10.*

> རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཚིག་བཅད་བཞུགས་པ་ལགས་སོ། །ཕ་བླ་མ་རྣམས་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ། །བཀའ་དྲིན་ཅན་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །དྲིན་ཅན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་བྱིན་གྱིས་བརློབས། །བདག་འདྲ་འཁོས་ཆུང་རི་ཁྲོད་པས། །སྲོད་ལ་རྗེ་ལ་གསོལ་བ་བཏབ། །ཐོ་རངས་མཚན་བརྟས་འདི་ལྟར་བྱུང༌། །སྤྱིར་སྲོད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བག་ཆགས་ཡིན། །ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་གདོན་དབང་ཡིན། །ཐོ་རངས་གང་བྱུང་བདེན་ནོ་ཞེས། །བླ་མ་མར་པའི་ཞལ་ནས་གསུངས། །དེ་ཕྱིར་རྣལ་འབྱོར་བདག་འདྲ་ཡང༌། །ཅུང་ཟད་རྨི་ལམ་ཡི་གེར་བཀོད།

«The verses on dreams. Homage to the lama-fathers. I take refuge in the kind ones; may the kind ones bless me. I, the like of a little hermit in the mountain retreat, prayed to the lord in the evening; at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) a sign of the night appeared thus. In general the evening (སྲོད, srod) dream is a habit-imprint (བག་ཆགས, bag chags); the midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) dream is the power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang); 'whatever comes at dawn is true', so the mouth of the lama Marpa said. Therefore, though I am a yogin of my kind, I set down a few dreams in writing.»

The verses are in the Third Karmapa Rang byung rdo rje's collected works. This is a rule for dreams in general, ascribed to Marpa; the dream signs are not death signs, and it names no span of life. The BDRC search finds the lines «srod kyi rmi lam bag chags» and «nam phyed kyi rmi lam gdon dbang» in this one text only.

The end of the Hevajra rite «The Mandala Rite joined to the Sūtras» (དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་གྱི་ཆོ་ག་མདོ་དང་སྦྱར་བ, dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga mdo dang sbyar ba) in the Lho brag Mar pa lo tsa'i gsung 'bum (etext [p96]; the verses of the dream signs of the empowerment night are earlier in the same rite, dds.19:p53-55). Rendered by the reader, where clear: «The second training is the teaching of the instruction on the generation stage and the completion stage, each as the variety of the mind requires. "This instruction, free of exaggeration, found among Nāropa's pupils, by relying on a holy lama in the right way, was written down for the sake of the meaning to be told; by its merit may all beings gain the state of Vajradhara." The (rite called) Mandala Rite joined to the Sūtras, begun at Lho brag Chu khyer, is complete. — (A second note:) May the venerable Rngog bless. From the mouth of the venerable Rngog: it is the instruction of the lama's physician («bla ma'i lha rje») Lho pa, a genuine (ཀ་མ, ka ma) transmission; "from my father, long ago, this written text of the empowerment stands, copied from an old copy by an evil son (ངན་བུ, ngan bu, the word is not understood)", he said; trusting it, (the copyist) changed no more than a few words, so it seems; in a spring in a year of the horse, with my head at the feet at «Zlum chung» (place not identified), the checking was done.»

The rite is the work of no named author: it says it was «begun» at Lho brag Chu khyer, takes its instruction from Nāropa's pupils, and the second note names «the venerable Rngog» (which Rngog the note does not say) as telling of a Lho pa instruction and an old copy. Neither colophon names the Hevajra Tantra, the Saṃvarodaya or Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa as the source of the dream verses.

## <a id="dds-15-p119-121"></a>dds.15:p119-121 Ras chung snyan brgyud skor, the Tilopa Tika on the death signs (BDRC, Mes po'i shul bzhag): the dream images, the monkey of the next dawn (RD374)

«The instruction of Jetsun Tilopa, the Tika, examining the death signs. This true and profound Tika of Jetsun Tilopa has three: the instruction on dreams, the grasping of the wind, and the key point of the drop. The first is: dreaming of putting on red cloth; picking a red flower; climbing the Rgya skyeg mountain; running naked downhill; the house filling with water; the sun and moon falling on the plain — these are taught as the signs of death. Riding a donkey and going to the south: one dies in the fourth month. Riding a monkey with a red nose and going toward the east: one dies at early morning (སྔ་དྲོ, snga dro) of the next day (ནང་པར, nang par).
… The bad (signs) are turned back thus: all bad dreams are turned back by any one of virtuous practice of body and speech. …
It is Jetsun Tilopa's instruction; it is Ras chung Rdo rje's special teaching. Finished. … (of Lama Bar ston Ras pa:) It was asked of Lama Bar ston Ras pa by «དངོས་ངན་སྟོན་པ» (the name or title is not understood); not at all equal to others; 'if you compete with me you will die well', he said.»

The text is the Ras chung snyan brgyud collection's own attribution: Tilopa's instruction, Ras chung's special teaching; the collection is not by Ras chung pa as an author.

## <a id="dds-15-p124"></a>dds.15:p124 Ras chung snyan brgyud skor, the examination of the shadow of life: the dream death signs as uncertain signs (RD374)

*Read: etext, 2026-11-10.*

> ལམ་གྱི་འཆི་ལྟས་ལ།བོང་བུ་ཞོན་ནས་ཁ་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་སུ་ལྟས་ཏེ་འགྲོ་ན།ཟླ་བ་བཞི་པའི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཡིན།སྤྲེའུ་དཀར་པོ་སྣ་དམར་ཞོན་ནས་ཁ་ཤར་དུ་ལྟས་ཏེ་འགྲོ་ན་ཐོ་རངས་ཀྱི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཡིན།གོས་དམར་པོ་གྱོན་པའམ།མེ་ཏོག་ག་དདམར་པོ་ཐུ་བའམ།རྒྱ་སྐྱེག་གི་རི་ལ་འཛེག་པའམ།གཅེར་བུར་ཐུར་ལ་རྒྱུག་པའམ།གནས་ཁང་ཆུས་ཁྱེར་བའམ།ཉི་མ་ཐང་ལ་ལྷུང་པའམ།དེ་ཡང་ཡིན་ནོ།།དེ་རྣམས་ནི་མ་ངེས་པའི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཡིན་པས།ལུས་ངག་དགེ་སྦྱོར་གང་བྱེད་ཡང་རུང་པ་ཞིག་གིས་སེལ་ལོ།

«As for the dream death signs: riding a donkey and going facing south is the death sign of the fourth month. Riding a white monkey with a red nose and going facing east is the death sign of the dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs). Putting on red cloth, or picking a red flower, or climbing the Rgya skyeg mountain, or running downhill naked, or the house being carried off by water, or falling on the plain of the sun — that too. These are uncertain death signs (མ་ངེས་པའི་འཆི་ལྟས, ma nges pa'i 'chi ltas), so any one of body, speech or virtuous practice, whichever is done, clears them.»

The text is «The examination of the shadow of life» (ཚེ་ཡི་གྲིབ་མ་བརྟག་པ, tshe yi grib ma brtag pa, etext [p122]-[p126]) of the same collection. Here the dawn is the time of death after the dream, not the watch of the dream; no watch of the night is named for the dream itself.

Whoever has the instruction as it is deceives death; by that very (instruction) he conquers death; he conquers death by the dharma; he who strives for the stages of perfect enlightenment thinks of the dharma as the best. This is the fifth section, the analysis of the stages of examining the signs of death.»

These are the verses of the Saṃvarodaya's chapter 19, verses 20-26 (dds.25:p580-583), word for word but for small spellings, standing in the treatise without naming the tantra on this page; the closing line is the treatise's own section colophon. The reading «ལྟོ་བར» (lto bar, "to the belly") is the Derge Kanjur's (and, by Tsuda's note, that of all five editions he collated), not the «ལྟ་བར» (lta bar, "to see") that his edition adopts from the Sanskrit; and the line after the sand-heap verse, «དེ་ཡང་ཟླ་བ་དྲུག་ནའོ» (that too in six months), makes the span explicit that «as before» (སྔོན་བཞིན) leaves to the reader.

«Namo guru. A yogin who takes up the heat of the transference of consciousness (འཕོ་བ, 'pho ba) must know the signs of death (འཆི་ལྟས, 'chi ltas); of these there are three, outer, inner and secret. The first has two: examining the midnight dream (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ, nam phyed kyi rmi lam brtag pa) and examining body and mind by day. The first is: going alone to a place one has never gone to before; riding a donkey naked, facing outward, on the road to the south; getting a red flower on the Gya skag mountain (རྒྱ་སྐག, rgya skag), and the like. The second, examining body and mind, begins with the body: the humming in the ears, the sound of the green-blue dragon, stops; the white light of the peacock's eye-ring goes out; the vapour of concentration, the vapour of the crown, is lost; the nose-water of the supreme horse, the drop, dries; the lightning-woman's … (the taste of the tongue is not felt: «ལྕེས་རོ་མི་ཚོར», the rest «རལ་བ» not understood); the central pillar, the pillar of the nose, fails — and strong robbers spread (these last images are rendered literally and are not understood). The signs of the mind: sorrow in the place where one lives; being spurned by companions; seeing and thinking of places never seen before; being unable to hold concentration.
… (the inner death signs, the flow of the wind through the right and left nostril and the years, months and days it gives, are left out here) …
These inner death signs appear in thirty-four or thirty-six days; up to the half (ཕྱེད་ཚུན་ཆད, 'up to the half', of that span) they are turned back by the methods of deceiving death (འཆི་བ་བསླུ་བ, 'chi ba bslu ba); once it is past that, it is not turned back.»

The teaching names no author in the text itself and no part of the night beyond «the midnight dream» (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed): what is dreamed at midnight is a sign, not a rule about the night. The three images of the dream are not given a span of life here. «Dream (signs): "A cat, monkey, tiger or fox; riding a corpse: the sign of being seized by the lord of death (འཆི་བདག, 'chi bdag). Riding naked a buffalo, horse, pig, donkey or rhinoceros and going to the south: he dies. … dancing with the flesh-eaters: these are the signs of having gone into the power of the lord of death." … Fifth, showing how the dream gives fruit: "The early-night dream (མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ, mtshan stod rmi lam) is certainly forgotten, no fruit; the dawn dream (ཐོ་རང, tho rang) seen and clear gives fruit." The meaning: in general the early-night dream is not clear, and what appears is forgotten, so there is no fruit; but at dawn, whatever dream comes, good or bad, if it is clear and not forgotten, the good or bad fruit comes. … "The early-night dream, its fruit is far; it makes the seeing little": a dream dreamed up to the half of the early night has a distant fruit, and the seeing is small and weak. "A dream seen in the morning (ནང་པར, nang par), without having slept again, not cut by unsuitable talk, that same day the fruit becomes great": 'nang par' is the time of letting the cattle out to graze; the dream seen then has its great fruit on that very day.»

The commentary: Byang pa Rnam rgyal grags pa bzang po, «Bshad rgyud kyi 'grel chen bdud rtsi'i chu rgyun», on the chapter of the signs of the body's destruction (the Explanatory Tantra of the Four Tantras): the etext [p213]-[p220] (printed pp. 177-184), and it quotes the sixth chapter of the 'Body' with the commentary «Zla zer» for the fuller words. The bad-dream list is the root verse (dds.21:p234-235); the span is not a number of months: the dream is a sign when disease blocks the channel where the mind enters, and a healthy dreamer may be freed by treatment.

## <a id="dds-21-p234-235"></a>dds.21:p234-235 Gso rig 'bum bzhi, the Four Tantras' Explanatory Tantra: the dream signs of the lord of death and the dream of the early night and of dawn (RD374)

> རྨི་ལམ་བྱི་ལ་སྤྲེའུ་སྟག་དང་ཝ།།རོ་ལ་ཞོན་རྩིས་འཆི་བདག་ཟིན་པའི་རྟགས།།མ་ཧེ་རྟ་ཕག་བོང་བུ་རྔ་མོང་དང་།།གཅེར་བུ་ཞོན་ནས་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་འགྲོ་རྨིས་འཆི།སྤྱི་བོར་ལྕགས་མ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་བྱ་ཚང་བཅས།ཏ་ལ་ཚེར་མ་ཅན་ཤིང་སྙིང་ཀར་སྐྱེས།།པད་མ་ཕྱུང་དང་གཡང་ལྷུང་དུར་ཁྲོད་ཉལ།།མགོ་བཅད་བྱ་རོག་ཡི་དྭགས་རྨེ་ཅན་བསྐོར།།རྐང་ལག་ལྷུང་དང་མ་ཡི་མངལ་དུ་འཇུག།ཆུས་ཁྱེར་འདམ་བྱིང་ཉ་ཡིས་མིད་པ་དང་།།ལྕགས་གསེར་རྙེད་ནས་རྩོད་དང་འཐབ་མོ་ཕབས།བྲོ་བའི་ཁྲལ་འདེད་བག་མ་ལེན་པ་དང་།།གཅེར་བུར་འདུག་དང་སྐྲ་དང་ཁ་སྤུ་བྲེག།གཤིན་དང་ལྷན་གཅིག་ཆང་འཐུང་དེས་དྲུད་དང་།།གོས་དང་ཕྲེང་བ་དམར་པོ་བརྒྱན་པ་དང༌།}གཤིན་པོ་དག་དང་ལྷན་གཅིག་གར་བྱེད་དང་།།དེ་དག་འཆི་བདག་དབང་དུ་སོང་བའི་རྟགས།།དེ་ལ་སོགས་ཏེ་རྨི་ལམ་མི་བཟང་བ།།ཡིད་འཇུག་བུ་ག་ནད་ཀྱིས་བཀག་ཕྱིར་མཐོང་།རྒྱུན་དུ་རྨིས་པའི་ནད་པ་འཆི་བར་འགྱུར།།ནད་མེད་མ་ངེས་རིམ་གྲོས་ཐར་བར་འགྱུར།།མཐོང་ཐོས་མྱོང་དང་གསོལ་བ་བཏབ་པ་དང་།།གྲུབ་པ་ནད་ལས་བྱུང་དང་རྣམ་པ་དྲུག།མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ་བརྗེད་ངེས་འབྲས་བུ་མེད།།ཐོ་རངས་མཐོང་དང་གསལ་ནས་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན།

«Dreaming of a cat, monkey, tiger, fox; riding a corpse: the sign of being seized by the lord of death. Riding a buffalo, horse, pig, donkey or rhinoceros naked and going to the south: he dies. A twig growing on the crown with a crow's nest; a thorny tala tree growing in the heart; a lotus coming out; falling into a precipice; lying in a charnel ground; the head cut off; surrounded by crows, hungry spirits and the 'rje can'; limbs falling; entering the mother's womb; carried off by water; sinking in mud; eaten by fish; finding iron or gold and selling; losing a fight; wanting the bride-price; taking a bride; sitting naked; hair and beard cut; drinking water with the dead who drag one; adorned with red clothes and a garland; dancing with the flesh-eaters: these are the signs of having gone into the power of the lord of death. Such bad dreams are seen because disease blocks the channel where the mind enters. A patient who dreams them continually will die. One without disease: uncertain, he may be freed by treatment. Seen, heard, experienced, asked for, … from disease: six kinds. The early-night dream (མཚན་སྟོད་རྨི་ལམ, mtshan stod rmi lam) is certainly forgotten, no fruit; the dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) seen and clear gives fruit.»

The root verses of the Explanatory Tantra of the Four Tantras (Gso rig 'bum bzhi, etext [p234]-[p235]); the Four Tantras are above WB on medicine (rule of sources). The parts of the night are two here, early night and dawn; the midnight (nam phyed) is not named, and no number of months follows the images.

«Then I shall explain the best sign of what makes death certain: the signs on one's own body or outside it are to be examined by the wise. … (the signs of the body, the drops, the wind and the shadow are left out) …
Urine that flows in five streams, or turns round from the left, or smells foul, or is sour and the like: death within six months. Whoever at the end of his dream climbs a heap of sand or of ashes, or a temple, or a stupa: he dies as before (སྔོན་བཞིན, 'as before', that is, as in the verse just given, within six months). If he rides a donkey or a monkey, openly climbs an ant-hill or a heap of rags (ཕྱག་དར, phyag dar, not understood) and goes to the south, whoever sees that at the end of his dream; a woman in black clothes, a black-coloured man given to desire — that is called Time-Night (དུས་མཚན, dus mtshan); he will go to Yama's belly. If one sees in a dream that vultures, jackals, dogs, crows, bears and flesh-eating spirits eat one: he dies within a year without fail. Covered with red clothes, adorned with a red garland, smeared with sesame oil, seen in a dream: he will not be alive in six months. … Whoever has the instruction as it is can deceive death; by that very (instruction) he conquers death; by dharma he conquers death …»

The passage is the nineteenth chapter, «Looking at the signs of certain death and the yoga of transference» («དེ་ནས་འཆི་བར་ངེས་བའི་ལྟས་ལྟ་བ་དང་འཕོ་བའི་རྣལ་འབྱོར་རིམ་པར་ཕྱེ་བ་སྟེ་བཅུ་དགུ་པ», colophon of the chapter), of «The Glorious Great King of Tantras called Saṃvarodaya» (Śrī Mahāsaṃvarodaya Tantrarāja, བདེ་མཆོག་འབྱུང་བ, bde mchog 'byung ba; Derge Kanjur, the etext volume VE4CZ5369_I1KG9204, whose [pN] markers are the scan images: the chapter opens on img. 580, the dream verses stand on img. 581-582). The scan (I1KG9204 img. 581 shows the folio, a faint print) was looked at; no syllable of the verse was read on it, so the quotation is the etext's. The words agree with those Tsong kha pa quotes, with small differences: «གང་ཞིག་གི» (gang zhig gi), «ལྷ་ཁང་ངམ་ནི་མཆོད་སྡོང་འཛེག» and «གཤིན་རྗེའི་ལྟོ་བར་འགྲོ་བར་འགྱུར» («to Yama's belly», where Tsong kha pa has «to Yama's, he is shown going»).

Rendered by the reader from the Tibetan; where the Tibetan is unclear the Sanskrit of Tsuda's edition (below, in the section's notes) was looked at and the doubt is said. (1-2) «Then I shall explain the best marks of what is certain to end in death. The wise man should examine the signs of his own body or of outside it.
(3) When the soles feel as if pierced and when the navel feels as if pierced, he goes after three days, before five (ལྔ་ཡི་གོང་དུ, lnga yi gong du; see the note on the Sanskrit).
(4) If at the times of faeces and urine a sneeze (སྦྲིད་པ, sbrid pa) arises at the same moment, he dies and is lost at that very hour of the time after a year.
(5) If in the middle and at the end of the joining of the two marks (the union of the two organs) a sneeze arises at the same moment, he certainly dies in a month.
(6) If a stabbing pain arises in the middle of the heart and the throat at the same moment and he does not rely on the dharma, he dies in two and a half months.
(7) If the image of the "fruit" (the pupil) of the left eye is not seen in a mirror and he does not turn it back, he certainly dies in seven days.
(8) At the root of the ear, between the eyebrows and at the crown of the head: if the four stab alike, he dies at once.
(9) If suddenly he feels (something) and is shaken by «rid» (word not understood), anger and fear, and does not rely on the dharma, he dies within a year.
(10) On the first day of the waxing fortnight, if he sees his semen black, then he dies in six months; red shows that illness will strike.
(11-12) Tears running constantly from the eyes; seeing forms as illusion; not seeing one's own shadow in a mirror or in water; a rainbow at night; a circle of stars by day; lightning that moves in the south in a cloudless sky; the road of shadows by day; a falling shooting star; geese, crows and peacocks seen mixed in one; two moons and two suns; one's own head ablaze; (13) a city of gandharvas on a treetop or a mountain top; fearful hungry ghosts, flesh-eaters and others seen; (14) sudden trembling and fainting again and again: if he sees even one of these, he dies within a month.
(15-17) A moon without its rabbit-mark (the hare in the moon); a sun deprived of light; the sun by night, the moon by day, and his own eyes ablaze; a star the size of Mount Meru; the ocean like a river; and if semen, faeces and urine fall at the same moment: if he does not rely on the dharma, he dies in half a month.
(18) Then, by day, one should look at the shadow, the one that has a person's shape; if its head is not seen, he dies within a year.
(19) If its left hand is not seen, son and wife are certainly lost; if its right hand is not seen, father, son and the like, the great ones, die.
(20) Urine that flows in five streams, or turns round from the left, and is foul-smelling, sour and the like: he dies within six months.
(21) Whoever at the end of his dream climbs a heap of sand or ashes, or the post of a temple (ལྷ་ཁང་ངམ་ནི་མཆོད་སྡོང, lha khang ngam ni mchod sdong): he dies as before.
(22-23) If he rides a donkey or a monkey, openly climbs an ant-hill or a heap of «phyag dar» (ཕྱག་དར), and goes to the south, whoever sees this at the end of his dream; a woman in black clothes, a black-coloured man given to desire: that is called Time-Night (དུས་མཚན, dus mtshan); he will go to Yama's belly (གཤིན་རྗེའི་ལྟོ་བར་འགྲོ་བར་འགྱུར, gshin rje'i lto bar 'gro bar 'gyur; the Sanskrit has «he goes to the sight of Yama», yamadarśanam).
(24) If he sees in a dream that vultures, jackals, dogs, crows, bears and flesh-eating spirits eat him: within one year he certainly dies.
(25) Covered with red clothes, adorned with a red garland, anointed with sesame oil, seen in a dream: in six months he will not be alive.
(26) Whoever has the instruction as it is can deceive death; by that very instruction he conquers death; by the dharma he conquers death.
(27) He who strives for the stages of perfect enlightenment therefore thinks of the dharma as the best. Further, the special points of the meditation will be explained beautifully.
(28) When the breath is cut off from moment to moment and the many signs are truly seen, the body's mandala should be purified by the joining of scattering and filling.
(29) When one has truly come to the time of death, perform the best yoga of transference (འཕོ་བ, 'pho ba): fill with the "fulfilling" breath (པཱུ་རི་ཀ, pū ri ka) the channels that reach the nine apertures;
(30) press the breath with the "emptying" (རེ་ཙ་ཀ, re tsa ka); bind with the "vase" (ཀུམ་པ་ཀ, kum pa ka); purify the door of the apertures; by perfect peace he becomes at peace.
(31) The yoga of snatching the consciousness (རྣམ་ཤེས་དབྲོག་པ, rnam shes 'brog pa), and for the one who goes to the further shore: the wise should join it with Āli and Kāli in equal measure.
(32) At the heart place the letter HŪṂ; put a letter and a half and two (half-letters) below and above; in its lower part the seed of the wind, looking downward.
(33) Make two seeds of wind; the yogin joins mouths (the two seeds); in order twenty-one times he utters the mantra of the letter and a half and two.
(34) The mount of consciousness is the wind; whatever door one thinks of as the wind's door by which it goes, that gives the siddhi of liberation.» (The Derge etext has the verses 28-34 on [p582]; the rest of the chapter on the transference is on [p582]-[p583].)
(35-38) «Of the best and the lowest I shall speak; listen, lord of secrets: from the navel one goes to the realm of desire, a good rebirth; from the drop (ཐིག་ལེ, thig le) to the realm of form, a body; from the crown to the formless realm: that is the way of going happily. From the nose one becomes among the yakṣas; from the ears, a human or a kinnara (མི་འམ་ཅི, mi'am ci); a goddess; if from the eyes, he becomes a king of men; from the door of the mouth, hungry ghosts; from urine likewise animals; by the passage of faeces one goes to hell; from liberation (the crown aperture) to elsewhere.
(39-40) If one transfers when the time has come (དུས་ལ་བབ་ན, dus la bab na); out of time one kills the gods. By killing the gods alone man is cooked in hell. Therefore the wise should know the signs of death truly.»

«(Of Marpa's four sheets, the shog dril bzhi:) in those four sheets there are the transference 'Breakthrough at the crown' (འཕོ་བ་སྤྱི་རྡོལ, 'pho ba spyi rdol), the 'Net-eyed wheel of channels and winds' (རྩ་རླུང་དྲ་མིག་འཁོར་ལོ), 'Cutting the bardo by mind' (བར་དོ་བློས་ཆོད) and the 'Great Seal condensed in words' (ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ་ཚིག་བསྡུས), it is said. …

(the end of the death-sign text:) The companion method of the 'Breakthrough' transference (འཕོ་བ་སྤྱི་རྡོལ་མའི་ལྷན་ཐབས, 'pho ba spyi rdol ma'i lhan thabs). Finished.»

The history is anonymous in its words: it speaks of Marpa in the third person and a later holder says «I» («བདག»), so Marpa cannot be its writer, although BDRC gives Marpa Chos kyi blo gros (1002/1100) as its main author (WA0XL4AAEF5910733, creator P2636). The death-sign teaching comes at the end of the transference text, after the sentence closing the 'Breakthrough' text («the special oral instruction of the noble one», གནད་ཀྱི་གདམས་པ་ཁྱད་པར་འཕགས་པའི་ཞལ་གདམས་སོ) and before the closing words quoted; so the teaching is the 'companion method' (lhan thabs), not Marpa's root verses (the root verses of the Breakthrough, the etext's [p170] of the Gdams ngag mdzod volume, end with «spyi skong wa ta» and carry no death signs).

## The Saṃvarodaya's and the Hevajra's dream signs of death (q1065, q1066): the whole of ch. 19 of the Śrī Mahāsaṃvarodaya Tantrarāja (Derge Kanjur etext VE4CZ5369_I1KG9204 from the page 580 on) read with its scan, the verses on the black woman «Time-Night» (དུས་མཚན) and the months, the Sanskrit (Tsuda's or another open edition, archive.org), the other Tibetan translations (Peking, Narthang, Dpe bsdur ma), the commentaries (Bhavabhaṭṭa, Jayabhadra, Sachen, Tsong kha pa's Sdom 'byung notes) and the Hevajra Tantra's own dream verses (Toh 417 and 418, Snellgrove's Sanskrit) against the Hevajra rite of Marpa's collected works (RD382)

*Read 2026-11-10 (RD382), 19:06-19:39.* The passages of this reading are `dds.25:p580-583` (chapter 19 whole), `dds.26:p129` (the Zhwa dmar's treatise quoting its verses), `dds.27:p41-44` (Rngog's exposition of the dream verses of 'Deceiving Death') and `dds.19:p95-96` (the colophons of Marpa's Hevajra rite); its facts are the claims `dds-sv19-sanskrit-gives-kalaratri`, `dds-marpa-hevajra-rite-dream-verses-are-deceiving-deaths` and `dds-rngog-gloss-time-night-is-yamas-yum`, and the questions q1065 and q1066 are answered in them (the launcher closes them). The Derge etext of chapter 19 was read whole, its scan images 580-583 (I1KG9204, a faint woodblock print) looked at and not read syllable by syllable, so every Tibetan line here is etext.

**What was found, in short.**

- The Sanskrit of the chapter (Tsuda's edition, chapter XIX, verses 21-25) has the dream verses without the black girl, without the seventh day and without the months of Marpa's rite: it has the sand and ashes (or the temple's pole), the donkey and monkey going south with the ant-hill and a heap of dust, the woman in black who desires a man («Kālarātrī»; Yama's sight), the eaters in a year, and red clothes, a red garland and oil in six months. «dus mtshan» is *kālarātri*.
- The verses of Marpa's Hevajra rite are those of 'Deceiving Death' (Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa, chapter 1, cbs:p247-249), word for word with spellings; the Hevajra Tantra (Toh 417, 418) has none. The rite names no source; its colophon says «begun at Lho brag Chu khyer» and «found among Nāropa's pupils».
- Of time and span of life: the Sanskrit gives six months («as before», pūrvavat, is verse 20's), one year and six months, and for the donkey, monkey and Time-Night only death; no watch or day of the lunar month is tied to any of them. The seven days and the «last night» belong to 'Deceiving Death'; Rngog's exposition calls the black girl Yama's mother (yum) and reads «last night» as death at once.

**The Sanskrit beside** (Tsuda's edition, read in the 1970 ANU thesis form, chapter XIX on the printed p. 134, on the page image; verse numbers his; only the verses the dream readings rest on are quoted, in his reading, without his apparatus; the thesis asks that its passages not be copied, and the verses are the tantra's):

> 21 *bālukābhasmarāśiṃ vā vihārayaṣṭim eva ca / svapnānte yo 'bhirohanti maraṇaṃ tatra pūrvavat*
> 22 *gardabhaṃ vānararūḍho valmīkaṃ pāṃsurāśikam / yo 'bhirohati svapnānte dakṣiṇādiśi niyatam*
> 23 *kṛṣṇavastrā tu yā nārī kālī kāmayate naram / kālarātris tu sā jñeyā gacchate yamadarśanam*
> 24 *śvakākagṛdhragomāyuṛkṣaiḥ pretapiśācakaiḥ / bhakṣyante svapnam paśyed ekavarṣād viniścitam*
> 25 *raktavastrapralipt'āṅgo raktamālyavibhūṣaṇaḥ / tailābhyakto yadā svapne ṣaṇmāsān na sa jīvati*

(23: «a woman clad in black, the black one, who desires a man: know her as Kālarātri; he goes to the sight of Yama». 22: donkey, monkey-rider, ant-hill, heap of dust: «whoever climbs (them) at the end of a dream, towards the south, certainly»; the construction of the first pāda is awkward in the edition (the accusative *gardabham* beside the nominative *vānararūḍho*; one manuscript has a different pāda) and is not settled here.)

### Open (RD382)

- Question: Tsuda's Sanskrit of the Saṃvarodaya 19-23 ends «yamadarśanam» («to the sight of Yama») where the Derge Kanjur, Tsong kha pa's quotation (a different wording), the fourth Zhwa dmar's treatise and 'Deceiving Death' (cbs:p247-249 «gshin rje'i gnas su 'gro» in the second verse of the woman) have «lto bar» («belly»), «'gro bar bstan» and «gnas»: is «gshin rje'i lto bar» a misreading of an earlier «lta bar» in all the prints, or is the belly of Yama a reading a commentator of the tradition knows (and which Tibetan commentary on the Sdom 'byung explains the line)?
- Question: In 'Deceiving Death' the verse of the black girl «bound» (bu mo nag mo rab khros nas / gang zhig bcings pas 'chi ba ni / de ni dus mtshan yin zhes bya / zhag bdun nas 'chi byed yin) has no match in the Sanskrit of the Saṃvarodaya 19; is there a Sanskrit of 'Deceiving Death' 1 (Schneider's edition, Vienna 2010) with this verse, and does it use *kālarātri* or another word for «dus mtshan» there?
- Question: Rngog's exposition says the black girl «dus mtshan» is «Yama's mother (or consort), yum», and the etext prints «dus mtshams»: is this «dus mtshan» (the black goddess of the Sakya praises, or Yama's consort Kālarātrī) and does any teacher of the tradition say why her sign is seven days?
- Question: Verse 2 of the Saṃvarodaya's chapter (Sanskrit *trayadivasāt … pañcatvaṃ gacchate*) reads in the Tibetan «lnga yi gong du 'gro bar 'gyur» («before five»): did a Tibetan commentator take «five» as five days, and do the commentaries explain the verse as «after three days, in dying», as 'Deceiving Death' puts it?
