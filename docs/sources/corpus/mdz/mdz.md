> ゆ二　夢はんじ画抄同辯
> 夢の説いろ〳〵ありといへども古へ周礼に占夢の官を置れたり。既に漢の丁公ハ松をミて三公の位にすゝミ、唐の李白ハ筆頭に花咲と見て古今独歩の文人となりしも皆夢のしるしにたがはず。○唐の段成式が酉陽雑俎に悪夢をさけんとならバ主夜神の呪を書て寝所の上に張置てあしき夢を除くべしといへり。則主夜神の呪ハ「婆珊婆演帝」如此書てはり置べし。

> ○天にのぼるとミれば大にたつとき身となるべし
> ○そらはるゝと見れハ大によし
> ○そらあかくなると見れバよし
> ○夜あくるとミれバやまひほんぶくすべし
> ○月日おつると見れハちゝはゝをうしなふ
> ○月日をのむとミれバたつとき子をまうく
> ○かミなりにうたるゝとミれハ大によし
> ○くも四方におこるとミれハあきなひごとよし
> ○ゆきふると見れハよろこびあり
> ○かミなりをきくとミれバりつしんする也
> ○しものふるを見れバあし
> ○あめにあふとミれバ酒しよくにあふべし

Heading: "Item yu-2 (ゆ二), the dream judgments, with pictures, abridged and with distinctions." The preface says that opinions on dreams differ, that the old Rites of Zhou set an office of dream-diviners, that Ding Gong of Han, who saw a pine and rose to the highest of the three offices, and Li Bai of Tang, who saw a flower bloom at the tip of his brush and became a peerless writer, both bore out their dreams, and that whoever wishes to avoid bad dreams should write the charm of the Night-Ruling God (主夜神, shuyashin) and fix it over the sleeping place; the charm is given in five characters, 婆珊婆演帝. Then: "the dream readings of the ancients are set down below"; the heading "dream judgments, 132 articles", and the articles themselves, each a plain "if one sees X, Y": climbing to the sky, one will become very noble; the sky clearing, very good; the sky turning red, good; the night dawning, the sick will recover; sun and moon falling, one loses father and mother; swallowing sun and moon, a noble son; struck by lightning, very good; clouds rising on all sides, good for trade; falling snow, joy; hearing thunder, success in the world; frost falling, bad; meeting rain, one will meet wine and food. Only the first twelve articles are quoted here; they name no date, no day of the cycle and no season.

## RD308 (2026-11-10): the slash of the table and the night of the 初夢; Open; Searched and not found

What this reading settled is small: one more open print, the 万代大雑書三世相 of 1886 (mdz:i108), carries the dream articles and the charm of the Night-Ruling God and no table of the days on which a dream is not told, so the table stays known only from the 万代大雑書古今大成 (mdz:p11) and the Ebara dream book (eb9:i9), and the slash inside its cells (q785) and the night of the 初夢 (q788) are not explained by any text reached.

Open:

- Question: does the 星槎ラボ 延宝2 大ざつしよ, the earliest print, or any other 万代大雑書 print (the NDL has some seventy-six 大雑書 titles, almost all restricted to the library's own reading rooms) set out the table 夢を見て語らざる日 with a legend for the slash, for instance day branches for a dream seen in the first half of the night and others for the second half, or the days of the sexagenary cycle against the days of the month?

The book is a pictured lexicon of dream objects **arranged by the iroha kana**: images 1-3 the covers and the publisher's label, 4 a loose handwritten note, 5 a picture, 6 the contents (目録, headed 夢合早占大成目録 with 一 六夢之辨解 and the iroha), images 7-27 the introduction on **the six kinds of dream (六夢之辨解, after the Zhou li: 一曰 正夢 and on image 10 二曰噩夢, read: «二曰噩夢とは驚くところありて見る夢なり», with examples from old stories that name Prince Sadazumi 貞純親王 and Ujimitsu), then the third to the sixth kind, on images 14, 17, 20 and 23 by their headings seen on thumbnails**, with a picture for each kind; image 28 the grid headed 丁附合文 of the iroha kana beside the first heading ○い 平安 (the author's line 如環子著); images 29-120 the entries under the iroha kana (い, ろ, は, に, ほ, へ, と, ち, ... て, and onward), each object a short article opening with a circle and ending in good, bad or a fortune (酒食, 病, 金銀, 立身); images 121-122 the appendix above (the heading 禳夢秘法 is a method of driving a dream out; the words are quoted from the enlarged images, the order of the pages of the spread 122 being left page 呪文 and 又一法 the burning, right page 悪夢著草木 ... and 又一法 the water); 夢合早占大成終 on the right page of image 121; images 123-124 the publisher's advertisement of the 夢合長寿鑑絵抄, 河内屋徳兵衛 梓, 安政四年 (1857); 125 the back cover. **No table of days on which a dream is not told, no slash legend, no table of the sixty days and no 初夢 night with a calendar** on any of the 125 images (the eight ordered sheets viewed); the day-keyed table of the 夢合長寿鑑絵抄 (mdz:kyoto-12284) has no counterpart here. The remedy for a bad dream is the only rite in the book: facing east at dawn, to blow water from the mouth three times with the charm, or to write down the whole dream and burn it.
