## <a id="mgz-6-p4"></a>mgz.6:p4 Thub chog byin rlabs gter mdzod: the Śākyamuni dhāraṇī

*Read: scan, 2026-11-04.*

> ཐུགས་རྒྱུད་བསྐུལ་བའི་ཚུལ་དུ་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ཡི་གེ་ཉུང་ངུ་ལས་གསུངས་པའི་གཟུངས་ནི། ཏདྱ་ཐཱ། ཨོཾ་མུ་ནེ་མུ་ནེ་མ་ཧཱ་མུ་ན་ཡེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ། ཞེས་ཅི་རིགས་དང་། ཨོཾ་མན་ཆད་ཅི་འགྲུབ་ཏུ་བཟླའོ། །

Vol. 6, img. 12. To stir his heart, the dhāraṇī taught in the Perfection of Wisdom in Few Letters (ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ཡི་གེ་ཉུང་ངུ, sher phyin yi ge nyung ngu): the mantra with its opening ཏདྱ་ཐཱ, as many times as suits; from ཨོཾ on, recite as many as can be managed. So the rite itself allows the mantra with its opening or from ཨོཾ alone. The same mantra stands in his hundred mantras, lg img. 90, where the blocks are too worn to quote it; the reset copy there has the same spelling, with the gloss that it is the heart of the Teacher Śākya Thub pa (ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ, shAkya thub pa).

## <a id="mgz-23-p7-8"></a>mgz.23:p7-8 Mipham's kriyā and caryā mantra manual: offerings in particular on the 8th, 14th, 15th and in the miracle fortnight

*Read: scan, 2026-11-16.*

> དེ་ཡང་མཆོད་པ་ནི་ཐུན་གྱི་མཐའ་སོགས་དང་། དུས་ནམ་ནམ་བྱེད་པ་བྱུང་ན་མཆོག་ཏུ་ལེགས་ཤིང་། ཚེས་བརྒྱད་བཅུ་བཞི་བཅོ་ལྔ་དང་། །ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་ནི་ཟླ་ཕྱེད་ལ། །ཁྱད་པར་དུ་ཡང་མཆོད་པ་བྱ། །ཞེས་རྒྱུད་ལས་གསུངས་སོ། །མཆོད་པའི་རྫས་ནི།

Mipham's Essential Points of How to Accomplish the Mantras of Action and Conduct [Tantra], in Brief (running title, read on the scan: «བྱ་སྤྱོད་ཀྱི་སྔགས་བསྒྲུབ་པའི་ཚུལ་གནད་མདོར་བསྡུས»), after the laying out of the maṇḍala and the making of the cleansing water. Vol. 23, the last two lines of p. 7 (image 16 of I1PD84582, which carries the printed number 7) and the first line of p. 8 (image 17), read by eye.

"As for the offering: [it is made] at the end of the session and so on, and it is excellent whenever it can be done; and 'on the eighth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth, and in the half-month of the miracles (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ, cho 'phrul), make offerings in particular', the tantra says. The substances of the offering are: …"

The practitioner of a kriyā or caryā mantra offers to the deity at the end of every session, and with special care on the 8th, 14th and 15th and through the fortnight of the Buddha's miracles (the first half of the first month in WB's almanac, wb.1:p157). Mipham quotes the verse as "from the tantra" without naming it. The text that follows lists the torma, flowers, incense and lamps by the kind of rite; it does not return to the days.

## <a id="mgz-27-p19-20"></a>mgz.27:p19-20 Mipham's Kālacakra empowerment commentary: householders with wealth offer to the yoginīs on the 8th and 14th

*Read: scan, 2026-11-16.*

> དེ་བཞིན་དུ་རྒྱུ་སྐར་དང་སྦྱོར་བ་དགེ་བའི་ཉིན་ཞག་གཞན་ལའང་བྱ་རུང་ངོ་། །ནག་པའི་ཉ་ལ་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བྲིས་ནས་སམ་ཡང་ན་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བྲིས་པ་མེད་པར་ཡང་རུང་། མཐའ་དག་ཡོན་ཏན་གཏེར་དུ་གྱུར་པ་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཆེན་པོ་ནི་སློབ་མ་རྣམས་ལ་བསྟན་པའི་སླད་དུ་ལྷའི་རྣལ་འབྱོར་བསྒོམ་པ་སྔོན་དུ་བཏང་ནས་ཕྱི་རོལ་གྱི་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མ་རྣམས་དག་ལ་བཟའ་བ་དང་བཏུང་བ་ལ་སོགས་པས་མཆོད་པ་བྱའོ། །ཅི་སྟེ་མཆོད་པའི་ཡོ་བྱད་མ་ཚོགས་ནས་ནག་པའི་ཉ་ལ་མཆོད་པ་མེད་པར་གྱུར་ན་དེའི་ཚེ་ལོ་གཅིག་དག་ཏུ་ནི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་དག་ལས་ཞེས་པ་དག་ནི་ཞེས་པ་སྟེ། བླ་མས་བསྟན་པར་མི་བྱའོ། །ཅི་སྟེ་སློབ་མར་འོས་པ་འབྱོར་མེད་འགས་རྒྱུད་དོན་དུ་གཉེར་ན་འབྱོར་ལྡན་སློབ་མ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཉ་བཅུ་གཉིས་སུ་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མ་མཆོད་པ་བྱས་ནས་དེའི་བསོད་ནམས་ཀྱི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་ཞར་ལ་གཞན་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་མཉན་པར་བྱའོ། །འདིར་ཕྱིའི་བགེགས་ཞི་བའི་དོན་དུ་རྟག་ཏུ་ཚེས་བརྒྱད་དང་བཅུ་བཞི་ལ་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མ་རྣམས་ལ་མཆོད་པ་ཇི་འབྱོར་བྱ་བ་དང་། འབྱུང་པོ་རྣམས་ལ་ཉིན་ཞག་སོ་སོར་གཏོར་མ་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་མཆོད་པ་མཎྜལ་སོགས་བྱ་བ་ནི། ཁྱིམ་གནས་ནོར་ལྡན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ངེས་པའོ། །ཀུན་སྤངས་ཀྱི་སློབ་མ་ལ་ནི་མཆོད་པ་དེ་ལྟ་བུ་ངེས་པ་མེད་པས་དེ་རྣམས་ལ་རྒྱུད་བསྟན་པ་ལ་མཆོད་པའི་ངེས་པ་བླ་མ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་མི་བྱ་སྟེ། གང་ཕྱིར་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་རྡོ་རྗེ་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མ་རྣམས་ཀྱང་ལྷག་པའི་བསམ་པ་གཙོ་བོར་འཛིན་པར་བྱེད་ཀྱི་མཆོད་པ་སོགས་ཀྱི་དངོས་པོ་གཙོ་བོར་འཛིན་པར་བྱེད་པ་མ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་རོ། །རྒྱུད་བསྟན་པའི་མཆོད་པའི་ངེས་པའོ།

Mipham's commentary on the empowerment chapter of the Kālacakra (running title of the etext: «དུས་འཁོར་དབང་ལེའི་འགྲེལ་པ»), on when the empowerment is given, the tantra taught and the offering made. Vol. 27, the lower part of p. 19 (image 27 of I1PD84586, which carries the printed number 19) and the first five lines of p. 20 (image 28), read by eye; the dots under the tantra's words are not reproduced, and a doubled «སློབ་མ» of the etext stands once on the scan. The sentence before, on the same page (etext, its upper lines not read on the scan), says that empowerment and the consecration of images are done on the twelve full moons, the full moon of Caitra (ནག་པ, nag pa) first among them.

"… Likewise it may also be done on another day whose star and yoga are auspicious. On the full moon of Caitra, having drawn the maṇḍala or even without a drawn maṇḍala, in order to teach the disciples the great king of tantras, the treasure of all qualities, one first cultivates the yoga of the deity and then makes offering to the outer yoginīs with food, drink and the like. If the means of offering have not come together and no offering is made on the full moon of Caitra, then for a year … the guru is not to teach [it]. If some who are fit to be disciples but have no means seek the tantra, another disciple, who has means, makes the offering to the yoginīs on the twelve full moons, and the others too may listen in the wake of his store of merit. Here, in order to pacify outer obstacles: always, on the eighth and the fourteenth, to make offering to the yoginīs as far as one's means allow, to give tormas to the elemental spirits day by day, and to make offering to the Buddha, the maṇḍala and so on, is the rule for householders who have wealth (ཁྱིམ་གནས་ནོར་ལྡན, khyim gnas nor ldan). For a disciple who has renounced everything no such offering is fixed, and for them the gurus too are not to make the offering a rule of teaching the tantra: because the buddhas and the vajra yoginīs hold the superior intention as chief and do not hold the things of offering and the like as chief. This is the rule of the offering for teaching the tantra."

A rule laid on lay people by name: the wealthy householder who has entered the Kālacakra offers to the yoginīs on every eighth and fourteenth, gives a torma to the spirits daily and offers to the Buddha; the renunciant owes no fixed offering. The verse of the tantra that Mipham glosses is not quoted apart from his gloss, and whether the eighth and fourteenth are of both halves of the month the page does not say.

## <a id="mgz-27-p45"></a>mgz.27:p45 Mipham's Kālacakra empowerment commentary: the six complete days of a month, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30

*Read: scan, 2026-11-16.*

> ཞེས་པ་ཟླ་བ་གཅིག་ལ་རྫོགས་པའི་ཞག་དྲུག་སྟེ། ཡར་ངོ་མར་ངོའི་ལྔ་པ་གཉིས་ནི། ལྔ་དང་ཉི་ཤུ། བཅུ་པ་གཉིས་ནི་བཅུ་ཉེར་ལྔ། བཅོ་ལྔ་པ་གཉིས་ནི་ཉ་སྟོང་སྟེ། དེ་ལྟར་དུས་མཚམས་དེ་ལ་དགེ་ལས་དཀར་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་རྫོགས་པ་བིཥྚི་སྤང་བའི་ཆ་ལ་དང་། མི་དགེ་བའི་ལས་ལ་ནག་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་རྫོགས་པ་རྣམས་ལ་ས་བསྐོས་ཏེ་ཟུག་རྔུ་རྣམས་དབྱུང་བས་སྦྱང་བ་དང་། སྔགས་དང་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་སོགས་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་སྦྱང་བར་བྱས་ལ། སླར་ཁུང་བུ་སས་དགང་ཞིང་འཐས་པར་བཅག་པར་བྱའོ། །དེ་ནས་ས་ཡོངས་སུ་གཟུང་བ་དང་། དེ་བཞིན་དུ་སློབ་བུ་རྣམས་དག་ནི་ཡང་དག་བསྡུ་བ་དག་ཀྱང་རྫོགས་པའི་ཚེས་ལ་བྱ་ཞིང་ཁྱད་པར་ཉ་ལ་བྱ་བར་བསྔགས་སོ། །དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བྲི་བའི་དུས་ཤེས་ནས་ཞག་བཅུ་གཉིས་པ་ལ་ཐིག་གདབ་པར་བྱ་ཞིང་འདོད་པ་དང་མ་ནུའི་ཉིན་ཞག་ཞེས་བཅུ་གསུམ་པ་དང་བཅུ་བཞི་པ་དག་ལ་དཔལ་ལྡན་རྡུལ་ཚོན་དབྱེ་བ་ཉིད་དུ་བྱའོ་

The same commentary, on the days for cleansing and taking the ground of the maṇḍala. Vol. 27, p. 45, image 53 of I1PD84586, ten lines read by eye; the etext's «བིའི་སྤང་བའི» reads «བིཥྚི་སྤང་བའི» on the scan. The tantra's words glossed, on the lines just above (etext), are that the ground is cleansed and taken "on the complete ones" (རྫོགས་པ, rdzogs pa).

"… that is, the six complete days in one month: the two fifths of the waxing and the waning half are the 5th and the 20th; the two tenths are the 10th and the 25th; the two fifteenths are the full moon and the new moon. So at those junctures, for a virtuous work on the complete days of the bright half, in the part that avoids viṣṭi, and for an unvirtuous work on the complete days of the dark half, the ground is dug and cleansed by taking out the thorns, and cleansed also by mantra, mudrā and the like, and the pit is filled again with earth and stamped firm. Then taking possession of the ground, and likewise gathering the disciples, are to be done on a complete date, and doing them on the full moon in particular is praised. Knowing the time for drawing the maṇḍala, the lines are struck on the twelfth day, and on the thirteenth and the fourteenth, the days called 'desire' and 'Manu', the glorious coloured powders are laid out …"

The one place found where Mipham names the twenty-fifth: as the tenth of the waning half, one of six "complete" dates of a month (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30) on which the works of a maṇḍala rite are begun, the bright half's three for virtuous works. It is a rule for the master of a Kālacakra empowerment, not an observance day for practitioners, and no feast, dākinī or guru is tied to the tenth or the twenty-fifth in it.

Mipham's King of Jewels, the Source of All the Activities of Mañjuśrī (running title of the etext: «འཇམ་དཔལ་གྱི་ལས་ཚོགས་ཀུན་འབྱུང་ནོར་བུའི་རྒྱལ་པོ»), after three recipes of substances "accomplished at the time of an eclipse". Vol. 33, the last eight lines of p. 51 (image 59 of I1PD84592, which carries the printed number 51), read by eye; the etext's «རྩེ་གཅིག་དུ» and «བསྒྲུབ་བར» read «རྩེ་གཅིག་ཏུ» and «བསྒྲུབ་པར» on the scan. The sentence runs on to p. 52 (etext only): an attendant gives the sign, and when the sun or moon is freed from the planet the substance is accomplished.

"Further, the boundless activities of every kind, whether the accomplishment [of the deity] has gone before or not, are to be accomplished at the time of an eclipse (གཟའ་འཛིན, gza' 'dzin), the special time at which the attainment is accomplished at once. First, knowing by the procedure of star reckoning when the planet will enter the sun or the moon, as that time draws near one sets out the image of the yidam in a good place, lays out abundant offerings and gathers the pledge substances of the accomplishment. Having first done one's own yoga, the offerings and so on, and guarded oneself, the place and the substances, when the planet begins to enter the sun or moon one gives oneself one-pointedly, in the practice room, to the yoga of the deity and the mantra and accomplishes the substance of attainment, …"

For Mipham the eclipse is "the special time" for mantra practice: what is done in it succeeds at once, even without a completed retreat. The practice named is the yoga of one's yidam with mantra recitation for the length of the eclipse; the reckoning of the eclipse comes first. No multiplier is stated.

Mipham's Summary of the Tenets of the Wish-fulfilling Treasury (the etext's title: «ཡིད་བཞིན་མཛོད་ཀྱི་གྲུབ་མཐའ་བསྡུས་པ»; its opening says it gathers the sense of the twelfth chapter of Klong chen pa's Yid bzhin rin po che'i mdzod), in the refutation of the six non-Buddhist teachers. Vol. 21, p. 135, image 143 of I1PD84580, four lines read by eye; the etext's «སྐའི», «མི་ཞི་བས དུས» and «སངས་ངས་རྒྱས» read «སྐྲའི», «མི་ཞི་བས་དུས» and «སངས་རྒྱས» on the scan.

"Ajita of the hair blanket holds that everything is done by time. Against that: the reasoning that examines meeting refutes a truly established time; and since, though there is time, the entanglements are not calmed unless the path is cultivated, liberation by time is refuted. On the great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen), the difference of multiplying (འགྱུར་ཁྱད, 'gyur khyad) arises from two things, the blessing of the buddhas and the devotion of their followers, so that is not like this."

Mipham forestalls an objection: if time does nothing, why is an act greater on a great occasion? His answer is that the multiplying on such days comes from the buddhas' blessing and the practitioners' devotion, not from the time itself. He names no day and no figure here.

In the morning, before the sun has risen and before one has eaten, with shame and regard for others, the vow of the one-day vow is to be received from another person. That other, the Vinaya explains, is a monk; the Sūtra on Setting in the Observance says 'a śramaṇa, or a brahmin, or whoever else', so that one who merely knows the rite is enough; and the Compendium of Ascertainments explains that where there is no such person one takes it oneself, and also that the one-day vows of a whole life may be taken at one sitting."

Mipham gives the hour, the posture, the giver and the eight branches; neither here nor in the lines that follow on p. 180 (the reasons for the eight branches; that one who has not gone for refuge has no one-day vow) does he name a day of the month on which the vow is to be taken.
