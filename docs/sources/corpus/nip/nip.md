## <a id="nip-p1"></a>nip:p1 初夢 (Hatsuyume): the night's history, the treasure ship

*Read: etext, 2026-11-09.*

> 初夢　はつゆめ（竹内利美）
> 新しい年を迎えて初めてみる夢。…以前は節分の夜（立春の朝）の夢を初夢としたが、暦制の関係から除夜や元日の夜に移り、やがて「事始め」の正月2日の夜の夢に一定したらしい。すでに室町時代には正月2日夜「宝船」の紙を枕の下に置いて寝る風習が始まっており、江戸時代に下ると「宝船売り」が江戸の風物詩として…

Quoted in part (copyrighted; the rest in our own words). The first dream seen after the new year comes in; the custom of judging the year's fortune by it is old. The entry says the dream of the night of 節分 (setsubun, the eve of 立春, risshun, the beginning of spring) was once the first dream; because of the calendar's arrangement the night moved to New Year's Eve and New Year's night, and in the end, it seems, settled on the night of 2 January, "the beginning of work". By the Muromachi period (1336–1573) the custom of sleeping with a paper of the treasure ship (宝船, takarabune) under the pillow on the night of 2 January had begun; in the Edo period (1603–1868) the treasure-ship sellers were a familiar sight of Edo. The picture of the Seven Gods of Fortune in their ship and the palindromic verse «ながきよのとおのねぶりのみなめざめなみのりぶねのおとのよきかな» had long since become fixed. Bad dreams were floated away on a river with the ship, or turned by a picture of the baku (獏, the dream-eating beast): the "dream exchange" (夢たがえ). The entry names no book.

## <a id="nip-p2"></a>nip:p2 夢占 (Yume-ura): Japan, the first dream, the treasure ship, what is done against a bad dream

*Read: etext, 2026-11-09.*

> 夢占　ゆめうら（井之口章次、上田紀行）
> …正月の初夢には、江戸時代は宝船の木版刷りを売る者があり、枕の下に敷いて寝た。…／…また正月二日の初夢に、とくに予知力を認めて、江戸時代以来、宝船の絵が売られ、悪夢は宝船とともに川に流し、吉夢ならば宝船を神仏に献じたりした。

Two short quotations (copyrighted; the rest in our own words). Inoguchi's part, on Japan: a dream is taken as a sign of what will come; the signs are read by direct association (a tooth falling out, a death in the family) or indirect (a flood, a fire); the good dreams of the saying "first Fuji, second hawk, third eggplant, fourth a funeral, fifth a fire"; against a bad dream one says "baku, baku" to have the baku eat it, or tells it first to a nandina tree and shakes the tree. For the first dream of the year, in the Edo period woodblock prints of the treasure ship were sold and laid under the pillow. Ueda's part, world-wide: Artemidorus's 2nd-century *Oneirocritica*; in Japan the dream hall (夢殿) of Hōryūji and the dream message (夢告) of the Heian period; again the special foreknowing power of the first dream of 2 January, the ship floated away with a bad dream and offered to the gods with a good one. No Edo book is named and no rule by day of the cycle, month or season is given other than the first dream's night.

## <a id="nip-rd293"></a>RD293 (2026-11-10): the calendar of the night of 2 January; Searched and not found

The 日本大百科全書 (nip:p1, nip:p2) names the night 正月2日 and no calendar; Which calendar counts it is not stated in the entry. No Edo calendar or almanac naming the night of the 初夢 was reached; the two 大雑書 read for it (mdz:i31, mdz:i72) hold no night of the year for it. Passage kept in its script: 初夢はつゆめ, the first dream of the new year; 正月2日しょうがつふつか, the second day of the first month; 節分せつぶん, the eve of spring.
