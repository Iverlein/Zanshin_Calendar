## <a id="kpd-p184-189"></a>kpd:p184-189 Kālaprakāśikā ch. XLII, the dreams, in Sanskrit: the watches and the spans (vv. 66–69)

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269), vv. 63–69; the verses 1–62 and the page numbers of the Tamil-press scan: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> सन्तापं बन्धुनाशं च व्यसनं च विनिर्दिशेत् ।
> रात्रौ पूर्वादियामेषु दृष्टे स्वप्ने क्रमात्फलम् ॥ ६६ ॥
> करोति वर्षाद्वर्षार्धात्रिमासान्मासतस्तथा ।
> अरुणोदयवेलायां द्वादशाहात्फलप्रदः ॥ ६७ ॥
> गोविसर्गे यदि स्वप्नं सुमहत्तदहःफलम् ।
> अशुभः प्राक्छुभः पश्चाच्छुभो वा प्रागथाशुभः ॥ ६८ ॥
> पाश्चात्यफलदः स्वप्नः तत्र पूर्वस्तु निष्फलः ।
> यत्किंचिदशुभं दृष्ट्वा तत ऊर्ध्वं तु न स्वपेत् ।
> दुःस्वप्नं कीर्तयेत्सद्यः सुस्वप्नं नैव कीर्तयेत् ॥ ६९ ॥

*Kālaprakāśikā* ch. XLII (*dvicatvāriṃśo 'dhyāyaḥ*), the dreams, 69 verses, pp. 184–189 of the Devanagari section of the Lawley Press printing (Internet Archive item ZBAx_kalaprakasika-n-p-iyer-tamil-lawley-press; the Devanagari pp. 181–190 are leaves 468–477 and the page n476 carries the printed number १८९, the running head "त्रिचत्वारिंशोऽध्यायः" belonging to the next chapter; the Tamil-press book has the English translation as a second sequence of pages numbered 1–, so that the English "p. 189" is another leaf, n224, which carries ch. XXXIV; the Devanagari is what is quoted here). The page n476 ends the chapter with "iti dvicatvāriṃśo 'dhyāyaḥ" and opens ch. XLIII, *Śrījayantīnirṇayaḥ*. This is the Sanskrit of the English at kps:p232-236. The author is named in the last chapter's colophon as Nṛsiṃhasūri (the first syllables garbled).

Verses 66–67, in our words, **read on the page**: dreams seen in the watches of the night "beginning with the first" bear their fruit in order after a year (*varṣāt*), half a year (*varṣārdhāt*), three months (*trimāsāt*) and a month (*māsataḥ*): four spans for four watches; at the hour of dawn (*aruṇodaya-velā*) the fruit is in twelve days (*dvādaśāhāt*); at the cows' release (*go-visarga*) a very great dream (*sumahat*) has its fruit that same day (*tad-ahaḥ-phalam*). The print joins "-ārdhāt" and "tri-" as "-ārdhātrimāsān" (a single *t*; sandhi would give *-ārdhāt trimāsān*); the quoted line is as printed. The fourth span is a month, not half a month: the RD265 restoration "half a month" was wrong, and the third watch has three months. Iyer's English (kps) has a year, six months, a month and twelve days at dawn; the Sanskrit's third span is three months and its fourth a month, so the English's "a month" is the Sanskrit's fourth span and its third is missing there.

Verse 68: if a bad dream comes first and a good one after, or a good first and a bad after, the later gives the fruit and the earlier is fruitless (*pāścātya-phala-daḥ svapnaḥ*; Iyer: "of two dreams the later prevails"). Verse 69, as printed: "after seeing something *bad* one does not sleep on after that" (*yat kiṃcid aśubhaṃ dṛṣṭvā tata ūrdhvaṃ tu na svapet*); a bad dream is told at once, a good one never. The other Indian texts (agp, mtp, bya) say "after a good dream one does not sleep again"; the print here has *aśubhaṃ*, a bad dream, and we do not mend it. Verse 66's first line (*santāpaṃ bandhunāśaṃ ca vyasanaṃ ca vinirdiśet*) ends the list of bad signs of vv. 63–65 (a dry river, sea or pond seen: death and grief; the loss of wealth; great sickness; teeth falling: sorrow, loss of kin, ruin) and is not a tail of the span.

The verses before (1–65) are the good and bad images with their gains and losses (a king, an elephant, gold, a bull, the sun and moon, milk, a woman in white; bad: dogs, jackals, vultures, black insects, a falling star, the teeth falling); one verse gives a time by the object (27: a white snake biting the right arm brings a thousand "in ten days"); no verse ties a dream to a *tithi*, a weekday, a nakṣatra, a month or a season.

Doubt: only the page with vv. 63–69 was read on the image (Devanagari press print, clear); vv. 1–62 are the OCR layer's.
