## <a id="kpd-p184-189"></a>kpd:p184-189 Kālaprakāśikā ch. XLII, the dreams, in Sanskrit: the watches and the spans (vv. 66–69)

*Kālaprakāśikā* ch. XLII (*dvicatvāriṃśo 'dhyāyaḥ*), the dreams, 69 verses, pp. 184–189 of the Lawley Press printing; the verses on time are 66–69 on p. 189 and the chapter closes with "iti dvicatvāriṃśo 'dhyāyaḥ". This is the Sanskrit of the English at kps:p232-236, which RD263 could not reach. The text layer is a Devanagari OCR with many wrong vowel signs; the lines above are the words as they read, the brackets what is lost. The author is named in the last chapter's colophon as Nṛsiṃhasūri (the first syllables garbled).

Verses 66–67, in our words: dreams seen in the watches of the night, "beginning with the first", bear their fruit in order after a year, half a year, a month and (apparently) half a month — the second half of the line is garbled, and the restoration "varṣād varṣārdhān … māsān māsārdhatas tathā" is ours; at the hour of dawn (*aruṇodaya-velā*) the fruit is in twelve days (*dvādaśāhāt*); at the cows' release (*go-visarga*) a very great dream (*sumahat*) has its fruit that same day (*tad-ahaḥ-phalam*). Compare Iyer's English (kps): a year, six months, a month, twelve days "before dawn", a day "before sunrise": the Sanskrit's first, second, dawn and cow-release terms agree; whether it has a fourth watch term (half a month) that Iyer leaves out is not settled by the layer.

Verse 68 (garbled): a bad dream followed by a good, or the reverse, the later one counts and the earlier is fruitless (Iyer: "of two dreams the later prevails"). Verse 69: after seeing something good, one does not sleep again; a bad dream is told at once, a good one never.

The verses before (1–65) are the good and bad images with their gains and losses (a king, an elephant, gold, a bull, the sun and moon, milk, a woman in white; bad: dogs, jackals, vultures, black insects, a falling star, the teeth falling); one verse gives a time by the object (27: a white snake biting the right arm brings a thousand "in ten days"); no verse ties a dream to a *tithi*, a weekday, a nakṣatra, a month or a season.

Doubt: OCR only; the page image of p. 189 not read; verse numbers are those the layer prints (the OCR garbles the numerals 66–69: "६६", "६७", "६8").
