Zhechen Gyaltsab's *Garland of White Utpalas*, the bases and the way of reckoning of the thirteen circles (pp. 303–304; BDRC's open scan I4PD2444 img. 347–348, image = printed page + 44, read by eye on the typeset page against BDRC's etext, VEIE0OPIDB78C0F0_I4PD2444, etext pp. 347–348, which it matches in the lines quoted; the text goes on to p. 313 with each circle's readings, white and black, and their remedies, and closes as written by Zhechen-pa Jamyang Lodrö Gyatso (ཟེ་ཆེན་པ་འཇམ་དབྱངས་བློ་གྲོས་རྒྱ་མཚོ, ze chen pa 'jam dbyangs blo gros rgya mtsho) as a branch of his table manual, the *Kumuda Pond* (ཀུ་མུ་དའི་རྫིང་བུ, ku mu da'i rdzing bu)). Found with BDRC's full-text search for «བལ་མོ་ལྡིང་སྐོར», the only worked circle the search gave.

It has three parts: the basis, the way of reckoning and the results. The basis, thirteen kinds: (1) the white crystal garuḍa: the earth lord, the king, counted from the bird of the element's enemy; (2) the white conch-lion of the snows: the lama and spiritual friend, from the element's enemy's hare; (3) the yellow golden snake of existence: the knowledge-holder, the mantra-holder, from the friend's snake; (4) the blue turquoise dragon of the sky: the Bon priest, from the mother's dragon; (5) the steadfast elephant of the earth: ministers and officials, from the friend's ox; (6) the striped Chinese tiger of the forest: young men in their prime, from the enemy's tiger; (7) the red bird: noble ladies and women, from the element's mother's rat; (8) the blazing wish-fulfilling jewel: small children and youths, from the enemy's tiger; (9) the blue young peacock: young girls, from the mother's dog; (10) workers and servants, two ways: roughly, the golden bird, from the mother's bird; finely, for tiger, horse and dog the friend's tiger, for rat, dragon and monkey the mother's dragon, for pig, sheep and hare the pig that agrees with the power's element (དབང་ཁམས, dbang khams), for bird, ox and snake the son's bird; and that, it is said, is applied to the master's year; (11) the long-horned yellow divine sheep: land, country and treasury, from the enemy's pig; (12) the golden vase: dry goods, gold, silver, copper, iron, turquoise and the like, from the friend's pig; (13) the great wild yak in its prime: horses, sheep, cattle and the blooded beasts, from the mother's ox.

Second, the way of reckoning. For example, if the earth lord is a wood monkey, from the iron bird, the enemy of his element: from whatever each one's basis is, count upward the years of the one reckoned, as many as he has lived (དུ་ལོན, du lon); here there is no difference of male, female or the like. The life force, body, power, luck and mewa of the year where it falls are fought by the life force, body, power, luck and body mewa (ལུས་སྨེ, lus sme) of the one reckoned. For example, a wood monkey and an iron bird: the life force meets itself, the body is the son, the power becomes the enemy. A meeting of mother or friend, and of earth or water with itself, lays a white pebble; of son or enemy, and of wood, iron or fire with itself, a black one.

So the *Garland* starts the first circle of a wood-monkey king from the iron bird, iron being wood's enemy, and the wood is the king's power (the wood of the wood monkey, the element the year is named by); it is his luck too (a monkey's luck is wood), so the example alone does not choose between the two, but the tenth circle's fine way names the power (དབང་ཁམས) outright, as the notes do (vol. 2, p. 533). It counts upward by the years lived for every person, man or woman; and it fights the reached year's mewa with the reckoned person's body mewa (claim circles-zg-worked).
