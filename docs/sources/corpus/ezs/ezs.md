## <a id="ezs-p10"></a>ezs:p10 The eve of spring (節分) and the 庚申 day among the calendar articles (frames 10-11)

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> 立春の前の日を節分といふ冬陰気終り春の陽気を待日なれば家内をはらひ浄め
> 庚申八寺の入より九月め庚申の日なり

OCR only, garbled. "The day before the beginning of spring (立春) is called 節分; it is the day that waits for the spring yang as the winter yin ends, so the house is swept and purified." The 庚申 article (frame 10) tells what is forbidden on a 庚申 day (quarrels, intercourse) and says nothing of dreams or sleep. Neither article mentions a dream.

## <a id="ezs-p30"></a>ezs:p30 Fuji, hawk, aubergine: the first, second and third lucky dream (frames 30-31)

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> 吾朝にては富士山を見るを第一の吉夢とし鷹
> […]茄子を見てを第三の吉夢と

OCR only, the garbled text of frames 30-31 read in outline. "In our country seeing Mount Fuji is the first lucky dream, a hawk (鷹) the second, an aubergine (茄子) the third." No night, date or season is named, and 初夢 is not in the book (the OCR of all 39 frames holds no 初夢).

## <a id="ezs-p31"></a>ezs:p31 A dream before midnight and one after (frame 31)

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> 夜半（よなか）より前に見る夢は其吉凶遠きに[…]
> 半より後に見る夢は其吉凶近きにあり

Read on the page image (the OCR is garbled here and reads 麦 and 翌 for 夢): "a dream seen before midnight shows its good or bad far off [the verb after 遠きに is not read]; a dream seen after midnight, its good or bad is near." The one rule of the night found in the Edo texts read: a time of night, not a night of the year. It stands among the lamp-wick omens and the advice on lucky dreams (wash the mouth with clean water, face the morning sun before telling it, ask a learned person).
