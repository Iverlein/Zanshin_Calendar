# LT, the eighth branch: the small reckonings, the thirty and the circles

Lha mo tshul khrims (ལྷ་མོ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས, lha mo tshul khrims), *Laying the
Pebbles of the Chinese Reckoning and Their Results* (ནག་རྩིས་རྡེལ་འགྲེམ་འབྲས་བཤད,
nag rtsis rdel 'grem 'bras bshad), whose eighth branch opens the treasury of
the scattered small reckonings (རྩིས་ཕྲན་ཐོར་བུ, rtsis phran thor bu) with the
thirty starting points and the circles of Balmo, in WB's own verses for the
most part. A modern work (2003): quoted no further than its facts need.
Read by RD44 (2026-10-23): pp. 65–67 (etext pp. 73–75; scan I1KG21886 img.
073–075, image = printed page + 8), for the thirty's and the circles' count.

LT, the eighth branch, the thirty starting points and the circles of Balmo (pp. 65–67; scan I1KG21886 img. 073–075, read by eye on the lines quoted; between them, not quoted, LT gives the thirty pairs and the eight fighting pebbles in WB's own verses, vol. 1, p. 453). Where the scan and BDRC's etext part, the scan is kept: «རྡེལ་བརྒྱད་རེ» (the etext «རྡེལ་བརྒྱུད་རེ»), «ཀླུང་རྟ» (the etext «རླུང་རྟི»). As printed, «དག» (22, read as ནད, the sick) and «ཤིན་རྩིས» (23, read as གཤིན་རྩིས, the dead). The numbers in round brackets are LT's own, set in the line.

Of the thirty Chinese and Tibetan starting points, in order: the king (1), the spiritual friend (2), the mantra-holder (3), the Bon priest (4), the old (5) and the young men (6), wives and women (7), small children (8), girls (9), servants (10), horses (11), livestock (12), the household (13), the bride's going (14) and the house and household (15), eight pebbles each, laid without error; merit and the means of work (16), armour (17), the la tree and a child's existence (18), which god helps (19), the harming demon (20), the old (21), the sick (22), the dead (23), the burning or keeping of the remains (24), the grave site (25), the rites (26), men's life force (27), small children's body (28), women's power (29) and great men's luck (30): eight pebbles to every starting point. … [The pairs as WB gives them, then:] from these, for one reckoned of a male year count down from the male year, for a female year up from the female; where it falls is like the progressed year (ལོག་མེན, log men) … So the pebbles are 240, each with its verdict.

How the pebbles of what is called the circles of Balmo (བལ་མོ་ལྡིང་སྐོར, bal mo lding skor) are laid: [the rule of colour as WB's, then] first, from the bird of the enemy of the reckoned person's element count upward; where it falls, its life force, body, power, luck and mewa, five, are fought by the reckoned person's five. Second, from the enemy's hare; third, from the friend's snake; fourth, from the reckoned person's mother's dragon; fifth, the friend's ox; sixth, the enemy's tiger; seventh, the mother's rat; eighth, the enemy's tiger; ninth, from the mother's dog; tenth, the mother's bird; eleventh, from the enemy's pig; twelfth, the friend's pig; thirteenth, from the mother's ox: counting upward as many years as lived (དུ་ལོན, du lon), the life force, body, power, luck and mewa of that [year] are fought by the reckoned person's five.

So LT numbers WB's thirty readings 1 to 30 in WB's order and sets WB's thirty pairs beside them without joining the two lists; it lays the thirty's count as WB does and adds nothing to whose element; for the circles it says outright that the count runs upward by the years lived, and gives the tenth circle only its rough start, the mother's bird.
