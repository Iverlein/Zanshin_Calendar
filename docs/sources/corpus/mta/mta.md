## <a id="mta-p52-53"></a>mta:p52-53 The year's empty vase, its clean directions and the opening of the soil

*Read: scan, 2026-10-26.*

> བོད་ཟླ་༡༢ ཚེས་༢༢ ནས་ཟླ་༡ ཚེས་༢༢ བར་བུམ་སྟོང་ལྷོ། བོད་ཟླ་༡ ཚེས་༢༢ ནས་ཟླ་༡ ཕྱི་མའི་ཚེས་༢༣ བར་བུམ་སྟོང་ནུབ། བོད་ཟླ་༡ ཕྱི་མའི་ཚེས་༢༣ ནས་ཟླ་༢ ཚེས་༢༣ བར་སྟོང་བྱང་དུ་གནས་པས་ཕྱོགས་དེར་འཛེམས། གནམ་ལོའི་ཕྱོགས་གཙང་ལྷོ་སྨད་དང་། །ནུབ་སྟོད་ཕྱོགས་སུ་སོ་ནམ་བྱེད་པ་སོགས་བྱ་བ་ཆེ་ཕྲའི་ལས་སྣེ་གཏོད་པར་ཤིས། །༧ཐོག་མར་བོད་ཟླ་༡༢ ཚེས་༢༩ ཉི་དྲོས་སྔ་ཆ། … ཕྱོགས་གཙང་ལ་ཁ་གཏད་དེ་ས་ལུད་ཁ་དབྱེ་བ་དང་། རྫིང་ཡུར་འདྲུ་བ། ཆུ་འགོ་འཛུག་པ་སོགས་བྱ།

Img. 56, last line, to img. 57, l. 4 (pp. 52–53), read by eye on the scan (1737 px, enlarged 1.6×) against Yigdzin-1; the elided words name the recitations that open the work. The print writes «སྟོང་» for the third *bum stong*.

The almanac's year section on farming: from the 12th month's 22nd to the 1st month's 22nd the empty vase (བུམ་སྟོང, bum stong) is in the south; to the later 1st month's 23rd in the west; to the 2nd month's 23rd in the north, "and as it stays there, that direction is avoided". The clean directions of the sky year (གནམ་ལོའི་ཕྱོགས་གཙང, gnam lo'i phyogs gtsang) are the lower south and the upper west: there it is auspicious to begin works great and small, farming and the like. Then a date and a part of the day: first, on the 12th month's 29th, in the early part of the morning warmth (and a second time named), after the recitations, the opening of the earth and manure (ས་ལུད, sa lud), the digging of ponds and channels and the starting of the water are done *facing the clean direction*.

What it shows for D4: the almanac gives the direction to avoid and the clean direction as a year's (and a span of dates') rule of place, and composes it with a chosen date and hour by doing the work on the chosen day facing the clean direction. It does not weigh the direction against the day's good or bad; the two are met together.

## <a id="mta-p84-100"></a>mta:p84-100 The pages of months 3 and 4: the great occasions in the month's head, no buddha or practice in the day cells

*Read: scan, 2026-11-16.*

> ཚེས་༡༥ལ་གཉིས་མེད་རྒྱུད་རྒྱལ་དུས་འཁོར་དཔལ་ལྡན་འབྲས་སྤུངས་སུ་གསུངས་པའི་དུས་ཆེན། … ཚེས་༧ལ་ཐུབ་དབང་སྐུ་བལྟམས་པ། ཚེས་༡༥ལ་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་པ་དང་། མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་དུས་ཆེན།

The pages of the third and the fourth Tibetan month (the almanac's first two months: its day pages begin with the third), read by eye on the scan (1737 px): the head of month 3 on p. 84 (img. 88), its days on pp. 88–92 (img. 92–96); the head of month 4 on pp. 94–95 (img. 98–99), its days 7 to 18 on pp. 99–100 (img. 103–104). Only the two festival entries of the heads are quoted, the first from p. 84, the second from pp. 94–95; they follow the formulas of the White Beryl's model almanac closely (wb.1:p160b, wb.1:p162b; the White Beryl's fourth month has «བདུན་ལ་ཐུབ་དབང་བལྟམས་པ་དང་། བཅོ་ལྔ་ལ་སངས་རྒྱས་པ་དང་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་དུས་སྟོན»). The rest is stated as facts: the almanac is under copyright.

*The month's head* is a verse on the season, then figures of the month's reckoning (among them the lengths of day and night), the span of the empty vase (བུམ་སྟོང, bum stong) from the month's 25th, and the great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen): in month 3 the fifteenth, when the Kālacakra was taught at 'Bras spungs; in month 4 the seventh, the birth of the Lord of Sages, and the fifteenth, his full awakening and his passing beyond sorrow. After them the head gives weather and omens for spans of dates. No tenth, no twenty-fifth, no eighth and no new moon is named in either head, and no practice, vow or offering is attached to the occasions it names.

*The day cells*, six to a page, each give the Tibetan date and weekday, the mansion, the pair of elements with its name, the yoga and the karaṇa, the hours at which they change, the day's animal, trigram and number, the spirits that move that day, a weather omen for some days, and three rows of figures. The cells of the 8th, 10th, 15th, 25th, 29th and 30th of month 3 and of the 7th, 8th, 10th and 15th of month 4 were read for a buddha, a deity, a vow or a practice: none is printed in any of them, not on the full moon of month 4 either, whose occasions stand only in the head.
