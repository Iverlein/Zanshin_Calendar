The answer to the hundred and sixty-second (vol. 2, I5876 img. 20, l. 5, to img. 21, l. 3): Yigdzin-1's reading, not checked on the scan (a running hand); BDRC's etext (pp. 20–21) agrees in the words quoted where it is legible. Elisions (…) are where the machine reading is not clear enough to quote.

The question restated: the fixed earth lords, and above all the Great Earth-Holding Hand, can never be avoided, so is there no need to set them down here? The answer: the chief purpose is as above [reply 161]. The Earth-Holding Hand rests its body on Jambudvīpa and holds power over the ground, so its body's seat cannot be divided as that of the earth lord *lto 'phye* (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ, lto 'phye) can; if a corpse, a wedding or violent works are done, its danger is not withstood and no especially great screen or remedy arises. "So these are fiercer than the ordinary earth lords, and even when [a work] does not meet the kings and ministers and the rest told in the reckoning texts, the reasons why grave works and the like need the remedies that clear the work's way are that they meet these." "For example, in a work such as a corpse or a bride, even when, as the reckoning texts tell, [it] is not touched by the least fault from all the [moving] lords and the rest, one knows why the general remedy, the rites against disturbance and the like are done, and knows them as precious."

What it shows: the fixed earth lords are a reason for a general remedy that holds whatever the day and whatever the moving lords. The regent sets them beside the moving lords, not against the day's factors.

The answer to the hundred and eighty-seventh (vol. 2, I5876 img. 49, l. 2, to l. 3; Taikhang margin number 577): read on the scan (8208 px, a running hand) in four pieces at 0.75× against Yigdzin-1, which it confirms word for word.

"The hundred and eighty-seventh: in the mansions' results, where the grouping and the particular results disagree, which is chief? The answer: the grouping is that rough scheme of Rgya lo's translation; mostly it does not disagree with the particular results either, and where in some places it disagrees, the particular result, being the place where [the result] is told in detail, the particular result itself is to be known as the chief (སྒེར་འབྲས་ཉིད་གཙོ་ཆེ་བ, sger 'bras nyid gtso che ba). There is no cause for doubt."

What it shows: the regent states for the mansions what WB's weighing (p. 376) states for the factors: the particular, told in detail, leads over the general grouping. He does not extend it to the earth lords; reply 188 (img. 49–50) goes on to the mansions' rise and decline, whose weaker way he calls without the strength of a pure source («ལུང་ཁུངས་རྣམ་དག་གི་ནུས་སྟོབས་མེད»), a grade by source again, not a rank among factors.

The answer to the hundred and ninetieth (vol. 2, I5876 img. 51, l. 3, on to img. 52): Yigdzin-1's reading, not checked on the scan.

On ch. 34's particular results: that it is hard to reckon them all in detail and to take up and give up accordingly is so; "but one who has trained skilfully in every way of reckoning and has it by heart knows what to take up and give up among them, and may practise the remedies, full or brief, as can be done"; and though every work falls under the four kinds of rite, small works such as plastering the house or sweeping the smoke are told by their own nature (plastering, an earth work, by Saturn's day), "and one must hit the like by the arrow of reasoning". Nothing in the reply sets an earth lord against the day.
