Page 268, lines 4–7, image 268 of I1344 (image = printed page; lines 1–3 hold the colophon of the preceding work, Vāgīśvarakīrti's *Deceiving Death*, "chapter four", which the etext also prints once more after this passage's first verses, see the note).

Title: in Sanskrit *Svapnohana* (the etext's «སྭབྣཱ་ཨུ་ཧཱ་ན»); in Tibetan the examination of dreams (རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ, rmi lam brtag pa). Homage to the Buddha.

"The examination of the many kinds of dream I shall tell. A dream in the first watch (ཐུན, thun) of the night gives its fruit within a year; in the second watch, within eight months; in the third watch, within three months; in the fourth watch, within half a month: it will be seen, without doubt. At the very time dawn breaks (སྐྱ་རེངས, skya rengs) the fruit comes within ten days (ཞག, zhag). After this I shall explain the images; listen, virtue and non-virtue." Then the first images: riding an ox, riding an elephant; a mansion, a mountain peak, a forest tree; sitting in a boat and holding a lute; eating food and ploughing: always goods are gained. Being struck with weapons, the body covered with small living creatures, the body's dirt washed away, following excrement, blood flowing from the body: in dreams, when it is not a road taken, these are auspicious. Then the next verse begins: a king, an elephant, an excellent horse, a traveller (འགྲོན་བུ, 'gron bu), oxen and cows, seen "at the end of sleep" (གཉིད་ཀྱི་མཐའ་མ, gnyid kyi mtha' ma), continued on page 269.

Continuing: the one who sees a king, an elephant, an excellent horse, a traveller, oxen and cows at the end of sleep (གཉིད་ཀྱི་མཐའ་མ, gnyid kyi mtha' ma): his kinsfolk become many. Climbing alone a tree with a milky fruit and, sitting on it, waking: wealth is soon obtained. Eating seated in a mansion; one who crosses the ocean: even born into a low family, he will become a king. Eating curd and milk porridge in a pond, inside a great lotus petal: lordship of land, no doubt. A lamp, a banner, a chariot, a parasol, a girl, leaves, fruit and cooked food, seen at the end of sleep: the glory with nothing above. Eating human flesh, cooked or raw, "listen to its merit": the foot eaten, five hundred; the hand, a thousand; the head, a hundred thousand or a kingdom. One's entrails winding a village or a city: a village, a petty king; a city, a king. Obtaining a pair of shoes, a parasol and a sharp clean sword and then waking: a journey on the road (ལམ་དུ་བགྲོད, lam du bgrod). One who crosses in a boat, even a broken one: the journey will surely end with a quick return.

Bad: one on whom the ground shakes greatly in the dream, his goods will be lost, or illness comes in the body. Horned beasts, fanged beasts, a monkey or a pig appearing: fear from the lord. Smeared with sesame oil, milk or butter, or anything like these: declared illness. A cart drawn by a donkey and a she-camel: one who climbs it alone and wakes seated on it, quickly gets death. A woman holding red clothes… (the sentence goes on in page 270).

The Svapnohana in two other prints of the Tengyur (Peking, Narthang) and in the Dpe bsdur ma etext, for the five places the Derge scan's 8-pixel letters would not settle. Peking (BDRC W1KG13126, vol. 28, printed on red; image group I1KG13197, img. 315–318, letters about 40 pixels high), read by Yigdzin-1 and each of the five places then by eye on enlargements: "(dreams) are not a road taken: auspicious" (བགྲོད་མིན, bgrod min), "traveller (འགྲོན་བུ, 'gron bu), ox and cow", "within ten (བཅུ་ཡི་བར, bcu yi bar)", "a horse, la mo (ལ་མོ), a pot-belly (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ)", "the Gāyatrī (ག་ཡ་ཏྲི) recited a thousand times". Narthang (W2KG5015, vol. 28 pp. 282–285, I2KG212312 img. 284–287, a hand-cut print with letters about 30 pixels high): the same «བཅུ་ཡི་བར» and «རྟ་ལ་མོ» read by eye on enlargements; the rest by Yigdzin-1, which reads the other three places as Peking does (the Gāyatrī's third syllable by eye as ཏྲི, less certain). The Dpe bsdur ma etext (W1PD95844 vol. 14 pp. 1364–1368, the Derge text collated with the Peking, Narthang and Co ne prints; its scans are restricted, the etext is OCR with the printed variant notes) has the same text as the Derge etext in all five places and a note that Peking and Narthang read «ལ» where it has «བླ».

### Open (RD228)

- Question: In the Peking and Narthang prints «རྟ་ལ་མོ» (without the subjoined la) stands where the Derge text has «རྟ་བླ་མོ», in "a horse, ___, a pot-belly, a water creature seized"; which animal or thing is the «ལ་མོ» (la mo) of the Svapnohana, and is «བླ་མོ» a mistake for it?
- Question: In the Lama Gongdü's list of goods of gossip (མི་ཁའི་རྫས, mi kha'i rdzas) the five prints give «རམ་རུག» in three places and then «སྤང་གཡེང», «སྤང་གཡེར», «སྦང་གཡེར», «སྦང་མ», «སྦར་ཁྱེར»; what are the goods called ram rug and, after it, spang g.yeng / sbang g.yer / sbang ma?
