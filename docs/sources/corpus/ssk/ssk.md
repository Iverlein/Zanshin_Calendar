[p351] The practitioner of mantra, in the discipline of the rite, takes refuge, confesses wrongs, gives rise to the thought of awakening and makes aspirations at each of the three times; does what the rite prescribes as far as he can; is steady in giving, free from weariness, compassionate, patient, loving and diligent; meditates constantly on the six recollections; hears the Dharma in its kinds and acts with devotion; reads the rites of the mantras closely and honours the mantras and seals; draws the mandala as the rite requires; holds the right view with a firm thought of awakening; keeps the four assemblies, monks and the rest; and, the pledge and seal made fast, teaches the tantras of his own mantra and spreads out all the scriptural mantras fully.

«The practitioner is to make offerings in particular on the eighth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth, in the half-month of miracles. Recitation, fire offering and offering, and likewise the taking of vows, and in particular the making of merit he is to take up with reverence and effort.»

Then the wise one is to consecrate with this mantra a hundred or a thousand times and so always hold the vajra of action (mantra: oṃ dhuna vajra ha). The vajra is to be made three-pronged of a tree struck by lightning, or of a nimba, a corpse-ground log or sandalwood; it is called "the one that accomplishes well"; the wise holds it in the left hand for recitation, fire offering and the other rites; held thus it makes all obstructers and misleaders and all who harm shake with terror and cling to it. [p413] Then, in addition: the wise one is to begin the rite for the accomplishment at a time that is good and has the right qualities, as the rite says. The great accomplishment is to be begun in the small winter month, the middle autumn month, the first and middle spring months, and the first summer month and the full moon. In the first summer month the rain falls constantly; the middle spring month has a circle of wind; in the first spring month obstructers of every kind appear; the first winter month has no obstructers; in the middle autumn month lightning flashes and great sounds come, and the obstructers are signs of success; so these months are to be examined. In these five months the best accomplishment is achieved, and the pacifying rites too are well begun in them.

In the seasons of the waning half-month of the five (months), what has been proclaimed there is good for accomplishing the middling and the lowest accomplishments. When the moon is seized by a planet (an eclipse), the best of materials is to be accomplished; when the sun is seized, the wise one is to accomplish the rites too. On the fifth, seventh, first, third and thirteenth days of the lunar fortnight all rites are auspicious; a lunar day joined with a mansion is to be known as the best; for the common run in particular the king of the mansions is auspicious for all; planets and the brief work and the fierce are for fierce rites. Whether taught in one's own rite or urged by the gods, all rites are auspicious in the half-month of miracles. The day on which a command was given, or the thirteenth day, will also do. Pacifying is for the most part in the autumn fortnight, increasing in the winter fortnight, fierce rites in the late spring as the rite has it; for accomplishments these are auspicious. In early spring all rites are done; in the summer fortnight rites are done.

[p414] Autumn, winter and late spring are the middling, the best and the lowest; each of the three is divided again into three. The accomplishments of the three times are, for the most part, taught as of three kinds: the first is from the evening, the middle at midnight, the best time is the dawn, when the accomplishments come in that order; the times of pacifying, fierce and increasing rites are declared to be these. For the signs of the lowest, the middling and the best accomplishments one is to know the three kinds as one sees them in their order. For the sun and the moon seized (eclipses) no time is fixed; for fierce practice in particular the time of the seizure is good. Of the best and the other grades, a fast of three days, of two days, or even of one day is declared right. This is the section on the time of accomplishment.
