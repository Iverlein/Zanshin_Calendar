## The Dunhuang Tibetan dream texts (RD366)

Read 2026-11-10 from published descriptions only: no edition of the Tibetan was
open, so no Tibetan passage is quoted and no passage id is written. The one
manuscript found is below; the images were looked at, not read.

### Pelliot tibétain 55, part IX (also numbered part 4 by Chen Jian)

*Read:* not read. Facts from the open abstracts and a footnote, in our own words;
the manuscript's images (13 views, 1024 px at most, IDP record
idp.bl.uk/collection/8F997A5E41D90F41AD24A4C68ACDE288, Bibliothèque nationale
de France, "Dunhuang Mogao") were looked at on 2026-11-10 and the cursive
(དབུ་མེད, dbu med) could not be transcribed with certainty at that size, so
nothing of it is set down as read.

- **What it is.** A scroll of several divination texts. A footnote in van Schaik's
  study of Or.8210/S.6878 (*Revue d'Études Tibétaines* 64, "Travel, Love and Lost
  Things") says that PT 55 carries a circular diagram in twelve parts, based on
  the twelve links of dependent origination, with a long text above it that looks
  like a divination by the day of the month (beginning lost), and immediately
  below the diagram, apparently unrelated to it, a text on magical practice for
  good and bad dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam); transliteration and translation in
  Crescenzi and Torricelli 1995.
- **Editions and studies (facts only, none read in full).** Antonella Crescenzi and
  Fabrizio Torricelli, "A Tun-huang Text on Dreams: Ms Pelliot tibétain 55 – IX",
  *The Tibet Journal* 20 no. 2 (1995), pp. 3–17 (transcription, notes, literal
  translation, commentary; JSTOR 43300529, closed). Chen Jian 陳踐, "法藏敦煌藏文文献
  P.T.55(4)号梦兆禳解译释", *西藏民族大学学报* 38 no. 1 (2017), pp. 67–70
  (title analysis, Tibetan in Latin letters, Chinese translation, notes; abstract
  open on CNKI). Zheng Binglin 鄭炳林 and Chen Yuzhu 陳于柱, "敦煌古藏文 P.T.55
  《解梦书》研究", *兰州学刊* 2009 no. 5 (abstract only).
- **Extent and date.** Chen's abstract: 19 lines, copied in 979 or soon after (the
  year 太平興國四年 in the abstract; the basis of the date is not given there and
  was not checked).
- **Sorting of the dreams.** Chen's abstract: the text has two parts, good dreams
  and bad dreams; for bad dreams it gives three ways of removing the harm: burning
  seven small wooden sticks after passing them three times round the head,
  dissolving three clods of earth in water, and scattering the ashes of burnt
  mugwort. So the images are not catalogued; the text is a remedy text, with no
  list of dream images and their fruits like the Svapnohana's.
- **Time of the dream.** None stated in any abstract read: no night watch, month
  or day is reported for the dream. Chen's and the Zheng–Chen abstracts say
  nothing of one; this is a silence of the abstracts, not proof that the
  manuscript has none (Open, below).
- **Against the pool.** The Svapnohana (SVO) ties the dream to the watch of the
  night and gives the fruit's term; WB ch. 30 reads dreams as signs at the
  asking, never by date; the Lama Gongdü text is a catalogue by good and bad
  signs. PT 55 differs from all three in being a ritual remedy with three
  procedures; only the good and bad division is shared.
- **Doubt kept.** Zheng and Chen's abstract says the text was adapted by a
  Dunhuang monk from a Bon dream book. No Bon text is a source here and the
  claim is the authors' inference; it is recorded as theirs, not as a fact of the
  manuscript.

### Other Dunhuang Tibetan manuscripts

- No Tibetan translation of the Chinese Duke of Zhou dream book (周公解夢書) was
  found; the Chinese dream books of Dunhuang are well attested and several
  copies are recorded in the Chinese, none with a Tibetan version found.
- Wang Yao's list of Tibetan divination manuscripts of the Dunhuang selections
  (via a search result, not opened): P.T. 55, 129, 351, 1043, 1046, 1047, 1051,
  1055. Only P.T. 55 is reported to contain dreams; the others are not
  reported as dream texts, and none was opened.
