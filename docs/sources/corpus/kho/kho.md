## <a id="kho-p48"></a>kho:p48 Kohlbrugge on the meteors: planets, nakṣatras, lunar days and the hour of the day

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> (German, summarised; no wording quoted)

Kohlbrugge's thesis (Utrecht 1938) is on the omens of the air and the earth in the Atharva-Veda *Pariśiṣṭas* 57–71 (earthquakes, meteors, lightning, whirlwinds, the twilights, halos, the wonders), compared with the *Bṛhatsaṃhitā*, the Purāṇas, the *Adbhutasāgara* and others. It does not treat *Pariśiṣṭa* 68, the dreams, which she leaves to Negelein (neg); the whole text layer holds no dream chapter and no dream rule (a search for *Traum* finds only her citations of Negelein). What bears on our question is how the same literature ties other omens to time. Page numbers are the layer's running numerals.

**Meteors** (ch. III, *Pariśiṣṭa* 58b, p. 48): the planets, the nakṣatras and the lunar days (*Monatstage*) must be observed, and they decide which classes are struck (*Bṛhatsaṃhitā*, *Vāṭakaṇikā*, *Nāradasaṃhitā*, *Mayūracitra*, Gārgīya, Parāśara); the time of day counts too (a meteor at midday is the end of kingdom, king and queen); one in the morning twilight touches the king in the city, in the evening twilight those on the road.

## <a id="kho-p58-61"></a>kho:p58-61 Kohlbrugge on the whirlwinds: watch of the night, 1st, 8th and 14th lunar days, nakṣatra

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> (German, summarised; no wording quoted)

Kohlbrugge, ch. V, the whirlwinds (*Pariśiṣṭa* 60), pp. 58–61 (the passage on time on pp. 59–60), in our words: a whirlwind's fruit depends, by Parāśara, on the time, the lunar days and the nakṣatra, and reaches as far as the sound.

- By the part of the day and night: Varāhamihira and Garga give the classes struck by the watches (first watch of the night the crops, the second the *piśācas* or the *rākṣasas*, the third horses and elephants or the vaiśyas, the fourth the travelling kings or the śūdras and bandits); Parāśara gives the morning, noon, afternoon, evening, midnight and the two twilights.
- By the lunar day: Parāśara says a whirlwind on the first, eighth and fourteenth day has little effect; by the nakṣatra it affects the rain.
- Her argument that the *nirghāta* is a wind and not a thunderbolt rests on the same fact: it is observed by direction, time, lunar day and nakṣatra, as a body would not be (see adb:p307-309 for the same Parāśara passage in the *Adbhutasāgara*).

## <a id="kho-p72-74"></a>kho:p72-74 Kohlbrugge on the halo of five bodies: the harm by the day of the half-month

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> (German, summarised; no wording quoted)

Kohlbrugge, ch. VI, the halos (*Pariśiṣṭa* 63), pp. 68–77, the list on pp. 72–74: a halo round five heavenly bodies together does harm by the day of the half-month, in our words: on the first days in order the four classes (brahman, kṣatriya, vaiśya, śūdra); on the fifth and the two after, the towns and the treasury (the *Bṛhatsaṃhitā*: the guilds; the *Nāradasaṃhitā*: the king from the fifth to the seventh); on the eighth the crown prince and the generals; on the ninth, tenth and eleventh the princes; on the twelfth the town is besieged; on the thirteenth there is mutiny; on the fourteenth the queen is afflicted; on the fifteenth the king. Kern, she notes, understands "month" for "half-month". The *Bṛhatsaṃhitā*, Parāśara and Garga have the same list with variants.

This is a tie of an omen to the lunar day of the fortnight (not to a dream), the clearest read in RD265.

## <a id="kho-p140-141"></a>kho:p140-141 Kohlbrugge on the omens that suit the seasons

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> (German, summarised; no wording quoted)

Kohlbrugge, ch. IX, "the omens that suit the seasons" (*Pariśiṣṭa* 64.8–10), pp. 140–141, in our words: wonders of nature are lucky when they come in their own season (a glowing sky in the two spring months, fog and high sunrises in winter) and terrible when they come in the wrong one. The *Bṛhatsaṃhitā*, the *Matsya* and the *Viṣṇudharmottara* give the same list (Varāhamihira at the end of his chapters on omens, the Purāṇas at the start). This is the rule of all the natural omens; Negelein states the same (neg:p17-19, the comet).
