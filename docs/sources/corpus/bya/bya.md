## <a id="bya-p41-46"></a>bya:p41-46 Bṛhadyātrā ch. 16, the dreams: the humours, the parts of the night and three seasons (v. 10); the watches and the spans (v. 30)

Varāhamihira's *Bṛhadyātrā* (the *Aśvamedhīyayātrā*), ch. 16, *Svapnādhyāya*, 32 verses, pp. 41–46 of Pingree's edition (Government Oriental Manuscripts Library Bulletin, Madras 1972; Internet Archive item BrhadyatraOfVarahamihira, two print pages to an image: pp. 42–43 are image n28, pp. 44–45 n29, pp. 46–47 n30, pp. 40–41 n27; the page heads read 41–46 and the colophon "iti Bṛhadyātrāyāṃ Svapnādhyāyaḥ ṣoḍaśaḥ ‖ १६ ‖" stands on p. 46 under v. 32). Read on the page images by RD269 (clear print, Devanagari with the Latin apparatus under it). The verses quoted are as the page prints them; in v. 10 the editor prints "prāvṛc-charan-mādhava-saṃjñite" with the note 10b "prāvṛḍ-śara° Viṣṇuśarman, prāvicaraṃ M" and in 10d "sādhāraṇaḥ syāt phalasannipātaḥ" against "°ṇe syāt khalu sannipāte M, °ṇaṃ syāt phalasannipātaḥ Viṣṇuśarman" (M is the manuscript of the edition, Viṣṇuśarman a commentator whose citation of vv. 4–32 the edition's testimonia note).

The chapter in outline, in our words: verses 1–7, a rite for asking a dream (the king, having recited "yaj jāgrato dūram upaiti daivam" (v. 4: *āvartya mantrān*), a light single meal and lying on the right side, with a prayer to Śiva, vv. 5–6, in one garment on a bed of *kuśa* grass, calm of mind, sees at the end of the night (*niśānte*, v. 7) a good or a bad dream); 8–12, what makes a dream (v. 8: for him in whom wind is aroused, climbing mountains and flying through the sky; v. 9: for the bilious gold, jewels, garlands, the sun, fire; for the phlegmatic the moon, white flowers, rivers, lakes, the sea; v. 10 the times; v. 11 what is said of the planets' periods (*daśā*), of worry and of a thing seen, then the overcoming by a horrible being, magic, an obstacle and a spirit (*guhyaka*) as causes; v. 12 dreams of habit, of worry, of a planet's fault, of things seen and of past deeds are fruitless); 13–29, the images, good and bad (white and bright things good, red things, falling, black things bad); 30–32, the time of the fruit and the remedy.

**Verse 10** ties the dream's kind to the part of the night and to the season, in three lists of three that run in one order: *jaghanya-madhya-prathame niśāṃśe* (the last, the middle and the first part of the night), *prāvṛṭ-śarad-mādhava* (the rains, autumn and Mādhava, the spring month), and *marut-pitta-kapha* (wind, bile, phlegm); the dream's fruit is then "common" (*sādhāraṇa*), a mixture (*phala-sannipāta*) by the disturbance of the three humours. The verse does not say in words which part goes with which season or humour; the three lists keep one order (last–rains–wind, middle–autumn–bile, first–spring–phlegm if paired by position, which the verse leaves to the reader). Verses 8 and 9 give the images of the windy, the bilious and the phlegmatic.

**Verse 30** gives the spans by the watch of the night: the first watch, a year (*varṣāt*); the second, half a year (*vatsarārdhāt*); the third, a quarter of a year, "a fourth part of the year" (*varṣa-pādāt*: three months); the last part of the night (*śarvarī-paścimāṃśa*), a month; at the cows' release (*go-visarga*), at once. **Verse 31**: after a good dream one does not sleep on (the later dream decides: "paścād dṛṣṭo yaḥ sa pākaṃ vidhatte"); a bad one is told to the good and learned, who bless the king with blessings. **Verse 32**: the remedies (no more sleep, not telling the dream, the Ganges bath, recitation, appeasing rites, blessing rites, serving the cows and the *aśvattha* at dawn, worship of brahmans with sesame, food, drink and flowers according to one's strength, reciting the Mahābhārata).

No verse ties the dream to a lunar day, a weekday, a nakṣatra or a month; the seasons of verse 10 are the only tie, and they are of the dream's kind, not of its fruit.

Doubt: v. 3 (p. 40) and the rest of the apparatus not read; the pairing of the three lists of v. 10 is the order, not a statement of the verse.
