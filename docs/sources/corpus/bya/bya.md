## <a id="bya-p41-46"></a>bya:p41-46 Bṛhadyātrā ch. 16, the dreams: the humours, the parts of the night and three seasons (v. 10); the watches and the spans (v. 30)

Varāhamihira's *Bṛhadyātrā* (the *Aśvamedhīyayātrā*), ch. 16, *Svapnādhyāya*, 32 verses, pp. 41–46 of Pingree's edition (the colophon reads "iti Bṛhadyātrāyāṃ Svapnādhyāyaḥ ṣoḍaśaḥ"). The Devanagari text layer is Tesseract output with the Latin apparatus mixed in; verse 30 appears twice in it (once in the apparatus) and reads the same; its words are restored here by the three Devanagari copies of the *Adbhutasāgara*, which quote the verse (adb:p513-514) as the work of Varāha. The lines above are the text as read, with the OCR's slips mended (a doubt is marked); verse 31 is given from the *Adbhutasāgara*, the layer of the Pingree edition being garbled there.

The chapter in outline, in our words: verses 1–7, a rite for asking a dream (the king, in one garment on a bed of *kuśa* grass, calm of mind, recites the verse "yaj jāgrato dūram upaiti daivam" and a prayer to Śiva, and sees at the end of the night a good or a bad dream); 8–12, what makes a dream (disorder of the humours, the ripening of the planets, worry, a thing seen or done, magic, a being; dreams from habit, worry, a planet's fault, a thing seen and the past are fruitless); 13–29, the images, good and bad (white and bright things good, red things, falling, black things bad; death-omens of the dream); 30–32, the time of the fruit and the remedy.

**Verse 10** ties the dream's kind to the part of the night and to the season: in the last, the middle and the first part of the night, and in the seasons called Prāvṛṣ (the rains), Śarad (autumn) and Mādhava (spring), by the disorder of wind, bile and phlegm, the fruit of the dream is "common" (*sādhāraṇa*, mixed). The OCR does not settle which part of the night or which season goes with which humour; the order of the words (wind, bile, phlegm; rains, autumn, spring) suggests the pairs wind–rains, bile–autumn, phlegm–spring. Verses 8–9 give the images of the bilious (gold, jewels, garlands, the sun, fire) and the phlegmatic (the moon, white flowers, rivers, lakes, the sea).

**Verse 30** gives the spans by the watch of the night: the first watch, a year; the second, half a year; the third, a quarter of a year (three months); the last part of the night (*śarvarī-paścimāṃśa*), a month; at the cows' release (*go-visarga*), at once. **Verse 31**: after a good dream one does not sleep on (the later dream decides: "paścād dṛṣṭo yaḥ sa pākaṃ vidhatte"); a bad dream is told to the learned and good, who bless the king. **Verse 32**: the remedies (not telling the dream, sleeping again, the Ganges bath, recitation, appeasing rites, blessing rites, serving the *aśvattha* and the cows, gifts of sesame, gold and flowers, reciting the Mahābhārata).

No verse ties the dream to a lunar day, a weekday, a nakṣatra or a month; the seasons of verse 10 are the only tie, and they are of the dream's kind, not of its fruit.

Doubt: the readings are of OCR; the ordering of the pairs in verse 10 and the "pādāt" of verse 30 are as printed there, not read on the page image (leaves 27–30 of the 73 two-page leaves).
