## <a id="sn-p70-71"></a>sn:p70-71 The household round: divided by four, and two charts

*Read: scan, 2026-10-25.*

> དེ་ནས་རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར་གྱིས། །བཟའ་མི་བྱིངས་ཀྱི་དགེ་མིན་བརྟག །བཞི་ལས་ཉུང་ལ་བྱ་མི་སྟུབ། །བཞི་ཡན་ཐོག་མར་ཕོ་རྒན་རིམ། །དེ་ནས་མོ་ཡི་རྒན་རིམ་བཞིན། །རང་རང་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་རླུང་རྣམས། །གཅིག་བསྡོམས་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་བཞི་དང་། །བབ་སྨེ་༼བཞི་[p. 71, img. 099]ལྗང་ལྔ་སེར་སོགས་རང་རང་མདོག༽གང་བབས་རྡེལ་གཅིག་དང་། །ལྔ་པོ་བཞིས་བགོས་གསུམ་ལྷག་ན། །གཅིག་ནི་ཡང་ཁུག་ལྷག་ཏུ་བཟུང་། །གཉིས་དང་གཅིག་ལྷག་ཡང་ཁུག་མེད། །དེ་ནས་རེའུ་མིག་ཨངྐ་ཡི། །གྲངས་བཞིན་བླངས་ལ་གྲངས་བཞིན་འགྲེམ། …

The household round of the Bon *Mirror* (SN, printed pp. 70–71, img. 098–099; etext pp. 98–99), read on BDRC's scan (3504 px) at a third of full size, against the etext, which agrees but for the number words of the charts. A witness only.

The verse: the Chinese pebble round examines the good and bad of the household members as a whole; it cannot be done for fewer than four; for four and more, the men first by age, then the women by age; each member's life force, body, power and luck gathered into four verdict pebbles (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar), and one pebble of the mewa of the year of age in its own colour ("four green, five yellow and so on", a note in the line); "the five divided by four, with three left, one is taken as the refold's leftover; with two or one left there is no refold"; then the pebbles are taken by the numbers of the chart and laid by the numbers.

The print lacks a line that the copy of the same text in the *Gyalrong collection* (རྒྱལ་རོང་དམངས་ཁྲོད་ཀྱི་རྩིས་ཡིག་ཕྱོགས་བསྒྲིགས, rgyal rong dmangs khrod kyi rtsis yig phyogs bsgrigs; MW1KG16672, etext p. 79, not read on the scan) has after "three left": «གཉིས་ནི་ཁུག་པའི་རྡེལ་ལྷག་དང་།», "two are the fold's leftover". BDRC's full-text search shows the same verse, with that line, in the *Good Vase* (བུམ་བཟང, bum bzang) of Situ Wön Karma Ngelek Tendzin (སི་ཏུ་དབོན་ཀརྨ་ངེས་ལེགས་བསྟན་འཛིན, si tu dbon karma nges legs bstan 'dzin; MW30023_79E1C5, search only, read in its snippets): «…བཞིས་བགོས་གསུམ་ལྷག་ན། །གཉིས་ནི་ཁུག་པའི་རྡེལ་ལྷག་དང་། །གཅིག་ནི་ཡང་ཁུག་ལྷག་ཏུ་བཟུང་། །གཉིས་དང་གཅིག་ལྷག་ཡང་ཁུག་མེད།» in the search's transliteration. This is the rule the notes reject by name (WB vol. 2, p. 491: the *Good Vase* and others have the fold's and refold's leftovers mixed up, and "with two or one left there is no refold" is coarse).

The laying is of three members, which the verse forbids, and the fold is of twenty-three pebbles, which no number of members gives: both are diagrams. The laying takes the columns by the zigzag (1 the first member's life force, 2 the last one's mewa, and so on), the middle column from both its ends (11, 12, 13, 14, 15). The fold is the count of 23 divided by four: four runs of five laid in turn, the first forward on top, the second reversed at the bottom, the two after it (11, 12) the fold's leftover, the third run forward in the house's row, the one after it (18) the refold's leftover, the last run reversed in the people's row: three left, two the fold's and one the refold's, as the *Good Vase*'s line has it.
