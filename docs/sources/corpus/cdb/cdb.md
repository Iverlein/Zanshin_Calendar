Yongle dadian (永樂大典), the two surviving rhyme-volumes 20181 and 20182 (the character 日, "day"), in the Zhonghua facsimile as transcribed on Chinese Wikisource (zh.wikisource.org, 永樂大典/卷20181, /卷20182, from the Commons PDFs 永樂大典20181.pdf and 永樂大典20182.pdf); the sheet numbers 九三三七 f. appear on the page images. The entry 日 gathers, for each day of the sexagenary cycle, the sayings of several selection-of-days books one after the other: here the days from 甲辰 (day 41) to 辛酉 (day 58) survive. One of them, 雷英發編『萬曆錦囊拾遺備用』 (the title as printed), gives under each day a line "(the months) 占夢 (a short omen) 牛黄 ... 不將 ... (the months) 占恠": for each day, a list of months in which a dream seen ("占夢", divining by a dream) on that day has the omen named (孝服 mourning dress; 行人凶 a traveller in danger; 竈不安 the stove not at peace; 天火 fire from heaven; 忌行人 a traveller to be avoided; 十人吉 good for ten people; 主喜 gladness; 大吉 great good; 酒食 wine and food; 口舌 quarrels; 信至 a letter comes; 半吉 half good), then the months in which "占恠" (divining a strange sign, a portent) is done. Another, 呂茂明編『陰陽備用選擇成書』, puts a short "夢" omen after each day's birth-star data (second paragraph). In both the dream is an omen of the day, not a rule of silence: nothing in the two volumes says that a dream is not told. The month lists are 8 or 9 months long (5 or 6 on three days), so they do not read as Edo's three branches a month. The strings were read from the Wikisource transcription; I checked the layout and two clauses (甲辰, 辛亥) on the page images (20181 sheet 5, 20182 sheet 5), the other sixteen not checked on the images; the lists' numerals 十十一, 十一十二, 十一一 (likely 10 and 11, 11 and 12, 11 and 1?) are as printed in the transcription and are not certain.

Pelliot chinois 3908 (BnF), the booklet 新集周公解夢書 (Xinji Zhou gong jie meng shu), images 13-16 of the e-Dunhuang IIIF set (the page spreads, 3300-3400 px wide, read by eye at the full size and at crops), chapters 19 to 23, the last five of the book. Chapter 19, "the dream got on the days of the twelve branches": a dream seen on a rat day means loss and quarrels in the eastern house; on an ox day wealth coming into the house and gladness; tiger, wine and a journey (and quarrels); hare, a guest from outside, avoid official business; dragon, wine and meat, outside wealth; snake, sickness of a child in the eastern house; horse, one arriving from a journey, a birth is good; goat, wine and meat, joy; monkey, official business and quarrels arise; rooster, wine and food; dog, a guest from afar, outside wealth; boar, officials, sickness. Chapter 20, "the dream got in the twelve double-hours" (rat: great good; ox: wealth; tiger: wine and meat; hare: rain; dragon: quarrels; snake: weeping; horse: loss of wealth; goat: a little sickness; monkey: lawsuits; rooster: a guest; dog: a message from afar; boar: official business). Chapter 21 (numbered 廿一 on the page), "the dream got on the days of the twelve officers (建除, build, remove, full, level, settle, hold, break, danger, complete, receive, open, close)": each with a short result. Chapter 22, headed "(the) bad dreams ... taboos and the like" (the second glyph doubtful), lists twenty things that bring bad dreams: a bed whose foot stands on a temple brick, a bed facing the roof beam, a bed of wood from a sacred tree, a bed laid facing north, bones under the bed, white chickens and dogs kept in the house, a well and a stove facing each other, a peach tree by a well, dead servants, old household things not burnt, a mortar set on a shrine, eating without the blessing, killing a turtle or snake for no reason, a knife put on the stove, sitting on a well-curb, wearing mourning clothes; then: sleeping on a floor mat on cold ground is inauspicious. Chapter 23 "driving off bad dreams": "If a person's bad dream is not told for three days it turns into pearls and treasures"; "whoever has a bad dream in the night, on rising early must not tell it to anyone; with a pure and reverent mind write this charm in ink, put it under the foot of the bed and let no one know, then recite the spell" ("Blazing, shining, the sun comes out in the east; this charm cuts off the dream and drives away the inauspicious; read it three times and the hundred ghosts hide; quick, quick, as the statute orders"), a drawn talisman, and a second spell naming the bad dream's demons ("the bad dream is surnamed 壘 Lei and named 行鬼惡想 [Xing-gui-e-xiang, the ghost-walker, the bad thought]; surnamed 賈 Jia and named 自直 [Zi-zhi]; I know your name and know your character; go a thousand li away; quick, quick, as the statute orders"; then "again, surnamed 于 Yu, styled 世〔驚?〕, I know your name and know your character"). The booklet's colophon is on a later image and was not read.

## RD375: the Chinese dream books and the days a dream is not told (q785, q786, q916 and RD368's table)

Found: no Chinese counterpart of the Edo table (mdz:p11, mdz:i234427b). Two nearby Chinese rules were read in the text itself: the rule of silence for a bad dream and the dream by day branch, hour and day officer in the Dunhuang booklet P.3908 (cdb:i13-16, claims `p3908-bad-dream-not-told`, `p3908-dream-by-day-hour-officer`), and the per-day lists of months for the omen of a dream in the Yongle dadian (cdb:box1, claim `yongle-dream-omens-by-day-and-month`); the summary is the claim `cdb-no-chinese-counterpart-of-the-edo-table`.

### Open

- Question: P.3908's rule of silence (chapter 23) is for a bad dream seen in the night; does any Chinese or Japanese text read as a rule for dreams "good and bad alike" on listed days, as the Edo table's closing line does (善悪ともに語らず), or is the Edo rule a Japanese widening of the bad-dream rule?
- Question: P.3908 numbers the chapter of the 建除 days 廿一 and has no chapter of the ten stems; a modern reference list gives a chapter 十干日得夢章 as 21 and the 建除 as 22. Does another manuscript of the book (S.5900 holds only the preface and chapter 1) have a ten-stem chapter, or is the list of that reference an error?
- Question: In the Yongle dadian's day entries the dream line carries the words 牛黄, 廨 or 欄 or 倉囷, and 不將 after the omen; what do they name (the ox-yellow spirit's place, the "not carried" day of marriage lists?), and what are the books 雷英發編『萬曆錦囊拾遺備用』 and 呂茂明編『陰陽備用選擇成書』 and their dates, given that the volume is of 1408 and the title names a Wanli?
