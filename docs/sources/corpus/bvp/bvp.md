## <a id="bvp-p662-665"></a>bvp:p662-665 Brahmavaivartapurāṇa, Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa 73, the Svapnādhyāya: the watches and the spans (vv. 5–6) that are the Svapnohana's

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269); machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265) for vv. 8–52.*

> स्वप्नस्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरफलप्रदः ।
> द्वितीये चाष्टभिर्मासैस्त्रिभिर्मासैस्तृतीयके ॥ ५ ॥
> चतुर्थे चार्धमासेन स्वप्नः स्वात्मफलप्रदः ।
> दशाहे फलदः स्वप्नोऽप्यरुणोदयदर्शने ॥ ६ ॥
> प्रातःस्वप्नश्च फलदस्तत्क्षणं यदि बोधितः ।
> दिने मनसि यद् दृष्टं तत्सर्वं च लभेद् ध्रुवम् ॥ ७ ॥

*Brahmavaivartapurāṇa*, *Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa* ch. 73 (the running heads read "73 a." and "Śrīkṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍam"), the *Svapnādhyāya*, of Jivananda Vidyasagara's edition (vol. 2, 1888). The verses above are on **p. 661** of the print (image n668 of the Internet Archive item; the head reads 661), not on p. 662 as RD265 had it from the OCR: p. 662 (n669) begins at v. 15 and the images carry the chapter on to p. 665. Read on the page image by RD269: verses 3–14 of p. 661 and verses 61–72 of p. 666 (n673, the head of which reads "(7)3 a."), above all the five lines of verses 5–7. The chapter is the answer of the Lord (Kṛṣṇa) to Nanda, who asks which dream gives what merit; to hear it gives the merit of a bath in the Ganges (v. 4: "svapnādhyāyaṃ naraḥ śrutvā gaṅgāsnānaphalaṃ labhet").

**The spans, verses 5–6, in our words:** the first watch (*yāma*), a year; the second, eight months; the third, three months; the fourth, half a month (*ardhamāsa*), "a dream that gives its own fruit"; at the sight of dawn (*aruṇodaya-darśana*), ten days; a dream of the morning bears its fruit at that moment if one is woken (7a, *bodhitaḥ*). This is the whole series of the *Svapnohana* (svo:p268: a year, eight months, three months, half a month, ten days at dawn), and Negelein prints the same words under the *Svapnacintāmaṇi* (neg:p14-16) as his "b 5–6", which the list of his letters (Introduction p. XIX, read on the scan) names as "the *Svapnādhyāya* of the *Brahmavaivarttapurāṇa*": settled. Negelein's print of b 6c reads *bhoditaḥ* where the Jivananda print has *bodhitaḥ*.

Verse 7b as printed: "dine manasi yad dṛṣṭaṃ tat sarvaṃ ca labhed dhruvam" (what was seen in the mind by day, all of it one surely gets); verse 8 says that a man afflicted with worry or disease who sees dreams has them wholly fruitless (*niṣphalam*); verse 9 says the same of the dull one pressed by urine or dung, the terrified, the naked and the loose-haired. The sense of 7b beside 8 is not settled by us; the lines are as the print has them.

The next verses, in our words, from the OCR layers (not opened on the images): one who has a good dream and sleeps again gets no fruit, even if he then tells it (10); telling a dream to a man of Kāśyapa's lineage, a poor man, a low man, an enemy, a fool, a woman, or at night brings harm (calamity, poverty, disease, fear, quarrel, loss of wealth, fear of thieves: 11–13; on the image, 10–13 read as the summary says); then the images, good (a cow, an elephant, a horse, a temple, a palace, a mountain, a tree; white garlands and clothes; a woman in white or yellow; gods, brahmans, cows, ancestors giving; sun, moon, fire; milk, curd, honey, sweets) and bad, ending with the ten names of the goddess to recite against a bad dream and a mantra of seventeen syllables (the 1935 print, v. 49–52).

No verse ties a dream to a *tithi*, a weekday, a nakṣatra, a month or a season; "white things are all praised, black things all blamed" (v. 37) is the only colour rule.

Doubt: of the rest of the chapter only verses 8–14 and 61–72 were seen on the images (vv. 15–60 and 73 onward are the OCR layers of two printings, which agree); in the head of p. 666 the second digit of the chapter number is blurred; the chapter's verse numbers are those the print prints.
