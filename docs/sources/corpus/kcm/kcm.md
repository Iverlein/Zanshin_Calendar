*The magic mirror, a method of examining dreams* (རྨི་ལམ་བརྟགས་ཐབས་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་མེ་ལོང, rmi lam brtags thabs 'phrul gyi me long) by Karma chags med (ཀརྨ་ཆགས་མེད, karma chags med), whole: four page images (I2638, img. 689–692) of the cursive (dbu med) manuscript reproduced in the Tashijong collection. Read 2026-11-09 by Yigdzin-1 alone (no second reader, the card's queue being long); the text below is the raw machine reading with its slips uncorrected (so «བསཾ་པ» and the like), and of the scan only its layout was checked by eye (the last page has four lines, as the reading has), not the syllables: state machine.

The text is not a table of dream images but a rite for calling a clear dream and asking in it. One visualises oneself as white Padmākara (པདྨ་འབྱུང་གནས, pad ma 'byung gnas) on a lotus and moon, with a white lotus stalk in the hand, the ḍākinī at the brow holding a crystal vase and a silver mirror, a crystal girl circling; the wisdom beings are invited, the offerings and praise made, and light from the heart gathers the worldly gods with clairvoyance, which dissolve into oneself and the girl. One then asks the two girls, the daughters of gods and nāgas, to show clearly tonight whatever is wanted; recites the mantra 108 times (Oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra guru padma siddhi hūṃ; hrīḥ ma ha ri ni sa phu phu ru ru, "open the gate of the eye"); at dusk binds a mirror in white silk and waves it, repeats the mantra 108 times, prays on falling asleep for what one wishes, and holds the picture and devotion again and again. The fruit named: one's own matters seen, the sky-goers' Oddiyāna and Akaniṣṭha, buddha-fields, the three realms. The magic clairvoyance is not to be passed to a bad vessel. A scribe's note, "abridged and written by Rā ga" (རཱ་གས་བསྡུས་ཏེ་བྲིས་པ), closes it.

**The second part** (p. 681 end – p. 682), headed "Also, examining a dream" (ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་བརྟགས་པ): a second mantra (Oṃ nanti mimiti yoti ṅi mo tsi la sa muṇṭi sha na ma ta maṅ la svāhā), said many times, or 108 times at bedtime, with a curse-seal laid on the bedding, and one sleeps without speaking to anyone: it will be clear. As to practice: if it agrees with a good time such as the tenth day of the waxing or waning moon, it is best; in any case for three days one makes the protectors' gathering offering with fulfilment and confession in full; at bedtime one says the meditation and recitation above, puts speech and hope and fear aside, and goes to sleep in the clear appearance of the deity and in devotion; then, in the "proper time" (the reading has «བཻ་རང་གི་དུས་ཚོད», doubtful), either words of prophecy or some sequence of signs appears, and one examines and knows by that.
