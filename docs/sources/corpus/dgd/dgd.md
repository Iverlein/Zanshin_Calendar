Step five: the instruction to make the pupils rejoice, to guard them, and to examine dreams (img. 80). The kusha grass (kusha) given to the pupils has its purpose stated in one line: "the purpose is the examination of dreams" (img. 80).

After the verses that rouse joy (the verses from the Vairocana abhisambodhi tantra) the teacher says: "Rejoice beyond measure." The instruction on examining dreams: from his own heart he sends out countless wrathful vajra yakshas and the pupils imagine the obstructors who would break the examination of dreams driven away; he says "oṁ vajra yakṣa hūṁ" and sprinkles the pupils with the vase water to drive them off (img. 81). "Now, pupils, when you lie down at night, with no unvirtuous thought, in a virtuous frame of mind, trusting and devoted to lama and deity, lay the large kusha as a seat and the small as a pillow, and lie with the head toward the side where the sal tree falls (or toward the lama), lowered on the right side, in the lion's posture, and at dawn tell me whatever dream has risen." Then the verse (with the Sanskrit sa-ra-ha-s opening): "Lie on the seat of kusha, sleep well tonight; tomorrow you will see the great mandala of the Blessed Vairocana." (img. 82) The author adds that whether or not these words stand in the text of the preparation rite, they are to be said, as the Great Mantra Stages says. The step ends there: no dream image is named as good or bad, no verdict or remedy is given; the one expected dream is the Vairocana mandala, and the reading of the dreams is left to the teacher.
