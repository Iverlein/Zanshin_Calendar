## <a id="st-p21"></a>st:p21 Ch. 2 of the Explanatory Tantra, the child in the womb: the mother's dream of a man marks a boy

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09.*

> ཟླ་དགུ་འདས་ནས་བཙའ་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ། དེ་ཡང་གཡས་ལོགས་ལ་བརྟེན་དཀུ་གཡས་མཐོ། ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་། ནུ་ཞོ་གཡས་པ་སྔར་འབྱུང་ཁྱེའུ་སྐྱེ། སྐྱེས་པ་ཕྲད་འདོད་གླུ་གར་རྒྱན་ལ་དགའ། གོང་ལས་ལྡོག་པ་བུ་མོ་སྐྱེ་བའི་རྟགས། དེ་དག་འདྲེས་ལས་མ་ནིང་འབྱུང་པ་སྟེ། དབུས་དམའ་མཐའ་གཉིས་མཐོ་ན་མཚེ་མར་སྐྱེ།

Serthang p. 21, img. 34, ll. 8–12 of the scan (the passage is the end of the chapter's account of the child in the womb, the second chapter by its colophon on p. 22 of the print, on the formation of the body, ལུས་ཀྱི་ཆགས་ཚུལ, lus kyi chags tshul, the Explanatory Tantra, བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud, by the run of colophons that begins again at the second after the Root Tantra's sixth on etext p. 28). When the nine months are over it is the time of birth. The mother lies on her right side with the right hip high. The body is light (ལུས་ཡང, lus yang, a dictionary headword: "the body is light"), and if in her dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) she sees a man (the word between, དག, dag, whether the plural marker or "clear, pure" is not settled), it is a boy; the right breast gives milk first and a boy is born; she wishes to meet men and delights in song, dance and ornaments. The reverse of these is the sign of a girl. A mixture of them gives a child who is neither (མ་ནིང, ma ning). If the middle is low and both sides are high, twins are born.

Read on the scan, 2026-11-09. The etext and Yigdzin-1 agree with the scan in every line. The print's page number is the image minus 13 here (the registry's rule for the first pages). RD227 (2026-11-09): the line (the eleventh row of the scan, 3548 px wide at 2×) read syllable by syllable on a 4× crop: ལུས་ཡང་རྨི་ལམ་དག་ན་སྐྱེས་པ་མཐོང་, every letter clear, ཡང with ཡ over ང and དག with ད over ག; the etext reads the same. The Drathang woodblock print (GT, img. 29, the fifth row, a crop at x 1090–1520, y 180–235, 4.5×) has the same words in the same order. So the letters are settled on two prints. ཡང is "light": the pair "lus yang" is a headword of Jim Valby's dictionary ("light body, the body is light") and ཡང་བ is "light, the opposite of heavy" in several; it sits among body-signs (side, hip, body, breast), so it is the body, not "also". དག stays open between the plural marker after "dreams" and "clear, pure" (the dictionaries give both); the dream of a man is a sign of a boy on either, but "clear" would restrict it to a clear dream. GT's interlinear red notes did not resolve at 1763 px.

## <a id="st-p34"></a>st:p34 Ch. 7, the signs of death: dreams are the distant, uncertain signs, and what turns them back

> འཆི་ལྟས་མི་ཤེས་གསོ་བ་བླང་དོར་འགག དེ་མ་ཤེས་པས་སྙན་གྲགས་ཐོབ་མ་ཡིན། དེ་ཕྱིར་མཁས་འདོད་འཆི་ལྟས་རྟོགས་པར་བུ། རྨི་ལམ་རྣམ་འགྱུར་རིང་དང་མ་ངེས་ལྟས། ཚོགས་གསོག་ཀློག་སྒྲུབ་ཡི་དམ་སུམ་འགྱུར་བཟློག ཉེ་དང་ངེས་པའི་ལྟས་ལ་འཆི་བསླུ་བྱ། ཤིན་ཏུ་ཉེ་བའི་ལྟས་ལ་ཟློག་ཐབས་མེད། ཅེས་གསུངས་སོ། བདུད་རྩི་སྙིང་པོ་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པ་གསང་བ་མན་ངག་གི་རྒྱུད་ལས་ལུས་ཀྱི་འཇིག་ལྟས་བསྟན་པའི་ལེའུ་སྟེ་བདུན་པའོ། །

Serthang p. 34, img. 44, ll. 9–14 of the scan (the end of the seventh chapter, on the signs of the body's destruction, ལུས་ཀྱི་འཇིག་ལྟས, lus kyi 'jig ltas, in the Explanatory Tantra, བཤད་རྒྱུད, bshad rgyud, by the same run of colophons as on etext p. 35). Not knowing the signs of death, the choice of what to take up and give up in treatment is blocked, and without knowing them one gains no good name; so whoever wishes to be wise should understand the signs of death. Dreams (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) and changes of bearing are the distant and uncertain signs: gathering merit, recitation and the practice of the yidam deity (ཡི་དམ, yi dam), three means, turn them back. For the near and certain signs the death ransom (འཆི་བསླུ, 'chi bslu) is done. For the very near signs there is no means of turning them back. Thus it was said. The colophon names the whole work, the Heart of Nectar in Eight Branches, the Secret Instruction (བདུད་རྩི་སྙིང་པོ་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པ་གསང་བ་མན་ངག་གི་རྒྱུད, bdud rtsi snying po yan lag brgyad pa gsang ba man ngag gi rgyud), the title of the Four Tantras together, and then the chapter showing the signs of the body's destruction, the seventh.

The word བ་ར (crop x 1180–1870, y 1585–1665, 3×) reads «བ་རའི་འོ་མར་དྲི་ཆེན་སྦྱར་བ་རྐང་མཐིལ་ལ་བྱུག» letter by letter clear. བ་ར (ba ra) is "cattle and goats" (བ་ཕྱུགས་དང་ར་, Tshig mdzod chen mo and others; also the cattle's pen), so "milk of cattle and goats", mixed with dung (དྲི་ཆེན, dri chen: "dung, ordure", one of the five nectars in Dan Martin's gloss); the earlier "unidentified" is dropped. ཕྱུ (phyu) in «མོ་ཕྱུ་རིམ་གྲོ་རྟེན་འབྲེལ» stays unsettled. The Drathang print (GT, img. 647, Yigdzin-1 only) has the root verse without either bracket: «གདོན་ཡིན་རྨི་ལམ་བུད་མེད་འདྲེན་པར་འགྱུར། །མོ་ཕྱྭ་རིམ་གྲོ་རྟེན་འབྲེལ་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་གསོ། །དགོས་པ་ནད་མེད་ལང་…», so the two bracketed remedies (the thread, the milk) are notes put into the Serthang text (whether they are interlinear notes in the Drathang print was not read: its folio is 1763 px wide), and the dream row's own verdict (a spirit; women leading away) is in the verse.
