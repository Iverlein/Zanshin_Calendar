## <a id="osv-i3"></a>osv:i3 The Odia print's first page: the verse of the spans of the watches, with its Odia paraphrase

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD277), the title page (PDF page 1) and the first page of text (PDF page 3, enlarged to 450 and 600 dpi), PDF pages 4–5 for the next verses; the OCR layer of the item is garbled Odia and was not used.*

> ସ୍ୱପ୍ନସ୍ୟ ପ୍ରଥମେ ଯାମେ ବତ୍ସରେଣ ଫଲଂ ଭବେତ୍ ।
> ଦ୍ୱିତୀୟେ ସପ୍ତଭିର୍ମାସୈସ୍ତ୍ରିଭିର୍ମାସୈସ୍ତ୍ରିଯାମକେ ।
> ଚତୁର୍ଥେ ସାର୍ଦ୍ଧମାସେନ ଦୃଶ୍ୟତେ ନାତ୍ର ସଂଶୟଃ ।
> ଅରୁଣୋଦୟବେଳାୟାଂ ଦଶାହେ ଚ ଫଲଂ ଲଭେତ୍ ।
> ପ୍ରାତର୍ଦୃଷ୍ଟ୍ୱା ଭବେତ୍ ସଦ୍ୟୋ ଯଦାସୌ ପ୍ରତିବୁଧ୍ୟତେ ।

The Sanskrit verses above, in Odia script, are as the print has them (the "e" and the "ai" of "māsais" are printed with the Odia signs; the transcription to Roman is ours):

> svapnasya prathame yāme varṣeṇa phalaṃ bhavet |
> dvitīye saptabhir māsais tribhir māsais triyāmake |
> caturthe sārdhamāsena dṛśyate nātra saṃśayaḥ |
> aruṇodaya-velāyāṃ daśāhe ca phalaṃ labhet |
> prātar dṛṣṭvā bhavet sadyo yadāsau pratibudhyate ||

The print's title page reads "No. 1. Svapnādhyāya, that is, the results of the dreams seen in the night in the dream state; published (printed) by the proprietor of Edward Pustakalaya" (Edward Pustakalaya, Bankabazar, Cuttack, year 1929 by the digitiser's record). The text opens with the verse "svapnādhyāyaṃ pravakṣyāmi yathoktaṃ muni-ṛṣibhiḥ | sarvārtha-phalaṃ puṇyaṃ sarvatīrtha-phalapradaṃ ||" and then paraphrases each Sanskrit verse in Odia rhyming couplets, so that the verse of the spans is followed by: "in the first watch the fruit comes in a year; the second watch, in seven months (*sapta-māse*); the third, in three months; the fourth, in *padara dina* (fifteen days); at the dawn, in ten days; at morning the fruit comes at once" (the Odia of the paraphrase as read at 220 dpi: "prathama prahare barṣaka-re … dvitīye sapata-māse phalai | tṛtīye tina-māsare ghaṭai | caturthe padara-dinare jāṇa | aruṇa udaye daśama dina | prātaḥkālare phale sadyadina").

What the passage says, in our words:

- **A third form of the series**: a year; seven months (the Sanskrit and the paraphrase both read "seven", not the eight of the manuscripts pn1871 and pn673: the Odia *saptabhir* and the Penn *aṣṭabhir* differ in one syllable, so the Odia may be a slip, which cannot be settled from this print alone); three months; **sārdhamāsa, a month and a half**, in the Sanskrit of the fourth watch, which the Odia paraphrase renders "fifteen days" (*padara dina*), the Brahmavaivarta's half-month (bvp:p662-665) and not the manuscripts' month; ten days at dawn; at once for a dream in the early morning, if the dreamer is woken.
- It is thus the pn1871 verse (same verse form, same first lines, the same list of good dreams after it, "ārohaṇaṃ go-vṛṣa-kuñjarāṇāṃ prāsāda-śailāgra-vanaspatīnāṃ …" on PDF page 4) with its second and fourth spans changed. The Sanskrit "sārdhamāsena" and the paraphrase's fifteen days are not the same period; the print does not say which the compiler meant.
- Page 3 goes on, after the verse of the spans, with the verse of the wind, bile and phlegm dreams and the rule about a dream followed by sleep or not ("yo na svapyeta svapnānte …", whose paraphrase says that one who wakes from the dream and does not go back to sleep gets its fruit; read at 220 dpi, not closely), the same sequence as pn673 verse 4.
- The print names no author: its Sanskrit is called "of the sages" (*muni-ṛṣibhiḥ*), not Bṛhaspati's.

Doubts: the "sapta" of the second verse line may be a printer's slip for "aṣṭa"; the paraphrase's "padara dina" was read at 220 dpi, not enlarged further; no page beyond PDF page 5 was read.
