## <a id="apv-p445"></a>apv:p445 Svapnādhyāya, verses 57–61: the last of successive dreams, the watches of the night and the fruit, the remedy

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), verses 57–61 on the full-size page image (leaf n223 of the Internet Archive item), the text as above; machine, 2026-11-09, before.

> śubhaṃ dṛṣṭvā tu yaḥ svapne punaḥ paśyaty apūjitam |
> śubhaṃ vā py aśubhaṃ vā pi yat paścāt tat-phalaṃ labhet || 57
> svapnās tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ |
> dvitīye 'ṣṭasu māseṣu tṛtīye tu tad-ardha-bhāk || 58
> māsiko go-visarge tu sadyaḥ-pākaḥ prabhātike |
> kālaḥ pañcasv avasthāsu śarvaryāḥ kīrtitaḥ pṛthak || 59
> viprebhyaḥ śaktito dānaṃ śāntiḥ svastyayanādayaḥ |
> vināśayanti duḥsvapnaṃ prātaś cāśvattha-sevanam || 60
> aśvattha-sevā tila-pātra-dānaṃ go-sparśanaṃ brāhmaṇa-tarpaṇaṃ ca |
> śānti-kriyā svastyayana-kriyā ca duḥsvapnam etāni vināśayanti || 61

Pariśiṣṭa 68, Svapnādhyāya, section 2, verses 57–61 (the numbering of Bolling and Negelein's edition; the Devanagari edition on the Internet Archive prints the same verses as 68.2.57–61 with the same text).

57. Whoever sees a good dream and then sees a second, unworshipped one, gets the fruit of whichever, good or bad, comes last (of successive dreams the last counts).
58. Dreams of the first watch (yāma) of the night ripen in a year; of the second, in eight months; of the third the dream takes half of that (tad-ardha-bhāj: half of the eight months, four, or half of the year; the verse does not say which).
59. A dream at the time the cows are let out (go-visarga) is a month's; at dawn (prābhātika) the fruit is at once. The time is told separately for the five states (avasthā) of the night.
60. Gifts to brahmans as one can afford, an appeasing rite, a blessing rite and so on destroy a bad dream, and so does serving the aśvattha tree in the morning.
61. Serving the aśvattha, a gift of a vessel of sesame, touching a cow, satisfying brahmans, an appeasing rite and a blessing rite: these destroy a bad dream.

Reading of the verses in Roman transliteration (the Bolling–Negelein text layer: māsiko is printed there as "müsiko" and "aṣṭa-" with a v.l. "ca 'ṣṭamāseṣu / cūṣṭasu māseṣu" of three manuscripts, so the second watch is eight months in every witness; sadyaḥ-pāka for sadyaḥ-phala in one manuscript, B). The two Devanagari editions of the Internet Archive (1976 and a later Hindi-annotated one) read the same verses with māsiko, sadyaḥ-pākaḥ, prabhātike (once prabhātike, once pramātike, an OCR error), pañcasv avasthāsu.

## <a id="apv-p456"></a>apv:p456 Bolling and von Negelein's variant readings for Pariśiṣṭa 68.2.58–61: svapnas in all manuscripts, māsiko go-visarge without a variant

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), on the full-size page image.*

> LXVIII. 2. 58. ABCDET svapnas. ACD cā ṣṭamāseṣu; E cāṣṭasu māseṣu.
> 59. ACDETRoth sadyaḥpākāḥ; B sadyaḥphalaḥ. BE kāla. E avasthāḥsu; T avasthāsu. Roth kīrttitāḥ.
> 60. XRoth śāṃti.
> 61. AD gosparśana; B gosparśanyam.

The apparatus of Bolling and von Negelein for the verses 58–61 on p. 456 (read on the page image n234 of the Internet Archive item, full size; the print of the verses themselves, p. 445, read on image n223): **for v. 58, all seven manuscripts (A B C D E T and Roth's) read *svapnas*** where the edition prints *svapnās tu prathame yāme*, the opening that the Penn manuscripts have as *svapnas tu prathame yāme* (pn1871, pn2730's "svapnas tu", pn673), so that their first half-line is the Parisiṣṭa's, with the editors' own emendation undone. For the second half of the verse, A, C and D read *ca 'ṣṭamāseṣu* ("and in eight months") and E reads *cāṣṭasu māseṣu*: the eight months of the second watch are in every manuscript. For v. 59, A C D E T and Roth read *sadyaḥpākaḥ*, B *sadyaḥphalaḥ* (the cows' release a month, the dawn at once); **no variant is marked on *māsiko go-visarge*** (the month for the cows' release), so that it stands in all the manuscripts, against Penn's "at once" for the cows' release and a month for the fourth watch. The page carries nothing on a date of the manuscripts.
Our reading is on the image; the editors' sigla are as they print them (A–E, T, Roth: the Roth–Whitney Atharvaveda text).
