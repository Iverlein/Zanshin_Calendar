## <a id="zbc-5-i123"></a>zbc.5:i123 The passing chapter: ill from the evening of the fourth of the middle winter month of the fire-female-bird year

*Read: scan, 2026-11-24 (RD411): read on the scan; image 123 carries printed p. 115.*

> … བདེན་ཏེ་ང་ལ་ཤི་བ་གཉིས་ཁྱུང་གི་སྒོང་ང་དང་འདྲ་གསུངས་སོ། །དེ་ནས་མེ་མོ་བྱའི་ལོ་དགུན་ཟླ་འབྲིང་པོའི་ཚེས་བཞི་ལ་བབས་པའི་ཉི་མ་དགོངས་མོར་སོང་ཙ་ན། དམ་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེའི་ཞལ་ནས། ང་ན་གསུངས་སོ། …

(The ninth chapter of the life, «the greatness of the peaceful passing beyond sorrow» (ཞི་བ་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་ཆེ་བ, zhi ba mya ngan las 'das pa'i che ba), which opens at image 114; image 123, printed p. 115.) "'It is true: for me the two, living and dying, are like the egg of the garuḍa,' he said. Then, in the fire-female-bird year (མེ་མོ་བྱའི་ལོ, me mo bya'i lo), on the day that fell on the fourth (ཚེས་བཞི, tshes bzhi) of the middle winter month (དགུན་ཟླ་འབྲིང་པོ, dgun zla 'bring po), when the evening came, the precious Dam pa said: 'I am ill.'" The year agrees with the Blue Annals' year of his passing (dsn.2:i402). The month is named by its season, not numbered.

## <a id="zbc-5-i152"></a>zbc.5:i152 The passing chapter: four days of signs, then at dawn on the tenth the body wrapped in white silk

*Read: scan, 2026-11-24 (RD411): the quoted words read on the scan.*

> … དེ་ཡང་ཞག་བཞིའི་བར་དུ་རྟེན་འབྲེལ་གྱི་བརྡ་སྒྱུར་གྱིན་གདའ་བ་ལ། … མཛད་ཀྱིན་གདའ་གསུངས། དེ་ནས་ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཐོ་རེངས་དར་དཀར་ཡུག་ཅིག་གིས་སྐུ་ལུས་ཅག་གི་འདྲིལ་དུ་བཅུག་པ་ལ། …

(Image 152, printed p. 144 by the offset of image 123.) "For four days (ཞག་བཞི, zhag bzhi) he went on giving signs of auspicious connection; asked why he did so, he said: 'The Buddha too did so when he passed beyond sorrow.' Then at dawn on the tenth (ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཐོ་རེངས, tshes bcu'i tho rengs) he had his body wrapped tightly in a whole bolt of white silk …" The page goes on (etext, not read syllable by syllable on the scan) to his lying down on his right side facing west like the Buddha at his passing, and to the sunrise; the sentence of the passing itself is on image 153 (zbc.5:i153). The month is not repeated with the tenth: the tenth follows the fourth of the middle winter month of image 123 in the same narrative.

## <a id="zbc-5-i153"></a>zbc.5:i153 The passing chapter: to common perception his mind dissolved into the expanse and he went to Khecara

*Read: etext, 2026-11-24 (RD411): etext only.*

> … དམ་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དེ་ཉིད་དོན་ལ་འདས་གྲོངས་མི་མངའ་ཡང་། ཐུན་མོང་བའི་སྣང་ངོར་དགོངས་པ་ཆོས་དབྱིངས་སུ་ཐིམས་པའི་ཚུལ་བསྟན་ནས་དག་པ་མཁའ་སྤྱོད་དུ་གཤེགས་སོ། །

(Image 153, etext only.) "Though the precious Dam pa himself in truth has no passing or death, to common perception he showed the manner of his mind dissolving into the expanse of phenomena and went to the pure Khecara (མཁའ་སྤྱོད, mkha' spyod)." No day is named in this sentence; it follows the dawn of the tenth and the sunrise of image 152.

## <a id="zbc-5-i157"></a>zbc.5:i157 The passing chapter: died on the tenth, cremated on the morning of the seventeenth

*Read: scan, 2026-11-24 (RD411): the quoted words read on the scan.*

> … པ་ལྟར་གནང་བ་ལས། དོན་ལ་འདས་གྲོངས་མེད་དེ། ཚེས་བཅུ་ལ་གྲོངས་ནས་ཚེས་བཅུ་བདུན་གྱི་སྔ་དྲོ་སྤུར་སྦྱངས་ཤིང་། …

(Image 157, printed p. 149 by the same offset; the quoted words read on the scan, the rest of the sentence from the etext.) "So, though he passed away, it was only as if he had; in truth there was no passing or death: he died on the tenth (ཚེས་བཅུ་ལ་གྲོངས, tshes bcu la grongs), his remains were cremated on the morning of the seventeenth (ཚེས་བཅུ་བདུན་གྱི་སྔ་དྲོ, tshes bcu bdun gyi snga dro), and on the next morning, at the Sna mar pass on the southern side, he met the patrons, men and women, and taught them …" The life states the tenth as the day of death and the seventeenth as the cremation; the stories that follow (his being seen afterwards by traders and disciples) are told as appearances after the passing.
