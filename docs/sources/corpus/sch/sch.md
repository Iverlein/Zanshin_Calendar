## <a id="sch-rd257"></a>RD257 Scheuermann's chapter by other routes: what was reached and what not

*Read: not read. 2026-11-09, RD257 (a second attempt after RD214). The chapter's text was not reached by any route; its published abstract and the editors' summary of the volume were, and their facts are in the claim [sch-svapnohana-abstract](../../claims/sch-svapnohana-abstract.md). No passage is quoted: the study is modern and copyrighted (facts only).*

What the routes gave:

- Crossref, OpenAlex and Unpaywall (checked 2026-11-09; Unpaywall's record updated 2026-10-04) give the chapter's abstract and say: closed, no repository copy. The volume was published 29 October 2019 (OpenAlex), Brill's imprint dates it 2020; the chapter is chapter 8 of the volume (Brill's page numbering BP000008), DOI 10.1163/9789004410688_009.
- The LMU Munich repository (epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/75271) holds the volume's record only, with "full text not available"; the author's FAU record (cris.fau.de/publications/240544364) has the metadata and no file.
- The other chapters of the volume have abstracts in OpenAlex; two are on dreams and both are on Bon texts (Ramble on signs in nature and in dreams, from Buddhist and Bon ritual and dream texts; Rossi on the Bon *gSal byed byang bu*), which the project does not take as a source.
- Open studies reached: Stearns 1996 (JIABS 19.1), on the translator, read whole (claim [svo-vibhuticandra-dates](../../claims/svo-vibhuticandra-dates.md)); the catalogues of the Tengyur on the Internet Archive (Tohoku 1934; Bose 1923; New Catalogus Catalogorum vol. 29) and a text layer of Bu ston's history, read for the work's attribution (claim [svo-authorship-witnesses](../../claims/svo-authorship-witnesses.md), type conflict).
- Wayman, "Significance of Dreams in India and Tibet" (History of Religions 7.1, 1967, pp. 1–12; a scan on the Internet Archive), read whole: the Indian rules of dream time by watch beside the Svapnohana's, claim [svo-indian-frame-of-watch-rule](../../claims/svo-indian-frame-of-watch-rule.md). It does not mention the Svapnohana.
- Tibetan use: BDRC's full-text search for strings of the text finds its watch verse quoted in two modern Tibetan books and its title in the Tengyur catalogues of Bu ston and of the Jo nang abbot Phyogs las rnam rgyal, claims [svo-modern-tibetan-quotations](../../claims/svo-modern-tibetan-quotations.md) and [svo-authorship-witnesses](../../claims/svo-authorship-witnesses.md).
- Parallels seen only as names, not read: Jagaddeva's Sanskrit *Svapnacintāmaṇi* (Negelein's German translation and study, Giessen 1912, of that Indian dream-omen book); Tohoku 4160 = 4555, an *Svapnacintāmaṇi-parikathā* (rmi lam yid bzhin nor bu'i gtam) of the Tengyur ascribed to Vasubandhu in the catalogue's text layer, a different kind of work; the Kangyur *Svapnanirdeśa* (Toh 48), a sūtra on a bodhisattva's dream signs, not a manual.
