"Samsara is like poison and a thorn; do not hold it as true," he said. "Fortunate disciple, like a jewel: cherish whatever good arises," he said. Then a yogin who sat near the lord Avalokiteśvara spoke thus: "The father, the emanation Tilopa the great, served a lama and practised austerity; being holy, he realised mahāmudrā. You too, do not be distracted; do not work for gain and fame; crush the head of the eight worldly dharmas where it sticks up; see that no one sees or notices; act like Mi[la], the yogin. If you do so it is very good." I heard these words clearly; at that instant both woke (the sense of «གཉིས་ཀྱང་སད» is a doubt). The hook of unlost mindfulness caught me. "Whatever I did before, whatever I did, those thoughts, even the mind's tie of no-self cut; now even if a divine power came, there is nowhere to place it, though I leave it in emptiness; if I do not wander in the mountain retreats..." The first: the way dual clinging arises in the appearance of the unpure, deluded dream is "taught at length in the Mañjuśrī root tantra; I gather it and teach it": one line (of the root text). The second, the detailed explanation, has two: how the dream arises from the condition of the place, and how it arises from the condition of the constituents (ཁམས, khams). The first has two, each taken singly and in common; the single has three: the dream that arises above, below, and in the middle. The first, "going up" and the following, three lines: the cause of the deluded double appearance in the dream is that from the gathering of the seven consciousnesses with their retinue on the all-ground in deep sleep, the life-wind itself, with mind and formation, rises and seizes it as a self. By the dream's channel-place and constituent the dream is made different: the channel has an upper, a lower and a middle, broad and narrow and so on; if the mind goes with the life-wind to the crown above and the dream arises, there are the appearances of god-realms, of going into the sky, of going up high mountains, (p. 233) of climbing ladders and trees; if it goes into the channel of the eye, forms are seen clearly; likewise into the ear channel, sounds, and so on, smell at the nose, taste at the tongue, touch at the body faculty: the appearances of each object arise unmixed. The second, "going down", three lines: the dream of going down from the heart by the motion of the life-wind: by the three passages, hells at the anal passage and so on, hungry spirits at the birth passage, animals at the water passage; going downward only, going down steeply, to cliff-caves, thick forest; dark pits and appearances in which all things are unclear arise. The third, "in the middle", three lines: the dream of the mind-body moved by the life-wind and going to the channel-place of the middle: in general the earth-surface appearances of the four continents arise; in particular, at the front channel-place appearances of the east, at the right channel the south, at the back channel the west, at the left the north. So the sense of "a dream of going to the south is a death sign" is: when the life-wind flows on the right, one dreams again and again of going to the south; "so the commentary (འགྲེལ་པ, 'grel pa, the author's own) says." The second, in common, a line and a half: a broad channel-place, a broad country appears; a crooked one, a very crooked country, and so on.

(p. 286-287, the root verses of the sixth chapter of the treatise, as the commentary quotes them) «The unpure, deluded appearances too: going up, to the crown, the god-realms; appearances of sky, going high, ascending; forms, sounds and the like arise unmixed. Going down, hells and the like appear; going downward, cliffs, caves, thick forest; dark pits and the like arise. In the middle, the four continents, the earth's surface: in front, right, back and left, east, south, west, north appear. A broad channel-place shows broad, a narrow one narrow; likewise, by the condition of the constituents (ཁམས, khams) there too: for wind, [blue] things and blue-black clothes, a black crow flying, riding a horse, a blue jewel, clear awareness, or else many unstable things arise. Gone to the place of bile and blood: fire, red clothes, earth and gold appear, and monks, and many other things appear. Touching the place of the white part, phlegm: water, snow, pearls, white clothes, a woman, an elephant, silver and many other things appear.»

(p. 287-288, the commentary) «The second (the way the dream appears): in that dream, in the state of unpure delusion, how do the appearances of the dream come about? The nature of the dream is as told above. By the sixth, the mental consciousness, moved by the life-wind: if it goes up to the crown, the place of the wheel of great bliss, appearances of the thirty-three and other god-realms, and of going into the sky and up a high mountain arise. If the dream-consciousness goes to the channel of the eye, forms are seen very clear and unmixed; likewise, if it goes to the channels of the ear, nose, tongue and body faculty, sounds, smells, tastes and touches appear very clear and unmixed. If it goes with the life-wind to the channels below the heart, to the secret place, appearances of hells, hungry spirits, animals and the like arise, and going downward, to a narrow cliff-cave, a thick forest, a dark pit, and all things unclear. If the dream-mind goes with the life-wind to the channels between, at the right and left of the heart, the earth-surface appearances of the four continents, Jambudvīpa in the south and the others, arise; when it goes to the front channel, appearances of the east arise, to the right channel the south, to the back channel the west (p. 288), to the left channel appearances of the north. If it goes to a broad channel-place, a broad country; to a narrow one, a narrow country appears. When the signs of death, the life-wind flowing right, are there, one dreams again and again of going to the south; that is the reason why going south is said to be a death sign.

»That things appear in the dream as they do through the condition of the constituents is also said from the tantras (རྒྱུད་རྣམས་ལས, rgyud rnams las): "At dusk (སྲོད, srod) phlegm (བད་ཀན, bad kan) is mostly strong; at midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, nam phyed) bile (མཁྲིས་པ, mkhris pa) is said; dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) is the very time of wind (རླུང, rlung); then, by the nature of the constituents, the dream appears as the object." By this, for the constituents' condition: the group of wind dreams: in the quarter gone to, all things blue, such as ne'u sing (ནིའུ་སིང, a name not identified here) and the like, blue and black clothes, a black crow, oneself flying in the sky, riding a horse, a blue jewel, clear awareness; or else all the dream's appearances unstable, changing in an instant, flickering. The group of bile's constituent: if the dream-consciousness goes with the life-wind to the place of bile and blood: fire blazing, red and yellow clothes worn, red earth, gold, monks and the like in red and yellow robes of many kinds, living beings and things mostly yellow, the appearance slow and steady, awareness tied to fear and the like. The group tied to phlegm: if the mind with the life-wind goes to the white part, phlegm's place: water, snow, pearls, white earth, white clothes, a woman, an elephant, silver and other white jewels (p. 289) and all that appears pleasant to touch and steady, many such. Thus the dream's appearance becomes unmeasurable through the sequence of channel, wind and constituent.

»The second, joining it to the scripture: "As for the sequence of channel, wind and constituent, the appearance depending on habit-imprints, the Jina spoke it in the 'Jam dpal rtsa rgyud (Mañjuśrī root tantra)": the manner in which, depending on the sequence of channel, wind and constituent and on habit-imprints, the dream's objects appear in many forms, is just as the perfectly complete Buddha said in the Mañjuśrī root tantra.»

ལངས་པ་ནི་རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ངོ་བོ་ཡིན་ལ། རྩ་གནས་རྣམས་ནས་བརྒྱུད་ནས་གཟུང་ འཛིན་གྱི་སྣང་བ་སྐྱེ་བ་ནི་འོག་ནས་རྒྱས་པར་བཤད་པ་བཞིན་ནོ། དེ་ལྟར་ཀུན་ གཞི་ལ་གནས་པའི་ཡིད་དང་། དྲུག་པ་ཡིད་ཀྱི་ཆ་ཤས་ཡིན་པས་ཞེས་དང་། རྨི་ ལམ་དེར་ཇི་ལྟར་མ་དག་པ་དང་འཁྲུལ་པའི་སྣང་བ་འབྱུང་བར་གྱུར་ན། ཐོག་ མར་སྙིང་གར་ཀུན་གཞིའི་སྟེང་དུ་ཚོགས་དྲུག་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྡུད་དོ། དེ་ན་ ཡང་སྲོག་གི་རླུང་དང་ལྷན་ཅིག་ལངས་ནས་ལུས་ཀྱི་སྟེང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སྤྱི་བོར་སོང་ ན་ལྷའི་ཡུལ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་སྣང་བ་དང་། ནམ་མཁའ་ལ་ཕྱིན་པ་དང་། རི་མཐོན་པོ་ སོགས་གྱེན་དུ་འཛེག་པ་དང༌། མིག་གི་རྩ་ལ་སོང་ན་གཟུགས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་ གསལ་བར་མཐོང་བ་དང་། དེ་བཞིན་སྒྲ་དང་དྲི་དང་རོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་མ་འདྲེས་ པར་འཆར་རོ།། གང་གི་ཚེ་ཡིད་སྲོག་གི་རླུང་དང་ལྷན་ཅིག་སྙིང་གའི་འོག་གི་ ཕྱོགས་གསང་བ་ལ་སོགས་པར་སོང་ན་དམྱལ་བ་དང་ཡི་དྭགས་དང་དུད་འགྲོ་ལ་ སོགས་པའི་སྣང་བ་དང༌། ཐུར་དང་བྲག་རོང་དང་ནགས་འཐུག་པོ་ལ་སོགས་ པར་འགྲོ་བ་དང༌། མུན་ཁུང་དང་དངོས་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་མི་གསལ་བའི་རྣམ་པ་ འཆར་བར་འགྱུར་རོ། ལུས་ཀྱི་བར་གྱི་རྩ་རྣམས་སུ་ཡིད་སྲོག་གི་རླུང་དང་ལྷན་ ཅིག་ཏུ་འགྲོ་བ་དེའི་ཚེ་གླིང་བཞི་ལ་སོགས་པ་སྟེང་གི་སྣང་བ་འཆར་རོ།། དེ་ཡང་ [p354] མདུན་གྱི་རྩ་རྣམས་ལ་སོང་བའི་ཚེ་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་སྣང་བ་འབྱུང་ངོ་།། གཡས་ཀྱི་ རྩ་ལ་སོང་ན་ལྷོ།། རྒྱབ་ཀྱི་རྩ་ལ་སོང་ན་ནུབ་བོ།། གཡོན་གྱི་རྩ་ལ་སོང་ན་བྱང་ ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་སྣང་བ་འབྱུང་སྟེ། རྩ་གནས་ཡངས་པ་ལ་ཡུལ་ཡངས་པ་དང་། རྩ་ གནས་དོག་པ་ལ་ཡུལ་ཤིན་ཏུ་དོག་པའི་སྣང་བ་འཆར་ཏེ། དེའི་ཕྱིར་སྲོག་ གཡས་པར་རྒྱུ་བའི་འཆི་ལྟས་འབྱུང་བའི་ཚེ་རྨི་ལམ་ན་ཡང་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་སུ་ཡང་ ནས་ཡང་དུ་ཕྱིན་པར་རྨི་བས། ལྷོར་འགྲོ་བ་འཆི་ལྟས་སུ་བཤད་པའི་དོན་ཡང་ དེ་ཡིན་པར་གོའོ། རྨི་ལམ་དེར་དངོས་པོ་ཇི་ལྟར་སྣང་བའི་རྐྱེན་ཡང་། སྲོད་ལ་ བད་ཀན་ཤས་ཆེ་སྟེ།། ནམ་ཕྱེད་དུས་སུ་མཁྲིས་པར་བརྗོད།། ཐོ་རེངས་རླུང་གི་ དུས་ཉིད་དེ། དེ་ཚེ་ཁམས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་གྱིས།། རྨི་ལམ་ཡུལ་དུ་སྣང་བར་ - Question: Is there a text that the Kagyu tradition calls the Mañjuśrī root tantra («འཇམ་དཔལ་རྩ་རྒྱུད») other than the Derge Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa (Toh 543), in which the verse «སྲོད་ལ་བད་ཀན་ཤས་ཆེ་སྟེ། ནམ་ཕྱེད་དུས་སུ་མཁྲིས་པར་བརྗོད། ཐོ་རངས་རླུང་གི་དུས་ཉིད་དེ» stands, since Zhwa dmar IV gives it as that tantra's, the Karmapa's commentators as «from the tantra(s)», and Toh 543's etext has only the four-watch verse (phlegm, bile, wind, true)?
- Question: Marpa's saying «སྤྱིར་སྲོད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བག་ཆགས་ཡིན། ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་གདོན་དབང་ཡིན། ཐོ་རངས་གང་བྱུང་བདེན་ནོ» (rrd.5:p57-59) stands in the BDRC etexts in the Karmapa's verses only: is it in a work or an oral teaching of Marpa (or of Mi la ras pa or Sgam po pa) that the Kagyu tradition holds, and is «gdon dbang» there the «spirits'» power or a different word?
- Question: In the Karmapa's counsel for retreat (rrd.5:p111-112) the day is six sessions, early morning, «གྲོ་ཁ», afternoon, dusk, «གུང», dawn: what time of the day is the «གྲོ་ཁ», and is the «གུང» the middle of the night or a longer part of it (from the end of the dusk session to the dawn session)?
- Question: In the Karmapa's verses (rrd.5:p57) the word «མཚན་བརྟས» in «ཐོ་རངས་མཚན་བརྟས་འདི་ལྟར་བྱུང» ("at dawn a sign of the night came thus"): is it a spelling of «མཚན་བརྟགས»/«མཚན་རྟགས», and is it the dream that follows or a separate omen?
