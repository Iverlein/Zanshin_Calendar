## <a id="mrs-p76-77"></a>mrs:p76-77 Mipham's commitments of offering: the tenth of both halves without a break, the twenty-ninth, the fifteenth

*Read: etext, 2026-11-21.*

Facts only, in the pool's own words (modern copyrighted work; the Tibetan is not quoted beyond the terms). Dilgo Khyentse's short life of Mipham lists the commitments of offering and accumulation that Mipham kept, "as he had promised before his family lord, the great-empowered Dgyes rab rdo rje" (the passage's own words for the lord; the page does not say whether the promise was a vow of the empowerment). The list: lamps, incense and food offerings in the hundred-thousands; flowers and mandala offerings in many hundred-thousands; the circumambulation of the Rnam rgyal shrine of Dzing and of two stupas (the Kah thog Rdo rje gdan stupa among them) five thousand times each; the recitation of the Names of Mañjuśrī (འཇམ་དཔལ་མཚན་བརྗོད་, 'jam dpal mtshan brjod) from the treasure of Guru Chos dbang, abridged, a hundred thousand times; **the feast offering of the tenth of the waxing and the waning half of the month (ཟླ་བ་ཡར་མར་གྱི་ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཚོགས་མཆོད, zla ba yar mar gyi tshes bcu'i tshogs mchod) without a break**; **on the twenty-ninth (ཉེར་དགུ, nyer dgu; the words that follow, «ནམ་ཚེས་ལ», are read here as "or on the days like it", not certain) the poison torma of the Khro chu protector (ཁྲོ་ཆུའི་དུག་གཏོར, khro chu'i dug gtor) and the regular offering and petition to the Dharma protectors (ཆོས་སྲུང་གི་གསོལ་མཆོད, chos srung gi gsol mchod)**; and, from the instruction of the Mind-clearing Sarasvatī rite in the Sngags kyi be bum, the offering to Sarasvatī (དབྱངས་ཅན་མཆོད་པ, dbyangs can mchod pa) **without a break on the fifteenth (ཚེས་བཅོ་ལྔ, tshes bco lnga) and on the day of Mars (གཟའ་མིག་དམར, gza' mig dmar) or on the like dates**. At the year's end, in the month of the exorcist's torma-throwing (སྔགས་པ་ཟོར་འཕེན་གྱི་ཟླ་བ, sngags pa zor 'phen gyi zla ba), the torma-averting rite of the Eight Pronouncements, the gathering of the Sugatas (བཀའ་བརྒྱད་བདེ་གཤེགས་འདུས་པ, bka' brgyad bde gshegs 'dus pa), always for seven days. The passage then goes on to what he had built and printed.

The author is a modern master of Mipham's lineage; the source tells the practice of Mipham himself, not a rule of a house.

## <a id="mrs-p117"></a>mrs:p117 Mipham's passing: he stayed to the 29th of the Saga month; on the 29th, a Friday under Snar ma, his body came to rest

*Read: etext, 2026-11-24.*

> ས་ག་ཟླ་བའི་ཚེས་ཉེར་དགུའི་བར་བཞུགས། ཚེས་ཉེར་དགུའི་གཟའ་པ་སངས་དང་སྐར་མ་སྣར་མའི་ཉིན་ཞབས་སེམས་སྐྱིལ།

Facts only, in the pool's own words (modern copyrighted work). Dilgo Khyentse's short life of Mipham, in its account of the last act (the eighth of the life's acts), says that Mipham, having given up the force that holds his life but blessed the force that sustains it, stayed until the twenty-ninth of the Saga month (ས་ག་ཟླ་བ, sa ga zla ba, the fourth month); that on the twenty-fifth day the explanation of the Kālacakra commentary was finished; and that on the twenty-ninth, a Friday (གཟའ་པ་སངས, gza' pa sangs) under the mansion Snar ma (སྣར་མ, snar ma), his body came to rest: with the gesture of teaching the Dharma, at sunset, he entered the original clear light (འོད་གསལ, 'od gsal). The funeral rites were done in the third of the joys of the waxing half of the fifth month. The text had earlier said that on the eighteenth of the fourth month Khenpo Kunpal came with the printed Kālacakra volumes.

The date is stated as the twenty-ninth, and no thirtieth is named. The two prints of the text (MW8LS20744 etext p. 117; MW3CN5285 etext p. 307-308) agree word for word on it. The year is the water-mouse (ཆུ་བྱི, chu byi), his age sixty-seven by the text's count.
