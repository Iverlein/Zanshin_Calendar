(v. 124, ending:) ...[the letters], with the part free of vowels, the drop and the nāda, in sequence: the speech, the mind and the gnosis of the pure body born of bliss, the three vajras, abide in existence. 124.

(v. 125:) Waking and dream, and again the other, [deep sleep], and the nature of the fourth: [in the first two] clinging to the body's place and to the breath, examining objects; [in the third] the unmoving clinging to mind; [in the fourth] abiding in gnosis, from close union with a woman the awakening-mind melts and moves for a moment. ## <a id="kcd-11-p221"></a>kcd.11:p221 The Vimalaprabhā's opening verses: the ascetic's seed leaks in dream and in waking

*Read: machine, 2026-11-10 (RD361): Derge Tengyur vol. 11 (I1327) etext p. 221 against Yigdzin-1, 99.5 % agreement; not read by eye on the scan.*

> ཇི་ལྟར་ལོ་ཡི་མཚམས་སུ་རྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །རི་དགས་ཟས་ཅན་འཁྲིག་པའི་བདེ་བ་ཚོལ། །རྡོ་ཡི་ཕྱེ་མ་དག་གི་ཁ་ཟས་ཅན། །ཕུག་རོན་རྣམས་ནི་རྟག་ཏུ་བྱེད་པ་སྟེ། །ལན་ཅིག་རྟག་ཏུ་ཁུ་བ་ལྟུང་བའི་ཕྱིར། །གཉིས་ཀ་ལ་ཡང་མཆོག་གི་བདེ་བ་མེད། །དེ་བཞིན་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་དང་འདོད་ལྡན་ལ། །རྨི་ལམ་སད་པ་དག་ལས་འཛག་ཕྱིར་རོ། །ཇི་ལྟར་གཉིད་ལོག་ལ་ཡང་སྦྲུལ་གྱི་ནི། །སོས་བཏབ་མི་འཚོ་དེ་བཞིན་དཀའ་ཐུབ་པ། །ཁུ་བ་སྡུད་པ་མེད་ཕྱིར་སྟོབས་མེད་མའི། །སྐྱེ་གནས་དེ་ཡིས་ཉམས་པར་བྱས་པའོ། །

Just as at the junction of the year, on the wide road [the sense of «རྒྱ་ལམ» is not settled], the flesh-eating beasts seek the pleasure of union, and the pigeons, whose food is powdered stone, do it always: since the seed falls each time or always, neither has the supreme bliss. So for the ascetic and the one with desire, because [the seed] leaks in dream and in waking. As a sleeping man's [two lines on a snake, «སྦྲུལ་གྱི་ནི། སོས་བཏབ་མི་འཚོ», not settled]: so the ascetic, for lack of gathering the seed, is without strength, and the womb's door has wasted it. As cloth of asbestos thread (རྡོ་དྲེག) is cleansed by being burnt in fire, so the mind of the best of men is cleansed by wisdom and gnosis. As quicksilver touched by fire flees, and does not stay unmoving, so the seed that is without the means comes always from touching the womb's door.

(The part that speaks of dream: «རྨི་ལམ་སད་པ་དག་ལས་འཛག་ཕྱིར་རོ», 'because it leaks from dream and waking'. The verse is about loss of seed in the practitioner, not about what a dream shows.)

From that unevenness the life [-wind] is uneven. So if the wind flows in one channel, the left one, by day and by night, then the lord of life is the year of time; with the hundred years of the complete central channel the count of men's lives is reached. If it flows without a break on one side, then at that time the years with a half become the life, and from then he dies. If without a break for a month, with no interruption, it flows in the left channel, then the life becomes three months. Thus the yogin is to know in his own body and speech the moon's course and death by the wheel of time: that means, in the matter of the death omen, the wheel of the twelve spokes, the wheel of the houses; draw it on the ground and in the first petal lay one day of the death omen and thirty-six months of life.

(Vimalaprabhā on the death signs of the individual chapter. The lengths in the first sentences, «one side» above all (ཕྱོགས་གཅིག, one side or one fortnight), are given as the etext has them and are not settled. Having placed these in the five places, [the master] then, in the south, for the sake of the signs of good and bad, has the bed-seat blessed with this mantra (given in the passage in Tibetan letters, not translated). This is the blessing of the bed-seat. Then the master lies down with the head to the west and the feet to the east. There he looks at the signs, by the power of meditation or by dream: if he sees Buddhas, bodhisattvas and goddesses with the hand-signs of the sign (a lute, a ḍāru-drum and so on), then it is good. Or if he sees the vajra ḍākinīs with knife and skull-bowl, or the lords of wrath, destroy as far as the rim of the wheel of protection the māras, the obstructors and the misleading spirits, it is likewise good. If he sees nothing at all, it is middling. If the master or the disciple sees the hungry, thirst-tormented demon kind with long, dried-up bodies, with knife and skull-bowl in hand, who delight in harming good people, then the sign is bad. Having seen that, the vajra master leaves the bed-seat, meditates the wheel of protection in the eastern place of the maṇḍala as before, and gives the great food offering to them by the rite told before.

Here the body that is a gathering of subtle atoms falls into the state of dream and stays; to the mind that is made to enter pain there is no harm at all done to it. This is known throughout the world: in that same state another body, a body of the mind's habit, free from the gathering of subtle atoms, is seen going to another place; if thieves and the like harm the body that has gone to the other place, that harm makes the mind enter pain and the pain makes him weep, though there is no body and no thief; that the knowing of the mind's pain enters self-aware is a great wonder. In the same way those who in dream enjoy great desires have bliss enter their minds, though there is no body and none of the great desires enjoyed: that the knowing of the beings' mind's bliss enters self-aware is itself the marvel. [p. 490] If even the wise cannot examine this knowing, how much less can the deluded examine the gnosis of great unchanging bliss of the yogin, born of the habit beyond saṃsāra and nirvāṇa. If the heretics of evil mind, who delight in the truth of the life-wind, say: «here, in the state of dream the body gathered of subtle atoms has the outgoing and the incoming breath, and by the power of these the three states come to be; it is not from the absence of the outgoing and incoming breath», then let this be examined: if, without the outgoing and incoming breath, the mind had no state of dream, then in the state of death and of fainting, without the outgoing and incoming breath, how could the mind appear for a watch (thun tshod, here a measure of time) and how could the messengers of the lord of death carry off the body and the city of the lord of death appear...

(Vimalaprabhā on the individual chapter's argument that the mind alone, not the breath and the atoms, holds dream, joy and pain. It treats a dream as a proof of mind-only, and names one measure of time, one thun tshod, for the mind's appearing in death and faintness; it has nothing on when a dream comes.)

Then, to know good and bad, make the sleeping place in the south and bless it; the blessing's mantra is given in Tibetan letters (not translated here). Then lay the head to the west and the feet toward the east. By the power of meditation and the like see the good and the bad dream. If he sees Buddhas, bodhisattvas, goddesses holding hand-signs, the flute, the ḍāru-drum and the like, it will be accomplished purely. Or if he sees vajra ḍākinīs, ḍākinīs with knife and skull-bowl, who destroy the obstructors and misleading spirits, it is likewise good. If he sees a little, know it as middling. Then if he sees the kinds of māra, hungry and thirsty and dried-up in the body, with knives in hand, who mock wise men, that is very bad. As soon as he sees it he throws away the sleeping place, begins the offerings and the rest, purifies the food offering with these mantras and gives it.

(The same rite as in the previous passage, in verse, in the Padminī pañjikā (Pad ma can zhes bya ba'i dka' 'grel), which BDRC ascribes to Dus zhabs pa chen po 'Jam pa'i rdo rje; the translation is the Somanātha one (colophon p. 441). The verse, not the prose, names the rite as one done «to know good and bad» (བཟང་ངན་ཤེས་པའི་ཕྱིར).)
