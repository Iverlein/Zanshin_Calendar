Title: in Sanskrit «མྲྀ་ཏྱཱ་པཉྩ་ནོ་པ་དེ་ཤ», in Tibetan "The pith instruction on deceiving death" (འཆི་བ་བསླུ་བའི་མན་ངག, 'chi ba bslu ba'i man ngag); homage to the Blessed Noble Mañjuśrī (འཇམ་དཔལ, 'jam dpal). Then the verses: the Blessed One, in the Dense Array (སྟུག་པོ་བཀོད་པ, stug po bkod pa), understood the ultimate; for beings' sake he became the Śākya (ཤཱཀྱ), who overcame the lord of death (འཆི་བདག, 'chi bdag); homage to the Complete Buddha who gained freedom from age and death and made others free. Then the promise: "For those of weak mind it is hard to understand, and for a wavering mind it is hard: the treatise, with the signs of death, on deceiving the lord of death I shall explain".

The dream verses at the end of chapter 1, Derge pp. 247-249 (the etext's page markers), from the shadow verse to the chapter colophon. The shadow: if one's body is steady but the shadow moves (ཡོ, yo), death in four months, "the learned in the scriptures say" (ལུང་, lung); one who sees his shadow go south dies himself. Then the dreams: "Though not all dreams are true, since they are said to be meaningless, deceiving, wrong and so on, it is not proper to disbelieve them; the god taught it in dreams (རྨི་ལམ་དག་ཏུ་ལྷས་བསྟན་དང་), or else the man who sees dreams as true, trusting dreams, should look at the signs of death."
The signs (the time in the line itself where there is one): climbing, at the end of the dream, the «རྒྱ་སྐྱེགས» (rgya skyegs), the «ཀོ་པི་ད་ར» (ko pi da ra) or the flowering karavira (ཀ་ར་བཱི་ར, ka ra bī ra) - dies in six months; climbing a god's offering post, or a heap of sand or ash - dies likewise; riding a donkey to the south and not returning - his life too; riding a monkey south and waking there - dies likewise; climbing a long tree, a stupa's life-pole (སྲོག་ཤིང, srog shing), an ant-hill, the «ཕྱག་དར» (phyag dar) or a heap in a charnel ground - does not live beyond six months; an angry black girl, and he who dies bound - "this is called the time-sign" (དུས་མཚན, dus mtshan), death on the seventh day; a woman in black and a black man of desire, a garland of karavira seen - goes to the Lord of Death's place; darkness, a pit or a prison, or falling from a tree - he dwells near the lord of death; trees, grass or wood without fruit seen on head and body - no life beyond six months; riding a monkey east and waking - this is his last night; eaten by a dog, a spotted one (the etext's «ཁྱི་ཁྲ»), a vulture, a «ཅེ་སྤྱང» (ce spyang), a demon (སྲིན་པོ, srin po), a hungry ghost (ཡི་དྭགས, yi dwags) or flesh-eater (ཤ་ཟ, sha za), or carried off riding a donkey, pig, she-camel or buffalo - surely from the year's end death; "when a sign is seen in a dream, quiet it with a mind like an illusion" (སྒྱུ་མ་ལྟ་བུའི་བློས་ཞི་བྱ). Then: a man with an iron staff in hand, black, in black clothes, seen before the sleeper - three months; the body anointed with red scent (དྲི་དམར, dri dmar), adorned with a red garland, smeared with sesame oil, terrifying, hair cut, in red clothes, riding a donkey fast south - dies in six months; a scent and a red garland, or red clothes, found in a dream - dies in eight months. Verse: the outer signs of death are beyond counting, "for eight years some may speak, but who is able"; use mantra and the like to deceive death quickly. Colophon: "The first chapter from the Teaching on Deceiving Death composed (sbyar ba) by the teacher Ngag gi dbang phyug grags pa".
