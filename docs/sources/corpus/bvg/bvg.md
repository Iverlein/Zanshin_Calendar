## <a id="bvg-p1-2"></a>bvg:p1-2 The Svapnādhyāya of the Brahmavaivarta in the 1935 Nepali print: the Sāmaveda and the Kaṇva branch's 'puṇyakāṇḍa' (v. 1–4), with the commentator's gloss

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), on the page images (leaf n6 to n8 of the Internet Archive item).*

> कोऽपि कोऽपि च सुस्वप्नस्तत्सर्वं कथय प्रभो ॥ १ ॥ (end of v. 1; the first lines on p. 1)
> ॥ श्रीभगवानुवाच ॥
> वेदेषु सामवेदश्च प्रशस्तः सर्वकर्मसु ।
> तत्रैव कण्वशाखायां पुण्यकाण्डे मनोहरे ॥ २ ॥

Pages 1–2 of the 1935 print: the end of Nanda's question (v. 1: "which dream is good, and every kind of good dream: tell it all, Lord"), then "The Lord said: Among the Vedas the Sāmaveda is best in all rites; **and in that same one, in the Kaṇva branch, in the lovely book/section of merit** (kaṇva-śākhāyāṃ puṇya-kāṇḍe manohare)" (v. 2). The print spells the branch with a short *a*, *kaṇva-*, where the *Śabdakalpadruma* has *kāṇva-* (skd:p470-471).
The Nepali paraphrase under v. 2 (our English): "Hearing this, the Lord Kṛṣṇacandra said: Father Nanda, among the Vedas, for the subject of the rites (kriyā-kāṇḍa), the Sāmaveda is the best of all for all actions; in it too, in the section (**khaṇḍa**) called Kaṇvaśākhā, there is something special; the dream chapter told there I tell you." So the 1935 commentator reads *kaṇvaśākhā* as the name of a section of the Sāmaveda and *puṇyakāṇḍa* as "special", and names no book. Verse 3 (p. 2): "This clearly seen good dream gives full merit as fruit; all of it is written (there), my dear, I shall tell it with a good decision (or: in due order)." Verse 4: "I shall tell the chapter on dreams that gives much merit; a man who hears the dream chapter gets the fruit of a bath in the Ganges."
Doubt: the first word of v. 3 on p. 2 ("suvyakto 'yaṃ ca" or "suvyaktoyaś ca") is not clear in the large type; the Nepali paraphrase takes it as "well seen".

## <a id="bvg-p3"></a>bvg:p3 The spans of the watches in the 1935 print, v. 5–6: the fourth with half a month, glossed fifteen days

*Read: scan, 2026-11-10 (RD357), on the page images (leaf n6 to n8 of the Internet Archive item).*

> स्वप्नस्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरफलप्रदः ।
> द्वितीये चाष्टभिर्मासैस्त्रिभिर्मासैस्तृतीयकैः ॥ ५ ॥
> चतुर्थं चार्धमासेन स्वप्नः स्वात्मफलप्रदः ।
> दशाहफलदः स्वप्नोऽप्यरुणोदयदर्शने ।
> प्रातःस्वप्नश्च फलदस्तत्क्षणं यदि बोधितः ॥ ६ ॥

Page 3 of the 1935 print, verses 5 and 6: the same spans as the Jivananda print (bvp:p662-665) and the *Śabdakalpadruma* (skd:p470-471): the first watch a year, the second eight months, the third three months (the print has the plural *tṛtīyakaiḥ*, "in the third watch's three months"), **the fourth "with half a month"** (*caturthaṃ cārdhamāsena*, an accusative where Jivananda's has *caturthe*), a dream at the dawn's sight ten days, a morning dream at once if one is woken.
The Nepali paraphrase of v. 6 (our English): "A dream seen in the fourth watch gives its fruit in **fifteen days** (*pandhra dinamā*); at the time of the dawn's red, in ten days; a morning dream ..." (the sentence goes on to the next page). So this commentator glosses *ardhamāsa* by fifteen days, the half-month of the Svapnohana, not as a month; the paraphrase of v. 5 gives "a year, eight months, three months" for the first three watches.
The verse numbers 5 and 6 are the print's. On p. 5 (v. 9–11) the print reads the rule of the dream seen and then slept over ("dṛṣṭvā svapnaṃ ca nidrāluḥ ... na labhet svapnajaṃ phalam", v. 10) and that told to a Kāśyapa-gotra man brings misfortune (v. 11).
