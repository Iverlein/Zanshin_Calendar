## <a id="gpu-p131"></a>gpu:p131 Garuḍapurāṇa: no chapter on dreams; where dreams are named

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> (English) "Poverty, misfortune, bad dreams, and anxious thoughts — all these sins are forsooth destroyed by morning ablutions."

*Garuḍapurāṇa* (Dutt's English, 1908): the whole text layer was searched for *dream*, and three Devanagari and Hindi editions for *svapna*, *prathame yāme* and *daśāhena*. The Purāṇa has no chapter on dreams and no verse on the time of a dream's fruit. The dream is named in four places, none about time:

- p. 131 (the daily duties): poverty, misfortune, bad dreams and anxious thoughts are destroyed by the morning bath.
- pp. 289–291 (Yājñavalkya on the signs of one possessed by Vināyaka): he dreams of water and imagines he is bathing in a pool.
- The medicine chapters: the dreams of the three humours (wind-type: talks in sleep; bile: fire; phlegm: water and rocks) and of the sick (lizards, snakes, monkeys, beasts of prey, climbing a hill of ashes, deserted villages).
- The teaching on the three states of consciousness (waking, dream, dreamless sleep) and a few verses on dreams as the place of a name of the Lord (a name said even in a dream destroys sins).

No tie of a dream to a *tithi*, a weekday, a nakṣatra, a month or a season; the humours' dreams are not tied to a season.
