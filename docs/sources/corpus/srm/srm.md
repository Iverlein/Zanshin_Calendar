The First Dalai Lama's instruction on examining a dream through the goddess Śramaṇā (lha mo shra ma Na), Gsung 'bum vol. 6, printed pp. 386-389 (scan images 392-395), machine-read, from the verse of homage to the end of the instruction's own colophon line "it is accomplished by experience".

Opening verse: bowing to the lama, the helmsman of the ocean of oral instruction, the instruction for examining dreams is written. The work has two parts: the preparation and the sadhana.

Preparation. Obstacles are cleared and favourable conditions gathered. A good time of the waxing half of the month (yar ngo'i dus bzang po) is chosen and a clean, secluded place made. A square earth mandala is smeared with fragrant substance, a vase is filled with nectar and clean water, its neck is adorned with white wool, its mouth with clean leafy twigs, and a canopy of blue cloth is spread. Offerings surround it and the objects of the Three Jewels are set out as one can. Conceptual elaboration is cut, body and speech are kept within bounds. Until the main rite is complete one keeps a fast, gives up afternoon food, meat, beer, garlic and onion, above all eating in company, travel, and food from shadowed (grib can) people, and avoids shadows; a four-powers confession is made at the three times of day before the Three Jewels. These points are said to be the key.

Sadhana. Refuge, the awakening mind and the four immeasurables first; emptied by the svabhava mantra; from that state a vase of four precious substances on Mt Meru, the ground all lapis lazuli, with a wish-granting tree above it and the sky beneath the canopy blue. On Meru the goddess Śramaṇā, blue, one face, two hands: the right holds a mirror (rnga yab), the left a branch of the wish-granting tree; seated with legs stretched, leaning on the tree trunk, slender-waisted, full-breasted, smiling and looking at oneself, in silks, jewels and flowers; knowing all that is to be known of the three times. One imagines her showing, directly, every prophecy. Holding seven pure grains threaded on a silk cord in joined palms, with faith and one-pointedness, one asks: "Śramaṇā, sovereign of wisdom, I, named so-and-so, ask you to show me without deceit, now, the place of my birth in a later life", from three to twenty-one times, and imagines the offering placed on the goddess's lap. The neck of the vase is bound and the request, whatever the aim, is made one-pointedly with the mantra «oṃ śramaṇa piśācani vimunda svāhā». If twelve times ten thousand of it are recited as a preliminary, even treasure beneath the earth is said to be seen directly. Between sessions the request is made as before and flowers are scattered.

The sleep. In the evening one exerts oneself especially. The head is turned north, the feet south, the mouth (face) west, the back east; one imagines the head laid in the goddess's lap, the mind not straying, only the matter to be examined in mind; the mantra is recited while falling asleep; the lying down is the holy instruction. One night yields one matter only: first the one weighty matter, and when it is settled, another is put and examined.

What it gives. One may examine for oneself and others the earlier and later births, long or short life, good or bad outcomes, what helps and what harms, all the worldly good and the definite goodness up to awakening.

The way the answer comes. The best: a prophecy in person or an appearance in meditation experience (nyams snang). The middle: a voice sounding in the sky. The lowest: it is shown in a dream. It comes as question and answer, or as signs and hints that are unclear and hard to judge; the unclear are to be judged by discernment, or all the unclear are dropped and the request repeated again and again, after which it becomes clear.

A dream, by the strength of the instruction, is for the time being settled as good or bad; but it can change by the strength of virtue and sin. If it is good, it becomes better still; for a bad one a way of turning it back is asked for, and when that comes the way is followed with effort.

Result. Practising so, it succeeds in 3 nights at best, 5 in the middle, 7 at least. If it does not, it is a sign of heavy shadow (grib) and sin: one guards against shadows, keeps the boundary, and recites confession; a thousand repetitions of the request three times over cannot fail, it is said. Whatever appears is not to be told to others, since then it is unclear; secrecy is important. "There is no instruction of an emanation of Tārā like this one; it was accomplished by experience, as the lama said."
