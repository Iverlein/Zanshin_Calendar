> མཆོད་སྦྱིན་གཏོར་མ་འདི་བཞེས་ལ། །རྣལ་འབྱོར་བདག་ཅག་འཁོར་ བཅས་ལ། །ནད་མེད་ཚེ་དང་དབང་ཕྱུག་དང་། །དཔལ་དང་གྲགས་དང་བསྐལ་པ་བཟང་། །ལོངས་སྤྱོད་རྒྱ་ཆེན་ཀུན་ཐོབ་ཅིང་། །ཞི་དང་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཡི། །ལས་ ཀྱི་དངོས་གྲུབ་བདག་ལ་སྩོལ། །དམ་ཚིག་ཅན་གྱིས་བདག་ལ་སྲུངས། །དངོས་གྲུབ་ཀུན་གྱི་སྡོང་གྲོགས་མཛོད། །དུས་མིན་འཆི་དང་ནད་རྣམས་དང༌། །གདོན་དང་ བགེགས་རྣམས་མེད་པར་མཛོད། །རྨི་ལམ་ངན་དང་མཚན་མ་ངན། །བྱ་བྱེད་ངན་པ་མེད་པར་མཛོད།

(A torma offering verse in a sādhana's opening rite; the same lines recur in the volume on images 17 and 36; whose composition they are is not stated on the page.) "Accept this offering, giving and torma; to us yogins and our following give freedom from sickness, long life and power, glory, fame and a good aeon, the gaining of all vast enjoyments, and the accomplishments of the actions of pacifying, enriching and the rest. Guard me, you of the pledge; be the companion of all accomplishments. Let untimely death and sickness, harmful spirits and obstructors be absent; let bad dreams (རྨི་ལམ་ངན, rmi lam ngan) and bad signs (མཚན་མ་ངན, mtshan ma ngan) be absent; let bad deeds and actions be absent." The remedy for a bad dream here is a request to the guardians, in the same breath as for untimely death, sickness and obstructors; no hour or day is named.

> དང་པོ་མ་ཡི་མངལ་དུ་ཞུགས་པའི་ཚེ། །ནུབ་མོ་ཨ་མ་ཉལ་བའི་རྨི་ལམ་དུ། །རྒྱ་གར་ཡུལ་ནས་ཡིན་ཟེར་ཨ་ཙ་ར། །སྐ་ཅོག་བསྡམས་ཤིང་ཨང་རག་ དཀྲིས་པ་ཞིག །ཕྱག་ཏུ་རྡོ་རྗེ་བུམ་པ་བསྣམས་ནས་སུ། ཁྲུས་བསྩལ་རྡོ་རྗེ་སྤྱི་བོར་བཞག་ནས་ཀྱང་། །རྡོ་རྗེ་སྩལ་འདི་འཛིན་པར་གྱིས་གསུངས་ནས། །སྤྱི་བོའི་གཙུག་ནས་འོད་དུ་ ཐིམ་པར་རྨིས། །ཕ་ཡི་རྨི་ལམ་ཤར་བའི་ངོགས་སུ་ནི། །དམ་པ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་ཟེར་ཧེ་རུ་ཀ །སྨུག་ནག་རུས་པའི་རྒྱན་དྲུག་ལྡན་པ་ཡིས། །རལ་གྲི་བཅུ་གསུམ་ལག་ཏུ་གཏད་ ནས་ཀྱང་། །བུ་ཡི་ལག་ཏུ་སྤྱོད་ཅིག་ཟེར་བ་རྨིས། ཁ་མཚར་ཚུལ་གྱིས་རྨི་ལམ་བཤད་པའི་ཚེ། །མི་མང་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་བྱད་ཁ་ཡིན་འོང་ཟེར། །སྔགས་པ་གདན་དྲངས་བྱད་བཟློག་ སྣ་ཚོགས་བྱས། །སྔར་གྱི་རྨི་ལམ་འདྲ་བ་ལན་གཉིས་བྱུང་། །རེས་འགའ་མ་བཤད་རེས་འགའ་ཀུན་ལ་བཤད། །གཞན་ལ་བཤད་པས་ཁ་ཤིགས་མང་དུ་བརྒྱབ། །རང་གིས་ བསམ་བློ་ཞིབ་མོར་བསྐོར་བ་ལས། །མཚོན་ཆ་འཛིན་པའི་བུ་ཞིག་སྐྱེ་འོང་སྙམ། །མ་ཡི་ལུས་ལ་རྒྱུན་དུ་གནས་པའི་ཚེ། །ཧཱུྃ་ཧཱུྃ་ཞེས་པ་ཡང་ཡང་ཐོས་པ་དང་། །བུད་མེད་མང་། པོས་དར་ཐག་འཐེན་པ་དང་། །ལུས་ལ་འཇའ་འོད་མང་དུ་ཟུག་པ་དང་། །ཟེར་ཐག་ཤར་ལྷོ་ནུབ་བྱང་གང་བ་རྨིས། །དེ་ནས་ལུས་ལ་བྱིས་པ་སྦྲུམ་པ་ ལས༑ །རྨི་ལམ་དེ་ལྟར་ནང་དུ་བཤད་ཙ་ན། །སྔར་ཡང་བྱིས་པ་མང་པོ་ཤི་བའི་ཤུལ། །རྨི་ལམ་དེ་ནི་སུན་ཀ་ཡིན་འོང་བས། །ལུས་ལ་ཡོད་པ་འདི་ཡང་མི་སོས་འདྲ། །སྐུ་རིམ་ ཕྲན་བུ་བྱས་ན་དགའ་འོང་ཟེར། །སྔགས་པ་རྣམས་ལ་སུན་ཟློག་བྱེད་དུ་བཅུག །དེ་ནས་མོ་མ་ཞིག་ལ་མོ་ཞུས་པས། ཁྱོད་ཚང་སྡོད་པའི་ཡུལ་དེ་ལན་འདུག་པས། །མི་རིང་པ་ནས་ རུ་བ་སྤོས་ཤིག་ཟེར། །བྱིས་པ་དེ་ཡང་བོན་ལ་བཅོལ་ན་དགའ། །དེ་སྐད་ཟེར་བས་བོན་པོ་བཙལ་དུ་ཕྱིན།

When I entered my mother's womb (first), in my mother's dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) at night, an ācārya (ཨ་ཙ་ར, a tsa ra) who said he came from India, hair in a topknot bound with a turban, holding a vajra and a vase, bathed me, set the vajra on the crown, said "hold this vajra I give", and dissolved into light at the crown of the head; so she dreamed. In my father's dream a holy one who said he was the Buddha, a Heruka (ཧེ་རུ་ཀ, he ru ka) dark purple-black with the six bone ornaments, put a sword (the etext reads «བཅུ་གསུམ», thirteen: unsettled) into his hand and said "use it in your son's hand"; so he dreamed. When they told the wonder of the dreams, the people said "it is a curse of sorcery (བྱད་ཁ, byad kha)". Mantra-holders (སྔགས་པ, sngags pa) were called and made various counter-rites. Dreams like the first came twice. Sometimes it was not told, sometimes told to all; telling others brought much slander. Turning it over closely myself I thought, "a son who holds weapons will be born". While he lay in the mother's body, "Hūṃ, hūṃ" was heard again and again, many women pulling at silk cords, many rainbow lights piercing the body, rays of light filling east, south, west and north: so she dreamed. Then, since a child was in the body, when the dreams were told within the house, they said: "many children have died here before after such dreams; this dream is a harm (སུན་ཀ, sun ka: the word is not settled), so what is in the body will not live; a little rite of the creation stage will please". The mantra-holders were set to repel it. Then a woman diviner was asked for a divination (མོ, mo): "the place where you live is bad (the etext reads «ལན», not settled); move away soon"; and "if the child is entrusted to a Bon priest (བོན་པོ, bon po) it will be well". So saying, they went to look for a Bon priest.

> དང་པོ་མངལ་དུ་ཞུགས་པའི་དུས། །རྟེན་འབྲེལ་བཟང་པོའི་རྨི་ལམ་ཤར། །བར་དུ་སྐྱེས་ནས་ མཐོང་བའི་ཚེ། །ལྷ་འདྲེ་མི་གསུམ་སྤྲོ་བ་འཕེལ། །ཐ་མ་ལུང་བསྟན་བརྟག་པའི་ཚེ། །རྟེན་འབྲེལ་ཆེ་ཞེས་གནད་ཀྱང་ཚོར། །ཡ་མཚན་ཅན་གྱི་ཁྱེའུ་ཆུང་ལ། །ཆོས་སྐྱོང་དཀར་ ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་དགྱེས། །སྲུང་མས་དཔུང་གཉེན་བགྱིས་པ་ལ། །བན་བོན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྟོག་པ་འཁྲུལ། །གཟུགས་མེད་སྟོང་པའི་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་ལ། །ངར་འཛིན་འགོང་པོས་སྒྲོ་བཏགས་ ཏེ༑ ༑དམིགས་གཏད་ཨ་འཐས་བཟློག་པ་ཡིས། །གྲོགས་སུ་མ་སོང་རྐྱེན་དུ་ལྡང་། །ནག་ཕྱོགས་འབྱུང་པོའི་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་ལ། །ཏིང་འཛིན་བཟློག་པས་མི་བཟློག་ན། །སྐུ་རིམ་མཁན་ པོས་ཅི་ལ་ཕན། །ཆོས་སྐྱོང་གསོལ་མཆོད་མི་ཤེས་པར། །ཟུག་རྔུའི་དམིགས་པས་ཅི་ལ་ཕན། །དག་སྣང་གུས་འདུན་མེད་པ་ཡི། ཁ་ཆོས་ལོ་མས་ཅི་ལ་ ཕན༑ །རྨོངས་ཁྱུའི་ཁ་ཚིག་འགྲིམས་པ་ཡིས། ཁོ་བོར་ལོག་ལྟ་ཅི་ལ་ཕན། །ཕ་ཁྱོད་ཐེ་ཚོམ་ཆེན་པོ་དེ། །རྨི་ལམ་བཟང་པོས་ཅི་ལ་ཕན། །དུས་ངན་སྙིགས་མའི་སྔ་ལྟས་དེ། །སྐྱོ་ བ་ཙམ་གྱིས་ཅི་ལ་ཕན།

Rtsa gsum gling pa's song to his mother, on the same affair (the sorcery charge of the previous passage): "First, at the time of entering the womb, a dream of good dependent arising (རྟེན་འབྲེལ, rten 'brel) appeared. In between, when I was born and seen, gods, spirits and men grew glad. Last, when the prophecy (ལུང་བསྟན, lung bstan) was tested, the point was felt: it is great dependent arising. To the marvellous little boy all the white (virtuous) Dharma protectors were pleased and the guardians made themselves his support; but the thoughts of the Bon and monks went astray." The rest of the page speaks against the counter-rites: "If the dark spirits' tricks cannot be turned back by turning back through contemplation, what use is the performer of the creation stage? ... Father, that great doubt of yours, what use is a good dream to it? That omen (སྔ་ལྟས, snga ltas) of the dark age, what use is mere sorrow?"

At night, in a dream while lying down, a filthy woman (བུད་མེད་རེ་ཐུལ་ཅན, the etext «རེ་ཐུལ», not settled) smeared dirt on my body, darkness and mist closed in, and many other bad things were dreamed. Then at dawn, when the sun was rising (ནམ་ལངས་ཉི་ཤར་དུས), the glow of the body, the signs and marks vanished; all the earlier trained habits, pure visions and clairvoyance were gone, like the traces left when a dream fades, and I became like an ordinary person. My mother was uneasy and said, "the former signs are gone, it must be some obstacle", and the creation-stage rite to remove faults was begun; it was said there was no discomfort from that. The reason the circumstance arose: formerly in the south of Mon, when I was a mantra-holder with a handsome, bright appearance, going to a creation-stage rite, a fierce woman fell for my form and spoke words of desire; I answered with nothing but hateful, rough and abusive speech; that woman of the demon class grew angry...

(Rtsa gsum gling pa, his life in verse, Zhal gsung ma, vol. 30, etext page marker p98 = image 98; printed page 87. The episode: as a boy, in the camp at the monastery, he has been insulted.) «One night, when I lay down in the tent, in a dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam) or an experience-appearance (ཉམས་སྣང, nyams snang), hazy (ཟ་ཟི, za zi), a woman with a wrathful body, adorned with a hundred unbearable signs, came before me with some five attendants and said: "Prepare the feast offering, torma and gathering; tomorrow a good vidyādhara (རིག་འཛིན, rig 'dzin) will come."» The author himself cannot tell whether it is dream or experience-appearance and gives both words, with "hazy" for the quality of the sight.

At the middle-session time (གུང་ཐུན, gung thun) in a dream while lying down (the line before: King Lha bzang raised a great army, the northerners fled in four divisions, the Sog army fell on some of the herders at Na ra gnam mthil, and he fled with the animals to a hill fort): a red man who said he was Mtshar stag dmar po, a btsan, with a white man who said he was Rga rgyal Jo bo and many attendants, and one who said he was Rme rgyal Rta rna in demon dress, pressed in on him and from the four directions and eight corners head-bearing, tusked ones made of many weapons («མགོ་ཅན་སྦུ་གུའི་མཆེ་བ་ཅན», not settled) came gathering; he dreamed that many lands were destroyed. Then the btsan army seized him in an instant and set him in a palace in the middle of a copper-coloured red-rock island, and said: "Now there is no need to seek flight; this war will not harm you; when this war has passed the time to flee will come." At once he woke. In the evening the cattle were driven back and they went home. ... Then, wishing to cut his hair (take vows) when a lama came to collect offerings, in a dream at night (ནུབ་མོ, nub mo) a red man in the form of a btsan said: "Son, you have no deeds that lead to cutting hair before these lamas. To the east-south of this place, at a place called Dpal thugs rten gling, Rngog Blo ldan shes rab's emanation lives; enter the Vehicle of the basic training there. Later, to the west, at Byang 'bri gung rtse, 'Brom ston's emanation (the etext «སྤལ་པ») lives, from whom you will receive the close instruction. Later again, at Rnga yab in the realm of light, Padmasambhava lives, and the meaning will be reached. I am 'Bar ba rdo rje; keep the prophecy in mind. If these signs, before their time has come, are told to people even by a fragment of speech, obstacles will cut you off at a thread; I will always be your helper." At once he woke...

(Before this the monks had heard him recite and said he would go mad with a prophecy without authority; household attendants and some people spoke ill of him; he thought of leaving, then stayed some years at home keeping up his daily practice.) One night in a dream and experience (ཉམས་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ཟ་ཟི, nyams dang rmi lam za zi, "in the flicker of experience and dream") a yogin in the dress of Heruka, holding a vajra phurba in his hand, came through the sky and he dreamed he saw him. He gave a prophecy: "Hey, listen, fortunate son. I am the Mantra-holder Hūṃ nag pa, a yogin of magical power. This is the Nine Expanses of the Great Perfection, the Nine Signs of the whispered lineage, the Nine Empowerments of creation and completion united, the Nine Meanings of the liberating one-knowing, the nine vehicles of the Dharma, the Nine Lineages, the profound essence. That night, at the time of the midday session (གུང་ཐུན, gung thun) in a dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam), a red woman adorned with bone ornaments came like a rainbow from the expanse of the sky and first spoke much in a speech called «མི་གོའི་བརྡ་སྐད» (a sign-language not understood by men? not settled), then said in a song: "Hey, living one of good fortune, I come from Rnga yab. By the command of Guru Rinpoche your one son Buddhajñāna ... you have fortune through all lives; above all at the holy place of Mchims phu, do you not remember the entrusted instruction? When you went at a few years old, do you not remember the prophecy given in person? At thirteen, «རང་ལོ་སྐག་ཐོག» (not settled), you had to take the list (ཁ་བྱང, kha byang) and the sealed command (བཀའ་རྒྱ, bka' rgya); but because of the evil shadow of a demoness the years up to fifteen were veiled. Then at sixteen the profound index had to be given, but through a religious official's agent and thick habits of greed for property, up to eighteen they were veiled. From now, within three years, if you do not reach the four districts of Mdo and Khams, the seal of the treasures of three lives is broken and the seventeen secret stores are in vain. Return now to your place quickly; the son has received his request from the father, the father sends rays of compassion; keep these words in mind." As she finished speaking the woman vanished like a rainbow. Once more at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) in a dream, a red man in the dress of a btsan came and said: "Son of the lineage-holding lord, listen well. The lama you constantly serve is Rngog lo tsā ba Blo ldan shes rab. The reason for your link now: once in India, as fellow students, you joined a pledge and made a prayer; that dependent arising has come together. Now the time has come for each to work for beings; you will go elsewhere; I will carry out your deeds. I am Chu mig byang chub btsan rgod. You will meet an emanation of 'Brom ston; the cycle of the instruction 'Gzer bu' will be received there; the key lies toward Gze rgyal Gangs ri; make the dependent arising and prayer agree there. Do not forget these words." He vanished like a rainbow, so he dreamed. The next morning, to the lama's side...

(At a rock, in an episode that the speaker herself calls "the stage of dream".) At once the rock opened its door; "what is the teaching you need from here?" said the speaker. Mtsho rgyal (Yeshe Tsogyal), looking, said "I will open the mirror of the list's meaning"; the lady stretched out her hand and with a red box bound with seals handed it to him, saying "the prayer and the request are obtained". He put it on his head, took the four empowerments and made a good prayer. As he thought of putting it in his shawl and going, Drag mo (the fierce one) said: "I have held you for seven days; now you are in a dream stage (རྨི་ལམ་སྐབས, rmi lam skabs), so though you take it, it is empty and has no power; come in reality and I will give". The box was handed to the lady; he said "I will take it from behind"; as he came a short way Ye shes mtsho rgyal said: "Now return inside; we two dakinis go to Orgyan and will meet before long." He asked "if this companion does not go with me, who will be my companion?"; she said: "Dependent arising companions are near; in the valley called Rta shod there is a special emanation of the wisdom ḍākinī with the signs of a jewel ḍākinī; by the power of the prayer she has come together once; seek her as dependent arising companion; Devadhara will come to the back door." So saying she went into the sky.

The signs of the two accumulations of merit and wisdom being completed (from the mandala offering of the three kāyas, with the bodhicitta-with-means and the prayer, repeated a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, a hundred thousand times, and so on): the body is at ease, experience glad, the senses clear; though no food and drink is taken, no thought of hunger comes; the six gatherings (consciousnesses) cling little to their objects; bliss and clarity flow without thought; wisdom opens and awareness's play is released; pure visions arise, and in dreams the sun and moon rise, one climbs upward, reaches a meadow of flowers and a garden, many women give food and drink, one gives the breast and looks into a mirror, and one gains the kingdom of a wheel-turning king. "Of these actual experiences in dreams, if not even one of each comes, the work was begun with effort (not truly given)." So it is said. At night (ནུབ་མོ, nub mo) a black woman riding a mule said "stay in this place and I will be your companion", gave a special sacred substance and went; so he dreamed. Next morning, walking among the many rock-clefts, in the cave of the rock-deity seen before there was a small pass-box the size of a thumb; he opened it and looked inside: there were about seven pills, "liberating on tasting, rainbow body" (མྱོང་གྲོལ་འཇའ་ལུས་རིལ་བུ); he ate all but two and put the remainder in a case. Next morning he came to the Chos 'khor gling at Lhasa and at night went to the foot of the Ma ga dhe va (tree). About ten days passed there. At that time, in a dream one night, a red man of fierce form riding a fine horse came before him and said "you must come to the Mon Kha shag 'ug place before long"; he asked "who are you, what is special about you"; the red man replied: "I am Chu mig byang chub, long ago heeded the command of the teacher Padma, the btsan Beng chen myur mgyogs; I come as your protector, entrusted by a fierce pledge. The profound teaching we have guarded, many demon-lines sought openings against it; the great point of dependent arising has been spoilt, yet the sealed signs remain hidden complete. Soon, before long, the demon-emanations that are not treasure take the bodies of iron sin-rākṣasas and from the time of the treasure seal come with harm and hatred... " (the speech goes on to a warning to go secretly; i296-297). "Your companion is now in this market; seek him tomorrow and come back, and I will meet you at Hu yu gha hi la." At once he left. Then before long the sky cleared; the host (གནས་མོ, gnas mo) carried in morning tea and said "Jo jo, was your sleep good?" He said "the dream was like a magical display" (རྨི་ལམ་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་འདྲ་བར་བྱུང). The host said: "you have the five elements in your power; no doubt it was a rough experience called a magical display; for our household it will be an omen of misfortune (རྐྱེན་ལྟས, rkyen ltas)."

That night in a dream while lying down: a leper (མཛེ་ཕོ, mdze pho) of fearful form, naked, forced the door open, breath steaming, hands waving. Then I made clear the Fierce Guru (གུ་དྲག, gu drag) and took the vajra phurba of sky-iron in hand, threw the leper down, concentrated, and drove the phurba into his heart. The leper then spoke: "Hey, hero, loosen your mind; as if offering life and heart, I will act at your command"; and he vanished like a rainbow. Then at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) on waking from the dream, the body was well, the experience clear, and the body's sores were healed. > དེ་སྐབས་ཟ་ཟི་བར་མའི་ཉམས་སྣང་ལ། །མི་བདུད་ཤན་པ་འདྲ་བ་མང་པོ་ཡིས། །ཚོགས་འཁོར་འབུལ་ལོ་ཅུང་ཟད་བཞུགས་དང་ཟེར། །སྙིང་དང་ཀླད་ཁྲག་ཆ་གཅིག་ཕུལ་ནས་བྱུང་།

(Vol. 30, etext page marker p473 = image 473; a treasure-finding episode.) «At that time, in a hazy, in-between experience-appearance (ཟ་ཟི་བར་མའི་ཉམས་སྣང, za zi bar ma'i nyams snang), many human-like bdud and butchers said: "We offer a gathering feast; stay a little", and offered heart and brain with blood.» "Hazy in between" (ཟ་ཟི་བར་མ, za zi bar ma) qualifies the experience-appearance; the sight is of beings that ask for a feast and is not read as an omen in the lines read.

## <a id="tsl-30-i505"></a>tsl.30:i505 Rtsa gsum gling pa's life: at Bal phur, the tulku's rough skag is great this year

*Read: etext, 2026-11-13.*

> འདི་ལོར་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ་རྩུབ་སྐག་ཆེ་ཡིན་པས། །སྲུང་སྐྱོབ་སྐུ་རིམ་མཛད་རོགས་གྱིས་ཤིག་ཟེར།

(A passage of the life told in the first person, in a stay at Bal phur: a messenger from the tulku's household has come.) "This year the tulku's rough skag (རྩུབ་སྐག, rtsub skag) is great, so please do a protective creation-stage rite (སྐུ་རིམ, sku rim)", so they said.

## <a id="tsl-30-i661"></a>tsl.30:i661 Rtsa gsum gling pa's life: a colophon on the tenth day of the early autumn month of the earth-pig year

*Read: etext, 2026-11-13.*

> དུས་དང་མཚམས་པའི་ཟབ་ཆོས་མ་ཧཱ་གུ་རུས་རྡོ་རྗེའི་ལུང་གིས་བསྔགས་པར་མཛད་པ་འདི་ཉིད་ས་ཕག་ལོར་སྟོན་རའི་ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཉིན་ས་སྤྱོད་དོན་གྱི་འོག་མིན་སྦསཾ་ཡུལ་པདྨ་བཀོད་འཁོར་ལོ་ལྔའི་ནང་གསེས་སྤྲུལ་པའི་འཁོར་ལོ་འབྲུག་ལ་མར་མེ་ཀོང་འབུམ་བྲག་ཕ་བོང་གུར་ཕུབ་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་ནུབ་ལོགས་ནས

(From a colophon in vol. 30, after verses on a treasure of the first of the five wheels of the hidden land Padma bkod.) "This Dharma of the profound time and juncture, praised by the prophecy of Mahāguru Rdo rje, on the tenth day of the early autumn (month) of the earth-pig year (ས་ཕག་ལོར་སྟོན་རའི་ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཉིན, sa phag lor ston ra'i tshes bcu'i nyin), in the hidden land Padma bkod, in the emanation wheel, from the west side of the Brug la mar me kong 'bum rock like a pitched tent ..." The sentence goes on to say that he thought to summon (སྤྱན་འདྲེན) the Dharma cycle Yab bka' sku gsum dgongs 'dus from there. «སྟོན་ར» (ston ra) is the month named by its season, the first month of autumn.
