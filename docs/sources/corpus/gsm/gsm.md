The text divides the key points into examining (བརྟག་ཐབས, brtag thabs), the manner of treating and the means of treating. The examining has thirteen sections: the dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam), the auspicious connection (རྟེན་འབྲེལ, rten 'brel), the place and road (ཡུལ་ལམ, yul lam), the body, the changes of form, sleep, the three dirts, the spittle, the flux, the vomit, the tongue, the urine and the pulse.
The first section is the physician's (སྨན་པ, sman pa) own dream at dawn on the night the messenger (ཕོ་ཉ, pho nya) from the sick person's region is to come. A good dream: he wears new clothes, the sun rises, he rides a well-saddled white, blue, grey or goose-coloured horse to a mountain top or to the north-east, meets well-dressed people he knows, hears song, dance and music, is at a feast with offerings in a fine house, sees flowers and leafy trees, and feels only gladness. Then the sick person will certainly not die, whatever the illness. A bad dream: he goes naked, falls into muddy water or mire, is weary in body and mind, kills or sees animals die, hears weeping, sees hatless men in worn clothes, quarrels, hail, the sun going down. Then the sick person will not live and the physician should not go to him.
