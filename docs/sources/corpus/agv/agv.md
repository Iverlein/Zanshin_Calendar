## <a id="agv-p120-121"></a>agv:p120-121 Chapter 42, the end: the time at which the dream was seen, by night and day, the fortnights and the seasons

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09.*

> तत्थ काले पुव्वाधारिते कंसि काले दिट्ठो सुविणो ? त्ति ।
> तत्थ कण्हेसु रत्तिं सुविणो दिट्ठो त्ति [बूया] ।
> सुक्केसु सव्वपभागतेसु य दिवा दिट्ठो सुविणो त्ति बूया ।
> कण्हेसु आदिमूलेसु पदोसे सुविणो दिट्ठो त्ति [बूया] ।
> … गाढेसु अद्धरत्तकालं दिट्ठो त्ति …
> … सुक्के अंतेसु अवरण्हकाले दिट्ठो सुविणो त्ति बूया ।
> … एवं पक्खोपलद्धीहिं सुक्कपक्ख-कण्हपक्खा उवलद्धव्वा भवंति जधा पुव्वमुद्दिट्ठं ।
> उलुउपलद्धीहिं उदू उवलद्धव्वा छप्पि भवंति जधा पुव्वमुद्दिट्ठं ।

Aṅgavijjā ch. 42, Suviṇajjhāya, the end (Puṇyavijaya's edition, 1957, about pp. 120–121). The Prakrit is normalised from the two OCR text layers of the Internet Archive (ids xuqq_angavijja… and dli.ernet.424497), which agree on the sentences marked complete and differ in the lines before them; the dots mark lines too garbled in both to give; brackets a supplied word.

The chapter's method: the diviner reads a dream from the contacts (āmāsa) the enquirer makes with his body or with things at hand, each kind of contact giving one kind of fact (the place of the dream, its direction, whether past, future or present, the person seen, the thing seen). The last item is the time: "In what time was the dream seen?"

- Dark things (kaṇha) touched: it was seen at night; bright (sukka) things and those of every kind of brightness: by day.
- Dark things at the beginning and the root: in the evening (padosa); the deep or thick (gāḍha) ones: at midnight (addharatta-kāla); bright things at the ends: in the afternoon (avaraṇha-kāla); the lines between, for the early morning and midday, are garbled in both layers but are of the same pattern (dark or bright by beginning, middle, end).
- Last: the bright and dark fortnights (sukka-pakkha, kaṇha-pakkha) are to be found by the indications of the fortnight, "as said before"; the six seasons (udu, "as said before") by the indications of the seasons. So, with all the senses, the dream is to be followed out in each of its facts.

What the chapter asks is when the dream was seen, not when its fruit comes; no span of time to the fruit is given in it.

## <a id="agv-p186"></a>agv:p186 Aṅgavijjā ch. 41, the end: the same six-fold time of day for the act of love (the witness of ch. 42's list)

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269).*

> तत्थ काले पुव्वाधारिए कंसि काले रतं ? ति — कण्हेसु रत्तिरतं ति, सुक्केसु दिवा रतं ति बूया, सामेसु संझाकाले रतं बूया,
> कण्हेसु आदिमूलीयेसु पदोसे रतं, सुक्केसु आदिमूलीयेसु अवरण्हेसु रतं, सुक्कमज्झविगाढेसु मज्झंतियए रतं बूया,
> कण्हेसु मज्झविगाढेसु अद्धरत्ते रतं, सुक्केसु अंतेसु अवरण्हे रतं बूया, कण्हेसु अंतेसु पच्चूसे रतं ।

*Aṅgavijjā* ch. 41 (*Rahassapaḍala*, the secret chapter, on love-making), the end: "In what time was it?" **The list stands on p. 185** of Puṇyavijaya's print (lines 28–30; Internet Archive item xpwp_prakrit-text-series-angavijja-vol.-1…, image n300), **not on p. 186** as the id (kept) says; p. 186 (n301) holds the chapter's last lines ("…kaṃsi vā kālaṃsi rataṃ ti? … sabbāṇi aṇugaṃtavvāṇi bhavaṃti", colophon "egatālīsimo sammatto ‖ 41 ‖") and the beginning of ch. 42. Read on the image by RD269, the list as printed (the apparatus notes at the line: for *ādimūlīyesu* "°mūlesu" in the other manuscripts, "kaṇhesu" in Si.). The same pattern as ch. 42 (agv:p190-191), whole: dark things, a night act; bright things, a day act; dusky things (*sāma*), the twilight (*saṃjhā*); dark at the beginning, the evening (*padosa*); bright at the beginning, **the afternoon** (*avaraṇha*), where ch. 42 has the forenoon (*puvvaṇha*): the two chapters differ in the print itself, and the apparatus of this page gives no variant for the word; bright in the deep middle, midday (*majjhaṃtiya*); dark in the deep middle, midnight; bright at the ends, afternoon (so that ch. 41's list has the afternoon twice and no forenoon); dark at the ends, the break of day (*paccūsa*). This chapter's list is a witness to the pattern of ch. 42's, not a proof of its reading of the bright beginning.

No ties to a fortnight or a season here.

## <a id="agv-p190-191"></a>agv:p190-191 Aṅgavijjā ch. 42, the end: the time of the dream in six parts, by dark and bright things, then the fortnight and the season

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269).*

> तत्थ काले पुव्वाधारिते कंसि काले दिट्ठो सुविणो ? त्ति । तत्थ कण्हेसु रत्तिं सुविणो दिट्ठो त्ति ।
> सुक्केसु सव्वप्पभागते य दिवा दिट्ठो सुविणो त्ति बूया । कण्हेसु आदिमूलेसु पदोसे सुविणो दिट्ठो त्ति ।
> क[ण्हेसु] मज्झिमविगाढेसु अद्धरत्तकालं दिट्ठो त्ति । कण्हेसु अंतेसु पच्चूसे सुविणो दिट्ठो ।
> सुक्केसु आदिमूलेसु पुव्वण्हे सुविणो दिट्ठो त्ति बूया । मज्झविगाढेसु सुक्के मज्झण्हे दिट्ठो सुविणो त्ति ।
> सुक्केसु अंतेसु अवरण्हकाले दिट्ठो सुविणो त्ति बूया ।
> एवं पक्खोपलद्धीहिं सुक्कपक्ख-कण्हपक्खा उवलद्धव्वा भवंति जधा पुव्वमुद्दिट्ठं ।
> उतुउपलद्धीहिं उदू उपलद्धव्वा छप्पि भवंति जधा पुव्वमुवदिट्ठं ।
> एवं सव्वाहिं आमास-सद्द-रूव-रस-गंध-फासपडिरूवोवलद्धीहिं आधारयितुं सुविणे सव्वत्ताणुगंतव्वं भवति ॥
> ॥ इति खलु भो ! महापुरिसदिण्णाए अंगविज्जाए सुविणो णामज्झायो बायालीसतिमो सम्मत्तो ॥ ४२ ॥

*Aṅgavijjā* ch. 42, *Suviṇajjhāya*, the end: the time at which the dream was seen. Read on the page images by RD269: Puṇyavijaya's print (Prakrit Text Series, Varanasi 1957), p. 190 lines 30–33 (Internet Archive item xpwp_prakrit-text-series-angavijja-vol.-1…, image n305, the head reads १९०) and p. 191 lines 1–6 (n306, the head १९१, which the OCR of two other layers had printed "121"); the bracket "क[ण्हेसु]" is the editor's own supply. Ch. 42 stands on pp. 186–191 (it begins on p. 186 under ch. 41's colophon). The text above follows the page word for word. The apparatus under the page has three notes on the list (the abbreviations of the editor's manuscripts, Haṃ. and Ta. against Si.): 10 at *saṃvappabhāgate* "°sappabhā°" in Haṃ. Ta.; 11 at the first *majjhima-vigāḍhesu* "°majjhavi°" in Si.; 12 at the second *majjhavigāḍhesu* "°majjhimagā°" in Haṃ. Ta. None concerns the words for the parts of the day.

The list is complete, and has a pattern. The diviner finds the time of the dream from what the enquirer touches: **dark** things (*kaṇha*) mean the night, **bright** things (*sukka*, "of every kind of brightness") the day; and within each, things at the beginning or the root (*ādi-mūla*), in the deep middle (*majjha-vigāḍha*) and at the ends (*anta*) give the part:

| dark things | night | bright things | day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| at the beginning, the root | evening (*padosa*) | at the beginning, the root | forenoon (*puvvaṇha*) |
| in the deep middle | midnight (*addharatta-kāla*) | in the deep middle | midday (*majjhaṇha*) |
| at the ends | the break of day (*paccūsa*) | at the ends | afternoon (*avaraṇha-kāla*) |

So the six parts are evening, midnight, dawn, forenoon, midday, afternoon. Last: the bright and the dark fortnight (*sukka-pakkha*, *kaṇha-pakkha*) are to be found "as said before" by the indications of the fortnight, and the six seasons (*udu*) by the indications of the seasons: the rules for those are in ch. 59 (agv:p259-260). The chapter ends "thus, by all the indications of touch, sound, sight, taste, smell and feel, the dream is to be followed out in all its facts" and with the colophon of ch. 42, "the forty-second chapter, *Suviṇa*, of the *Aṅgavijjā* given by the great man".

The chapter asks when the dream was seen; it gives no span to the fruit. The list ties the dream to the part of the day and night, the fortnight and the season (not to a *tithi*, a weekday or a nakṣatra). This answers RD263's doubt that the lines for the morning and midday were lost: they are the break of day (dark ends) and the forenoon and midday (bright beginning and middle). Ch. 41 (agv:p186) has the bright beginning as the afternoon; ch. 42's print has the forenoon, and the editor's notes on both pages give no variant for that word.

## <a id="agv-p191-193"></a>agv:p191-193 Aṅgavijjā chs. 43–44, travel and its length: counts by fortnights, days, months, years; no dream

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> तत्थ पुरत्थिमायं दिसायं अव्वत्तसद्द-रूवे वा अद्धमासे वा पवासं गमिस्ससि त्ति बूया ।
> दक्खिणायं दिसायं ... पक्खगणणायं पवासं गमिस्ससि त्ति बूया ... दिवसगणणाय ...
> पच्छिमायं दिसायं ... वस्सगणणाय ... मासगणणाय ... एएसु चेव एकवीसाय मासाणं पवासं गमिस्ससि त्ति बूया ।

*Aṅgavijjā* ch. 43 (*Pavāsajjhāya*, travel abroad, pp. 191–192) and ch. 44 (*Pavāsaddhākāla*, the length of the journey, pp. 192–193), read in the same layers. Neither is on dreams.

Ch. 43 asks whether the enquirer will go abroad; the signs (the touches of the feet, shoes, umbrella, vehicle) say yes or no, and near the end it says that the time of the journey is to be found "by the indications of time as said before" (*jadhā kālakālopaladdhīhiṃ*, the rules of ch. 59). Ch. 44 turns the direction of the contact into a unit of counting, in our words: in the east the journey is of half a month; in the south it is counted by fortnights, then by days; in the west and in the north by years, then by months ("these same, twenty-one months"); then the distance (a *gāuya*, half a *yojana*), the place (the king's realm, a country, a town, a camp, a village) and the purpose (his own affairs, another's, a friend's).

The units are the day, the fortnight, the month and the year, never a *tithi* or a weekday; the season and the fortnight are used as counts. The time to the fruit of a dream is not given anywhere in chs. 43–44.

## <a id="agv-p259-260"></a>agv:p259-260 Aṅgavijjā ch. 59, the division of time (the 27th section): the five measures, the fortnight, the six seasons and the months by the diviner's signs

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269), p. 260 lines 19–30 and p. 261 lines 1–18 of the print.*

> तत्थ अवदातेसु सुक्कपक्खं दिवसं वा बूया । कण्हेसु कालपक्खं रत्तिं वा बूया । सामेसु संझं वा पक्खसंधिं वा बूया ।
> णिद्धेसु वासारत्तं बूया, कण्हेसु वि वासारत्तं बूया । सुक्केसु सरदं बूया, पसण्णेसु वि सरदं बूया ।
> सीते हेमंतं बूया, संवुतेसु य हेमंतं बूया । [……सिसिरं बूया, ………वि सिसिरं बूया ।]
> सामेसु वसंतं बूया, मुदितेसु वि वसंतं बूया । लुक्खेसु गिम्हं बूया, उण्हेसु वि गिम्हं बूया ।
> बालेयेसु पाउसं बूया, उवणिद्धेसु णिद्धेसु वा पाउसं बूया ।
> तत्थ मासेसु पुव्वाधारितेसु कतमो मासो ? त्ति । तत्थ पुरिमेसु गत्तेसु कत्तियं वा मग्गसिरं वा पोसं वा बूया ।

*Aṅgavijjā* ch. 59 (*Kālajjhāya*, the chapter of time; the page heads read "ekūnasaṭṭhimo kālajjhāo"), the 27th section (*paḍala*, "kālappavibhāga", "the division of time") of Puṇyavijaya's print (Prakrit Text Series, Varanasi 1957; Internet Archive item xpwp_prakrit-text-series-angavijja-vol.-1…, images n374–n376). It **begins on p. 260 (n375) at line 19, under the 26th section's colophon, and runs on to p. 261 (n376)**, not pp. 259–260 as RD265 and the id (kept) have it; p. 259 (n374) holds the end of the 25th section, in verse. Read on the images by RD269 (clear print); the brackets with dots are the editor's own, a lacuna of the manuscripts for winter's neighbour, *sisira*. It is the rule that ch. 42 means by "as said before" (agv:p190-191).

The section says time is of five measures: the moment (*muhutta*), the day, the fortnight, the month, the year (p. 260: "muhuttappamāṇaṃ divasappamāṇaṃ pakkhappamāṇaṃ māsappamāṇaṃ saṃvaccharappamāṇamiti"), and divides each by the number the contact gives (a moment from a hair or a nail touched; a day from the nose, the brows, eyes, tongue and teeth; a fortnight from the thighs, hands, feet, arms and neck; a month from the back, belly, waist, breast and head, the year from the head raised, p. 260 lines 26–30). Then, on pp. 260–261:

- **Fortnight and day or night** (our words): bright things are the bright fortnight or a day, dark things the dark fortnight or a night, dusky things the twilight or the joint of the fortnights; so the same colour code of ch. 42 serves for the fortnight, as ch. 42's closing lines say.
- **Season** (p. 261, lines 1–5): oily or moist things are the rains (*vāsāratta*, and dark things too), bright and clear ones autumn (*sarada*), cold and covered ones winter (*hemanta*), the dusky and the glad spring (*vasanta*), dry and hot ones the hot season (*gimha*), the *bāleya* ones and the oily or moist ones the *pāusa* (the first rains); after winter a line for *sisira* (the cool season) is lost, the editor's dots; so that the six seasons are *vāsāratta*, *sarada*, *hemanta*, *sisira*, *vasanta*, *gimha* with *pāusa* beside the rains. Then: "in a night or a day, in the bright or the dark fortnight, in any of the six seasons, or in the twilights, the joints of the fortnights or of the seasons, what is asked is to be found by these contacts, and followed out by the sounds and shapes that appear".
- **Month** (p. 261, lines 6–18), by the direction of the contact: from the front or east, Kattika, Maggasira or Posa; from the south, Māha, Phagguṇa, Cetta or Vesāha; from the west, Jeṭṭhāmūla, Āsāḍha or Sāvaṇa; from the left (*vāmesu*), Poṭṭhapada or Assayuja (Assoja); by the kind of thing touched: firm things, Phagguṇa, Āsāḍha or Poṭṭhapada; moving things, Sāvaṇa; oily or moist things, Sāvaṇa, Poṭṭhapada, Assoja or Kattiya; dry things, Cetta, Vesāha, Jeṭṭhāmūla or Āsāḍha; cold and covered things, Maggasira, Posa or Māha; the year (*saṃvaccharo*) from a name, *aggeya*, *somma* or *posa* (so printed); "with the rites of the gods of the nakṣatras" (*nakkhatta-devaya-kammovayāra*) among the tests. Then the counts: a fortnight from what is sent out (*nīhāra*), a month from the upright and still, three fortnights from food, a day from one-ness, two days from pairs, and so up to a week (p. 261 lines 19–30).

So the work does tie the time of an event to the fortnight, the season and the lunar month by the diviner's signs; it does so for what is *asked*, and for the dream it makes the fortnight and season facts of the dream's own time (ch. 42), not rules of its fruit. No *tithi* by number and no weekday.
