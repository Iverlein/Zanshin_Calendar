## <a id="mtp-p453-455"></a>mtp:p453-455 Matsyapurāṇa 242, the Svapnādhyāya: the watches and the spans (vv. 17–20)

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269), verses 17–20 on p. 494 and the pages' set-up; the summary of the rest of the chapter below: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265), checked against the images only for its sense.*

> स्वप्नास्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरविपाकिनः । १७
> षड्भिर्मासैर्द्वितीये तु त्रिभिर्मासैस्तृतीयके ।
> चतुर्थे मासमात्रेण पश्यतो नात्र संशयः ॥ १८
> अरुणोदयवेलायां दशाहेन फलं भवेत् ।
> एकस्यां यदि वा रात्रौ शुभं वा यदि वाऽशुभम् ॥
> पश्चाद्दृष्टस्तु यस्तत्र तस्य पाकं विनिर्दिशेत् ।
> तस्माच्छोभनके स्वप्ने पश्चात्स्वप्नो न शस्यते ॥

*Matsyapurāṇa* ch. 242, the *Svapnādhyāya*, 35 verses, of the Ānandāśrama edition (Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series 54; Internet Archive item ozcr-matsya-purana-aa-54-ananda-ashram), **pp. 493–494, not pp. 453–455** as RD265 had it from the OCR layer: the chapter opens on p. 493 (image n509, verses 1–9 on it, after ch. 241's colophon "…yātrānimittakadehaspandanaṃ nāma ekacatvāriṃśadadhikadviśatatamo 'dhyāyaḥ ‖ 241 ‖") and ends on p. 494 (n510) with the colophon "iti śrīmātsye mahāpurāṇe yātrānimitte svapnādhyāyo nāma dvicatvāriṃśadadhikadviśatatamo 'dhyāyaḥ ‖ 242 ‖ āditaḥ ślokānāṃ samaṣṭyaṅkāḥ ‖ 12124 ‖" ("the chapter on dreams, in the omens of the king's march"); the page heads read "[242 adhyāyaḥ]". The chapter is introduced by Manu's question ("svapnākhyānaṃ kathaṃ deva gamane pratyupasthite | dṛśyante vividhākārāḥ kathaṃ teṣāṃ phalaṃ bhavet") and Matsya's answer; the next chapter, 243, is the omens of the march. The verses above are 17–20 as the print numbers them: 17 and 18 carry their numerals, 19 and 20 run on without (the next numeral printed is 21). Against the *Agni* (agp:p280-281) this print has *tasmāc chobhanake svapne* in the last line and, in 17–18, *tṛtīyake* and *māsamātreṇa … paśyato nātra saṃśayaḥ*.

In our words: the chapter first lists the bad dreams (14 verses: a plant growing from the body, shaving, nakedness, falling from a height, riding a boar, ass or camel, the mother's womb, the sun and moon falling, anger of gods or brahmans, being embraced and others) and the remedies (telling it to no one, sleeping again, a bath with sesame, burnt offering, brahmans honoured, praise of Vāsudeva, hearing the release of the king of elephants), then the times: the first watch of the night, a year; the second, six months; the third, three months; the fourth, a month ("paśyato nātra saṃśayaḥ", "of him who sees it, no doubt"); at the hour of dawn (*aruṇodaya-velā*), ten days. If in one night one sees a good and a bad dream, the one seen last decides, so after a good dream one does not sleep again. The rest (21–35) is the good dreams: a mountain, a palace, an elephant, a horse or a bull climbed, white flowers, the sun, moon and stars seized, victory in a lawsuit, at dice and in battle, blood, milk, being anointed king, and so on.

This is the same text as the *Agnipurāṇa*'s (agp:p280-281, which is on pp. 281–282 of its print) with one difference in the spans: the *Matsya* of this edition has a month for the fourth watch (and "tṛtīyake" for the third), while the *Agni* has half a month (and "triyāmikāḥ"). The *Adbhutasāgara's* quotation under the names of the *Matsya* and the *Viṣṇudharmottara* has "triyāmikāḥ … caturthe māsamātreṇa pacyante": the words of the *Agni* for the third, those of the *Matsya* for the fourth, so the two Purāṇas are not kept apart even by the compilers. The *Adbhutasāgara* (adb:p513-515) quotes the *Matsya* and the *Viṣṇudharmottara* together for this verse, which gives a year, six months, three months and a month. No tie to a lunar day, weekday, nakṣatra or month; no span for midnight or "the cows' release".

Doubt: the verses other than 17–20 were looked through on the images for the sense of the summary, not read syllable by syllable; the apparatus under p. 494 (readings of other manuscripts) was not studied.
