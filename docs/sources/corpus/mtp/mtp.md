## <a id="mtp-p453-455"></a>mtp:p453-455 Matsyapurāṇa 242, the Svapnādhyāya: the watches and the spans (vv. 17–20)

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> स्वप्नास्तु प्रथमे यामे संवत्सरविपाकिनः ।
> षड्भिर्मासैर्द्वितीये तु त्रिभिर्मासैस्तृतीयके ।
> चतुर्थे मासमात्रेण पश्यतो नात्र संशयः ॥
> अरुणोदयवेलायां दशाहेन फलं भवेत् ।
> एकस्यां यदि वा रात्रौ शुभं वा यदि वाऽशुभम् ।
> पश्चाद्दृष्टस्तु यस्तत्र तस्य पाकं विनिर्दिशेत् ॥
> तस्माच्छोभने स्वप्ने पश्चात्स्वप्नो न शस्यते ।

*Matsyapurāṇa* ch. 242, the *Svapnādhyāya*, 35 verses, pp. 453–455 of the Ānandāśrama edition (the colophon: "iti … Mātsye mahāpurāṇe yātrā-nimitte svapnādhyāyo nāma dvicatvāriṃśadadhikadviśatatamo 'dhyāyaḥ", "the chapter on dreams, in the omens of the king's march"). The next chapter, 243, is the omens of the march seen on setting out (what is seen and heard at the door). Devanagari text layer; the verses above are 17–20 as the layer numbers them.

In our words: the chapter first lists the bad dreams (14 verses: a plant growing from the body, shaving, nakedness, falling from a height, riding a boar, ass or camel, the mother's womb, the sun and moon falling, anger of gods or brahmans, being embraced and others) and the remedies (telling it to no one, sleeping again, a bath with sesame, burnt offering, brahmans honoured, praise of Vāsudeva, hearing the release of the king of elephants), then the times: the first watch of the night, a year; the second, six months; the third, three months; the fourth, a month ("paśyato nātra saṃśayaḥ", "of him who sees it, no doubt"); at the hour of dawn (*aruṇodaya-velā*), ten days. If in one night one sees a good and a bad dream, the one seen last decides, so after a good dream one does not sleep again. The rest (21–35) is the good dreams: a mountain, a palace, an elephant, a horse or a bull climbed, white flowers, the sun, moon and stars seized, victory in a lawsuit, at dice and in battle, blood, milk, being anointed king, and so on.

This is the same text as the *Agnipurāṇa*'s (agp:p280-281) with one difference in the spans: the *Matsya* of this edition has a month for the fourth watch (and "tṛtīyake" for the third), while the *Agni* has half a month (and "triyāmikāḥ"). The *Adbhutasāgara's* quotation under the names of the *Matsya* and the *Viṣṇudharmottara* has "triyāmikāḥ … caturthe māsamātreṇa pacyante": the words of the *Agni* for the third, those of the *Matsya* for the fourth, so the two Purāṇas are not kept apart even by the compilers. The *Adbhutasāgara* (adb:p513-515) quotes the *Matsya* and the *Viṣṇudharmottara* together for this verse, which gives a year, six months, three months and a month. No tie to a lunar day, weekday, nakṣatra or month; no span for midnight or "the cows' release".
