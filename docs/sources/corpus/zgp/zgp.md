Zhe chen rgyal tshab's text on the tenth day (BDRC's title: Tshes bcu'i phan yon, ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཕན་ཡོན), Paro print of his collected works, vol. 13, printed p. 536 whole and the first line of p. 537 (images 540–541 of I2883). Machine reading (Yigdzin-1), given as the machine wrote it and not checked on the scan: «ཚེ་བཅུ» (for ཚེས་བཅུ), «ནང་རེ་དགོངས་རེ» and «རཏ་གླིང» are its readings. The passage opens in the middle of a sentence; p. 535 (image 539), where the text begins, was not read.

"... it is taught that one who wishes [for that] must hold chiefly to the offering of the tenth day (ཚེས་བཅུ, tshes bcu) of the waxing half (ཡར་ངོ, yar ngo). The night of the waning half (མར་ངོ, mar ngo) and the day of the waxing half are special great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) on which accomplishments are quick, so the great vajra holders of India and Tibet, of the new schools and the old, also kept the offering of the time (དུས་མཆོད, dus mchod) on the tenth of the waxing and of the waning half without a break, as their practice. In particular, the great occasion of the one who knows the three times, him of Uḍḍiyāna (ཨོ་རྒྱན, o rgyan), the emanated form of Amitābha, the vajra speech of all the Victors, whose wondrous life is famed through oceans of realms and whose kindness to the beings of the six classes, and to us of the Land of Snows above all, is greater than the Buddha's: the occasion on which he showed his inconceivable wondrous deeds and on which he thinks of his followers with special compassion is, of the two, waxing and waning, this tenth of the waxing half. The Northern Treasure (བྱང་གཏེར, byang gter) says: 'If happiness is wished for Tibet: when the tenth of the waxing half dawns, an emanation of Padma of Uḍḍiyāna comes.' The Tshes bcu'i bskul byang (ཚེས་བཅུའི་བསྐུལ་བྱང, tshes bcu'i bskul byang), a treasure of Ratna gling pa, says: 'When I go to the land of the rākṣasas, to the king, ministers, mantrins and disciples of Tibet I give my oath that on the tenth of every [month] that comes I will come myself. Padma 'byung gnas does not deceive others: followers, give up doubt.' And further he promised: 'Every morning and every evening I come for Tibet's sake; I come riding on the radiance of the sun's rays as they rise and gather; at the time of the tenth of the waxing half I come in person.'"

> སྟེར། །མི་ཚེ་བླ་མ་སྒྲུབ་པ་ལ། །བསྐྱལ་ན་ཚེ་ཡི་དུས་བྱེད་ཚེ། །ཨོ་རྒྱན་ང་ཡི་ཐུགས་ཀར་ཐིམ༔ ཞེས་དང་། པད་གླིང་གཏེར་བྱོན་ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཕན་ཡོན་ལས༔ དུས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཚེས་བཅུ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ༔ དབང་ཁྲིད་ལ་སོགས་དཀར་པོ་དགེ་བའི་ལས༔ བྱས་ན་ཡོན་ཏན་ཁྲི་དང་འབུམ་དུ་འགྱུར༔ འཆུགས་མེད་སྤྲེལ་ལོ་སྤྲེལ་ཟླའི་ཚེས་བཅུ་ལ༔ པདྨ་ང་ཉིད་འབྱོན་པའི་ཚེས་གྲངས་ཡིན༔ འདི་ལ་ཚེས་བཅུ་བྱས་པའི་ཕན་ཡོན་ལྷག་པར་ཆེ༔ ཞེས་གསུངས། སྤྲེལ་ཟླའི་ངོས་འཛིན་ཡང་། ཕུག་ལུགས་རྗེས་འབྲང་དབུས་གཙང་མཁས་པ་ཡོངས་ལ་གྲགས་ཤིང་གྲུབ་པ་ནི་ཧོར་ཟླ་ལྔ་པ་སྣྲོན་གྱི་ཟླ་བ་འདི་ཉིད་ཡིན་པས། །འགྲན་ཟླ་དང་བྲལ་བའི་མཁས་གྲུབ་ཆེན་པོ་ཆོས་རྒྱལ་གཏེར་བདག་གླིང་པ་ཡབ་སྲས་སོགས་ཚད་མར་གྱུར་པའི་སྐྱེས་ཆེན་ཁྱད་པར་དུ་འཕགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཚེས་བཅུའི་དུས་མཆོད་ཟླ་ཁོངས་འདིར་མཛད་པ་བཞིན།

"'... give. If a human life is spent accomplishing the guru, at the time of death one dissolves into my heart, Uḍḍiyāna's.' And from the Benefits of the Tenth Day (ཚེས་བཅུའི་ཕན་ཡོན, tshes bcu'i phan yon), a treasure of Padma gling pa: 'On all the tenths, the king of times, if white, virtuous deeds are done, empowerment, instruction and the rest, the qualities become ten thousandfold and a hundred thousandfold. The tenth of the monkey month of the monkey year (སྤྲེལ་ལོ་སྤྲེལ་ཟླ, sprel lo sprel zla), without mistake, is the date on which I, Padma, come myself: the benefit of keeping the tenth on it is greater still.' As for identifying the monkey month (སྤྲེལ་ཟླ, sprel zla): what is known to and established among all the learned of Dbus and Gtsang who follow the Phugpa tradition (ཕུག་ལུགས, phug lugs) is that it is this very month of Snron (སྣྲོན, snron), the fifth Hor month (ཧོར་ཟླ་ལྔ་པ, hor zla lnga pa); so, just as the unrivalled great scholar and adept, the Dharma king Gter bdag gling pa, father and sons, and the other outstanding great beings who are authorities held the offering of the time (དུས་མཆོད, dus mchod) of the tenth day within this month, ..."
