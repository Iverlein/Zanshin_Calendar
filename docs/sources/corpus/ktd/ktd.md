## <a id="ktd-i36-37"></a>ktd:i36-37 Dudjom Rinpoche's life: the father's dream before the birth that gave the name

Facts only, in my own words (modern copyrighted work; the Tibetan is not quoted). Dudjom Rinpoche's own account of his name, given in his own words: he was born on a Sunday (གཟའ་ཉི་མ, gza' nyi ma) and the mantra-holders called to his birth rite named him Nyi ma rgyal mtshan; his father, the elder khenpo-tulku, said the name was good, because before the child was born a ḍākinī had given the father the name in a dream (རྨི་ལམ, rmi lam): a beautiful woman said a son would come to him and gave the name for him, and in the dream a very clear mirror with a golden vajra set on it was put into the father's shawl; the mirror is the meaning of wisdom (ཡེ་ཤེས, ye shes) and the vajra set upright gives Ye shes rdo rje, so the name should be given; it was given and he is so known; in childhood the villagers called him the tulku Ye shes. The tie is to the time before birth and to the father as dreamer; no hour or day is given. The lines at the end of image 36 give his birth as on the tenth day (ཚེས་བཅུ), the great day of Padmasambhava's self-arising, with rays of light and many wonders, the mother unharmed.

## <a id="ktd-i181-184"></a>ktd:i181-184 Dudjom Rinpoche's own account: the dawn dream of the verse, the phurba given in a dream at thirty-four, and the outline written at forty-five

Facts only, in my own words (modern copyrighted work; the Tibetan is not quoted). Dudjom Rinpoche's own account, quoted in the biography («རྗེ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཞལ་ནས», in his own words), of dreams that gave him texts or practices, with their times. (1) After disciples had made a hundred dākinī feast offerings (ཚོགས་བསྐང་བརྒྱ་ཕྲག) and the creation-stage practice, "at dawn at the end of that night" (ནུབ་ཀྱི་ཐོ་རེངས) a woman in a dream spoke a verse of offering, confession and dedication that he still had when he woke; he wrote it down; the day after, he was freed from an illness: a good omen followed. (2) At thirty-four, in the Fire-Ox year of the sixteenth cycle (རབ་བྱུང་བཅུ་དྲུག, rab byung bcu drug), while practising the phurba accomplishment of medicine nectar at the cave Seng phug of Spa gro stag tshang in Mon, in a dream a young woman in fine ornaments, saying she was Mtsho rgyal, handed him a hand's-length sky-iron phurba, said it was Guru Rinpoche's real instrument of subjugation, and told him he had been given it earlier; he took it by force, wished first for an empowerment, touched it to crown, throat and heart, and it merged with his body, so that below the chest he felt his body a blazing iron phurba; the girl said this was the empowerment and blessing, the treasury of transmission, tantra and instruction in one, not to be let lapse; he woke as she tore it from his hand; at once many old habits came back and the whole phurba teaching became clear in his mind; he judged it laborious to bring such new teachings down in this age and left it. (3) Later, at forty-five, on the tenth day of the Earth-Mouse miracle month (the year of his age, ས་བྱི་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་ཟླ་བའི་ཚེས་བཅུ), urged by many circumstances, he wrote only a brief outline of the phurba root text; after finishing it, a lovely «གཡུའི་ཁྱི་མོ» (g.yu'i khyi mo, the words not settled) lady came before him in an experience he calls «འཁྲུལ་སྣང» (an appearance, not named a dream) and spoke verses of encouragement and prophecy.

## <a id="ktd-i185-187"></a>ktd:i185-187 Dudjom Rinpoche's own account: the consort's dreams and his own dream at dawn that led to a supplication

*Read: machine, 2026-11-10 (RD363): Yigdzin-1 over all 251 images of BDRC I1GS105464 (image N = N; the scans fetched with bdrc.py), searched for the dream words; the passage is read from the OCR text only, not on the scan, and recorded as facts in my own words, the Tibetan not quoted.*

Facts only, in my own words (modern copyrighted work; the Tibetan is not quoted). Dudjom Rinpoche's own account of how he came to write a supplication-and-prayer text, told in dreams and dates. On the evening of a day in the waxing half (ཡར་ཚེས་ཀྱི་དགོང་མོ) of the month Smin zla in a Water-Pig year (the year is not placed in the page), a girl who regularly appeared in the dream of his consort, Shes rab ma, came and said "now help to write a supplication to the Precious One"; on the night of the tenth of Smin zla she came again and asked why he had still not written it; he answered that he had no one to supplicate and so had left it; she told him to write, even briefly, a supplication without regard to length. He thought a supplication on the heart-instruction of the time, for protection from plague, famine and war, was needed, but busy and distracted he left it. On the night of the tenth of Rgyal zla she came as before and said not to set aside the supplication he had asked about: "there is great need". He then decided to begin on the fifteenth day; on the evening of the fourteenth (ཚེས་བཅུ་བཞིའི་དགོང་མོ) he supplicated Guru Rinpoche single-mindedly and lay down praying that the work be blessed to be to purpose. At dawn (ཐོ་རངས) in his own dream he sat in a great temple-like house when a white young man in white, hair loose, came from inside through the door playing very sweet music and dancing a lively right-turning dance, and sang verses: to establish the teaching, set it in one's own mind; the Buddha is found at the bottom of the mind; to visit pure lands, train out ordinary clinging, the pure land is close by; strive in the heart of the teaching; the fault in oneself is hard for oneself to see, to draw out one's faults is one key of instruction; as the faults diminish, the good qualities grow. At the end of a verse a hard stroke on the instruments woke him; the words stayed; he understood them as a teaching on what to take and drop; he regretted that he had not asked more of the Guru, whom he had met in person once, and wrote the text in the form of his experience. Times named in order: waxing-half evening; the tenth night; the tenth night of the next month; the fourteenth evening; the dawn after; and the fifteenth as the day to begin.
