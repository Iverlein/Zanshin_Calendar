## <a id="neg-p14-16"></a>neg:p14-16 Svapnacintāmaṇi 1.15–17: the watches of the night and the span to the fruit, with Negelein's parallels

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269); the Arabic and Greek report of pp. XX–XXI: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> svapnaḥ prathame yāme | dṛṣṭaḥ saṃvatsareṇa phaladaḥ syāt ||
> saṃvatsarā-'rdha-phalado | dvitīya-yāme triyāminyāḥ || 15
> māsatrayeṇa rātreḥ | tṛtīyayāme nṛṇāṃ sphuṭaṃ phaladaḥ |
> māsai-'ka-phalo dṛṣṭaś | caturthayāme bhavet svapnaḥ || 16
> carama-niśā-ghaṭikā-dvaya-samaye phalati dhruvaṃ daśāhe 'ntaḥ |
> sūryodaye tu dṛṣṭaḥ sadyaḥ phaladāyakaḥ svapnaḥ || 17

Jagaddeva's *Svapnacintāmaṇi*, book 1, verses 15–17, in Negelein's Roman transliteration, with his German beside, pp. 14–16 of the 1912 print (Internet Archive item von-negelein-der-traumschlussel-des-jagaddeva: p. 14 = image n43, p. 15 = n44, p. 16 = n45; image = page + 29). Read on the page images by RD269. v. 15 is printed with the readings "dṛṣṭaḥ saṃvatsareṇa phaladaḥ syāt" (his note: B °tsaraika-, C °tsaraikaḥ; A dvitīyāme, C dvitīyāyāme; BC triyāmāyāḥ), v. 16 with the second line as above, v. 17 "daśāhe 'ntaḥ" with the notes B varame, C dhrutam, ACD daśāhena. The German beside v. 17: "a dream dreamt in the time of the last two *ghaṭikā* (= the last 48 minutes) of the night goes surely into fulfilment within ten days; a dream seen at sunrise, however, is fulfilled at once"; "Cf. 1.15".

The verses of the work itself give six times, by the part of the night in which the dream was seen: the first watch (*yāma*), a year; the second, half a year; the third, three months; the fourth, a month; in the last two *ghaṭikā*s of the night, within ten days; at sunrise, at once. His note on v. 17 adds that the half-verse "aruṇodaya-velāyāṃ sadya eva phalaṃ bhavet" (at the hour of dawn the fruit is at once) is ascribed in manuscript r (fol. 151a) to the sage Bṛhaspati, and that the Atharva-Veda appendix (AP 68.2.55) names kings' sons, thieves and courtiers as the groups for whom dreams are fulfilled.

**Negelein's parallels** (his letters are explained in the same print, Introduction p. XIX, below; every line is as the page prints it):

- **b 5–6**, the *Svapnādhyāya* of the *Brahmavaivartapurāṇa* (bvp:p662-665): "svapnas tu prathame yāme saṃvatsaraphalapradaḥ | dvitīye cā'ṣṭabhir māsais tribhir māsais tṛtīyake || caturthe cā'rdhamāsena svapnaḥ syāt tu phalapradaḥ | daśāhe phaladaḥ svapno 'py aruṇo-'daya-darśane | prātaḥ svapnaś ca phaladas tatkṣaṇaṃ yadi bhoditaḥ ||": a year, eight months, three months, half a month, ten days at dawn.
- **f 2** (very like m 2–4 and n 2–4, which differ only in 2d "tribhir māsaiḥ triyāmakaḥ"; q 12–14): "svapnās tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ | dvitīye cā'ṣṭabhir māsaiḥ ṣaḍbhir māsaiḥ triyāmakaiḥ(!) || caturtha-prahare svapne(!) māsena phaladāḥ smṛtāḥ | aruṇo-'daya-velāyāṃ daśāhena phalaṃ bhavet || govisarjana-velāyāṃ sadyaḥ svapno vidhīyate"; he compares o 2–3. The manuscript f is the Leipzig 1180, "called the *Svapnādhyāya* of the *Matsyapurāṇa*"; its second line has both "eight" and "six" months, which he marks (!): a year, eight or six months, three months, a month, ten days at dawn, at once at the cows' release.
- **h 16f.**, from the *Adbhutasāgara* p. 514, "very like s 17f." (h is Agni 229, s Matsya 241.2ff. of the Calcutta 1876 print): "svapnās tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākinaḥ | ṣaḍbhir māsair dvitīye tu tribhir māsais triyāmikāḥ | cathurthe māsamātreṇa pacyante nātra saṃśayaḥ |": a year, six months, three months, a month.
- **i 2f.** (like s 2–3; i is the Leipzig manuscript 1178): "svapnasya prathame yāme phalaṃ saṃvatsaraṃ bhavet | dvitīye cā'ṣṭame māse triyāme ca tripakṣake || 3 tūrye yāme tu yaḥ svapno māsena phalado bhavet | aruṇodaya-(pra)velāyāṃ daśāhena phalaṃ bhavet ||": a year, eight months, three fortnights, a month, ten days at dawn.
- **a 3–5b**, "cf. AP 68.2.58f.", the Oxford manuscript: "svapnas tu prathame yāme saṃvatsara-vipākadaḥ | dvitīye yāma aṣṭabhis tribhir māsais triyāmake || caturthe prahare svapnaṃ(!) māsaikena vipacyate | aruṇo-'daya-velāyāṃ dhvakha(!) 'phalaṃ diśet || godauhana(!)-velāyāṃ sadhva(!) svapno vidhīyate ||": a year, eight months, three months, a month, then a garbled dawn line and the milking hour of the cows (*godohana* for *govisarjana*).

So the dream book's own verse has the series year, half-year, three months, month, ten days, at once; the parallels differ in the second span (six or eight months), the third (three months, or three fortnights) and the fourth (a month or half a month; the Brahmavaivarta alone gives half a month with the eight months). None ties a span to a lunar day, a weekday or a month: the only clock is the watch of the night. **Correction to RD265:** the layer's letters were wrong in two places: the "s 2f" verse is **i 2f**, and the manuscript a (not the Atharva appendix itself) has a month for the fourth watch.

The footnote goes on (p. XXI) that the German "Traumbuch Apomasaris" has a like stepped series: a dream between sunset and 9 o'clock is fulfilled within 20 years; between 9 and 12 in 8, 10 or 15 years; between 12 and 3 or 4 in 5, 4 or 3 years; between 4 and sunrise after a year, or ½, ¼ or 1/12 of a year, or even within ten days; "cf. our Traumschlüssel 1.17". ## <a id="neg-p17-19"></a>neg:p17-19 Negelein on the time in which an omen is fulfilled: never over a year; the twilights; the comet's 45 days

*Read: scan, 2026-11-09 (RD269), the verses and the sentences on the time, the comet and the twilights on p. 19; scan, 2026-11-09 (RD272), pp. 17 and 18 in full; machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265), the rest (the page image of p. 18 shows the groups of persons struck, AP 71.19.7 and Matsya 229–230, which the summary below does not use).*

> tatro 'tpāta-phalaṃ divyaṃ pūrṇe varṣe vipacyate |
> āntarikṣaṃ tathā bhaumaṃ māsaiḥ ṣaḍbhis tu bhartṛṣu ||
> anukteṣv api cānyeṣu paraṃ saṃvatsarāt phalam |

Negelein's note on the time in which an omen is fulfilled (p. 18, on 1.17), pp. 17–19. He draws on the *pākasamayādbhutāvarta* of the *Adbhutasāgara* (his pp. 744–51), which tells the times well, and on Parāśara, whose verses are quoted above in Roman transliteration with the German.

What the note says, in our words:

- The dream books let the time of the dream decide the time of the fruit, take the dreamer (sometimes with his kin) as the person struck, and speak only of good or bad sights; the omen books in the wider sense (the king's omens) name the king and the realm, and the oldest texts, such as the Atharva-Veda appendices, name the single person.
- The time in which an omen ripens never exceeds a year as far as he knows. Heavenly omens, the strongest, are usually quickest, but Parāśara teaches the reverse: a heavenly omen a full year, an omen of the air and of the earth six months; and for the others not named, "more than a year".
- A comet's fruit comes after as many years as it was seen months, or as many months as days (*Bṛhatsaṃhitā* 11.7), and it has no effect in its first 45 days (three fortnights), which Utpala extends to all omens.
- There are "critical times", the morning and evening twilights, in which an omen is stronger (*Vāṭakaṇikā* in the *Adbhutasāgara* 738; *Bṛhatsaṃhitā* 27.2); the twilights are very holy and watched.

Nothing here ties a dream to a lunar day, a weekday or a month; the fortnight appears only in the comet's 45 days.

## <a id="neg-p32"></a>neg:p32 Negelein's notes on the Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya (manuscript i) and on the half-verse ascribed to Bṛhaspati in manuscript r

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD272), the text layer of pp. 16, 31–32 (the page images not opened); the sentences below that rest on p. 16 are on the page image read by RD269 (neg:p14-16).*

> bṛhaspatir ita(?) puṇyaṃ pavitraṃ pāpanāśanam | yaḥ paṭhet paramaṃ prītyā duḥsvapnaṃ tasya naśyati ||
> etat puṇyaṃ ca pāpaghnaṃ dhanyā(?)duḥsvapnanāśanam | paṭhed yaḥ śṛṇuyāc caiva gurormāhātmyam uttamam ||
> (i 86ff., as the text layer prints them, "in very corrupt verses")

What the pages say, in our words. On p. 32 (in the note on the remedies of bad dreams, where the Atharva appendix, the Purāṇas and the Svapnādhyāya texts are set side by side) Negelein writes that the treatise **i** "gives itself out as" *bṛhaspatiproktaṃ svapnādhyāyam* (the dream chapter spoken by Bṛhaspati), and that its verses 86ff. recommend reading the treatise early in the morning as a remedy of a bad dream: whoever reads or hears it has his bad dream destroyed and a good one comes. The same recommendation, in other words, stands in the Jagaddeva text itself (2.164–166: Bṛhaspati's *śāstra*, "whoever reads it early in the morning when he has risen"). On p. 16 (neg:p14-16): the half-verse "aruṇodaya-velāyāṃ sadya eva phalaṃ bhavet" is ascribed in the manuscript **r** (Chambers 326, fol. 151a) to the sage Bṛhaspati. Together: the Bṛhaspati *Svapnādhyāya* Negelein collated (i) has the series year, eight months, **three fortnights**, a month, ten days at dawn (i 2f., neg:p14-16), not the Brahmavaivarta's.

## <a id="neg-p35"></a>neg:p35 Negelein on the astrologer as interpreter of dreams, and his gloss of Yogayātrā 2.24

*Read: machine, 2026-11-09 (RD265).*

> [Yogayātrā 2.24, as Negelein cites it from Weber, Indische Studien 10, p. 185: the astrologer "beseitigt böse Einflüsse von Träumen, indem er das Datum, das Gestirn und den Wochentag derselben feststellt"]

Negelein's note on the astrologer as interpreter of dreams (about p. 35, on 1.19). He writes that the astrologer removes the bad influences of dreams by fixing the date, the star and the weekday "of the same" (*derselben*), and cites *Yogayātrā* 2.24 in Weber's *Indische Studien* 10. The verse itself says something else: see yyt:p28-29. There the king hears the day's *tithi*, *nakṣatra* and weekday read before he sets out, and the hearing is one of the fruits; the verse does not date a dream.

In the same note: because the art of the dream belongs to astrology, a competent interpreter must have been assured at court, and an Atharvan priest was chosen for it (*Pariśiṣṭa* 68.5.18); the astrologer ranks among the first of the court (*Channabasava-Purāṇa* 29); the whole of *Pariśiṣṭa* 68 is called an appendix (*upāṅga*) to a treatise on the course of Venus (*Śukracāra*), which the surviving collection lacks.

## <a id="neg-p376"></a>neg:p376 Negelein on the sources the Jagaddeva names for his dreams (2.160 and 2.163–166, 182f.)

> ye bhadrabāhu-suśruta-vācaspati-caraka-ratna-kośeṣu | uktāḥ svapna-viśeṣāḥ śubhāśubhās te mayā 'py uditāḥ || (2.160; one manuscript reads "bhaṭṭa-vāgbhaṭṭa" for "bhadrabāhu", as the layer prints)

What the page says, in our words. In verse 2.160 the Jagaddeva says that the dreams, good and bad, set out in "the treasuries" of Bhadrabāhu, Suśruta, Vācaspati and Caraka are told by him too; Negelein concludes that the author learnt from physicians and that Bhadrabāhu's *Saṃhitā* (the *Jyotiṣa* work Aufrecht lists) and a physician Vāgbhaṭa would be the works to compare, and adds that his investigation showed that the compiler drew on a series of other sources, which suited the matter better. ### Settled by RD269 (2026-11-09), on the page images

- Negelein's "b" is the *Svapnādhyāya* of the *Brahmavaivarttapurāṇa* (Introduction p. XIX); his "b 5–6" is its verses 5–6 on p. 661 of the Jivananda print (bvp:p662-665), one reading apart (his *bhoditaḥ*, the print's *bodhitaḥ*). The layer's "h 16f" is right (h is Agni 229); its "s 2f" is in fact i 2f.
- The *Kālaprakāśikā* v. 66–67 (p. 189 of the Devanagari section, kpd:p184-189) gives year, half a year, three months, a month, twelve days at dawn: the fourth watch has a month, not half a month.
- The *Bṛhadyātrā* 16.10 names three lists in one order (bya:p41-46): the last, middle and first parts of the night; rains, autumn, Mādhava (spring); wind, bile, phlegm. The verse does not pair them in words; the order of the lists is the only pairing.
- The *Aṅgavijjā* ch. 42 reads *puvvaṇha* (forenoon) for the bright beginning on the page (p. 190), ch. 41 reads *avaraṇha* (afternoon) on p. 185; the apparatus notes no variant for the word in either (agv:p190-191, agv:p186). Which is the original reading by the manuscripts is not settled; ch. 42's own list (evening, midnight, break of day, forenoon, midday, afternoon) is the regular one.

What follows from them for the row: q852 is answered (b is the Brahmavaivarta's *Svapnādhyāya*, RD269); for q853 no Indian text older than the Purāṇa's earliest layer (Hazra: about the 8th century for the first version, the dream chapter undated) has the half-month series; the Śaunaka *Kārikā* and the physician Vāgbhaṭa have no span; the Bṛhaspati *Svapnādhyāya* (i) and its copies, the Matsya (f, s), the Agni (h), the *Kālaprakāśikā* and the *Ācāramayūkha* all give a month for the fourth watch (claim neg-acaramayukha-quotes-month-series, text layer only).

For the Tibetan side of the comparison the Svapnohana's own series, as the Narthang manuscript carries it (svo.md, skd.md; RD291, RD311): «ཐུན་གཉིས་ཟླ་བ་བརྒྱད་པ་ལ། །ཐུན་གསུམ་ཟླ་བ་གསུམ་གྱི་བར། །ཐུན་བཞི་ཟླ་བ་ཕྱེད་བར་དུ།» (the second watch eight months, the third up to three months, the fourth to half a month), the fourth watch's half-month standing against the month of every Indian text above except the Brahmavaivarta.
