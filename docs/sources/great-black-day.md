# The great black day (*nyi ma nag chen*) and the meeting of the nine bad

Sigla and the rules for quoting are in [README.md](README.md). Read on
2026-10-09 for ROADMAP T2.14: Yigdzin-1 (`tools/sources/hf_read.py`) on
WB vol. 2, img. 233–235, BDRC's etext as a witness, and every line used
here read on the scan of img. 234 (p. 226), enlarged 3×. Built as
`GreatBlackDay` (SPEC §5.7, §10.3).

## Where it stands

WB vol. 2, ch. 31, p. 226 (img. 234), after the earth lords of the date's
animal and their witnesses ([earth-lords.md](earth-lords.md)): «ཚེས་ལ་རྒྱུ་བའི་ས་བདག་བསྟན།»,
"the earth lords that move by date are shown". The first is the great
black day, the second the small (*nyi ma nag chung*, the *bya ma gung
rgyal*, the next verses on the same page), then the courses of pp. 227–235
(ROADMAP T2.13).

## What it says

Of Ya bdud rdo rje glog phreng and Sa srin 'bar ma were born a son,
Rāhula, and four daughters: 'Brog sring, crocodile-faced; below her Stag
sring, *zor*-faced; below her Kong sring, *ba yang*-faced; the youngest, Bam
sring, bear-faced. Rāhu circles Mount Meru (*ri rab*) to the right and
Bam sring to the left; on the 7th of the first month of spring, when she
is defiled (*mnol shor*), this is called **the meeting of the nine bad**
(*ngan pa dgu 'dzom*): the winds below and above change places, the
level wind and the five elements are in turmoil, the sun rises like a
strong poison. The nine may be told as nine kinds, but "nine" is the
word for many bad things, and the name means the worst of the bad.

Then one date in each month:

| Season-month | Date | What happens | Hor month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spring, first | 7 | the defilement: the meeting of the nine bad | 11 |
| Spring, middle | 14 | the time of arising (*skyes pa'i dus*) | 12 |
| Spring, last | 21 | the time of bursting (*rdol ba'i dus*) | 1 |
| Summer, first | 8 | Stag sring defiled | 2 |
| Summer, middle | 16 | a belly of blood (*khrag grod*) arises | 3 |
| Summer, last | 24 | the belly bursts | 4 |
| Autumn, first | 9 | Kong sring defiled | 5 |
| Autumn, middle | 18 | a belly of iron (*lcags grod*) arises | 6 |
| Autumn, last | 27 | the belly bursts | 7 |
| Winter, first | 10 | Bam sring defiled | 8 |
| Winter, middle | 20 | a belly of water (*chu grod*) arises | 9 |
| Winter, last | 30 (*gnam gang*) | the mute one (*lkugs pa*) bursts | 10 |

The dates go up by one a season in the first months (7, 8, 9, 10), by two
in the middle (14, 16, 18, 20) and by three in the last (21, 24, 27, 30):
each season's three are its first date doubled and trebled.

**The months.** Chapter 31 counts its seasons by the Chinese reckoning
([tibetastromed.md](tibetastromed.md), *WB's months and seasons*): the
first month of spring is the 11th, the tiger month. So the meeting of the
nine bad is **11/7**, not the 11/6 of the Russian calendar or of Rabten's
Ten Good Omens.

**The results.** Generally, works for the dead and for the living are
bad (*shi gson bya las ngan*); above all sending wealth and goods out,
*dbang rab* (read as empowerment and consecration, *dbang* and *rab
gnas*, which the small black day's list beside it names in full),
a bride, building (*mkhar las*), «དགེ་བཤེས་ཆེ་འདོན» (not identified, not in
the app's lists), burial (*dur gdab*) and trade; virtuous, white work is
not done; harmful means (*ngan thabs*) go along with it. As with the
particular cases of the month's results, avoiding it is important.

**Another tradition.** «འདི་ལ་མི་གཅིག་ལུགས་ཤིག་ལ། །དཔྱིད་སྟོན་དགུན་ཐ་ཉི་ཤུ་བཞི། །དགུན་རའི་ཚེས་དགུར་རྒྱུ་བ་ཡང་།»:
in one person's tradition the last months of spring, autumn and winter
have the 24th and the first of winter the 9th. WB gives it as one view
and does not adopt it; not built. The line after it, «ཁྱུང་གི་བུ་བརྒྱུད་ཕྱི་མ་འཆད»,
is not understood.

**The remedies.** A spear with a black yak tail, five kinds of wood, a
white silk scarf and black dog's hair (and *rtsi bo che*) tied to it, set
up on the side where the sun rises; where a black month or a black day
has defiled, white silk and white cloth with fringes hoisted and a white
burnt offering (*dkar gsur*), a white-headed yak or sheep adorned with
silk, a water torma and the Collected Dhāraṇīs recited; or the substances
against harmful talk (*mi kha*) poured out where three roads meet, earth
and stones scattered, and fleeing without looking back.

## As built

`GreatBlackDay`: the twelve dates by season-month of `SeasonReckoning.CHINESE`,
keyed by the date as it stands (like Rāhu's course by month): a skipped
date has none, a doubled one has it on both days, and a leap month as its
month, since WB's almanac writes the earth lords for a doubled month
too (vol. 1, p. 178; [almanac-page.md](almanac-page.md), entry 19).
`Texts.GREAT_BLACK_DAY`: the date's event and the reading above, with the
avoid list (works for the dead and the living, sending wealth out,
empowerment, consecration, taking a bride, building, building graves,
trade, virtuous work) and black rites good. Shown in "Also today", not
weighed: it is one of chapter 31's earth lords, like the *bla mkhyen*,
and the verse that ranks the day's factors (vol. 2, p. 376) does not
place the earth lords. On 11/7 the row is titled the meeting of the
nine bad. Rabten's Ten Good Omens on 11/6 is removed (owner's decision,
2026-10-09: WB above all). `GreatBlackDayTest` checks the dates against
the text.

**Not built:** the small black day (*nyi ma nag chung*, the next verses
on p. 226), with the courses of pp. 227–235 (ROADMAP T2.13); the other
tradition's dates.
