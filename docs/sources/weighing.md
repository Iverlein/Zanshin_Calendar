Sigla and the rules for quoting are in [README.md](README.md). How the
factors of a day are weighed against each other, from KP's opening prose
(box 1, the "errors of the calendar-makers"), img. 13–14, and from its

the scan (2550 px); the corrections of the machine reading are noted after
the text. WB's verse found and read 2026-10-06 (below, "The source"). The
app's weighing (SPEC §5.12) is built from this file.

**What WB says that KP does not:**

- **The weights are not the Phugpa rule.** "The way to gather and practise
  these is the view of the great paṇḍita of Kashmir: the date has one
  quality, the planet four, the mansion eight, the combination period ten
  million, he said; but to take them up undiminished, as the Phug tradition
  has it, do this: as the order of strength stated above, …". The rules
  that follow replace the weights; they do not apply them.
- **A line KP left out:** «དམིགས་བསལ་བྱུང་ན་དེ་ཉིད་གཙོ།», "where a *dmigs
  bsal* occurs, it leads; where there is none, the *phyogs sdebs* leads".
  WB's p. 371 summary has the same line.
- **Three reasons, not one.** The powers of all are complete in the
  *tatkāla dus sbyor* (one pāda); then the reason the *dmigs bsal* leads,
  like a craftsman's tools; the reason the concord (*mthun 'phrod*, the
  combination of planet and mansion) leads, like two people agreeing in
  counsel; the reason the combination period leads, the work of the four
  being complete in its house. KP's prose runs the first two together,
  which made the *dmigs bsal* look like the combination period.
- **The tie:** «མང་ཉུང་སྟོབས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་བརྩི», "count the difference of greater
  and lesser strength", followed by the seven in order. KP renders it
  «ནུས་སྟོབས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་བརྩིས་ནས་ནུས་སྟོབས་ཆེ་བ་ཉིད་གཅེས», and Tshul khrims rgyal
  mtshan (below) «བཟང་ངན་སྟོབས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་རྩིས་ནས་སྟོབས་ཆེ་བ་ཉིད་གཙོ»: both by strength
  alone, with no count of sides. *Mang nyung* stands where KP's opening has
  *che chung* («ནུས་སྟོབས་ཆེ་ཆུང་གི་ཁྱད་པར»).

"The remedies and screens of the day animals are to be known with the
work aimed at first. In the twelve hours of the Chinese reckoning these
works are good to accomplish": in the hare hour guarding curd and beer
and binding the mouths of the *'gong po*; at sunrise, the dragon hour,
raising fortune and prosperity (*phywa g.yang*); when the sun is warm,
the snake hour, reciting the mantras that tame the earth lords; at noon,
the horse hour, fierce recitation that turns harm back; as the sun
declines, the sheep hour, work that tames madness and fainting
(*smyo ba*, *'bog pa*); late in the afternoon, the monkey hour, driving out
adversity; as the sun grows old, the bird hour, fulfilment and offerings
to the protectors; at dusk, the dog hour, binding the mouths of the *bse
rags*; as night comes on, the pig hour, taming the *gre mo*; at midnight,
when the mouse rises, the mantras that summon fortune; past midnight, the
ox hour, fierce mantras succeed; in the tiger hour, mantras to slay
enemies. "When the hour of one's own year rises, avoid fierce and harsh
work, gatherings, confrontations and great displays (*rngam chen*).
Moreover, in the hours' pits (*brub khung*), the rough times when the four
māras move, the four, the two *phyed* and the two *zer*, no act at all
turns out well but averting, killing and fierce work: the hour is a sharp
weapon." The hours' names are KP's from daybreak ([combination-period.md](combination-period.md)),
with *myur smad* for its *nyi myur* and *nyi rgas* for its *nyi nub*. The
spirits as [lunar-day-signs.md](lunar-day-signs.md) found them: *bse rag*
the ghost that eats the essence of food and wealth, *gre mo* a female
*gdon* (*gre bo* the male); *'bog* with *smyo* is fainting, as in that
file's «སྨྱོ་འབོག».

### The four rough times in three manuals (O9, 2026-10-13)

"In this way the four sessions (ཐུན, *thun*) are the two *gung* and the two
*zer*, which are called the four times of change of «མཱ་ར་ཡ་ཛ»: midnight and
noon, and the two rays, the ray of the sun about to rise in the morning
and the ray of the sun drawing in in the evening. The body of the
session, summoning, pressing, killing and the rest, is to be brought to
arrive at these four. For example, the first main recitation (གཞུང་སྲོང,
*gzhung srong*) is begun when the sun has just passed its middle and must
arrive at the time of the evening session, when the sun draws in its
rays. By the same pattern midnight, sunrise and noon: the four all in
just that order of the rite; the earlier session goes through the
meditation deity's (ཡི་དམ, *yi dam*) main text, the later one through this
rite's daily confession up to the offering, and it succeeds. Or again:
dusk (སྲོད, *srod*), when the ogres (སྲིན་པོ, *srin po*) crouch for food;
midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད, *nam phyed*), when the Lord of Death (གཤིན་རྗེ, *gshin rje*)
strikes his tally (ཁྲམ, *khram*); dawn (ཐོ་རངས, *tho rangs*), when the *ging*
(གིང) smiles at its prey; sunrise, when the great tiger opens its mouth;
noon, when the sea monster (ཆུ་སྲིན, *chu srin*) swings its tail; evening
shadow (དགོང་གྲིབ, *dgong grib*), when Māra's (བདུད, *bdud*) army sets out, as
the line of the shadows (གྲིབ་སོ, *grib so*) comes on dark and gloomy (question

session's frame as above, press on with summoning the la, the
life-spirit (བླ, *bla*), until the sign of capture has come."

What it says of the four rough times: they are named and defined, two
middles and two rays, as the four times of change of «མཱ་ར་ཡ་ཛ», which may
be Māra rāja, the king of the māras (a question below). A fierce
rite's sessions are fitted to them: each session is a span that is made
to arrive at its moment (one begun just after noon arrives at the
evening rays), and the acts of summoning and killing fall in them, as
WB's verse allows only averting, killing and fierce work in its four.
The six-session list after them names midnight ནམ་ཕྱེད where the four
have ནམ་གུང, the same moment under WB's word *phyed*; WB's own hours on
p. 359 call noon ཉི་ཕྱེད and midnight ནམ་ཕྱེད. The six add dusk, dawn and
evening shadow, and leave out the evening rays.

and instants" shorter than an hour. It says nothing of Māra or of
rough work: at the breaks it sets offerings and praise.

Great Perfection guide and the Vajrabhairava manual call them the best
time for meditation.

## *Dmigs bsal* and *phyogs sdebs*

*Dmigs bsal* is the particular case, set against the general (*spyi*,
*spyir btang*); *phyogs sdebs* is the general grouping of factors or works
by kind, good and bad among them. Neither word names a factor of its own.

**In WB.** The other 29 places of *dmigs bsal* in the 1996 etext (both
volumes) have that sense, the particular against the general: a special
month, year or place, an exception to a rule. In the mansion verses, the
works a mansion names one by one against its classes of work:
«དམིགས་བསལ་མ་གཏོགས་ཞི་རྒྱས་དབང་» (except the special cases, pacifying,
increasing and controlling works; p. 325, img. 333; p. 326, img. 334),
«དམིགས་བསལ་མ་གཏོགས་ལས་བཞི་དང་།» (p. 313, img. 321), «ཡུལ་བཟུང་ཡིག་བསླབ་དམིགས་བསལ་ཡིན»
(taking land and learning to write are its special works; p. 320,
img. 328). In the special
days, «འགྲུབ་སྦྱོར་ཟུང་སྦྱོར་ཤར་བ་ན། །རང་རང་དམིགས་བསལ་ལས་སྤང་དང་།» (each one's

"…as these show, whatever the general result, it matters to hold first
what has to be reckoned as a special case for the particular one; as is
said, over a treatise's general rule the particular is strong." His
examples are a person's own weekdays and mansions: «བླ་གཟའ་དར་དུས་བྱ་ལས་འགྲུབ། །སྲོག་གཟའ་དགེ་ལ་གཤེད་གཟར་བྱོལ།»
and the *bla*, *srog* and *dbang* mansions good for anything, the *keg*,
*bdud* and *gshed* mansions bad (the first verse is in the Bhutanese
anthology *Dkar rtsis dang nag rtsis kyi gzhung*, Thimphu 1981, BDRC
MW26041; neither is in WB). For the tie he writes strength alone, as
above.

## What it says

1. **Weights,** the Kashmiri paṇḍita's (WB): the date once, the planet
   (the weekday) four times, the mansion eight times, the combination
   period ten million times (བྱེ་བ). WB sets them aside for the Phugpa order
   of strength.
2. **Two that disagree.** Planet against mansion: the planet leads. Mansion
   against date: the mansion leads. Yoga against karaṇa: the very dangerous
   karaṇa leads. The day (*nyi ma*) against the hour (*dus tshod*): the hour,
   "a sharp weapon", leads.
3. **All mixed.** When good and bad are mixed among them all, the
   combination (*'phrod*) leads; where a particular case (*dmigs bsal*)
   occurs, it leads; where there is none, the general grouping (*phyogs
   sdebs*) leads; where good and bad still do not come out alike, count the
   difference of greater and lesser strength, and the stronger counts.
4. **Ranking.** Rāhu, the weekday (*res gza'*), the mansion (*rgyu skar*),
   the date, the karaṇa, the yoga, the *nyi ma*: each is stronger than the
   one after it.
5. **Above all.** The powers of all are complete in the combination period
   (*tatkāla dus sbyor*). The reasons: a particular case leads as a
   craftsman's tool fits its job; the concord leads as two who agree in
   counsel; the combination period leads because the work of planet,
   mansion, karaṇa and yoga is complete in its house, so it is held
   highest of all.

## Notes for the app

- The *dus sbyor* is a period within the day, computed from the hours by
  sign (KP's second part §9, img. 78–80), and so is the hour of rule 2
  (WB p. 359, above): neither is a factor of the whole day. A day reading
  has no hours; the rules that remain for the day are 2 (without the
  hour), 3 and 4, and the hour of rule 2 is built on the hours panel.
- Rāhu's course by date and time is KP §7–8 (img. 76–77). The app weighs
  the White Beryl's detailed course on the sixteen dates it names, first
  of the seven ([rahu.md](rahu.md), SPEC §5.13); the course by the hour is
  not built.
- The weights of rule 1 put the mansion above the planet, rules 2 and 4 the
  planet above the mansion. WB settles it: the weights are the Kashmiri
  paṇḍita's, and the Phugpa tradition takes the order of strength instead.
  No sum by the weights (open question 12, answered 2026-10-06).
- The *'phrod* of rule 3 is the combination of weekday and mansion: the ten
  element pairs (*'byung 'phrod*, the app's element pair) and the 28 named
  combinations (*'phrod chen*, KP §3, WB pp. 331–333). WB says more than
  rule 3: even where the planet and the mansion are each good, a bad
  combination decides (p. 333). The app therefore makes the combination,
  where its two parts agree, the result of the day and of every work.
- The *dmigs bsal* of rule 3 is the particular case (open question 13,
  answered 2026-10-06): for a work, a reading that names that work, as
  against the class of work it falls in (WB ch. 34, the mansion verses'
  «དམིགས་བསལ་མ་གཏོགས»); for a person, their own weekdays and mansions
  (Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan). It is neither the combination period nor
  the special days of weekday and mansion. The *phyogs sdebs* is the
  general grouping, a factor's place among the good or the bad (Mkhyen rab
  nor bu: nine yogas bad, eighteen good). Neither word, nor *mang nyung*,
  asks for a count of the factors on each side: where the particular and
  the general leave good and bad mixed, the stronger factor decides.
- What this means for the app (built 2026-10-06, SPEC §5.12): no count of
  sides. A work is decided by the factors whose lists name it (its *dmigs
  bsal*), the strongest of them where they disagree; the day's tone, which
  names no work, by the factors' own good or bad (their *phyogs sdebs*),
  the strongest where they disagree. Until then the app counted sides
  where the combination did not decide: on 2000–2049 that decided the
  tone of 7,314 of 18,263 days, and the strongest voice gives 1,533 of
  them the other tone and turns 5.6 % of the works' sides. The person's
  own weekday and mansion, a *dmigs bsal* for Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan,
  are not weighed: neither text says where they stand against the
  combination.
- The special days of weekday and mansion are none of these terms; WB puts
  them below planet and mansion, "the individual results of planet and
  mansion are the main thing" (p. 337, [combinations.md](combinations.md)),
  and the app counts them as one voice in that rank.
- The *nyi ma* is the day's animal sign, the lunar date's animal (open question 9, answered 2026-10-04).
  The trigram and the sme ba are not among the seven.

## What the three searches give D4 (2026-10-26)

RD6 searched WB's notes, KD's readers of WB and KP's prose. On
2026-10-26 three readers took the search further, each recorded in its
own section below: WB itself and the regent's replies (A); the manuals
(B); the earth rites, the almanacs and modern works (C). Their
*Searched and not found* lists say what was covered. **No text ranks
the earth lords against the seven of the order of strength or against
the combination period.** The silence is now general: WB ch. 13, 23,
29–31, 33 and 34; the regent's replies 161–193; KP, SY, DZ and its
supplement, LT, UT, ZG, NM, JL, CN and KD vols. 1–3; Kongtrul's
*Treasury*; the Kālacakra and Guhyagarbha ground rites; the
Men-Tsee-Khang almanac of 2000; Gyurme Dorje. What the texts say
instead agrees across all three readers:

1. **The weighing is the day's results; the earth lords stand beside it
   and are met by remedy.** KP closes its weighing of the seven and the
   combination period (img. 14, read on the scan), then turns to "the
   earth lords and the other bad kinds". For these "one must know how to
   apply, as the occasion comes", the screenings and remedies (སྒྲིབ་བཅོས, sgrib bcos), in full or in
   short ([kp:i14](corpus/kp/kp.md#kp-i14)). SY copies the weighing and
   turns at once to "the earth lords' general screening"
   ([sy:p389-392](corpus/sy/sy.md)). WB's notes keep the two as separate
   heads, as RD6 found. WB's own lists of what to examine before a work
   name the earth lords beside the day's factors, with no order between
   them (wb.2:p428b, wb.2:p369, wb.1:p365).
2. **An earth lord bars a place, a direction or a time for a work, and a
   good day does not lift the bar.**
   - In the fierce rites the lord's own days are "the best", yet "the
     place facing it is shunned" (wb.2:p427b, scan).
   - The black-year lord is answered by a rite done on a day good by
     planet, mansion and concord (wb.2:p189b, scan): the good day serves
     the remedy and does not outweigh the lord.
   - The Men-Tsee-Khang almanac says "avoid that direction" for the empty
     vase and puts work in the clean directions (mta:p52-53, scan).
   - Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan: "no important work is begun in their
     places or in the empty vase" (kd.1:p493, etext).
   - The ground rites name where to dig, the serpent's belly and never its
     head, apart from choosing the day (lcm:p181-182, sdp:p238-241, etext).
   - A bride's manuals give the earth lords as one check of ten: each is
     avoided where it can be and mended by its own rite where it cannot
     (dz:i045, kd.3:p634, kd.3:p641-642, lt:p38-41 on the scan; ut:p240-243
     etext).
3. **The date's earth lords are among the day's results, not under the
   year's and month's rule.**
   - WB treats them inside ch. 33's results (p. 359, etext), the results
     whose closing verse names «ས་བདག» (p. 376). It grades by source those
     that are not in ch. 31: they were written down only because other
     books carry them (wb.2:p377, scan).
   - The almanac writes them as entries of the day (kd.1:p495, etext).
   - The rites name them as a quality of the time, beside the fierce
     planets.
   - Still no text gives them a place in the order.
4. **A work avoided in one direction is kept apart from the day.** Every
   text meets it by choosing the place or the facing: work facing the
   clean direction, keep the lord at one's back, or dig at the belly. No
   text counts it for or against the day's good or bad.
5. **The only ranks are within the earth lords:**
   - the month's sky dog above the day's and hour's (WB's notes);
   - the sky dog of particular weight for a bride (the supplement), with
     the day's and month's sky dog as the chief (UT);
   - a report of Kham custom, from search snippets only: "for a corpse and
     a bride the earth lords are held chief" (SPD). It does not say chief
     over what (question 417).

**For the owner's choice.** The texts do not rank the earth lords, so
any rank remains a choice and not a reading. What the texts do support,
as an inference that no text states in these words, is a model with no
rank at all:
- the year's and month's earth lords stay out of the weighing of the
  day's good and bad;
- each bars its works in its own place, direction or time whatever the
  day's verdict, with its remedy beside;
- a direction-only bar counts for neither side of the day.

This differs from the owner's rule of 2026-10-11 ("Counted": the works an
earth lord names are counted in the weighing). If that rule stands, the
texts' nearest place for an earth lord is beside the day's other entries
that are not in the order of strength, such as the trigram, the date's
mewa and the link. A lord that names whom it harms might instead count
as a particular case (*dmigs bsal*) that leads (question 409). The
questions these searches raise are 408–419.

### What WB says, chapter by chapter

- **Ch. 34's closing verse** (wb.2:p428b, scan, p. 428, img. 436), WB's own last word on foretelling a work's result: «…ཏཏྐཱལ་དང་སྤར་སྨེ་དང་། །ཉི་མ་རགས་དང་གསང་བ་གཉིས། །ས་བདག་བཟང་ངན་ཁྱད་པར་དུ། །ལས་བརྩམས་གང་ཟག་དེའི་དར་གུད། །དེ་ལ་གནོད་པའི་སྐྱོན་ལ་སོགས། །ཞིབ་ཏུ་བརྟགས་ལ་ཁ་དམར་སྨྲ།», "… the *tatkāla*, the trigram and the mewa, the rough and the two secret day signs, the good and bad of the earth lords; above all (ཁྱད་པར་དུ, khyad par du) the rise and decline of the person who begins the work and the faults that harm him: examine them closely and then give the verdict (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar)." The earth lords are one item of a list that runs from the date's results through the planets, mansions, concords and yogas; the only item singled out is the person's own state. Claim: [wb-work-result-checklist](claims/wb-work-result-checklist.md).
- **Ch. 33 holds the date's earth lords among its results.** After the hours, «དེ་ནས་ཚེས་ཀྱི་ས་བདག་གི། །བཟང་ངན་བྱེ་བྲག་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ།» (p. 359, etext only), "then the good and bad of the date's earth lords one by one": the black months and days, the courses, the strikes and the sky doors (pp. 359–370; [earth-lord-courses.md](earth-lord-courses.md)). So the verse of p. 376 that names «ས་བདག» among the results "set out unmixed" covers these date's earth lords of ch. 33; the year's and month's of ch. 31 are a chapter apart, as the notes' sections 3 and 4 have them (RD6, above). The general line for them (wb.2:p364-365, scan, img. 370–371): «ཉི་ཚེས་དུས་ཚོད་ལ་སྤང་བའི། །སྤྱིར་ནི་དྲག་པོའི་ས་བདག་རྣམས། །དགེ་ལས་གསོན་པོའི་བྱ་བ་སྤང་། །དྲག་ལས་རྒྱུ་ཕྱོགས་བསྟུན་པས་ཐེབས།», "the fierce earth lords to be avoided on the day, date and hour, in general: virtuous and living works avoided; fierce works, done in accord with their course, strike home." The general remedy (wb.2:p369, etext only) wards off together "the harm of the bad year, month, day, hour, planet, mansion and earth lord", one list.

### What this gives D4

1. **The rank of the year's and month's earth lords.** Nothing found: WB and YS set them against neither the seven of the order of strength nor the combination. WB's own lists put the earth lords beside the day's factors without an order (wb.2:p428b, wb.2:p369, wb.1:p365), and its remedies answer a lord by its rite done on a good day (wb.2:p189b), not by outweighing it. Two readings are nearest to a rule and are inferences, not statements of any text: (a) the departure calls the lords that name whom they harm «དམིགས་ཀྱིས་བསལ་བ», the weighing's word for the particular case that leads; (b) ch. 34's closing verse puts only the person's own state "above all", which would set a lord that strikes the person's own year or direction above a general earth lord. Neither is a rank against the order of strength.
2. **The date's earth lords already built.** WB places them inside ch. 33's results (p. 359), which the weighing verse of p. 376 names with «ས་བདག», and grades by source those not found in ch. 31 (wb.2:p377). It gives them no place in the order of strength either. Nothing says the date's earth lords and the year's and month's are counted by one rule; WB keeps them in two chapters, as the notes keep them under two heads.
3. **A work avoided in one direction.** WB keeps direction and day apart: for the fierce rites the earth lords' own days are "the best", yet "shun the place one faces them" (wb.2:p427b), and the date's fierce lords favour fierce works "in accord with their course" (wb.2:p364-365). So a direction avoidance stands whatever the day's goodness, and a good day does not lift it. How such a work counts against the day's general good or bad (whether the day itself is then good or bad for the work) no text says.

## Settled, 2026-10-18 late night (open question 127)

## The earth lords against the results in the manuals (D4, B, 2026-10-26)

Read for the owner's D4 (ROADMAP T2.26) by the second of three readers: the manuals and digests of the element and almanac reckoning (ནག་རྩིས, nag rtsis; འབྱུང་རྩིས, 'byung rtsis) apart from the *White Beryl* itself. **No manual ranks the earth lords (ས་བདག, sa bdag) against the date, the weekday, the mansion, the karaṇa, the yoga, the *nyi ma* or the combination period, and none puts them into the weighing.** What the manuals do is of two kinds, both below: in the bride's going they list the earth lords as one check among ten or more, each avoided or mended in its own right; and the *Kun phan me long* (ཀུན་ཕན་མེ་ལོང, kun phan me long; KP) and Nam mkha' seng ge's (ནམ་མཁའ་སེང་གེ, nam mkha' seng ge) *Mirror* (SY) close the weighing by sending the earth lords to screening and remedy (སྒྲིབ་བཅོས, sgrib bcos), beside it. The passages are in the corpus layer; the facts are the claims [earth-lords-bride-ten-checks](claims/earth-lords-bride-ten-checks.md), [earth-lords-beside-the-weighing](claims/earth-lords-beside-the-weighing.md) and [sky-dog-weight-for-bride](claims/sky-dog-weight-for-bride.md).

### What this gives D4

1. **The year's and month's earth lords against the seven and the combination.** No manual ranks them. KP and SY, which digest WB's weighing, leave them out of it and turn to them in the next clause as things met by screening and remedy, «applied as the occasion comes» (kp:i14, sy:p389-392): beside the weighing, not in its order, not as a veto stated in words. The bride's manuals (DZ, the supplement, LT, UT) list them as one check among ten or more, each avoided where it can be and mended by its own rite where it cannot (dz:i045, kd.3:p634, kd.3:p641-642, lt:p38-41, ut:p240-243). The nearest to a veto is the plain avoidance, «do not send at the time the earth lords go about»; the nearest to a grade across factors is LT's three that cannot be mended, and they are a mansion, a weekday and the sky year, not an earth lord. Whatever rank the app gives is the owner's choice.
2. **The date's earth lords.** The same: SY and the KP digest give the date's earth lords as results of their own before the weighing, and the weighing names none of them; the supplement and LT flee their «fierce and rough kind» as one check. Within the earth lords only, the bride's manuals single out the sky dog (the supplement: «of particular weight for a bride»; UT: the day's and month's sky dog «the chief»), as WB's notes take the month as the chief of the sky dogs (RD6). No manual sets the black day (ཉི་མ་ནག, nyi ma nag), *pi ling* (པི་ལིང, pi ling), *zin phung* (ཟིན་ཕུང, zin phung) and the like against the date's own result.
3. **A work avoided in one direction only.** The manuals keep the direction apart from the day: SY's general screening places objects and images in the direction where the earth lord goes, or keeps it at one's back; UT makes avoiding the earth lords' faces in the direction «the crux» of a bride's going; the supplement's directions follow the ten checks as a by-topic, «ཞར་བྱུང་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་སྤང་བླང», "avoiding and taking with the directions, in passing" (KD vol. 3 p. 642, etext only). None counts a direction-only avoidance against the day's general good or bad, or says that it spoils the day.

## The earth lords against the results in the earth rites, the almanacs and modern works (D4, C, 2026-10-26)

Read for the owner's D4 (ROADMAP T2.26) in reader C's territory: the rites
of the ground (ས་ཆོག, sa chog) and building, the printed almanacs (ལོ་ཐོ, lo tho) and
how they are explained, and modern works. Searched with BDRC's full-text
search (`bdrc_search.py`, 14 runs of up to six phrases), the etexts it
pointed to, the scans of one Men-Tsee-Khang almanac, Gyurme Dorje's book
(GD, the archive.org OCR) and the web. The passages are in the corpus
(below, by id); what they share is the claim
[earth-lords-place-beside-day](claims/earth-lords-place-beside-day.md).
**No text in this territory ranks an earth lord (ས་བདག, sa bdag) against
the day's factors of WB's order or against the combination.** What the
texts do is keep two tests apart and ask that both be passed: the earth
lord says *where* (or which way), the day's factors say *when*.

### The rites of the ground: the serpent says where to dig, the day is chosen apart

- **Longchenpa (ཀློང་ཆེན་པ, klong chen pa), *Dispelling the Darkness of the
  Ten Directions* (LCM), etext pp. 181–182**
  ([lcm:p181-182](corpus/lcm/lcm.md#lcm-p181-182), etext): the rite of the
  ground for a maṇḍala opens the ground "from the side of the earth lord,
  the serpent (ལྟོ་འཕྱེ, lto 'phye)", whose head turns with the seasons
  (quoted from the commentary on the *Bam brgyad* (བམ་བརྒྱད, bam brgyad)): «དེའང་མགོ་ལ་ཕོག་ན་སློབ་དཔོན་འཆི་ཞིང༌། མཇུག་མ་ལ་ཕོག་ན་སློབ་མ་འཆི་བས་ལྟོ་བ་ནས་བརྐོས་ཏེ་བྱའོ།»,
  "if it strikes the head the master dies, if the tail the disciple dies,
  so dig from the belly". A prohibition of place with a death as its
  penalty, stated with no reference to any day. The same lines stand in
  three other prints of the commentary (BDRC search, «སྐྱེས་བུ་ཆེན་པོ་ལྟོ་འཕྱེ», 5 hits).
- **Sādhuputra's Kālacakra maṇḍala rite (SDP), Tengyur, etext pp. 238–241**
  ([sdp:p238-241](corpus/sdp/sdp.md#sdp-p238-241), etext), the Indian
  witness: first the time, «དཔྱིད་ཟླ་འབྲིང་པོ་དང་དཔྱིད་ཟླ་ཐ་ཆུངས་སམ། ཟླ་བ་གཞན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཚེས་ལྔའམ་བཅུ་ལ། གདུག་པའི་གཟའ་ལ་འཇུག་ཕོད་ལ་སོགས་པ་དང་བྲལ་བའི་དུས་ཀྱི་སྔ་དྲོ་ལ།»

  navel. Two steps of one rite, neither weighed against the other.
- **Chos kyi grags pa of Drigung (འབྲི་གུང་ཆོས་ཀྱི་གྲགས་པ, 'bri gung chos kyi grags pa; CKG), a fierce rite, vol. 11, etext p. 6**
  (etext): the rite is timed for «གཟའ་སྐར་གདུག་ཅིང་རྩུབ་པ་དང༌། གཟའ་དང་ས་བདག་དྲག་པོ་འགྱུ་བའི་དུས», "fierce and rough
  planets and mansions, and a time when fierce planets and earth lords
  move", and then «ས་རྐོ་བའི་དུས་ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་ཁ་ལྟ་ཕྱོགས་བརྟག་པར་བྱ།», "at the time of digging, examine
  the direction the serpent faces". Here the moving earth lords are a
  quality of the *time*, named with the planets (as DZ's «ས་བདག་རྒྱུ་དུས»
  above), and the serpent a test of *place*. His treatise on the serpent
  (CKG vol. 4, *Sa bdag lto 'phye chen po brtag pa'i rab tu byed pa nyes pa
  kun sel*; the etext holds only its opening, etext p. 2) gives the
  serpent's seasons in several systems and a body of 360 cells it walks
  one a day, and says nothing in that part of any day's factor.
- **A Sakya biography (SLL), p. 414 (etext p. 421)** (etext), on building an
  assembly hall: «ལྷག་པ་ལྷ་མཚམས་འགྲུབ་སྦྱོར་གཟའ་སྐར་དགེ་བའི་ཉིན་འདུ་ཁང་གི་ས་བརྟག་སློང་དང་ས་བདག་ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་ཚད་ལྡན་བྱས་ནས», "on a
  Wednesday, an accomplishing combination, a day of good planets and
  mansions, the examination and asking of the hall's ground and [the
  reckoning of] the earth lord serpent were done in due measure". The
  practice joins the two: a good day *and* the serpent.
- **Practice in Amdo (ཨ་མདོ, a mdo), *A General Account of Tibet* (བོད་སྤྱི་བཤད, bod spyi bshad; BSB), pp. 435–436**
  ([bsb:p435-436](corpus/bsb/bsb.md#bsb-p435-436), etext; a modern work,
  facts only): the work on a new house begins on a day of an
  "accomplishing mansion" (འགྲུབ་སྐར, 'grub skar); the first stroke falls best
  on the serpent's armpit, middling on its belly, never its head or limbs;
  "once the day of good planets and mansions is fixed" the first earth is
  dug. Again both, and the serpent's body is said to move "with the course
  of the planets and mansions", so the place of the stroke follows the day
  rather than outweighing it.

### The almanacs: the earth lords print as places, the day's results apart

- **The Men-Tsee-Khang's almanac of the iron dragon year 2000 (MTA),
  pp. 52–53** ([mta:p52-53](corpus/mta/mta.md#mta-p52-53), read on the
  scan): the empty vase (བུམ་སྟོང, bum stong) stands in a direction for a span
  of dates, «ཕྱོགས་དེར་འཛེམས», "avoid that direction"; in the sky year's clean
  directions (གནམ་ལོའི་ཕྱོགས་གཙང, gnam lo'i phyogs gtsang) beginning works great and
  small is auspicious; and the year's first opening of the earth, digging
  of channels and starting of water is set on a named date and part of
  the morning, *facing the clean direction*. The almanac composes the
  rule of place with the chosen time; it never sets one against the
  other. Its front matter (img. 3–80, machine-read) has no instruction on
  weighing anything; the year's earth lords' wheels are drawings.
- **Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan's account of the almanac (KD vol. 1),

### Practice: the closest statement to a rank

- **Bsod nams dpal ldan (བསོད་ནམས་དཔལ་ལྡན, bsod nams dpal ldan; SPD), essays, at character 62 697** (search only;
  read by chaining four snippets): among the reckoners' customs heard of
  in Khams (ཁམས, khams), «རོ་བག་མ་ལ་ས་བདག་གཙོ་བོ་འཛིན་པ། བག་མ་ལ་ཧལ་ཁྱི་བརྩི་བ། མཁར་ལས་སྐབས་དགོན་སྡེ་ཆེ་ཁག་གིས་ས་བདག་ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་བརྩི་བ།» (put in script from the
  snippets' Wylie), "for a corpse and a bride, the earth lords are held
  as the chief thing; for a bride the hal dog is
  reckoned; when building, the great monasteries reckon the earth lord
  serpent". Beside it: when moving camp the hal dog is watched, and when
  the dragon lord is at the door, wealth is not taken out. This is the only
  line found that says an earth lord leads, and it is a report of local
  custom for two works (the corpse's road and the bride's going), not a
  rule; "chief" over what is not said.
- **Gyurme Dorje (GD), p. 120**, in our words: the favourable sectors of
  the years, months, days and hours are those the earth lords do not
  occupy; an activity that coincides with an earth lord's presence in a
  place may be undertaken only with the counteracting rites. A modern
  summary of WB ch. 31 (reader A's text): a rule of place with its remedy,
  not a rank among the day's factors. The same page keeps the earth
  lords' obstacles and passages (འགག་འགྲོ་ལམ, 'gag 'gro lam) as a cartouche of their
  own beside the year's other aspects.
- **Petra Maurer**: her chapter on time in geomancy (ས་དཔྱད, sa dpyad) ("Landscaping Time,
  Timing Landscapes", in *Glimpses of Tibetan Divination*, Brill 2019,

  2025-03-25 (CIUHCT, Lisbon) describes the same order as the texts above:
  the site first by the land, then the astrologer's reckoning of the date.

### What this gives D4

1. **The rank.** Nothing found ranks the year's or month's earth lords
   against WB's seven or the combination. The texts treat them as a test
   of place beside the test of time, both to be passed: the serpent's
   head, the empty vase, the month's hal dog forbid a *place or
   direction*, on any day, and a good day does not lift them (LCM, SDP,
   MTA, KD p. 493); a bad place has its remedy (GD p. 120; WB's notes,
   above). The closest to "above all" is SPD's report of Khams custom
   ("for a corpse and a bride the earth lords are held chief"), local and
   unsourced. So, for the app, an inference and not a reading: an earth
   lord does not enter the weighing of the day's good and bad; it bars or
   allows the work *in its direction or place*, whatever the day's
   verdict, as a veto of place, not of the day.
2. **The date's earth lords.** The almanac writes them as entries of the
   day among the other results (KD p. 495); the rites name them as a
   quality of the time with the fierce planets (CKG vol. 11; SDP's "free
   of fierce planets"; DZ above). So they count as the day's own factors,
   not by the place rule; no text gives them a rank either.
3. **A work avoided in one direction.** Every text here meets it by
   choosing the place or the facing, not by counting it against the day:
   avoid the direction, work facing the clean one on the chosen day
   (MTA), dig at the belly (LCM, SDP, BSB). Such a work therefore weighs
   for neither side of the day in these sources; it is a condition on how
   the work is done.
