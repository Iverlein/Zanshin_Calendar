# The 旧暦 page's other great days: the deities' days, the Shinto days, 節分 and 彼岸

Sigla, numbering and quoting rules are in [README.md](README.md).


## The great days beyond the nine festivals (RD412)

Read 2026-11-24 (RD412). For each deity the question is which day of the
year is its main one, the monthly 縁日 (ennichi) only as the background; and
for each day its date rule, its calendar today, how widely it is kept, and
what it is kept with. Amida, the Prajñāpāramita (般若) and Kannon were added
to the reading on the same day.

### Answer in short

| Deity or day | The main yearly day the sources name | Calendar today | How widely | What it is kept with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inari (稲荷) | 初午, the first 午 day of the second month; 二の午 where the first is hindered | February of the new calendar at Fushimi (KB-S: «2月の初午大祭»); KB-S 初午 calls the old second month the start of farm work | Across Japan (KB-S: «全国的に»); in Edo every shrine of the city (TS) | Banners (幟) on the shrine, lanterns, food offerings, boys beating drums all day; deep-fried tofu and red rice (Kantō, KB-S); vermilion torii and banners, foxes (KB-S 稲荷信仰); TS's picture of Ōji Inari: torii, crowds on the stone steps, tall banners «正一位王子稲荷大明神» |
| Daikoku (大黒天) | Every 甲子 day (six a year); KB-S (old ed.): the 甲子 of the 11th month most of all; KB-N: in Tōhoku the 12th month's «大黒の年取り» | 甲子 is a sexagenary day, the same in either calendar; the month is the old one in the sources | Every 甲子 in Edo at a dozen temples (TS) | Forked daikon (二また大根), red-bean rice, black beans; lamp-wicks (灯心) sold in the streets (TS, KB-N); the god in a hood with a mallet on rice bales (KB-N, KB-S) |
| Fudō Myōō (不動明王) | 初不動, 1/28 (KB-D; KB-S: many visitors); TS: the 28th of the first, fifth and ninth months are Meguro's festivals | 1/28 (KB-D, no calendar named) | Edo: Meguro and about a dozen temples on every 28th (TS) | Meguro's souvenirs: sweets, millet cakes, rice-cake flowers (飴 粟餅 餅花, TS); the god with sword and rope in flames (KB-N, KB-S) |
| Jizō (地蔵) | 地蔵盆, the 24th of the seventh month | Now 23–24 August (KB-S; KB-N «主として8月（旧暦では7月）24日») | Kyoto and the Kinki, West Japan (KB-N, KB-S); not an Edo day (TS has only one temple's visit on 7/24) | The Jizō dressed and painted, offerings, lanterns, children, dumplings and flowers, fires (KB-S, KB-N) |
| Kannon (観音) | 四万六千日, 7/10 at Sensō-ji (KB-S, KB-N, TS); 初観音, 1/18 (KB-D, KB-S) | Sensō-ji now 9–10 July, new calendar; the Kansai a month late, 9–10 August (KB-S) | Sensō-ji the best known; Kiyomizu, Shitennō-ji (KB-N) | Ground-cherries (ほおずき) since mid-Meiji, before them red maize against thunder (KB-S, KB-N) |
| Amida (阿弥陀) | 十夜 (御十夜), 6th–15th of the tenth month; the two 彼岸 (六阿弥陀参) | 十夜: the sources give the old date; Shinnyo-dō now 5–15 November (KB-S) | 十夜: the Jōdo school's temples (TS, KB-N); 六阿弥陀: Edo at both 彼岸 (TS, KB-N) | 十夜: night-long nembutsu, red-bean gruel (KB-S); 六阿弥陀: TS's landscape of the six temples' route |
| Prajñāpāramita (般若) | No one yearly day: the 大般若 reading (転読) is held at many temples on many days (TS) | — | Every school (KB-N) | — (no motif in the sources read) |
| 夏越の祓 | The last day of the sixth month (the leap sixth if there is one, TS) | The sources say «6月晦日» (KB-N); not stated which calendar | Shrines across Japan (KB-N, KB-S, KB-Y) | Passing through the 茅の輪, the reed ring; paper cut as garments thrown into the river (TS) |
| 年越の祓 | The last day of the twelfth month | As above | Fewer: KB-Y says the sixth-month rite is the one widely kept by the people | Shrine rites; at Ōji the foxes' fires that night (TS) |
| 節分 | The day before 立春 | Now 3 or 4 February (KB-N, KB-S) | Across Japan (KB-S) | Roasted soybeans thrown, «鬼は外，福は内», sardine head on a holly twig at the door, the 年男 (TS, KB-N, KB-S) |
| 彼岸 | Seven days round 春分 and 秋分 | Since 1844 the equinox is the middle day (KB-S); TS (1838) gives an older rule | Across Japan (KB-S: Japan's own) | Grave visits, dumplings, temple sermons (KB-N); 六阿弥陀 (TS) |

### 初午 and 二の午 (Inari)

TS, pid 8369317, frame 35, left leaf, the last three columns (second month,
after the 1st's entries), read on the scan:

> 初午（ハツウマ）○江戸中稲荷祭　前日より賑へり　〔割注〕江府はすへて稲荷勧請の社夥しく武家は屋敷毎に鎮守の社あり市中には一町に三五社勧請せさる事なし　寺社の境内に安する所は神楽を奏し幣帛をさゝけ市中には挑灯

and on, frame 38, right leaf, first columns, read on the scan:

> 行燈をともし五彩の幟を建つらね神前には供物燈火をさゝけ修験を請て法〔楽〕… 男児輩群集りて終日鼓吹す○千社参りと号して稲荷の社へ詣るもの小さき紙に〔…〕名を記したる札をはりてかへる此族殊に多し何れも中人以下の態なり○神田紺屋町の辺には…を造りて商ふ…　○初午の…太鼓…

("First horse day: the Inari festival throughout Edo, busy from the day
before. [Note:] In Edo the shrines of Inari invited in are without number;
the warrior houses have a guardian shrine in every mansion, and in the town
there is no ward without three or five [三五 read with doubt]. Where it stands
in a temple's or shrine's precinct, kagura is played and offerings of cloth
are made; in the town, paper lanterns and standing lanterns are lit, banners
of five colours are set up in rows, offerings and lights are put before the
god, a mountain ascetic is called for the rite …; boys gather and beat drums
all day. Those who go round the Inari shrines calling it the thousand-shrine
visit paste small slips with their names [read with doubt] and go home; most
of them are of the middling sort.") The words marked … are cursive not read
on the crop; the OCR has «○初牛のお給する大鼓商人街ふ…» in the Meiji copy and
«東都稲荷の社は前に人街に多し» here, which point to drum-sellers in the
streets before the day, not read on the scan.

Frame 36 is a double picture: «王子稲荷社 初午詣» (Ōji Inari, the visit on the
first horse day), a wooded hill with a great torii, stone steps crowded with
visitors and tall banners lettered «正一位王子稲荷大明神», with two haikai on
初午. Frame 37 is a second picture, «烏森稲荷社 初午祭» (Karasumori Inari's
festival; its caption *OCR only*, the frame not looked at). Frame 38 goes on with the
procession of the Hibiya and Karasumori shrines (神輿, 獅子頭, 花出し) and a list
of some sixty Inari shrines of Edo (*OCR only*); among them «王子稲荷 別当金輪寺
関八州稲荷の司なりといへり» (Ōji is said to be the head of the Inari of the eight
Kantō provinces).

Frame 39, right leaf, read on the scan:

> ○今日上野清水堂へ百度参りをなす者多し　かくすれは福智を得る由云ならはせり
> ○此日小児手習読書の師匠へ入門せしむる者多し　「いの字より習ひそめてや稲荷山」其角
> 二午　三午　○古例により又は初午の日さはる事あれは今日いなり祭を行ふ所あり　尤武家に多く町家には大かた初午に執行ふなり
> ○二午日　聖坂功運寺稲荷祭 …

("On this day many make the hundred-times visit at the Kiyomizu hall of
Ueno … Many children are entered with a writing or reading teacher on this
day. The second and third horse days: by old custom, or where something
hinders the first horse day, some keep the Inari festival on this day; most
of them warrior houses, the town keeping it mostly on the first.")

KB-N 初午: the first 午 day of the second month, held to be Inari's festival
day, with Inari confraternity rites; making 初午 dumplings and children
gathering to beat drums are widespread; where the first falls too early
(the 1st or 2nd) it is put off to the second 午 in some places; read as a
rite that foretells the year's harvest. KB-S 初午: "全国的に稲荷信仰と結びついている"
(tied to Inari across the country); the old second month's first 午 falls at
the start of farm work; in the Kantō, banners set up at the Inari shrine,
deep-fried tofu and red rice offered (油揚げや赤飯), children beating drums at
the shrine; some places keep 二の午 and 三の午 too, Amami a fourth-month one,
others an eleventh-month one. KB-S 稲荷信仰: Inari's feast is the first 午 of
the second month, with vermilion banners, flags and torii (朱色の幟・旗・鳥居),
the rank 正一位, the fox as the god's messenger. KB-S 伏見稲荷大社: the shrine is
said to be founded on the first 午 of the second month of 711; among its
rites now «2月の初午大祭», famous since the Heian period; KB-N: the 2nd month's
first 午 has long drawn visitors from the whole country.

### 甲子 (Daikoku)

TS, pid 8369317, frame 16, right leaf, under the first month's monthly days,
read on the scan:

> 甲子日（毎月）大国神参　神田社地　小石川伝通院寺中福聚院〔開帳あり参詣群集す〕　東叡山護国院　本所亀沢町大黒院〔開帳〕　麻布一本松大法寺　同浅草寺中長寿院〔出世大黒〕　下谷どぶ店蓮光寺〔開帳〕　牛込原町経王寺　同駒込追分東横町大恩寺　日暮里経王寺　青山仙寿院　同立法寺
> 〔割注〕今日俗家にも此神を祭り二また大根小豆飯黒豆等を供す　街に燈心（トウシン）を商ふ

("Every 甲子 day: the visit to the god Daikoku, at [twelve places]. [Note:]
On this day lay houses too worship this god and offer forked daikon,
red-bean rice, black beans and the like; lamp-wicks are sold in the
streets.") The second character of «大国神» is read as 国 with the OCR; it
could be 黒 in the cursive. TS's preface (frame 10, *OCR only*) says the
days kept by the sexagenary cycle (甲子を以て行ふの類) are entered after the
first day of each month. Vol. 4, frame 51 (*OCR only*; the eleventh month by its place before the
大師講 of frame 54, not checked on the scan), a first 子 day:
«初子日○子祭まつるこれを子まつりといふ赤小豆飯等を供す» followed by three Daikoku
temples («本所亀沢町大黒院大こく開帳» and two more): the 子 day, not 甲子.

KB-N 甲子: 甲子 is Daikoku's ennichi; staying up until the hour of the rat
on the night of 甲子 to worship him is 甲子祭 or 甲子待, with soybeans, black
beans and forked daikon (大豆、黒豆、二股大根), for worldly luck; the 日次紀事
says the court worshipped on all six 甲子 nights of the year; in the town a
lamp-wick bought on each 甲子 was called the «子灯心». KB-S 縁日 (old edition,
as Kotobank quotes it): Daikoku's are the six 甲子 days of the year, and «とくに
11月の縁日が重んじられた» (the eleventh month's above all). KB-N 大黒天: in
Tōhoku the «大黒の年取り» in the twelfth month offers forked daikon; the
familiar figure wears a hood, carries a great sack on the left shoulder and
a mallet in the right hand and stands on rice bales (頭巾、大袋、小槌、米俵).
KB-S 大黒天: the rat as his day's animal from the myth of the rat that saved
Ōkuninushi; the 甲子講. No source read names a «初甲子».

### The 28th (Fudō Myōō)

TS, pid 8369317, frame 31, left leaf, read on the scan:

> 廿八日（毎月）不動参　目黒瀧泉寺〔正五九月は祭礼にて廿日頃より参詣群集し廿七日廿八日の両日は殊に賑へり廿七日の夜より籠りあり〕　飴粟餅餅花を土産とす常に百度参り等ありて詣人たゆる事なし　目白新長谷寺　目赤南谷寺　深川寺町法乗院〔開帳〕　赤坂一ツ木威徳寺　四谷新宿太宗寺　下谷通新町永久寺〔開帳〕　三田寺町宝生院　〔荒浪不動〕両国薬研堀〔開帳〕　坂本町成田旅宿
> ○不動尊正五九月開帳の場所　駒込追分願行寺〔大山同木不どう〕　牛込原町報恩寺　亀戸東覚寺

("The 28th, every month: the visit to Fudō. Meguro Ryūsen-ji [in the first,
fifth and ninth months it is the festival: crowds from about the 20th, the
27th and 28th busiest, vigils from the night of the 27th]; sweets, millet
cakes and rice-cake flowers are taken home as gifts; the hundred-times
visit goes on always … [eleven more places] … Places where Fudō is shown in
the first, fifth and ninth months: …") The «正五九月» was read on the scan;
the OCR has «正五月».

KB-D 初不動: the year's first ennichi of Fudō, 28 January (精選版 cites 1716 for
the word); KB-S 縁日: Fudō's day is the 28th of every month, «初不動は参詣者が多い».
KB-N 縁日 gives Fudō the 2nd, 7th and 28th. KB-S 不動明王: in the Kantō Meguro
(Ryūsen-ji) and Narita (Shinshō-ji) are the best known; the figure holds a
sword in the right hand and a rope in the left, blue-black, with flames over
the whole body (右手に剣，左手に羂索，青黒色の全身に火焰); KB-N adds the rock seat,
the braid over the left shoulder and the two boy attendants. TS's twelfth
month has a market at Yagenbori Fudō (frame 64 region, *OCR only*:
«薬研堀不動尊年の市»); no 終い不動 is named in TS or on Kotobank.

### The 24th and 地蔵盆 (Jizō)

TS, pid 8369317, frame 30, read on the scan (under the 24th of the first
month, among the monthly days):

> （毎月）地蔵参　芝愛宕権現社〔毎月参詣群集す〕　同所下不動堂の内　石像地蔵参　湯嶋霊雲寺　三田松秀寺〔日限地蔵開ちやう〕　深川寺町本誓寺〔鼻欠地蔵開帳　放生会〕　日本橋西河岸〔夕方より参詣多し〕
> 又六地蔵参南方四十八所東方四十八所山の手四十八所の地蔵参等あり付録に記せり

("Every month: the visit to Jizō, at Shiba Atago [crowds every month] …
Nihonbashi Nishigashi [many in the evening]. There are also the six Jizō,
the forty-eight Jizō of the south, of the east and of the Yamanote; they are
in the appendix.") The seventh month's 24th in vol. 3 (pid 8369318, frame 16,
*OCR only*) has only «廿四日○小石川戸崎町喜雲寺地蔵参» among other temples'
rites: no 地蔵盆 in Edo. TS's appendix (pid 8369318, frames 67–68, *OCR only*)
lists the six bronze Jizō of the Edo roads and the forty-eight-place rounds.

KB-S 地蔵盆: held on 23–24 August, by children, in the Kinki around Kyoto;
it came from the round of the six Jizō at Kyoto's entrances on the old
7/24 (室町時代半ばころ) and the Hataeda custom; on the coast of the Sea of Japan
the Jizō are painted with colours (絵具などできれいに化粧); Bakin (1802) saw each
ward set up a curtained Jizō with offerings and lanterns and keep vigil; in
Tajima dumplings and flowers are brought to the Jizō at the crossroads and
straw fires lit for Atago. KB-N 地蔵盆: «主として8月（旧暦では7月）24日», West
Japan and the Kinki above all; decorating the Jizō, offerings, children's
play, hymns, Bon dances, fires. KB-S 縁日: 地蔵's day the 24th since the
Heian period, «とくに7月24日は地蔵盆».

### 四万六千日 and 初観音 (Kannon)

TS, pid 8369318, frame 5, read on the scan (seventh month):

> 九日○観音千日参今明日　〔割注〕世俗四万六千日ともいふ　この日詣つれは其の日数に向ふといふ

("9th: the thousand-day visit to Kannon, today and tomorrow. Commonly also
called forty-six thousand days: a visit on this day is said to count for so
many days.") The first month (pid 8369317, frame 26, *OCR only*) has the 17th
and 18th «観音参» at Sensō-ji, «十七日には夜中まて参詣ありて賑し». The sixth
month's 24th (frame 90, *OCR only*) has the thousand-day visit at Shiba
Atago with green ground-cherries in the precinct («境内にて青酸醤を備ふ諸人是を…»,
the verb not checked on the scan).

KB-S 四万六千日: Sensō-ji's 10 July «（現在は8，9日）» [sic, KB-N says from the 9th]
is the best known; the name used at Sensō-ji from the Edo period (続江戸砂子,
1735); tea whisks sold until Kansei, then a powder, from late Bunka red
maize against thunder, now the ground-cherry market; in the Kansai the
month-late 9–10 August, Kiyomizu-dera best known. KB-N: 10 July at
Sensō-ji, now from the ground-cherry market of the 9th; red maize against
lightning, later tea bags and ground-cherries; Kiyomizu (9–16 August) and
Shitennō-ji (9–10 August). KB-S 縁日: Kannon's 18th of every month, the first
month's 初観音, a New Year's Day visit worth a hundred days, and 7/10;
Sensō-ji's ennichi is «その代表的事例». KB-D 初観音: 18 January.

### 十夜 and the 彼岸 round (Amida)

TS, pid 8369318, frame 41, read on the scan (tenth month):

> 六日○今日より十五日に至る迄浄土宗寺院十日十夜（ジフニチジフヤ）法要執行　〔割注〕此間説法別時念仏等ありて参詣多し今日を十夜紐解（ヒモトキ）と云十四日には…あり此間俗家にても法事をなす　増上寺〔十四日檀林よりも出る　此日は庶人拝する事なし〕　本所回向院　深川本誓寺　三崎法住寺　南品川願行寺　青山善光寺　奥沢浄真寺〔九品仏〕　浅草寺念仏堂

("6th: from today to the 15th the Jōdo temples hold the ten days and ten
nights rite; sermons and special nembutsu, many visitors; today is called
the opening of the ten nights; … lay houses too hold services [read with
doubt].") Frame 42 (*OCR only*) gives its history: begun at Shinnyo-dō in
Kyoto in the Eikyō era, brought to the Jōdo school at Kamakura's Kōmyō-ji
by 観誉祐崇 in Meiō 4.

KB-S 十夜: the Jōdo school's ten-day nembutsu from the old 10/6 to 10/15;
Shinnyo-dō now keeps it 5–15 November; in Edo-period Yamato those who kept
vigil on the 14th got 十夜粥 (red-bean gruel). KB-N 十夜: lunar 10/6–15, also
御十夜, from the Shinnyo-dō vigil of 平貞国, a Jōdo rite since 1495.

TS, pid 8369317, frame 39, read on the scan (second month), and the double
picture «彼岸 六阿弥陀 参» on the same spread (the Sumida river with 大川橋
and the six temples numbered among fields and villages):

### The versions that differ

- **When 彼岸 began.** TS (1838, before the Tenpō calendar of 1844) gives both 彼岸
  as starting on «春分〔秋分〕の初日より三日に当る日»; KB-S says that rule is the
  one of the time before mid-Edo, and that under the Hōreki and Kansei
  calendars the spring one began on the sixth day before 春分 and the autumn
  one on the day before 秋分. Today's rule (equinox in the middle) is from
  1844 (KB-S).
- **Daikoku's main day.** KB-S (old edition, 縁日): the 甲子 of the eleventh
  month; KB-N (大黒天): the twelfth-month «大黒の年取り» in Tōhoku; TS: every
  甲子 alike, and the eleventh month's first 子 day (frame 51, OCR only).
- **Fudō's.** KB-D and KB-S: 初不動, 1/28; TS: Meguro's festivals on the 28th
  of the first, fifth and ninth months alike.
- **Kannon's.** 四万六千日 at Sensō-ji: TS and KB-S give 7/10 (TS: the 9th
  and 10th); KB-S «現在は8，9日» against KB-N «現在は9日のほおずき市から»; the
  Kansai keep it a month late, 9–10 August (KB-S, KB-N). 初観音 1/18 (KB-D,
  KB-S).
- **Jizō's.** 地蔵盆 is a Kinki day (KB-S, KB-N), now 23–24 August; in Edo
  (TS) the 24th of every month is the visiting day and the seventh month's
  24th has no great rite.

The sources make **醍醐寺's 五大力尊仁王会 on 23 February** the strongest

### 醍醐寺, 五大力尊仁王会 (23 February)

DJ (detail1.html), facts in its words where they are short: held every
year on 23 February, «「五大力さん」として親しまれている»; the five wisdom
kings (五大明王: 不動, 降三世, 軍荼利, 大威徳, 金剛夜叉) and their
manifestation the five Great-Power bodhisattvas are asked for the peace of
the land and the people's happiness; its history goes back to the time of
the emperor Daigo, 907. The charm 御影 against theft and disaster is pasted
at the entrance of Kyoto's town-houses and old shops and of homes, and the
queue for it does not break from early morning till evening; «全国から十数万人»
come; «醍醐寺最大の年中行事». On the day the 仁王会 rite runs all day in the
金堂 for the state and for «七難即滅» «七福即生», and the 御影 is given; inside
the 金堂 visitors hold a sword tied by five-coloured cords to the five
kings; before the 金堂 the rice-cake lifting (餅上げ) is offered to the
five: red and white great mirror-cakes, 150 kg for men and 90 kg for
women, lifted for as long as one can. The page's pictures are captioned
with a 柴燈護摩 before the 不動堂 and the offered mirror-cakes in rows before
the 金堂. The 前行 (15–21 February), twenty-one sittings by more than a
thousand priests and 修験 teachers of the Daigo school from the whole
country, was formerly held at the 五大堂 of 上醍醐 and is now held in the
金堂 of 下醍醐 so that people can attend. Besides the 御影 for the house
there is a 懐中守, a pocket charm for the person.

DJ (list.html), the yearly rites: «23日 五大力尊仁王会－五大力さん－ 金堂»;
«15日～21日 五大力尊仁王会前行 金堂»; and «3月15日 桜会開白大般若転読法要
下醍醐 清瀧宮拝殿».

KB-N 醍醐寺: «おもな年中行事に五大力尊仁王会(ごだいりきそんにんのうえ)（2月23日）、
太閤(たいこう)花見（4月第2日曜）がある»; on the summit (上醍醐) stand among
others the 五大堂 (rebuilt 1940, its date as given there «昭和一五年新造»
in the 日本歴史地名大系 article quoted on the same page) of the five wisdom
kings. KB-S 醍醐寺: besides its visitors, Daigo-ji is a centre of faith
through the 准胝 Kannon of 上醍醐 (the 11th of the Saigoku pilgrimage) and
its rites, «2月の〈五大力さん〉» named first, then the 清滝 festival and the
Hideyoshi blossom-viewing procession in April and the Ōmine entry in June.

What the 五大力 are, KB-S 五大力菩薩: the five bodhisattvas taught in the
«《仁王般若波羅蜜経》», who go to protect a land whose king upholds the
Three Jewels, venerated as the 本尊 of the 仁王会; KB-B: «その画像は奈良時代以来，
仁王会の本尊として用いられた», wrathful figures. KB-D (日本国語大辞典,
語誌): unusual among bodhisattvas, they are drawn wrathful with flames at
their backs; from the Middle Ages their picture was pasted on gates and
doors as a charm against disaster, and a paper with the words 五大力菩薩 was
a charm against theft. KB-S 仁王会 and KB-N 仁王会: the 仁王会 (also 仁王般若会)
is the rite of the 仁王経, one of the three sūtras for the protection of
the state, held at court since 660 and as a yearly rite of the great
temples (KB-N: «諸大寺でも年中行事の一つとして定着»).

HN, the first month (pid 1170263, frame 33), read on the scan: the same
kind of charm at another temple of Nara, Kōfuku-ji (Hossō, not Shingon), a
parallel for the motif:

> 南都興福寺〔講堂揭五大尊畫像是謂〕五大力菩薩其札梓板在寺中金藏院正月十四日修牛王加持而後貼札至十八日仕丁武則以下請其札而於講堂下賣之相傳貼是於門戸則免災難

("At Kōfuku-ji in Nara [pictures of the five great ones hang in the
lecture hall; they are called] the five Great-Power bodhisattvas. The
block for their charm is kept in the sub-temple Konzō-in; on the 14th of
the first month the ox-king (牛王) blessing is made, then the charms are
stamped; until the 18th the servants and those below them take the charms
and sell them below the lecture hall. It is handed down that pasted on a
gate or door they ward off disaster.") «仕丁武則以下» is read so on the scan
but its sense is not sure.

### The other mountain temples (Kotobank)

- 室生寺, KB-S: its prayers for and against rain were made not by esoteric
  rites but by the exoteric 《大般若経》 and 《仁王経》, and so in the
  Middle Ages too; among its treasures a 大般若経 of 581 folding books,
  copied in the early Kamakura period. No yearly day.
- 神護寺, KB-N: in 810 Kūkai, by a memorial to the throne, performed the
  «仁王経(にんのうきょう)大法» there with the scriptures he had brought from
  China. A single rite, not a yearly one.
- 金剛峯寺 and 根来寺: their Kotobank entries (KB-S, KB-N and the others on
  the page) name no Prajñāpāramita rite.

### 甲子 and 子祭 (Daikoku, q1386)

HN, the first-month volume (pid 1170263), frame 40, among the notes for the
whole year, read on the scan:

> ○凡一年中六甲子夜禁裏被祭子大乳人被獻小豆粥於御前并饗殿中男女凡每甲子民間買燈心俗称子燈心其内以十一月甲子爲最

("In the six 甲子 nights of a year the court worships the 子 [the rat];
the chief nurse offers red-bean gruel before the emperor and the men and
women of the palace are feasted. On every 甲子 the townspeople buy
lamp-wicks, commonly called 子燈心; of these [甲子 days] the eleventh
month's is the foremost.") This is the second source q1386 asked for, older
than the 世界大百科事典's 縁日 article: the eleventh month's 甲子 is
foremost of the six. The passage does not name Daikoku; KB-N 甲子 (RD412)
ties the 甲子 night and the 子燈心 to him.

HN, the 11th-month volume (pid 1170283), frame 17, read on the scan (the
first words «○此» by the OCR only):

> ○此月子日備供物爲子祭○禁裏亦有此儀四辻家參內於御前被奏林歌此樂舞衣因有鼠紋也樂了後則以所供子之調味被饗四辻家[…]○銀座大黒屋尊崇子祭而特祝之家内宴遊間催狂言爲大黒天授槌之戲凡諸商此月子日子時祭之盖買賣之間取其利也欲比鼠子之蕃息也凡所供子之膳食每品加大豆又供兩股大根大豆鼠之所好食也兩股大根俗稱福來

("This month, on the 子 day, offerings are set out: the 子祭. The court
too: the Yotsutsuji house comes to play the piece 林歌 before the emperor,
whose dancers' robes bear a rat pattern; then they are feasted with the
food offered to the 子. ... The Daikoku-ya of the silver mint holds the
子祭 in honour and celebrates it above all, with feasting and a comic
play of Daikoku handing over his mallet. All merchants worship on this
month's 子 day at the hour of the rat, to gain in trade and to multiply
like the young of rats. Soybeans are added to every dish offered, and
forked daikon and soybeans, the rats' favourite food, are offered; the
forked daikon is commonly called 福來.") The 子 day is not said to be the
first; the passage stands among the month's moveable days.

HN, the same volume, frame 41, read on the scan, among the year's
unveilings (開帳):

> ○東寺御供所所有弘法大師所刻之大黑像每子年十一月子日開帳寺僧修法事諸人羣集

("Tō-ji's Daikoku, carved by Kūkai and kept in its 御供所, is shown in
every year of the rat on the eleventh month's 子 day; the monks hold a
rite and crowds gather.")

KB-D 子祭: デジタル大辞泉, «陰暦10月または11月の甲子(きのえね)の日に行う大黒天の祭り»,
with sake and food, brown rice, black beans, forked daikon; 日本国語大辞典,
«旧暦一〇月か一一月の初子(はつね)の日に行なう収穫祭», the same offerings, its
earliest example «今夜子祭如例» from 実隆公記, 11th month of 大永4 (1524).
KB-D 甲子待ち: on the night of a 甲子 one stays up until the hour of the
rat and worships Daikoku with soybeans, black beans and forked daikon; KB-B
甲子祭: Daikoku's night rite at Buddhist temples on a 甲子, «特に甲子の日が
重視され», with fairs at the Daikoku temples.

So for a year whose eleventh month has no 甲子 the sources read give the
same month's 子 day (日本国語大辞典: the first 子 of the 10th or 11th old
month) as Daikoku's 子祭, with the same offerings; a 子 day falls in every
month. None of them says the 子 day stands in for a missing 甲子, and none
says whether the eleventh month is now taken as the old month or as
November.
