### What a pebble is: a tally, not a throw

Every reckoning chapter counts its *pebbles* (རྡེལ, rdel; also རྡེའུ,
rde'u), and none of them is a throw of chance. The pebbles are counters
laid on a chart, one for each place the reckoning fills, white, black or
both by a fixed rule from the relation of two elements. The notes say so
at their start (vol. 2, p. 474, img. 482, read on the scan):

> སྐབས་དང་པོ་ཚེ་རབས་ལས་རྩིས་ལ། སྤྱིར་གྱི་སྐོར་མགོའི་རྡེལ་གྲངས་རྣམས་ནི་ས་ཁོངས་འཛིན་པའི་གྲངས་ཙམ་ལས། ངོས་འཛིན་རྡེལ་སོགས་ཡི་གེར་བྲིས་ཀྱང་ཆོག

In the first section, the reckoning of lives and karma: the counts of
pebbles of the starting points are only the number of places they hold;
the identifying pebbles and the like may as well be written as letters.

The rule of colour, as the illness chapter gives it (vol. 1, p. 417,
img. 427, read on the scan):

> རྡེལ་བོང་མ་གྲོགས་དཀར་པོ་དང་། །དགྲ་ནག་བུ་ལ་དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་། །ནང་ཕྲད་ས་ཆུ་དཀར་པོ་དང་། །ཤིང་ལྕགས་མེ་གསུམ་ནག་པོར་བོར།

Lay a white pebble where the element meets its mother or its friend, a
black one where it meets its enemy, a white and a black where it meets
its son; where it meets its own element, white for earth and water,
black for wood, iron and fire. Ch. 27 opens with the same rule (vol. 2,
p. 3, img. 11, read on the scan).

So the answer to R1's question "what is a casting" is: within ch. 20–30
no reckoning is decided by chance, as Mo is. What a reckoning cannot
take from dates is of three other kinds, named chapter by chapter below:
**a fact to ask** (the clan's element, a person's role, the siblings, who
lives in the household, the dead's burial), **an observation** (the sky
at dawn on given dates, a sneeze, a dream, the caller's words, the shape
of the ground), and **the moment of reckoning** itself, which several
chapters count as a pebble and which the clock gives.

### WB's own division of the reckonings

The notes, *The Memoranda of the Chinese Reckoning, Its Essence Made Clear*
(ནག་རྩིས་བརྗེད་བྱང་སྙིང་པོ་གསལ་བ, nag rtsis brjed byang snying po gsal ba;
vol. 2, pp. 474–541),
divide WB's Chinese reckoning (ནག་རྩིས, nag rtsis) into eight sections
(vol. 2, p. 474, img. 482, read on the scan):

> སོ་སོའི་སྐོར་མགོ་ལ་ཚེ་ཀག་ནད་དུར་ས་བག་རྩིས་དྲུག་དང་སྤྱི་རྩིས་དང་རྩིས་ཕྲན་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་སྐོར་ཚོ་དང་བརྒྱད་ལས།

### The nine means of reckoning (ch. 21)

Ch. 21 (vol. 1, pp. 252–258, img. 262–268) is not a reckoning but the
tool all of them use (p. 253, img. 263, read on the scan with Yigdzin-1):

> བརྩི་ཐབས་བསྡུ་ན་རྣམ་པ་དགུ། །ཁམས་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ཚོད་དང་། །སྨེ་བ་སྤར་ཁ་གཟའ་སྐར་མ།

The means of reckoning, gathered, are nine: the element (ཁམས, khams),
the year, the month, the day, the hour, the mewa, the
nine numbers (སྨེ་བ, sme ba), the trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha), the planet (the weekday)
and the mansion. By the etext the chapter goes on to the life force
(སྲོག, srog), body (ལུས, lus), power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang) and luck (ཀླུང་རྟ,
klung rta) of each year animal (pp. 253–254), the element relations of
mother, son, friend and enemy, the months and hours as animals, the
mewa and the trigram circles (pp. 255–257), the mansions' elements
(p. 257), and the twelve stages of rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud),
from taking breath to entering the grave (p. 258). Much of this is read already in
[year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md) and
[personal-mansions.md](personal-mansions.md).

### The inventory, chapter by chapter

Each chapter states its own size at its start: what other traditions
expand it to, and what WB keeps, as result pebbles (འབྲས་རྡེལ, 'bras rdel,
or root pebbles, རྩ་རྡེལ, rtsa rdel) and identifying sign pebbles (ངོས་འཛིན་
མཚན་མའི་རྡེལ, ngos 'dzin mtshan ma'i rdel). "Whose dates" lists what the
etext names as inputs; RD12–RD17 settle each on the scan.

#### Ch. 20, the framework of the element reckoning (vol. 1, pp. 218–251, img. 228–261)

Not a reckoning: the turtle and the forming of the world (pp. 218–221),
the transmission of the Chinese reckoning to Tibet and its books
(pp. 244–248), the parts of an older system ascribed to the translator
Khams pa Khra mo (ཁམས་པ་ཁྲ་མོ, khams pa khra mo) (pp. 248–250). That system's sizes (p. 249, img. 259,
read on the scan):

> དེ་ལ་སྤྲོས་པའི་མཐའ་གཅོད་གྲངས། །གཏན་རྩིས་རྡེའུ་བརྒྱ་སོ་དྲུག །ཀེག་ལ་ཞེ་བདུན་གཉེན་སྦྱོར་ལ། །བཅུ་གསུམ་ནང་སྲི་བཅུ་དགུ་དང་། །གསོན་ཁོང་གཤིན་འདོན་ཉི་ཤུ་ཐམ། །བླ་རྡེལ་བཅུ་གཉིས་གཤིན་འདུར་ཤིད། །གཉིས་ལ་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་བཅོ་ལྔའོ། །བསྡོམས་པས་བཞི་བརྒྱ་བཞི་བཅུར་བཞེད།

The counts of the expanded part: the fixed reckoning (གཏན་རྩིས, gtan
rtsis, the life reckoning of the birth) 136 pebbles; the obstacles 47;
the marriage match (གཉེན་སྦྱོར, gnyen sbyor) 13; the inner harmful spirits
of the dead (ནང་སྲི, nang sri) 19; the living household and taking out the
dead (གསོན་ཁོང་གཤིན་འདོན, gson khong gshin 'don) 20; the pebbles of the la,
the life-spirit (བླ་རྡེལ, bla rdel) 12; the burial and the funeral of the
dead (གཤིན་འདུར་ཤིད, gshin 'dur shid), the two, 215; in all, 440. 136, 47
and 13 are the root counts WB gives again in ch. 22, 24 and 23, and 19
that of ch. 25 (p. 446, etext). The parts add up to 462, not 440 (below,
*Open*).

#### Ch. 22, the reckoning of lives and karma: the birth (vol. 1, pp. 259–350, img. 269–360) — RD13

- **What it reckons.** A child's lot from its birth: the four precious
  pebbles (གཅེས་རྡེལ, gces rdel) of the birth year, month, day and hour as
  king, minister, soldier and weapon (p. 260); the special pebble (སྒོས་
  རྡེལ, sgos rdel) of the year's relation to the clan element (p. 261);
  the five unchanging luck pebbles (མི་འགྱུར་ཀླུང་རྡེལ, mi 'gyur klung rdel):
  the father's year, the mother's year with her trigram and mewa at the
  birth, and the birth month, a daughter taking the mother's year for the
  father's (p. 261; the notes, p. 474); the hundred pebbles of the life
  force, body, power and luck against self, mother, son, friend and enemy
  (p. 261); then, by the etext, the sixty years' characters, lifespans and
  obstacles (pp. 263–279), births on each trigram and mewa with their
  former and next lives (pp. 280–303), the children of the gods, of
  glory, of life and of demons (ལྷ་དཔལ་ཚེ་བདུད, lha dpal tshe bdud, p. 294),
  the planets (p. 309), the marks of a child that will not live (p. 319),
  the hundred pebbles' readings (p. 329), the birth pebbles (p. 343), and
  how to raise a child hard to raise: protections, names, rites
  (pp. 347–349).
- **Whose dates.** The child's birth year, month, day and hour; the
  father's and mother's birth years (the mother's trigram and mewa
  follow from her year and the birth year); the clan's element; for some
  readings the forefathers' years (p. 349). The notes (p. 474, img. 482,
  read on the scan): «ཐོག་མར་བརྩི་བྱའི་ནམ་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་དང་ཕ་མའི་བབ་སྤར་སྐྱེས་སྨེ་གཟའ་སྐར་རྣམས་ལེགས་པར་ངོས་ཟིན་དགོས་ཤིང་། གཞན་ཡང་དུས་ཁམས་དང་ཕ་མེས་བརྒྱུད་པའི་ལོ་སོགས་ཤེས་ནས་བརྩི་བ་ཡིན་ལ།»
  First the year, month, day and hour of the person's birth, the
  parents' trigrams, the birth mewa, planet and mansion must be known
  well; besides, the element and the years of the forefathers' line are
  known before reckoning.
- **The clan element is asked, not computed** (p. 474, read on the scan):
  «དུས་ཁམས་ནི་ངོས་ཟིན་ན་དེ་དང་མིན་ན་བླ་རྟགས་གཡག་སོགས་གང་ཡིན་ངག་རྒྱུན་དང་རྨི་ལམ་སོགས་ལས་བརྟག་པ་དང་། མི་གཤིས་སོགས་ཀྱིས་རུས་ཆེན་ལྔ་གང་ཡིན་ངོས་ཟིན་པར་བྱའོ།»
  If the element is known, take it; if not, find from family tradition,
  dreams and the like which la-sign, a yak or another, is the family's,
  and from the person's character which of the five great clans (རུས་ཆེན,
  rus chen) it is. The print reads «དུས་ཁམས», "the element of the time",
  both times; the five great clans point to «རུས་ཁམས», the clan's element
  (below, *Open*).
- **Size** (p. 262, img. 272, read on the scan):
  «སྤྲོས་ཏེ་རྡེལ་གྲངས་མང་འདོད་ན། །ཉི་ཁྲི་ཉིས་སྟོང་སུམ་བརྒྱ་དང་། །བརྒྱད་ཅུ་ལ་སོགས་འབྱུང་ན་ཡང་། །ལྷག་པོ་སྤྲོས་པ་མ་ཡིན་པའི། །འབྲས་བུ་དངོས་ཀྱི་རྡེའུ་དང་། །བརྩི་དུས་མི་ལུས་ཁྱད་པར་གྱི། །ངོས་འཛིན་མཚན་མའི་རྡེའུ་རྣམས། །ཡོངས་བསྡོམས་རྡེའུ་སུམ་སྟོང་དང་། །བདུན་བརྒྱ་དོན་གསུམ་གཏན་ཕབ་པར།»
  Expanded, as others want, 22,380 pebbles and more; WB fixes the result
  pebbles and the identifying pebbles not to be left out at 3,773 in all.
  The page goes on to break the count down (the precious and luck
  pebbles, the 120 of the life force and the rest against the five
  relations, the four of rise and decline, …); that breakdown was seen on
  the scan but not transcribed. The root count of the older system is
  136 (p. 260, etext; p. 249 above).
- **Calculable** from the dates, with the clan element asked once.

#### Ch. 23, marriage and the road (vol. 1, pp. 351–379, img. 361–389) — RD15

- **What it reckons.** Three parts (p. 351, read on the scan): whether two
  match, the reckoning for setting out on the road (from p. 365), the
  remedies. The first in fifteen points (p. 351, read on the scan, the
  sixth's first letter by Yigdzin-1):
  «དང་པོ་ལ་ཡང་བཅོ་ལྔ་སྟེ། །བྱི་སྤངས་ལོ་འགྲོས་སྡེབ་ཚུལ(༡)དང་། །ལོ་ངན་མི་རུང་ངོས་འཛིན་ཚུལ(༢)། །རྐྱང་པ་དབང་ཐང་སྒོས་ཀྱི་འབྲས(༣)། །ཁུག་པ(༤)ཡང་ཁུག(༥)ཎི་ཤ(༦)དང་། །སྤུན་གྲངས(༧)ཕ(༨)དང་མ(༩)ཡི་རུས། །སྐྱེས་ཟླ(༡༠)སྤར་ཁ(༡༡)སྨེ་བའི(༡༢)ཀླུང་། །དར་གུད་ཁྲི་སྒོ(༡༣)གཟའ(༡༤)སྐར(༡༥)དང་། །བཅས་ཏེ་བཅོ་ལྔས་གཏན་ལ་དབབ།»
  (1) avoiding kin, and how the years go together; (2) the bad years that
  will not do; (3) the single power's own result; (4) the hidden and (5) the deeper hidden; (6) «ཎི་ཤ», not identified;
  (7) the number of siblings; (8) the father's and (9) the mother's clan;
  (10) the birth month, (11) the trigram, (12) the mewa's luck; (13) the
  throne doors of the rise and decline; (14) the planet and (15) the
  mansion. The second part's seventeen parts are in
  [wb-contents.md](wb-contents.md) (*Found in passing*).
- **Whose dates.** Both partners' birth years, months, trigrams, mewa,
  planets and mansions; their fathers' and mothers' clans, the number of

#### Ch. 30, the reckoning of the moment, small examinations of the ground and of pebbles (vol. 2, pp. 144–173, img. 152–181) — RD17

- **What it reckons.** An answer from the moment of asking (དུས་ཚོད་རྩིས,
  dus tshod rtsis). First the dreams (p. 144, read on the scan) and the
  caller's signs and words (p. 145); then, by the etext, the waxing dates
  set on the twelve links (p. 146), the moment's year, month, day and hour

  the thirteen circles (p. 154), the obstacles, the dead and building
  (p. 155), the ground by its shapes and colours (pp. 159–162), the mewa
  (p. 162), the sick (p. 168), the house and its sides (p. 169), the
  asker's posture (p. 170). The notes (p. 535, etext) set the moment's
  trigram by the date and eight parts of the day, and its mewa likewise.
- **Whose dates.** The year, month, day and hour of the question; the
  asker's year for some points.
- **Size** (p. 144, img. 152, read on the scan): «དེ་ལ་རྡེལ་གྲངས་ཁ་ཅིག་ལྟར། །སྤྲོས་ན་བདུན་སྟོང་བདུན་བརྒྱ་དང་། །བདུན་ཅུར་འབྱོར་ཀྱང་དགོས་མེད་ཀྱི། །ལྷག་པོ་མ་ཡིན་འབྲས་བུའི་རྡེལ། །སུམ་བརྒྱ་བཅུ་དགུ་གཞན་ཡང་ནི། །བརྩི་དུས་མི་ལུས་མཚན་མའི་རྡེལ། །ཆིག་སྟོང་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་སོ་ལྔ་རྣམས། །བསྡོམས་པས་སྟོང་དང་ལྔ་བརྒྱ་དང་། །ང་བཞིས་གཏན་ཕབ»
