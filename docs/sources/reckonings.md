### What a pebble is: a tally, not a throw

Every reckoning chapter counts its *pebbles* (རྡེལ, rdel; also རྡེའུ,
rde'u), and none of them is a throw of chance. The pebbles are counters
laid on a chart, one for each place the reckoning fills, white, black or
both by a fixed rule from the relation of two elements. The notes say so
at their start (vol. 2, p. 474, img. 482, read on the scan):

> སྐབས་དང་པོ་ཚེ་རབས་ལས་རྩིས་ལ། སྤྱིར་གྱི་སྐོར་མགོའི་རྡེལ་གྲངས་རྣམས་ནི་ས་ཁོངས་འཛིན་པའི་གྲངས་ཙམ་ལས། ངོས་འཛིན་རྡེལ་སོགས་ཡི་གེར་བྲིས་ཀྱང་ཆོག

In the first section, the reckoning of lives and karma: the counts of
pebbles of the starting points are only the number of places they hold;
the identifying pebbles and the like may as well be written as letters.

The rule of colour, as the illness chapter gives it (vol. 1, p. 417,
img. 427, read on the scan):

> རྡེལ་བོང་མ་གྲོགས་དཀར་པོ་དང་། །དགྲ་ནག་བུ་ལ་དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་། །ནང་ཕྲད་ས་ཆུ་དཀར་པོ་དང་། །ཤིང་ལྕགས་མེ་གསུམ་ནག་པོར་བོར།

Lay a white pebble where the element meets its mother or its friend, a
black one where it meets its enemy, a white and a black where it meets
its son; where it meets its own element, white for earth and water,
black for wood, iron and fire. Ch. 27 opens with the same rule (vol. 2,
p. 3, img. 11, read on the scan).

So the answer to R1's question "what is a casting" is: within ch. 20–30
no reckoning is decided by chance, as Mo is (one exception: ch. 25's p. 442 gives a second way for the twelve stages that is a pebble cast, *mo*; [health.md](health.md), RD12). What a reckoning cannot
take from dates is of three other kinds, named chapter by chapter below:
**a fact to ask** (the clan's element, a person's role, the siblings, who
lives in the household, the dead's burial), **an observation** (the sky
at dawn on given dates, a sneeze, a dream, the caller's words, the shape
of the ground), and **the moment of reckoning** itself, which several
chapters count as a pebble and which the clock gives.

### WB's own division of the reckonings

The notes, *The Memoranda of the Chinese Reckoning, Its Essence Made Clear*
(ནག་རྩིས་བརྗེད་བྱང་སྙིང་པོ་གསལ་བ, nag rtsis brjed byang snying po gsal ba;
vol. 2, pp. 474–541),
divide WB's Chinese reckoning (ནག་རྩིས, nag rtsis) into eight sections
(vol. 2, p. 474, img. 482, read on the scan):

> སོ་སོའི་སྐོར་མགོ་ལ་ཚེ་ཀག་ནད་དུར་ས་བག་རྩིས་དྲུག་དང་སྤྱི་རྩིས་དང་རྩིས་ཕྲན་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་སྐོར་ཚོ་དང་བརྒྱད་ལས།

Of the starting points of each: the six reckonings of life, obstacles,

### The nine means of reckoning (ch. 21)

Ch. 21 (vol. 1, pp. 252–258, img. 262–268) is not a reckoning but the
tool all of them use (p. 253, img. 263, read on the scan with Yigdzin-1):

> བརྩི་ཐབས་བསྡུ་ན་རྣམ་པ་དགུ། །ཁམས་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ཚོད་དང་། །སྨེ་བ་སྤར་ཁ་གཟའ་སྐར་མ།

The means of reckoning, gathered, are nine: the element (ཁམས, khams),
the year, the month, the day, the hour, the mewa, the
nine numbers (སྨེ་བ, sme ba), the trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha), the planet (the weekday)
and the mansion. By the etext the chapter goes on to the life force
(སྲོག, srog), body (ལུས, lus), power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang) and luck (ཀླུང་རྟ,
klung rta) of each year animal (pp. 253–254), the element relations of
mother, son, friend and enemy, the months and hours as animals, the
mewa and the trigram circles (pp. 255–257), the mansions' elements
(p. 257), and the twelve stages of rise and decline (དར་གུད, dar gud),
from taking breath to entering the grave (p. 258). Much of this is read already in
[year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md) and
[personal-mansions.md](personal-mansions.md).

### The inventory, chapter by chapter

Each chapter states its own size at its start: what other traditions
expand it to, and what WB keeps, as result pebbles (འབྲས་རྡེལ, 'bras rdel,
or root pebbles, རྩ་རྡེལ, rtsa rdel) and identifying sign pebbles (ངོས་འཛིན་
མཚན་མའི་རྡེལ, ngos 'dzin mtshan ma'i rdel). "Whose dates" lists what the
etext names as inputs; RD12–RD17 settle each on the scan.

#### Ch. 20, the framework of the element reckoning (vol. 1, pp. 218–251, img. 228–261)

Not a reckoning: the turtle and the forming of the world (pp. 218–221),
the transmission of the Chinese reckoning to Tibet and its books
(pp. 244–248), the parts of an older system ascribed to the translator
Khams pa Khra mo (ཁམས་པ་ཁྲ་མོ, khams pa khra mo) (pp. 248–250). That system's sizes (p. 249, img. 259,
read on the scan):

> དེ་ལ་སྤྲོས་པའི་མཐའ་གཅོད་གྲངས། །གཏན་རྩིས་རྡེའུ་བརྒྱ་སོ་དྲུག །ཀེག་ལ་ཞེ་བདུན་གཉེན་སྦྱོར་ལ། །བཅུ་གསུམ་ནང་སྲི་བཅུ་དགུ་དང་། །གསོན་ཁོང་གཤིན་འདོན་ཉི་ཤུ་ཐམ། །བླ་རྡེལ་བཅུ་གཉིས་གཤིན་འདུར་ཤིད། །གཉིས་ལ་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་བཅོ་ལྔའོ། །བསྡོམས་པས་བཞི་བརྒྱ་བཞི་བཅུར་བཞེད།

The counts of the expanded part: the fixed reckoning (གཏན་རྩིས, gtan
rtsis, the life reckoning of the birth) 136 pebbles; the obstacles 47;
the marriage match (གཉེན་སྦྱོར, gnyen sbyor) 13; the inner harmful spirits
of the dead (ནང་སྲི, nang sri) 19; the living household and taking out the
dead (གསོན་ཁོང་གཤིན་འདོན, gson khong gshin 'don) 20; the pebbles of the la,
the life-spirit (བླ་རྡེལ, bla rdel) 12; the burial and the funeral of the
dead (གཤིན་འདུར་ཤིད, gshin 'dur shid), the two, 215; in all, 440. 136, 47
and 13 are the root counts WB gives again in ch. 22, 24 and 23, and 19
that of ch. 25 (p. 446, etext). The parts add up to 462, not 440 (question 21).

#### Ch. 22, the reckoning of lives and karma: the birth (vol. 1, pp. 259–350, img. 269–360) — RD13

- **What it reckons.** A child's lot from its birth: the four precious
  pebbles (གཅེས་རྡེལ, gces rdel) of the birth year, month, day and hour as
  king, minister, soldier and weapon (p. 260); the special pebble (སྒོས་
  རྡེལ, sgos rdel) of the year's relation to the clan element (p. 261);
  the five unchanging luck pebbles (མི་འགྱུར་ཀླུང་རྡེལ, mi 'gyur klung rdel):
  the father's year, the mother's year with her trigram and mewa at the
  birth, and the birth month, a daughter taking the mother's year for the
  father's (p. 261; the notes, p. 474); the hundred pebbles of the life
  force, body, power and luck against self, mother, son, friend and enemy
  (p. 261); then, by the etext, the sixty years' characters, lifespans and
  obstacles (pp. 263–279), births on each trigram and mewa with their
  former and next lives (pp. 280–303), the children of the gods, of
  glory, of life and of demons (ལྷ་དཔལ་ཚེ་བདུད, lha dpal tshe bdud, p. 294),
  the planets (p. 309), the marks of a child that will not live (p. 319),
  the hundred pebbles' readings (p. 329), the birth pebbles (p. 343), and
  how to raise a child hard to raise: protections, names, rites
  (pp. 347–349).
- **Whose dates.** The child's birth year, month, day and hour; the
  father's and mother's birth years (the mother's trigram and mewa
  follow from her year and the birth year); the clan's element; for some
  readings the forefathers' years (p. 349). The notes (p. 474, img. 482,
  read on the scan): «ཐོག་མར་བརྩི་བྱའི་ནམ་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་དང་ཕ་མའི་བབ་སྤར་སྐྱེས་སྨེ་གཟའ་སྐར་རྣམས་ལེགས་པར་ངོས་ཟིན་དགོས་ཤིང་། གཞན་ཡང་དུས་ཁམས་དང་ཕ་མེས་བརྒྱུད་པའི་ལོ་སོགས་ཤེས་ནས་བརྩི་བ་ཡིན་ལ།»
  First the year, month, day and hour of the person's birth, the
  parents' trigrams, the birth mewa, planet and mansion must be known
  well; besides, the element and the years of the forefathers' line are
  known before reckoning.
- **The clan element is asked, not computed** (p. 474, read on the scan):
  «དུས་ཁམས་ནི་ངོས་ཟིན་ན་དེ་དང་མིན་ན་བླ་རྟགས་གཡག་སོགས་གང་ཡིན་ངག་རྒྱུན་དང་རྨི་ལམ་སོགས་ལས་བརྟག་པ་དང་། མི་གཤིས་སོགས་ཀྱིས་རུས་ཆེན་ལྔ་གང་ཡིན་ངོས་ཟིན་པར་བྱའོ།»
  If the element is known, take it; if not, find from family tradition,
  dreams and the like which la-sign, a yak or another, is the family's,
  and from the person's character which of the five great clans (རུས་ཆེན,
  rus chen) it is. The print reads «དུས་ཁམས», "the element of the time",
  both times; the five great clans point to «རུས་ཁམས», the clan's element
  (question 23).
- **Size** (p. 262, img. 272, read on the scan):
  «སྤྲོས་ཏེ་རྡེལ་གྲངས་མང་འདོད་ན། །ཉི་ཁྲི་ཉིས་སྟོང་སུམ་བརྒྱ་དང་། །བརྒྱད་ཅུ་ལ་སོགས་འབྱུང་ན་ཡང་། །ལྷག་པོ་སྤྲོས་པ་མ་ཡིན་པའི། །འབྲས་བུ་དངོས་ཀྱི་རྡེའུ་དང་། །བརྩི་དུས་མི་ལུས་ཁྱད་པར་གྱི། །ངོས་འཛིན་མཚན་མའི་རྡེའུ་རྣམས། །ཡོངས་བསྡོམས་རྡེའུ་སུམ་སྟོང་དང་། །བདུན་བརྒྱ་དོན་གསུམ་གཏན་ཕབ་པར།»
  Expanded, as others want, 22,380 pebbles and more; WB fixes the result
  pebbles and the identifying pebbles not to be left out at 3,773 in all.
  The page goes on to break the count down (the precious and luck
  pebbles, the 120 of the life force and the rest against the five
  relations, the four of rise and decline, …); that breakdown was seen on
  the scan but not transcribed. The root count of the older system is
  136 (p. 260, etext; p. 249 above).
- **Calculable** from the dates, with the clan element asked once.

#### Ch. 23, marriage and the road (vol. 1, pp. 351–379, img. 361–389) — RD15

- **What it reckons.** Three parts (p. 351, read on the scan): whether two
  match, the reckoning for setting out on the road (from p. 365), the
  remedies. The first in fifteen points (p. 351, read on the scan, the
  sixth's first letter by Yigdzin-1):
  «དང་པོ་ལ་ཡང་བཅོ་ལྔ་སྟེ། །བྱི་སྤངས་ལོ་འགྲོས་སྡེབ་ཚུལ(༡)དང་། །ལོ་ངན་མི་རུང་ངོས་འཛིན་ཚུལ(༢)། །རྐྱང་པ་དབང་ཐང་སྒོས་ཀྱི་འབྲས(༣)། །ཁུག་པ(༤)ཡང་ཁུག(༥)ཎི་ཤ(༦)དང་། །སྤུན་གྲངས(༧)ཕ(༨)དང་མ(༩)ཡི་རུས། །སྐྱེས་ཟླ(༡༠)སྤར་ཁ(༡༡)སྨེ་བའི(༡༢)ཀླུང་། །དར་གུད་ཁྲི་སྒོ(༡༣)གཟའ(༡༤)སྐར(༡༥)དང་། །བཅས་ཏེ་བཅོ་ལྔས་གཏན་ལ་དབབ།»
  (1) avoiding kin, and how the years go together; (2) the bad years that
  will not do; (3) the single power's own result; (4) the hidden and (5) the deeper hidden; (6) «ཎི་ཤ», whose rule RD15 found
  (the man of a male year and older, the woman of a female year and
  younger, p. 359), the word not identified (question 24); (7) the
  "siblings' number", pebbles counted by element, not a count of real
  siblings (RD15); (8) the father's and (9) the mother's clan;
  (10) the birth month, (11) the trigram, (12) the mewa's luck; (13) the
  throne doors of the rise and decline; (14) the planet and (15) the
  mansion. The second part's seventeen parts are in
  [wb-contents.md](wb-contents.md) (*Found in passing*).
- **Whose dates** (RD15, [marriage.md](marriage.md)). Both partners'
  birth years, with the sex of the year and their elements, mewa, trigram,
  progressed sign (log men), birth month, day animal, weekday and mansion;
  the bride's age; the bride's mother's marriage year; the year the
  groom's father set up house; the parents' birth mewa; the fathers' and
  mothers' clans and whether the two are kin (asked); the clan element;

  the birth day against the day of death, the birth hour against the hour
  of death, twenty each, a hundred pebbles. Sex and age (the year of age,
  ལོག་མེན, with its trigram and mewa, follows from birth year and year of
  death).
- **Of the death:** its year, month, day (lunar date, the day's animal,
  weekday, mansion) and hour. The notes (p. 500, img. 508, read on the
  scan):

  > གཤིན་རྩིས་དངོས་ལ་གཤིན་གྱི་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཀླུང་དང་ཁམས་ཏེ་སྐྱེས་སྨེ་ལྔས་རྩ་བ་བཅུ་དང་འཐབ་པ་སྟེ། ཤི་བའི་གནམ་ལོ། གཤིན་པོའི་ལོག་མིན། གཤིན་པོའི་བབ་སྤར་བབ་སྨེ། དར་གུད་ཤི་བའི་ཟླ་ཞག་དང་གཟའ་སྐར་ཤི་བའི་དུས་བཅས་ལ་འཐབ། སྤར་སྨེ་གཟའ་སྐར་རང་ཁམས་རྐྱང་པ། གཞན་སྲོག་སོགས་ནང་མཐུན་དུ་འཐབ། གཤིན་གྱི་སྨེ་བས་ནི་གནམ་ལོའི་སྨེ་བ་དང་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་ལོག་མིན་གྱིའང་བརྩི་བྱ་དང་སྨེ་ཕྲེང་མཐུན་པའི་སྨེ་བ་དེ་དང་འཐབ་དགོས། སྲོག་སོགས་ལྔ་པོའི་དར་གུད་གནམ་ལོ་ཁོ་ནར་གཏུགས།

  In the reckoning for the dead proper, the dead's life force, body,
  power, luck and element, with the birth mewa, five, fight the ten roots:
  the year of death, the dead's year of age, the dead's descended trigram
  and descended mewa (བབ་སྤར, བབ་སྨེ: those of the year of age), the rise and
  decline, the month and day of death, the planet and mansion, and the
  hour of death. Trigram, mewa, planet and mansion are fought by the
  element alone; life force and the rest fight their own kind. The dead's
  mewa fights the year's mewa and the mewa of the month, day, hour and year
  of age that agrees with the mewa sequence of the one reckoned. The rise
  and decline of the five are set against the current year only.
- **Of the living:** every member of the household, by the male line, by
  seniority and sex: birth year (with element, power, mewa), so that each
  gets the obstacle reckoning; the kin's relation to the dead (father,
  uncles, elders, maternal kin, sons-in-law and nephews, the host, elder
  men, women, children, servants: the rows of the leftover pebble and the
  death's eye); who has died in the household before, and when, how many,
  at what age, how and in which direction (the six lamps; the earlier dead's
  power in the fourth examination).
- **Facts to ask:** the manner of death and the illness (the seventh, the
  second), whether the dead was married, had children, how old (the old,
  the infant, the heirless), the posture in which the body lay at death
  (thirteen, the tenth), what goods are to go with it.
- **Observations:** the shape and colour of the ground where the corpse is
  sent, the fire at the burning, the water's course where it is cast (the
  tenth's omens, p. 93, etext); the reckoner's own la-stone (the twelfth).
- **The moment:** the days on which to carry out, burn or bury and hold
  the feast come out of the twelfth and fourteenth from the calendar: the
  planets and mansions, dates, the earth lords of year, month, day and hour,
  Rāhu's course.

So the reckoning is calculable from the dates of the dead (birth year,
and for the sounding birth month, day and hour), the date and hour of
death, and the household's birth years; everything else is asked or
observed. None of it is a throw of chance.

### What the reckoning gives: the examinations one by one (pp. 51–143)

Outlined from Yigdzin-1 and the etext, **not read on the scan**; page
ranges by the etext. Each examination gives readings and rites; no
examination gives a single number.

- **The living household** (pp. 51–55, img. 59–63). Readings for the
  paternal kin by its element and years when the dead is taken out: a
  water kin with many pig and rat years and one or two horse or snake
  years, demons have soaked the house, a separate grave helps; iron kin
  with bird and monkey years and a tiger or hare; and so on for each
  element (p. 51, read on the scan up to «བདུད་ཀྱི»). Then the mewa
  pebbles, white, black, red, yellow, green many, each with its cause and
  rite (p. 52); the twelve la pebbles in three verdicts, general, special,
  particular: the great and small cut-off, the mother, son, power, enemy
  and friend years empty or with black pebbles before them, each named a
  "place" (of the grave, of the bride, of the mother who bore, …) with
  what it says of the forefathers' graves, the brides and the line, and
  their remedies (pp. 52–54); "the mewa's copper vessel" (p. 54); the six
  lamps (སྒྲོན་ཆེན་དྲུག, sgron chen drug): deaths repeating by the count of
  years, by the number of persons, by age, by the month of illness, by the
  manner of death and by direction, each with its remedy, and the general
  rite "when the death's mouth is open" (p. 55).
- **1, the time of death** (pp. 55–57, img. 63–65). Seven tests: life
  force (black: the life's tree is broken, the death came at its time),
  body (black sweat: the body wanted the grave), power (the food count
  filled), luck (the branch broken), trigram (death on the trigram's
  enemy or own place, its demon door, *'phung chags* (འཕུང་ཆགས)), mewa (the four small
  obstacles, one's own two-black), the years' course (own obstacle, the
  seventh, the three in accord, the four executioners, the seven rough
  ones; the nine-eye and grave-eye). Many white: an untimely death,
  carried off by a demon; WB rejects newer ways of testing it (the
  father's and mother's trigrams at the birth) and the "remaining life"
  by the hour of death (death at the hare hour leaves three years, …) as
  some take them (p. 57).
- **2, cause and reason** (pp. 57–58, img. 65–66). The outer reason by
  the years (life force, body, power, luck; year, month, day, hour), the
  inner by the trigram, the mixed by the mewa: what was eaten, met,
  bought or broken, and in which direction; the demon that cut the la in
  the month that is the death year's seventh (died in a tiger year, cut in
  a monkey month, …); by the trigram of death (on *li*: a horse, turquoise,
  a hat bought; demon cut in the north-west, …); by the mewa.
- **3, which demon carried it off** (pp. 58–68, img. 66–76). By the dead's
  year animal (pp. 58–60), the trigram of death (pp. 60–62), the mewa
  (pp. 62–63), the day's animal (p. 63), the weekday and its rise and
  decline (pp. 63–65), the mansion of death (pp. 65–66) and the hour of
  death (pp. 66–68): which spirit took the dead, in which object the
  "eating demon" (ཟ་འདྲེ, za 'dre) hides in the house, to whom of which year
  the executioner (གཤེད, gshed) goes next, within how many days or months,
  and the rite and the skulls to lay on the corpse.
- **4, the funeral share** (pp. 68–70, img. 76–78). The funerals "by the
  pure way of Khyung" (ཁྱུང, khyung), by rank and household (a teacher's,
  a lord's, a minister's, a rich man's, a mother's, a childless person's,
  a young unmarried one's, a child's, a servant's), with the forbidden
  ones (monks dying in the assembly, lepers, …), who may perform them, and
  the thirteen to whom food may not be offered; white element pebble: the
  dead obtains a funeral; black: it does not; where the living are much
  at risk, a secret funeral (pp. 69–70).
- **5, rebirth** (pp. 70–72, img. 78–80). Gods, the lord of death's realm
  or the intermediate state; by the mewa colour of death, each with the
  image to make to turn a bad birth (2 black: hell if all four are black,
  else a bear's or monkey's body; 3 blue: an ox or a grouse; …); by the dead's life force and the rest against year, month, day
  and hour; by the day's animal of death (former and next life); by the
  hour; by the manner of dying and the posture of lying (lion-like,
  middling, low).
- **6, help by virtue** (p. 72, img. 80). Twelve ways of funeral, three
  each by Bon, Dharma, treasure and aspiration; by the animal of the year
  of death which texts to read and works to do (hare: the *Diamond
  Cutter* and a thousand *tsha tsha* (ཚ་ཚ, clay images); dragon: the *Sūtra of the Wise and
  the Foolish*; …).
- **7, kind of corpse** (pp. 72–74, img. 80–82). The thirteen kinds of the
  new tradition; the old (*bkra*) by four older examinations, men from
  sixty (once seventy), women from fifty; infants in six; violent deaths
  in thirteen; childbed in three; boys and girls by age; the unmarried over
  twenty; the heirless in three; hanging (ལྕེབས, lcebs), death in pregnancy (སྣེལ, snel), poison; kin-slaying;
  lepers; seven chronic, seven epidemic, seven sudden illnesses; seven
  disabilities.
- **8, the five graves** (pp. 74–75, img. 82–83). The scattered reckoning
  of the grave: mountain, fire, earth, *bsgrub*, water by element; which
  corpses may not be burnt, buried, given *bsgrub*, cast into water or sent
  to the mountain (by year, trigram, mewa, mansion, month, illness), and
  when each is good (burning, when the year and the dead are on the four
  executioners, horse and snake, fire trigram and mewa, …).
- **9, equipment and provisions** (pp. 75–86, img. 83–94). The provisions
  by the dead's trigram (*li*: a *se* tree (སེ་ཤིང), a bird, turquoise, a hat, …);
  the old's rites in general and their nine kinds by age, each with a
  name, garment, praise, place, food and funeral (old men pp. 77–78, old
  women from p. 78); children; the graves that continue a line (a king's,
  a minister's, a scholar's, a monk's, a tantrika's; p. 85).
- **10, repetition** (pp. 86–94, img. 94–102). The eight great
  strikes (the demon's belly strike, the heart strike, the nine-eye, the
  grave days, *kung kung*, iron-four fire-eight, planets and mansions,
  wrong deeds) with their small strikes (pp. 86–89); "the copper vessel's
  sounding" (p. 90); the corpse's posture (p. 93); the omens at the mountain,
  the fire and the water (pp. 93–94).
- **11, the death's eye** (p. 94, img. 102). Where each factor looks (life
  force at the host, body at the kin, power at the paternal aunt, luck at
  the maternal uncle, clan at the king's seat, …), whom it harms by the
  dead's age group, "joined hands" of dead and living sharing trigram and
  mewa, the bad kinds of death's eye.
- **12, sending the remains** (pp. 94–104, img. 102–112). After four days
  have passed; the sky's bird path, good planets and mansions; the earth's rat
  path, numbered: the year's, month's, day's and hour's earth lords
  (*bum stong*, *hal khyi*, *lto 'phye*, the sky dog and others); Rāhu;
  the places that are not allowed even if clean, in a numbered list
  (p. 104).
- **13, cutting off the run of deaths** (pp. 104–126, img. 112–134).
  Rites: effigies, skulls of the animals of the trigrams, *liṅga*s with
  mantras (the mantras printed), the trigram's remedies, the mewa's, the
  dates', planets' and mansions' (to p. 125).
- **14, the funeral assembly** (pp. 126–128, img. 134–136). When the
  living have obstacles and repetitions and the dead does not enjoy the
  food: the feast's day by planets, mansions and earth lords, its council,
  the forty-five pebbles of the palace, the workers and the reckoner's
  protections (the counts of p. 51; the layout explained in the notes,
  p. 521); it ends, by Yigdzin-1, by sending the reader to "the eighty of
  the assembly reckoning" («ཁྲོམ་རྩིས་བརྒྱད་ཅུ», not identified).
- **15, the grave** (pp. 128–142, img. 136–150). Its opening (p. 128,
  img. 136, read on the scan): «ས་དཔྱད་བཅོ་ལྔ་དུར་རྫོངས་དང་། །ས་འཚོལ་དུར་ལྔའི་སྐྲང་སྒོམ་དབྱེ། །གསོན་གཤིན་ལག་འབྲེལ་བཀྲ་གཡང་འགུགས། །རྣམ་དྲུག་ཉེར་མཁོ་འདིར་བཤད་བྱ།»
  The fifteenth: the grave provisions (དུར་རྫོངས, dur rdzongs), searching
  for the ground, the five graves' «སྐྲང་སྒོམ» (not identified), the
  joined hands of the living and the dead, and calling back the fortune of
  the old (བཀྲ་གཡང་འགུགས, bkra g.yang 'gugs): six needful things told here.
  By Yigdzin-1 it goes on with a rite in which the reckoner arises as
  Mañjuśrī (འཇམ་དཔལ, 'jam dpal) (p. 128), the search for the ground from Mañjuśrī's turtle of becoming
  (སྲིད་པའི་རུས་སྦལ, srid pa'i rus sbal) (p. 134),
  and the calling back of fortune, a long verse (pp. 141–142); then the
  colophon, which lists the same contents (p. 143, etext).
