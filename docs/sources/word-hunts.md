## Ch. 29's unexplained words, by the other texts: a word hunt (RD161)

RD161, 2026-11-05. Sixteen questions of WB ch. 29 and the notes (q275, q277, q278, q284, q287, q288, q290, q291, q293, q296, q298, q310, q312, q320, q334, q338), each word looked at on the print first, then sought outside WB. Yigdzin-1 and MITRA were not run: every find below was read by eye on the image named, or is marked etext.

**The new witness.** The Bhutan collection (*Collected Astrological and Prognosticatory Texts from Bhutan*, Thimphu 1981, I1KG11905, printed page = image − 10; etext VEIE0OPI1829B6AF_I1KG11905) holds, from about printed p. 479, a prose digest of WB's reckoning for the dead, set in dbu med with tables: the White Beryl's Meaning, the Reckoning for the Dead Easy to Carry (བཻ་དཀར་དགོངས་དོན་གཤིན་རྩིས་འཁྱེར་བདེ, bai dkar dgongs don gshin rtsis 'khyer bde; the title as the machine reads the collection's list of contents, etext p. 10, not read on the scan). It follows WB line by line, cuts the verse to lists and marks each item off with a shad, so that it shows where WB's run-together words divide. Below it is "the digest". Its school is not established; it is a witness to WB and answers nothing where it only repeats. The second new witness is the verse manual Wish-Granting Tree of the Pebble Laying (རྒྱ་རྩིས་དགེ་ལྡན་མཁས་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱུན་དུ་བསྟར་བའི་རྡེལ་འགྲེམ་དཔག་བསམ་ལྗོན་ཤིང, rgya rtsis dge ldan mkhas pa'i phyag rgyun du bstar ba'i rdel 'grem dpag bsam ljon shing; etext VEIE0OPIE709B49A_I4949, 250 pages), whose verses on the dead LT repeats word for word (the same lines at I4949 etext pp. 74–93 and LT etext pp. 57–72).

### q298: the spud pa are bellows (answered)

WB prints «སྤུད་པ» (spud pa) on pp. 64–65 and 111; the digest prints the word with ba:

> ཁྱིམ་གྱི་ནུབ་བྱང་མཚམས་ན་སྒྲོ་བའམ། ཁམ་ནག་གམ། སྦུད་པ། རྐྱལ་པ་གང་རུང་ནང་། ཞྭ། སྣམ། སྤུ་འམ་དྲུ་བུ་བཅུག་པར་ཟ་འདྲེ་གབ།

(Digest, printed pp. 494–495, img. 504–505, by eye. At the north-west of the house, in a basket, a black *kham* (ཁམ, kham, a vessel not identified), a bellows or a leather bag, with a hat, cloth, fur or thread put in, the eating demon hides.) This is WB's «ནག་གམ་སྤུད་པ་རྒད་པོ་འམ། །རྐྱལ་གསུམ་ནང་ན་མགོ་འམ་ཞྭ།» ("black, or an old *spud pa*, or inside the three bags, a head or a hat").

The bellows (སྦུད་པ, sbud pa) is a blower of goat or other hide for the fire (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs). It fits every place WB has the word: an old one, a worn one («སྤུད་གོག», spud gog, beside the worn bag «རྐྱལ་གོག»; a worn bellows, «སྦུད་གོག», is something a pot is set on in a Shangpa Kagyu story, BDRC snippet, VE3JT13356_006), and the rite of p. 111, whose nine and nine in the four directions have their mouths turned outward and are blown (read again on the reprint, I3CN5029 img. 117, l. 15):

> ཕྱོགས་བཞིར་སྤུད་པ་དགུ་དགུ་ནི། །ཁ་ཕྱིར་བསྟན་ལ་འབུད་པ་དང་།

(In the four directions nine and nine bellows, mouths turned outward, blown.) So «རྒྱ་ཡི་སྤུད་པ་དགུ་གདུག» (rgya yi spud pa dgu gdug) is the rite of the nine bellows of China. Other prints of the title: the 1996 etext reads the notes' «སྦུད་པ་དགུ» at p. 517 and again at the notes' index (etext p. 552; machine), and a manual in the Khams collection (MK's series, I2PD18783, search only, snippet) «ཁ་བཅད་རྒྱ་ཡི་སྦུད་པ་དགུ་སྡུག་གཏོ». On the Gser thang reprint (img. 523, p. 517, l. 9, 2.5×) the stack is ས over a lower letter not told as པ or བ.

The notes' second word stands: «སྤུད་པ་དགུ་རྒྱུག་དྲུག་ནི་གཤེད་བཞི་ཟིན་ཤི་དང་། དུར་ཞུགས་དང་…» (reprint, by eye for «རྒྱུག་དྲུག»), "the nine bellows' six *rgyug* are: the deaths on the four executioners, the entry to the grave …". The six are the occasions of the rite, as p. 111's list of occasions suggests; what «རྒྱུག» (rgyug) names is not stated. The 1996 etext's «རྒྱུ་བཞི» is the OCR's.

### q334: «ལྷང» is «ལྒང་ཕུག», a bladder (answered)

P. 106, l. 4 is «སྙིང་སྣ་ར་ཡི་ལྒང་ཕུག་བཅུག» in the 1996 etext (BDRC's text of the print, etext p. 114), and the stack on the reprint (img. 112, 2×) is blotted but agrees with a head letter over ga, with no ha below. The bladder (ལྒང་ཕུག, lgang phug; Tshig mdzod chen mo: the urine vessel, and a blown bladder; Valby, Ives Waldo) is common in ritual and medical writing (BDRC, 190 hits). So: "the heart-and-nose [offering] put into a goat's bladder". The Zhol print has a shorter line at this place, «སྙིང་སྣ་རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ནང་གཞུག» (img. 783, 5×), "the heart-and-nose put into the corpse's mouth", with no goat's bladder, and the digest agrees with it («སྙིང་སྣ་རོ་ཁར་འཇུག», printed p. 541, img. 551, etext).

### q338: «ཕ་གུ» stands for «ཕ་ཁུ» (answered)

WB's own ch. 24 has the formula (vol. 1, p. 388, img. 398, l. 8, by eye):

> སྤྱིར་ནི་དེ་དག་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ། །ཤིང་གིས་ཕ་ཁུ་མེས་གསུམ་བརྩི། །མེ་ཡིས་བུ་ཚ་མ་སྨད་བརྩི། །ས་ཡིས་མ་ཕྱི་ཡུལ་…

(In general, in all of these: by wood reckon the three, father, uncle and grandfather; by fire sons, grandchildren, mother and lower kin; by earth grandmother and home …) Zhe chen rgyal tshab repeats it (ZG's volume I4PD2444, etext p. 310, machine: «ཤིང་གིས་ཕ་ཁུ་མེས་གསུམ»), and BDRC has 24 hits of «ཕ་ཁུ་མེས་གསུམ» (pha khu mes gsum), LT and UT among them; «ཕ་གུ་མེས» has none. So p. 135's «ཕ་གུ་མེས་གསུམ», carved so on the blocks and the reprint, is a spelling of the three, father, paternal uncle and grandfather.

### q293: «རྨང་པོ» is the line's failure (answered)

To the claim's headword and idiom, two manuals of reckoning add sentences that fix the sense (etext, not read on the scan): LT p. 128 (etext p. 136) «རྨང་པོ་ལ་བུ་གསུམ་སྐྱེ», three sons are born to the *rmang po*, among the blessings; LT p. 163 (etext p. 171) «བུ་བརྒྱ་ཡོད་ཀྱང་རྨང་པོར་འགྲོ», even with a hundred sons he goes *rmang po*; UT p. 99 (etext p. 121) «གཤིན་རྩིས་དབུལ་དང་རྨང་པོ་འོང», in the reckoning for the dead come poverty and *rmang po*. So the childless man (རྨང་པོ, rmang po) of Dan Martin and the Brda dkrol stands, and p. 104's fourteenth place brings childlessness, beside the fifteenth's poverty.

### q291: the btsad po is a lord, among ranks (answered)

The digest lists him among the corpses not to be hidden (printed p. 556, img. 566, by eye):

> སྦ་རུ་མི་རུང་རོ་རིགས་ནི། །བཙད་པོ་དགེ་བཤེས་སྔགས་པའི་རོ།

(The kinds of corpse that may not be hidden: the corpses of the lord, the spiritual friend and the mantra-holder.) The verse manual has «བརྒྱད་དཀར་ཡུགས་ས་དགུ་དམར་སྟེང་། །བཙད་པོ་རྒན་ཁོག་རོ་མི་གཏོང་།» (I4949 etext p. 93, LT etext p. 72, machine): on the eight-white a mourning, on the nine-red the lord (བཙད་པོ, btsad po) and the worn old are not sent. With the old-word gloss of the dictionaries (claim *btsad-po-old-word-for-lord*), the lord is a rank of the living world, not one spirit.

### q277: «འཆོལ་བ», irregular (answered)

The Zhol blocks carve the same letter in both places, the count line (img. 723, last line, 8×) and the heading (img. 725, l. 3, 9×): two-lobed with the vowel o and no tick at the top right, read «འཆོལ་བ་དྲུག». The search-only copy of WB in the medical collection (MW2PD17386; etext I1KG81102) has «འདུར་ཐང་འཆོལ་བ་དྲུག» too, and Gyurme Dorje, in *Tibetan Elemental Divination Paintings* (2001, search only, its English seen in BDRC's snippet), renders «'dur-thang 'chol-ba drug» as the six irregular modes of funeral status. So the disordered shares (འཆོལ་བ, 'chol ba) it is; the 1996 line's «འཚོལ་བ» on p. 68 is that print's slip.

### q284 and q296: the mansions

The digest heads the twelfth entry of the mansions with «དབོ» (dbo) in its boxed heading and again in the remedy (printed p. 503, img. 513, by eye):

> དབོ། གཟའ་བདུད་ཀྱིས་ཁྱེར། ཕ་མེས་རུས་པའི་དུར་རྙིང་གིས་གནོད། … དབོ་ལ་འབྲུ་སྣའི་ཟན་གླུད་ནི། །ཁྲག་གིས་དམར་པོ་བྱས་ནས་ཀྱང་།

(*Dbo*: the planet-demon carries off; the old grave of the fathers' clan harms. … For *dbo*, a dough ransom of grains, reddened with blood.) This is WB's «སྦོ་ལ་ཤི་ན་གཟའ་བདུད་ཁྱེར། །ཕ་མེས་རུས་པའི་དུར་རྙིང་…»: q284 is answered, the stack is U. Phalgunī (དབོ, dbo), as *sbo*. WB2's etext has this page too garbled to find the line, and its 1210-px scan was not read.

The digest repeats p. 122 by year (printed p. 514, img. 524, by eye): «འབྲུག སྦོ་ལ་གསོན་པོར་ཆགས་ཆེ་མང་།» (dragon: on *sbo*) and «སྦྲུལ སྦོ་སྨད་ལ་གསོན་པོར་ཆགས་ཆེ།» (snake: on *sbo smad*), then «རྟ མཆུ་འམ་གྲེར།» (horse: on *mchu* or *gre*) and «ལུག བྱི་བཞིན་ལ» (sheep: on *byi bzhin*). So the 1996 print's «སྦ་སྨད» is «སྦོ་སྨད» (sbo smad), as the blocks carve it. Lag sor (ལག་སོར) has a second witness as Anurādhā: «བཅུ་དྲུག་ལྷ་མཚམས་ལག་སོར» in the Bhutan collection's list of mansions with their second names (etext p. 116, machine). Which mansion *sbo smad* is stays open: that list has no such second name («བཅུ་གཅིག་དབོ་དང་ཁྲ»), and the only other «སྦོ་སྟོད» and «སྦོ་སྨད» found are the parts of the sky dog (UT etext p. 257, Bhutan collection etext pp. 228, 242: «མཇིང་པ་སྦོ་སྨད», neck, lower belly), which is a different use.

### q312: the provisions, divided by the digest (mostly answered)

The digest gives the provisions of each trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) as a list (by eye), for khon (ཁོན), dwa (དྭ), zin (ཟིན) and zon (ཟོན):

| Trigram | Digest | Page, image |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *khon* | «ལྕང་མ། བྱ་ཐི་བ་དང་། ཟར་མ། ཕུ་ཐུང་། དྲོད་སྨན། སོག་པ། སྲང་བཅས་དུར་སར་རྫོངས།» | 533, 543 |

| *zin* | «སྟག་ཤིང་དང་། བྱ་ནེ་ཙོ། སོ་བ། སོར་ལེ། སྟག་ལྤགས། བོང་བུའི་སྐྱུར་རྩིབ། སྐ་རགས་བཅས་དུར་སར་རྫོངས།» | 541–542, 551–552 |

(Khon: willow, the pigeon, sesame, a sleeved garment, warm medicine, *sog pa*, *srang*. Dwa: the *tshar* tree, the *'jol mo* bird's feather, *ju rtse*, goat and donkey, clothes and armour, the left sour rib. Zin: the birch, the parrot, coarse barley, *sor le*, tiger skin, the donkey's sour rib, a belt. Zon: the *gsom* tree, the hoopoe, *phug*, silk, the flesh of a male yak-cross (མཛོ་གླང, mdzo glang): each sent to the grave.) The other trigrams, li (ལི), khen (ཁེན), kham (ཁམ) and gin (གིན) (etext, machine): *li* «སེ་ཤིང་དང་སྐྱ་ཀའི་སྒྲོ། འབྲས་གཡུ། ཞྭ། ཕྱིང་པ། མགོ་ཤ། མར་ཁུ», *khen* «སྦྱར་པ་དང་། བྱ་ཁྲུང་ཁྲུང་། བྲ་བོ། ཁྱི་ཤ», *kham* «སྤེན་ཤིང་དང་། བྱ་ཁ་ཏ། སྲན་མ། བྲ་བོ», *gin* «ཤུག་པ་དང་། བྱ་འུག་པ། ཟ་འོག །གྲོ». Each list runs tree, bird, grain, then clothes and flesh.

From this:

- «སོག་པ་སྲང» is two items, *sog pa* and *srang* (སྲང, an ounce of money, as the recitation's «ཕུག་སྲང་སོག་པ» as the old woman's wealth would have it); «ཕུག་དར» is two items, *phug* and silk; «སོ་བ་སོར་ལ» is the coarse barley and *sor le*; «སྟག་ལྤགས་བོང། །སྐྱུར་རྩིབ» is tiger skin and the donkey's rib; WB's «དང་ཁྲག» at *dwa* is «ཁྲབ», armour, in the digest.
- «ཇུ་ཚེར» is «ཇུ་ཚེ» or «ཇུ་རྩེ» and the next word «ར་བོང» (the 1996 etext has «ཇུ་ཚེ་ར་བོང་གོས»): the grain ju rtse (ཇུ་རྩེ, ju rtse). The word stands in the grain's place, and the sources call it a grain: Sde srid's own Blue Beryl, «ཇུ་རྩེ་ལ་སྤྱི་དང་འདིར་མིང་གཅིག དེའང་འཁྲུངས་དཔེ་ཁུངས་མ་མ་བྱུང་ཡང་རྒྱ་ལྗང་ནས་འོང་བའི་འབྲུ་དེས་རྨ་ལ་ཕན» (etext VEIE0OPICF5AF372_I1PD411 p. 450, machine: one name, generally and here; no sound description of it, but the grain that comes from China and Jang helps wounds); a medical note, «རྒྱུ་འཇང་སོགས་ནས་འོང་བའི་འབྲུ་ཕྲ་མོ», a small grain from Jang and elsewhere (BDRC snippet); Dge rtse Paṇḍita's list «ནས། འབྲས། གྲོ། སྲན་མ། ཇུ་རྩེ། ཁྲེ། ཏིལ» (barley, rice, wheat, pulse, *ju rtse*, millet, sesame; I8LS20838 etext p. 228, machine; the image of that number holds another page, so not read on the scan); and WB's own «ཟར་མ … ཇུ་རྩེ་ཉུང་མ་ལ་སོགས་པ» among the grains of China (the Zhol impression's etext, I4CZ74285 p. 261, machine). ### q287: the third examination's objects

- **«སླ་ང་རྩེས་བྱིན་བརྒྱ་བཞི» (p. 59): answered.** The digest's cell for the ox and bird (printed p. 488, img. 498, by eye): «སླ་ངར་གཤེད་གབ། བན་བོན་ལྔས་གླང་ངམ། ལུག་གསོབ་སྟེང་དུ་གཤེད་གསོད། ཟླ་བཞིའི་མཚམས་ལྡོག་ངོ་། སླ་ང་རྩིས་མཁན་ལ་སྦྱིན། བརྒྱ་བཞི་བྱ།» (The executioner hides in a pan. Five monks and bon priests kill the executioner on a stuffed ox or sheep. At four months it returns. Give the pan to the reckoner. Do the four hundred.) So the pan (སླ་ང, sla nga; roasting pan, all the dictionaries) is given to the reckoner, «རྩེས» of the print (read so on the reprint, img. 065) being «རྩིས», and the four hundred (བརྒྱ་བཞི, brgya bzhi) is the rite of that name. The verse manual agrees: «སླ་ངར་གཤེད་གབ་བརྒྱ་བཞི་བྱ» (LT p. 49, etext p. 57; I4949 etext p. 74, machine).
- **«ཡུ་མོའི་གསོབ»: answered.** The same page: «བན་བོན་གསུམ་གྱིས་ར་དང་། ཡུ་མོའི་གསོབ་དང་ཐོད་པའི་སྟེང་དུ་གཤེད་གསོད།»: the stuffed dummy (གསོབ, gsob) of a hornless beast, with a goat and a skull, on which the executioner is killed.
- **«སེལ་གོག», «ཏིང་མཉྫི»: narrowed.** The digest folds the whole line into «མཆོད་རྫས་ཡོད་སར་གབ་འདུག» (hides where there are offering things): they are offering goods. *Ting* (ཏིང) is the small offering bowl or cymbal of the dictionaries. The reprint's last stack is ma over ñ or r with ja and i (img. 065, 2×; Yigdzin-1 «མརྫི», the Gser thang etext «མནྟི»); «སེལ་གོག» has no other use in BDRC.
- **«ཟོ་བ་ཞབས་བརྡོལ» (p. 61): answered in the words.** The pail (ཟོ་བ, zo ba; Tshig mdzod chen mo, Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs) with its bottom holed (ཞབས་བརྡོལ, zhabs brdol). «ཟོ་བ་ཞབས་མེད», the bottomless pail, is a stock figure of the wind yogas (BDRC, 64 hits of «ཟོ་བ་ཞབས»). What it does in the line is not stated.
- **«སིག་ལྐོགས» (p. 67): narrowed.** The digest has «ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་སུ་སིག་ལྟོག་དང་། རྐྱལ་གོག་ནང་དུ་གབ» (printed p. 527, img. 537, by eye), the verse manual «ཤར་ཕྱོགས་རྐྱལ་སིག་ཟ་འདྲེ་གབ» (LT p. 56, etext p. 64; I4949 etext p. 83, machine): a thing beside the worn bag, or a kind of bag, «སིག» being the constant syllable. ### q278: the funeral share and the corpse kinds

- **«བསེ་རགས་བྱུར་ཤིད»: answered.** Not a hide belt. The ghost of poverty (བསེ་རག, bse rag; Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Tshig gter rgya mtsho: a spirit that eats the essence of food and wealth; spelled «བསེ་རགས» in BDRC's 173 hits, titles of rites among them), with the funeral rite (ཤིད, shid; Tshig mdzod chen mo, Dan Martin). ### q320: rows, and the box reads «གཉིས» (answered)

The Chengdu typeset notes print the tables whole (I1PD34647 img. 570, its p. 524, at full size). Over the fourth and fifth columns of the fold the box reads:

> ཕྲེང་བ་འདི་གཉིས་དོར

(Cast off these two rows.) The columns are headed «ལྷ་ས་དང་པོ», «གཉིས་པ», «གསུམ་པ», the boxed ༤ and ༥, then «ལྷ་ས་ཐ་མ» (the god's place first, second, third, the two, the god's place last), the rows below them the people's, house's and enemy's places, and the footer reads «ཁུག་པ་ཡང་ཁུག་གི་རྡེལ་རོ་འགྲིམས་ལུགས་ད་ལྟ་ཡང་ཁུག་རྡེལ་རོ་མེད» (how the leftovers of the fold and refold (རྡེལ་རོ, rdel ro) go; here no refold leftover). ### q275: the manuals have no «སེར་གསུམ» (answered as far as print goes)

LT p. 57 (img. 65, by eye) and the verse manual (I4949 etext p. 85, machine) repeat the verse without the words:

> སྨེ་བ་དཀར་གསུམ་ལྷ་རུ་སྐྱེ། །ནག་གཉིས་ལྗང་ཁུ་ངན་སོང་སྐྱེ། །དམར་གཉིས་བར་དོ་དག་ལ་འཁྱམ། །ལྔ་སེར་ལ་ཤི་མི་རུ་སྐྱེ།
