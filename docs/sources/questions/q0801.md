# Is «བཻ་རང་གི་དུས་ཚོད» in KCM p. 682 a mistake for a named watch or day, or the text's own phrase?

Cursive, not checked on the scan; rendered 'the proper time'.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The phrase is a misreading of the cursive and the text's own words are «ཐོ་རངས་ཀྱི་དུས» (tho rangs kyi dus), "the time of dawn". The same closing paragraph stands on two other prints: the Rin chen gter mdzod etext (VE1KG14_037) reads «ཐོ་རེངས་ཀྱི་དུས་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་ལུང་སྟོན་པའི་ཚིག་གམ། བརྡ་རྟགས་ཀྱི་རིམ་པ་གང་རུང་འབྱུང་བས་…» and Kun dga' bstan pa'i rgyal mtshan's bka' 'bum vol. 20 (I1KG81132, img. 482, printed p. 474, read by eye at 3x) reads «ཐོ་རེང་གི་དུས་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་ལུང་སྟོན་པའི་ཚིག་གམ…» (kcm:p474). Both agree with the cursive's one joined form before «རང་གི་» on the Tashijong scan (I2638 img. 692), which is ཐོ joined into the head of ཐོ་རངས་. So བཻ (Yigdzin-1) and ཕམ (MITRA) are machine slips, not a named watch or day; the phrase says that at dawn the words of prophecy or a sequence of signs appear. Reserve: the Tashijong syllable itself was not read by eye (the hand's ligature); the answer rests on the two other prints and the cursive's shape. «ཚོད» after «དུས» is only in the Tashijong print, where «དུས་ཚོད» is the same "time".
