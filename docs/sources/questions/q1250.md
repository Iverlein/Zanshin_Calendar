# KP boxes 52–55: where in WB or Mipham do the earth-lord runs at the foot of their bad halves come from?

WB's verses for works 49–55 (white-beryl-ch34.md, machine grade) name no earth lord; KP's runs are its own addition or come from another passage (WB p. 427b is the nearest). KP box 56 matches WB work 53, so the box-to-work offset needs checking.

## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-e)

New route: the same kind of run stands in other almanac handbooks, so KP's foot clauses are probably a common formula for the bad times rather than its own addition. BDRC phrase «ཉི་ནག་ཀླུ་བཟློག» finds one hit, Gnyer stag snang rgyal's Rtsis rig shes bya kun gsal (MW1KG4867, search-only snippet): «དུས་སྦྱོར་ནི། སྡིག་ལུག་སྲང་འཕུང་ཐུན་མཚམས། སྡེ་བརྒྱད་ཐེབས་ཀླུ… བྱེད་ཟིན་འཕུང་དབུལ་ཉི་ནག་ཀླུ་བཟློག་པ། སྦྱིན་སྲེག་བྱེད་པ་ལ་ངན་པ།», whose tail «zin phung, dbul, nyi nag, klu bzlog pa» is the tail of KP's box 55 (kp:i55s), after the sdig, lug (sheep), srang (balance), 'phung and the junctures that KP's box 35 and box 55 also have. The nag rtsis text Dkar rtsis dang nag rtsis kyi gzhung (snippet, Wylie as BDRC gives it: «… phung zer ki kang ka khyung dang / dbul nyi bar khyi gnam khyi sbyor / klu gza' bzlog bcas nyi ma dang / nyi nag che chung ro dag rgyu / zin phung hal gnam zas 'tshol dus») lists the same names as courses. So the names are the earth lords of the nag rtsis; neither WB's verses for works 49-55 nor Mipham give the run, which stays open as to the immediate source (q1251 settled the last item as the nāgas' turning). Box-to-work offset not rechecked.
