# The Kun 'dus chen mo, from the Mchog dga', gives each of the eight half-watches four chu tshod; eight times four is thirty-two and the day-time at an equinox has thirty: is the four a round figure, or are the half-watches of the Mchog dga' reckoned otherwise?

RD326.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered by RD338 (2026-11-10), on the etext: the Mchog dga' is Phug pa Lhun grub rgya mtsho's commentary on the Dbyangs 'char, printed whole in the Kun 'dus chen mo vol. 3 (etext I4717 pp. 21–505, printed page = etext page − 20); its ch. 9 on the half-watch Rāhus (etext pp. 449–454) has eight half-watches by day run right-wise and by night left-wise, four chu tshod each, and says 'four and four' is for an equal day and night, the real half-watch being the day's or the night's measure divided by eight. The four are dbyu gu, the Kālacakra's 56¼ chu srang taken as one chu tshod: 4 × 56.25 = 225 chu srang = 3¾ ordinary chu tshod, so the day's eight half-watches come to 30 ordinary chu tshod and the day-and-night holds 64 dbyu gu (3600 chu srang = 60 chu tshod). The 'thirty-two against thirty' is thirty-two short chu tshod, not ordinary ones; the sixty-four is written out in the Karma pa gsum pa's collected works vol. 13 p. 207, not in the Mchog dga' etext. No nāga is named in these pages. Claim mchog-dga-half-watch-four-short-chu-tshod-56-and-a-quarter-chu-srang.
