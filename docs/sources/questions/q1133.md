# Why is the Pleiades month called the Dream month

The dictionaries gloss rmi lam gyi zla ba as smin drug zla ba (tenth Hor month; the Dus tshigs list has Smin drug twelfth); no source says why it is called dream.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

Searched again on 2026-11-13 without result: the lexica (svo:p395d) and the full-text search give the name as a month name only, never with a reason. Dead end after two approaches.

## Narrowed again 2026-11-14 (inv-x)

Still no reason. BDRC's exact phrases «རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཟླ་བ» (17 hits) and «རྨི་ལམ་ཟླ་བ» (18): nearly all are the simile «the moon in a dream» (Sa skya bka' 'bum, the Tshad ma rigs gter commentaries, Pema Karpo), not the month. The month stands only in the Dus tshigs lists (Mkhan zur Ngag dbang nyi ma's collected works: «རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཟླ་བ དང་བཅུ་གཉིས», after «འཚོ་བའི་ཟླ་བ»; the Dus tshigs commentary *Lde mig*) and in the *Bod rgya skar rtsis rig pa'i tshig mdzod* (MW1PD137843, search-only), whose entry reads «ming gi rnam grangs … རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཟླ་བ སྨིན་དྲུག་ཟླ་བ་ལ … རྡོ་རྗེ་གདན་བཞིའི་རྒྱུད་སོགས་ནས་རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཟླ་བ་ཞེས་» (a synonym; it names the Catuṣpīṭha as the source of the name, snippet cut there). No reason is in the snippets. Steinert's dictionaries have only «rmi lam gyi zla ba» = the tenth Hor month (Ives Waldo; Tshig mdzod chen mo with «mngon»). The twelve names of the tantric list are the Tantra's own, so the question goes to the Catuṣpīṭha and its commentaries: not read.

## Narrowed again 2026-11-14 (inv-bb)

The twelve names are code names (brda) of a cycle laid on months, lunar days and moments in the fourth Zhwa dmar's treatise on omens (dds.26:p119-120), which quotes the Vajraḍākatantra; no reason for «dream» is given, and the Catuṣpīṭha itself was still not opened (see q1035).

## Narrowed again 2026-11-14 (inv-last2)

The Catuṣpīṭha's Tibetan is not reachable as text: BDRC's Derge Kanjur record has it as Toh 428 (MW4CZ5369_0428, vol. 80 pp. 364-465, the explanatory tantra Toh 429 after it), but exact phrases from the month list («འཕེལ་བ་དང་ནི་གཏི་མུག་དང», «གཏི་མུག་བཟང་པོ་གླང», «བཟང་པོ་གླང་དང་རུལ་སྦལ», title lines of Toh 428-429) give no Kanjur hit, and the Tengyur hit for «རྡོ་རྗེ་གདན་བཞི་པའི་རྒྱུད» (VE23703_1427) was not opened. What the search did find is a second witness to the list, in a commentary on the *Mdo rgyud rin po che'i mdzod* in a BDRC etext titled «Mdo rgyud mdzod» (I4959, p. 595; the other hit, I1KG2752, is the «Tshangs chen bskal pa'i mdzes rgyan» commentary, search-only; BDRC search hit on «གཏི་མུག་བཟང་པོ་ཁྱུ་མཆོག» and «རྨི་ལམ་ས་ཡི་དབང་ཕྱུག»; the same lines are in I1KG2752). It lists the twelve house-months (khyim zla) with their Indian names, then adds: «also the names of the twelve house-months, spoken in the Catuṣpīṭha (gdan bzhi), the Vajra Ḍākinī, beginning from Rgyal zla: hita, mohi, bhadra, ṣabha, kūrma, makara … (garbled in the etext) … , the twelve lords; rendered they are Increase, Delusion, the Good, the Bull, the Tortoise, the Crocodile, Yugs sa mo, Sme ba, 'Bug pa, 'Phyo ba, the Dream, the Lord of the Earth» (འཕེལ་བ་དང་། གཏི་མུག་བཟང་པོ་ཁྱུ་མཆོག་གོ། རུས་སྦལ་ཆུ་སྲིན་ཡུགས་ས་མོ། སྨེ་བ་འབུག་པ་འཕྱོ་བ་དང་། རྨི་ལམ་ས་ཡི་དབང་ཕྱུག་གོ).

Two facts follow. The cycle starts at Rgyal zla (Pauṣa, «rgyal zla chu srin khyim» in the list given just before it, which also has Kārttika as «smin drug sdig pa'i khyim»), so the eleventh name, the Dream, falls on Kārttika, counting eleven months on from Pauṣa, the Pleiades month: the identification of the dictionaries rests on a published text, not on the dictionaries alone. And the names are said to be translations of Indian words (hita, mohi, bhadra, ṛṣabha, kūrma, makara …), so «Dream» in the Tibetan is a rendering of the Sanskrit name at that place, and the reason for it, if the Sanskrit gives one, is in the Sanskrit; the commentary gives none, and its Sanskrit for the last places is too corrupt to read (the etext has «kamaṇa» at the end). Still open: the Sanskrit name of the eleventh month in the Catuṣpīṭha (a reading of Toh 428 on the scan, vol. 80, or the Sanskrit edition) and whether it means sleep or dream.
