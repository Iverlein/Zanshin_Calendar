# Is the tenth of the waning half kept for the same reason as the waxing tenth in Nyingma texts, or only as the dākinī day? The promise texts read do not name it.

Raised by the practice-days investigation (pd-a, 2026-11-16); see the cited passages.

## Answered 2026-11-21

Answered for what the Nyingma texts read say: the waxing tenth is kept for the Guru's coming, the waning tenth is named as the dākinīs' gathering day, and no text read gives the waning tenth the Guru's reason.

The waxing tenth: the Rin chen gter mdzod's introduction to the tenth-day Guru-practice empowerment (BDRC MW1KG14, Gsang thig snying po, pp. 8-9, etext) says that all the main deeds of the great teacher were shown and blessed on the waxing tenth (ཆེན་པོའི་མཛད་པའི་གཙོ་བོ་མཐའ་དག་ཡར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུ་ལ་བསྟན་ཅིང་བྱིན་གྱིས་བརླབས་པ་ཡིན་པས), so that time is remembered with offering and prayer, and quotes for it the Northern Treasure («ཡར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུ་ནམ་ཤར་ཚེ༔ ཨོ་རྒྱན་པདྨའི་སྤྲུལ་པ་འབྱུང༔») and Ratna's prophecy (the oath; see q1253). The waning tenth: BDRC etexts name it «མར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུ་མཁའ་འགྲོ་འདུ་བའི་དུས» (mar ngo'i tshes bcu mkha' 'gro 'du ba'i dus), the time of the dākinīs' gathering: the Klong chen snying thig retreat rule («ཤིང་མོ་ཡོས་ཀྱི་ལོའི་ཟླ་ཚེས་བཟང་པོ་མར་ངོའི་ཚེས་བཅུ་མཁའ་འགྲོ་འདུ་བའི་དུས་ཆེན་ནས་མགོ་བརྩམས་ཏེ», where a Nyingma retreat community is begun on that great time), the writings of Khri rgan 'Jam dbyangs thub bstan rgya mtsho and of Sle lung Bzhad pa'i rdo rje (the dākinī gathering's night), and the Bod ljongs nang bstan 2001 listing of the waxing and waning tenths of the winter and other months as «mkha' 'gro 'du ba'i dus» (search snippets). The Cakrasaṃvara root tantra itself gives the torma for the waning tenth (claim cakrasamvara-root-tantra-ch27-torma-on-the-waning-tenth), without the Guru. The promise texts of the pool name «the tenth of each month» without a half (claim tenth-day-waning-half-not-named); Zhe chen rgyal tshab keeps both and calls the waxing one the Guru's (claim tenth-day-waxing-half-is-the-gurus-both-halves-kept). So the two tenths are kept in the same feast, for different reasons: the Guru on the waxing, the dākinīs on the waning. State: etexts and snippets, no scan.
