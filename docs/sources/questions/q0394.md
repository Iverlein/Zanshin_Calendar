# Whether «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམ་ན» counts the sick person's own triad

Does the verse «མཐུན་གསུམ་རང་དང་ལྔ་འཛོམ་ན» of WB vol. 1, p. 445 count the three year animals of the sick person's own triad («མཐུན་གསུམ» as in the Dpal bsdus, p. 43), oneself and the five people, or the three relations named before it, oneself and the five?

RD166 (2026-11-06), etext only: LT p. 49 lists «མཐུན་གསུམ» among the five people needed and elsewhere uses it for the year triad; this leans to the triad of the sick person's own year.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-last).** The verse is WB's own (BDRC phrase search, the Serthang etext MW3CN5026, where it closes the five people needed) and is quoted in two medical works with a gloss: Takla Norbu (*Gzhan phan zla shel nor bu'i sgron me*, MW20065, Lhasa 1996; and his *Sman yig*, MW1KG8532): «ཞེས་ནད་པའི་སྲོག་གི་མ་བུ་དགྲ་གྲོགས་ལ་བྱའོ» ("this is applied to the sick one's life's mother, son, enemy and friend"), and Tshul khrims rgyal mtshan (MW1KG10375, search-only): «ཞེས་པ་ལྟར་ནད་པའི་སྲོག་ཁམས་ལས་རྩིས་ཏེ» ("so, it is reckoned from the sick one's life element"). Both tie the verse to the life reckoning's five (self, mother, son, friend, enemy), not to the year triad; this leans to the second reading, the relations named before it. The two glosses are later readers of WB's verse, and neither gloss names the «མཐུན་གསུམ» itself; not settled.
