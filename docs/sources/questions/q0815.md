# Is the SVI sign «བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ» (a bird sent as messenger, Amoghasiddhi's family) a bird, and is it a fortunate sign?

Left unsettled.

## Answered 2026-11-14

A bird, and a fortunate sign. Every witness of the line opens it with བྱ (bird): Derge and Co ne «བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ», Peking «བྱ་ཤིང་ཏེའུ» (read on the scan), Narthang «བྱ་ཤ?་ཏེ…» (Dpe bsdur ma note: «ཤང་ཏེའུ»), and the verb is «ཕོ་ཉར་གཏོང» (sends a messenger): the bird is the messenger. It stands in the run of signs of Amoghasiddhi's family, between "set to the work of peace" and "a forest burnt by fire", in the verses that list the images of each Buddha family before the text turns to the bad signs and the remedy, so it is counted with the fortunate images (svi-images-by-family); the text gives no verdict of its own on this image beyond its place in the family's list.

What stays open is the bird's name: the spellings agree best on the shang shang te'u (jīvajīvaka) of the lists of singing birds (svi-amoghasiddhi-bird-is-shang-shang-te-u, an inference; no commentary on the Svapnekṣā says it; q913, q835). The sign is therefore offered as "a bird sent as messenger", a sign of the family, with the bird's species not named.
