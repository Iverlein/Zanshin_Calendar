# The notes' «བཅོ་ལྔའོ» against fourteen

Does the notes' «བཅོ་ལྔའོ» (vol. 2, p. 517, l. 8), after 'six each counted', count the smith's paper-roll and its remedy as two items, or the found trigram's verdict with the six, where WB (p. 51, l. 1) and the head of the same sentence say fourteen? See verdicts.md, RD125.

RD177 (2026-11-06): not decided; WB p. 51 says «སྤར་བཅོས་ལ་བཅུ་བཞི» (14); the notes' six items sum to 14 with the eight; no commentary counts them.

inv-xx (2026-11-14): the count is fourteen (the 202 of the thirteen classes closes with 14 and the six of the sixth class, or 15 and 5, and both WB and the notes give six); the fifteenth is not in that sum. Most likely the trigram class's own verdict pebble, 8 + 6 + 1, since the notes' total gives each of the thirteen classes a verdict outside the 202 (202 + 11 + 13 + 1 = 227); an inference, not a sentence. Still open: that, or the paper-roll and its remedy as two. See open-questions.md 214.
