# Is the sign of Amoghasiddhi's family «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ» (shang shang te'u, the jīvajīvaka bird of the lists), and what does a bird of that name sent as a messenger (ཕོ་ཉར་གཏོང) foretell in the commentaries of the tradition?

RD309.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Most likely yes, as an inference from the spellings and the lexica; no commentary on the Svapnekṣā says it. Steinert's dictionaries (sparse clone, 2026-11-14) give «shang shang te'u» the Sanskrit jīvaṃjīvaka in the Mahāvyutpatti (Mvy 279 in the 1989 scan), the Yogācārabhūmi glossary, Negi's dictionary and the 84000 glossary (a partridge or pheasant; later a mythical bird with a human upper body, or two-headed), and the Tshig mdzod chen mo and Ives Waldo have «bya shang shang» and «bya shang shang te'u» as headwords with the «བྱ» in front: so the Narthang's «བྱ་ཤང་ཏེའུ» and the Peking's «བྱ་ཤིང་ཏེའུ» are each the bird's name with one «ཤང» written once or miswritten, and BDRC full-text search finds «བྱ་ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ» in 16 etexts, a real compound. The Derge's «བྱ་ཤར་ཏེ་རུ» has the same count of four syllables as «བྱ་ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ», with «ཤར» for «ཤང» and «ཏེ་རུ» for «ཏེའུ». Against another bird: no headword «shar te ru», «shar te'u», «shing te'u» or «bya shar» in the 64 dictionaries; the myna-like sārikā is «ti skag» (Negi, after Śabdakośa), not a ཤ-word.
What stays open: the second half of the question. Nothing found says what a «shang shang te'u» that sends a messenger foretells: BDRC phrase search «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ་ཕོ་ཉར», «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ་ཕོ་ཉར་གཏོང», «ཤང་ཤང་ཏེའུ་ཡིས་ཕོ་ཉར» 0 hits, «ཤར་ཏེ་རུ» 3 (the Derge Tengyur etext, the Dpe bsdur ma etext, one medical collection); the lexica give the bird a sweet voice, so a fair sign is likely but no text says so. The Sanskrit of the Svapnekṣā is not published as far as found.
