# Does sdom pa 'bog pa, said of the 11th in Dpal sprul's verse on the days' results (dpk.2:p138-140), mean giving, taking or laying down vows

## Answered 2026-11-21

It means giving: to confer the vows. The Great Tibetan-Chinese Dictionary (Bod rgya tshig mdzod chen mo), in the entry 'bog pa, takes the transitive verb first (the pair 'bogs / phog, "to give", sbyin pa) and gives as its examples «sdom pa phog pa», to give the vows, and «gdams ngag 'bog pa», to give instruction; the intransitive 'bog pa / bog pa (to faint, fall, rot) is the second sense, and does not fit an object like vows. The Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho has the entry word for word, and Ives Waldo glosses 'bog pa as "release; give" and 'bog chog as "conferring ritual" (tmz:box10). So «བཅུ་གཅིག་སྡོམ་པ་འབོག་པ་བཟང» (bcu gcig sdom pa 'bog pa bzang, dpk.2:p138-140) reads "on the eleventh it is good to give the vows", the day for the lama or the vow-holder to confer the vows, not for taking them or laying them down. The verse itself does not say who gives to whom; that is the dictionary's sense of the verb, not a gloss of Dpal sprul on this line. Lexicon only (state etext, the dictionaries as keyed in Steinert's public set), no commentary on the verse found.
