# The father wheel and mother wheel of a wheel with rims

What are the father wheel and mother wheel of a «wheel with rims» (འཁོར་ལོ་མུ་ཁྱུད་མ): two discs facing each other, as «letters face down» and «letters face up» suggest, or something else?

RD171 (2026-11-06), narrowed: མུ་ཁྱུད is a rim of the wheel, so a 'wheel with rims' has concentric rims. The father and mother wheels are a pair of separate diagrams, accomplished separately and 'joined face to face' (ཞལ་སྦྱར), male right, female left, in a 'Bri lugs text; a rain-wheel text adds a middle wheel (བར་འཁོར). No text says the prayer wheel's contents are such a pair in so many words.

## Answered 2026-11-16

Answered from the commentary on Karma Chagme's Sukhavati prayer by Glag bla Bsod nams chos 'grub (BDRC MW3CN4729, etext I3CN4731, found by the phrase «ཡབ་འཁོར་ཡུམ་འཁོར» 11 hits, read at the hit): «དེ་ཡང་འཁོར་ལོ་མུ་ཁྱུད་མ་ལ་ཡབ་འཁོར་ཡུམ་འཁོར་འབྱུང་སྟེ། ཡབ་འཁོར་ཟེར་བ་ཆོས་བསྐོར་ལྟར་ན། ཡིག་ངོ་འོག་ཏུ་བསྟན་ཏེ་འཇོག་རྒྱུ་ཡིན། སྔགས་མགོ་དང་ཡིག་མགོ་ཕྱིར་གཏད་ནས་མཇུག་ནང་དུ་ཐོན་པར་བྱ། ཡུམ་འཁོར་དེ་ཡིག་ངོ་སྟེང་དུ་བསྟན་ཏེ་སྔགས་མགོ་དང་ཡིག་མགོ་ནང་དུ་གཏད་ནས་མཇུག་ཕྱིར་ཐོན་པར་བྱ»: in a wheel with rims there are a father cycle and a mother cycle. The father cycle, by the Dharma tradition, is laid with the letters' face turned down, the mantra's head and the letters' head set outward and the tail coming out inside; the mother cycle is laid with the letters' face up, the mantra's head and the letters' head set inward and the tail coming out outside. It goes on that when the wheel is set out without error and turned, it goes with the reading direction and is as good as reading; if wrong, the tail is taken out upward in a backward going («ལྡོག་འགྲོ») and there is no benefit but a fault. The same for a rolled scroll («བྱང་དཀྲིས»): in the Dharma way the mantra head out and the letters' face turned out; in the Bon way (བོན་བསྐོར་) the mantra head in, the letters' face in, the tail out.

So the father and mother cycles are two ways of laying the written mantra in the one wheel, by which way the letters face and which end lies out or in, not two separate diagrams or two discs; the earlier reading from a 'Bri lugs text (a pair of diagrams joined face to face) is a different use of the words. What the commentator does not say is which face is the outer rim. (Karma Chagme's own text, MW1KG11869 etext I1KG18520, lists among the faults of one turning a wheel: «ཕྱིན་ཅི་ལོག་ཏུ་བསྐོར་བ», turning it backwards, and «ཡབ་སྐོར་ཡུམ་སྐོར་མེད་པར་སྐོར་བ», turning it without the father and mother cycle; see q617.)
