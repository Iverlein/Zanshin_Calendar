# Mipham's thirteen remedy classes against the chapter's twelve

Mipham (vol. 2, p. 517) counts thirteen classes of remedy for the thirteenth examination; set against the chapter's sections only twelve appear. Which group does he count as two, and are his counts (10, 24, 9, 20, 13, 8, 27, 17, 24) the chapter's items or only those marked with the snake mark?

**Narrowed 2026-11-05 (RD164, [reckonings.md](../reckonings.md)).** The snake marks are wavy strokes above verse heads on the Zhol print (img. 804, four seen), unlike the 1996 setting's dots. Not counted; Mipham's counts untested.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-lll, from the notes' own line, wb.2:p517).** The line closes with «བཅོས་སྡེ་ཚན་བཅུ་གསུམ་ལ་ནང་གི་གྲངས་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་གཉིས། རྩ་རྡེལ་བཅུ་གཅིག ངོས་འཛིན་བཅུ་གསུམ་གྱི་ཁ་དམར། དེ་ཡི་བཏང་བཅས་འབྲས་རྡེལ་ཉེར་ལྔ། ཁྱོན་བསྡོམ་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་ཉེར་བདུན»: thirteen classes hold 202; the 25 result pebbles are the eleven roots, the thirteen identifying classes' verdicts and the deciding pebble (11 + 13 + 1), and 202 + 25 = 227. So the notes' thirteen is the number of the identifying classes whose verdicts are counted, and the sum fixes it as thirteen; the twelve groups with counts do not name a thirteenth. The spud pa and rgyug clause gives no number, but the clause «གཤེད་བཞི་ཟིན་ཤི་དང་། དུར་ཞུགས་དང་གཉིས། ཀུ་ཀུང་ལ་གསུམ། ཤི་འགྲམ་བཅས» reads as 2 + 3 + the strike = 6, which with the trigram class at 14 and the thirty dates at 30 makes 14 + 10 + 24 + 6 + 9 + 20 + 13 + 30 + 8 + 27 + 17 + 24 = 202 (inference: the clause's own pieces, not a stated number). That fills twelve groups; the notes' thirteenth is still not named, and the snake marks on the Zhol print (img. 804) were not counted against 10, 24, 9, 20, 13, 8, 27, 17, 24.
