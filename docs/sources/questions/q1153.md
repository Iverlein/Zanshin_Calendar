# In Karma chags med's verses on the hours, are Māra's obstacles from evening to midnight the reason the midnight dream is not Māra's, or only hours of recitation

From dusk to midnight the heroes and ḍākinīs gather and Māra's obstacles begin; from midnight the clear light of sleep is held.

## Answered 2026-11-14

No: he gives no reason of the kind, and his two passages keep Māra and the midnight dream apart. In the verses on the hours (rrd.19:p328) the time from dusk to midnight is the time the heroes and ḍākinīs gather, good for the torma offering, the feast gathering and fierce mantras, and above all, since the Sage subdued Māra in the evening, the time when Māra's obstacles begin, for which the wrathful yidam's recitation is done; from midnight to the middle of the dawn the clear light of sleep is taken hold of. These are hours for recitation and for practice; no dream is named in the passage, and Māra is not set against the midnight. In the verses on the signs in dreams (rrd.19:p271) the dream of the evening is habit, and the dream of midnight is the display of gods and spirits, neither certain: so the hour that is Māra's in the first passage has habit for its dream in the second, and the midnight dream is not called Māra's in either. The two passages cannot give a reason one for the other. Where Marpa's song puts Māra's emanation at midnight (rrd.23:p102-104), Karma chags med, in the hours as in the dreams, does not.
