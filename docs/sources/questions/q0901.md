# Is Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal's quotation of «gsum cha phyis 'byung gsal byed yin» beside the Four Tantras' two lines (BDRC MW8LS18598, search snippet only) a joining of two rules of the same night, as the modern Tibetan books do, or does he read them as one?

The etext is closed (RD299).

## Answered 2026-11-14

Answered from BDRC's search snippet of MW8LS18598 (inv-kkk, 2026-11-14; the etext answers 403, so the snippet is all there is). BDRC's exact-phrase search «གསུམ་ཆ་ཕྱིས་འབྱུང་གསལ་བྱེད་ཡིན» gives eight hits, and the third, Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal's «Bod lugs gso rig gi sems khams rig pa», reads in its Wylie snippet: «… brgyud tho rengs stag gi dus su rmi lam byung na 'bras bu 'byin nges par bstan yod de / ji skad du / “tho rengs gsum cha phyis 'byung gsal byed yin” zhes dang / rgyud rang las / “mtshan stod rmi lam brjed nges 'bras bu med” …». He therefore introduces both lines for one point, that a dream at the tiger hour (the dawn, tho rengs) surely gives its fruit, with «as it is said: … and, in the Tantra itself: …» (zhes dang … las): the dawn line of Klong rdol's verse is the first witness, the Four Tantras' line on the first part of the night (forgotten, no fruit) the second. He joins two rules of the same night, one for the dawn and one for the evening against it, as the modern Tibetan books do; he does not read them as one rule and does not equate the verse's three thirds with the Tantra's two parts. The snippet ends inside the Tantra's quotation, so what he says after it is not seen.
