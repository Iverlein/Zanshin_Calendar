# ནམ་སྟོད and ནམ་སྨད with ཐན and ཞོད

ནམ་སྟོད (*nam stod*) and ནམ་སྨད (*nam smad*) with ཐན (drought) and ཞོད: the parts of the year, the season, or the sky? (continues q682 on ཞོད). (RD190, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-13 (inv-x)

The Gser thang etext (VEIE0OPI3864355D_I3CN5028) has «ནམ་སྟོད» and «ནམ་སྨད» as the pool does, in the pairs «ནམ་སྟོད་ཐན་ལ་ནམ་སྨད་ཞོད» and «ནམ་སྟོད་ཆར་ཆེ་ནམ་སྨད་ཆུང་», so the two stand as a pair, the first part against the second, with ཐན / ཆར་ཆེ and ཞོད / ཆར་ཆུང་ set against each other; the verses also say «སྔ་ཐན» and «ཕྱི་ཞོད» in the same slot. This fits the parts of the year, not the sky; no text glosses it, so it is a reading of the pairs, not a statement. Stays open (q682 on ཞོད).

## Looked at 2026-11-14 (inv-m)

Steinert's dictionaries (sparse clone of the public folder): «nam stod» and «nam smad» are the first and second part of the night (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives-Waldo; *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «nam gyi cha stod» «mtshan mo cha gsum du bgos pa'i cha dang po», the first of the night's three parts; Negi's Sanskrit dictionary *pūrvarātra* and *apararātra*); the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* has «nam» as «mtshan dus kyi ming» (a time of night: «nam stod», «nam smad») and also «ston nam ring mo» (a long autumn), and the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «nam» 1 «gang gi dus su» (at what time). No dictionary gives «nam» alone as the sky (only «nam mkha'»), so q682's "upper sky" rests on nothing; «nam gzhug» (q711) is the end of a season of the year. «than» is drought or a bad portent (Valby, Dan Martin: drought), as the pairs have it. No dictionary has «nam stod» as a half of the year: the pairs' parallel with «snga than, phyi zhod» is still the only ground for the parts of the year. Stays open: no text glosses the pair; the sky is excluded, the night is the dictionaries' literal sense, the early and late part of the year the pairs' use.
