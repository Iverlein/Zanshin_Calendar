# The skya of «སྐྱ་རྒྱལ» and «སྐྱ་འཕམ» (WB ch. 18)

What are the *skya* (སྐྱ) of «སྐྱ་རྒྱལ» and «སྐྱ་འཕམ»? The key on p. 209 defines the pair by the king and the minister contending (the minister against the king is *skya rgyal*, the king against the minister *skya* defeated) but not the word; the 1996 etext reads «སྐུ་རྒྱལ» (*sku rgyal*) throughout, Yigdzin-1 on the reprint «སྐྱ་རྒྱལ», and the one crop read at 4× (p. 198, l. 22) looks like «སྐྱ». Which word is meant, and who are the *skya*? (RD184, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Answered 2026-11-13

The word is «སྐྱ» (*skya*), and the 1996 etext's «སྐུ་རྒྱལ» (*sku rgyal*) is a misreading. The *Tshig mdzod chen mo* and the *Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho* (read through Steinert's dictionaries) have the headword «སྐྱ་རྒྱལ» (*skya rgyal*) with two senses: (1) a good, not rare kind of grain and food and drink; (2) "that a minister contends against the king is called *skya rgyal*, it is explained, in the code-words of the *Mdo sde stag rna*" (བློན་པོས་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལ་རྒོལ་བ་ལ་སྐྱ་རྒྱལ་ཞེས་མདོ་སྡེ་སྟག་རྣའི་བརྡ་ཚིག་ཡིན་པར་བཤད་པ). Sense 2 is the key of p. 209 word for word, and the *Mdo sde stag rna* is the *Tiger-ear* sūtra whose list p. 209–212 reproduces. The single «སྐྱ» is likewise an old code-word for the crops: the same dictionaries give «སྐྱ» as «ལོ་ཏོག་དང་བཟའ་བཏུང» (crops, food and drink), «ལོ་ལེགས་སྐྱ་རྒྱལ་བ» (the harvest good, *skya* winning), and the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* «སྐྱ་རྒྱས» (a good crop came). So *skya* are the crops (and the food of them), and *skya rgyal* / *skya 'pham* are the crops winning / defeated, which the key's second, political sense (minister against king) is the sūtra's code for; neither dictionary glosses «སྐྱ་འཕམ» itself. Dictionaries only: no passage of the *Tiger-ear* sūtra read.
