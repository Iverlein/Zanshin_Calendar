# p. 205: «རྐུ་འཕྲོག» or «སྐུ་འཕྲོག»

Is the head of the stack in «རྐུ་འཕྲོག» (p. 205, line 15: «སྐྱེ་བོ་བརྩེ་མེད་རྐུ་འཕྲོག་བྱེད») ra or sa (MITRA reads «སྐུ་འཕྲོག»), and is «སྐུ་འཕྲོག» a word anywhere? Read as ra by sense, not by the letter. RD184 (2026-11-08).

## Answered 2026-11-12

On the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5028 img. 204, printed p. 205, line 15, crop at 5-6x) the stack before ka in «རྐུ་འཕྲོག» (steal and rob) has a flat, straight-topped head, as a ra-head has; the sa-headed stack that opens the same line, «སྐྱེ་བོ» (beings), has a rounded, hooked head. The two are different shapes on the page, so the word reads «རྐུ་འཕྲོག» with ra, and MITRA's «སྐུ་འཕྲོག» is a misreading. The same word, with ra, stands in the pool's other verses (the day-animals verse and WB ch. 33; the earth snake and water sheep years of life; the day-letters verse), and «སྐུ་འཕྲོག» is found in none of them. The reading rests on the head's shape, not on a clear separate ra stroke: the head and the ka are fused in the print.
