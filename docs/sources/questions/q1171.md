# Why does the Derge translation of the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa use «སྦལ་པ» (frog) for the crab sign throughout (ch. 1, 15, 18; mmk.1:p246, p429)? Is it an older Tibetan name for Cancer, as WB's period name «སྤལ་བ / སྦལ་བ» suggests?

Raised by the 2026-11-13 investigation b; see the question's cited passages.

## Answered 2026-11-14

It is the lexicon's name for the crab, not a slip. The Tshig mdzod chen mo and the Tshig gter rgya mtsho list «སྦལ་པ་རྐང་མང» (frog many-legs) and «སྦལ་ཆེན» (big frog) as poetic synonyms (mark «མངོན») of «སྡིག་སྲིན» (the crab); Valby gives «sbal pa» "frog, crab" and «sbal chen» "crab, lizard"; Ives Waldo gives «sbal pa'i khyim» "house of Cancer", Rangjung Yeshe "the mansion of Cancer, sixth of the twelve". The Sgom sde tshig mdzod chen mo says the word is used for the crab because it is called «སྦལ་པ», «སྦལ་ཆེན», «སྦལ་པ་རྐང་མང», and quotes the Mngon brjod mkhas pa'i rna rgyan: «སྡིག་སྲིན་གྱི་མིང། འཕྲོག་བྱེད་མིག་དང་རྐང་མང་པོ། སྦལ་ཆེན་སྡིག་སྲིན་ཀརྐ་ཊ» (crab: robber, eye, many-legged, big frog, crab, karkaṭa). The Negi dictionary (the Tengyur's Sanskrit) gives «sbal pa» = karkaṭaka the sign, with the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa itself as the example (ma.mū. 105ka, the list of thirty-six houses «ལུག་དང་…སྦལ་པ་དང་…»), and the ITLR gives karkaṭa as rendered «sbal pa» or «ka rka ṭa». So the Derge's «སྦལ་པ» is the Tibetan name of the sign in the Abhidhāna tradition, and WB's «སྤལ་བ/སྦལ་བ» is the same word. Why a frog stands for a crab is not said in any dictionary read; the likely reason of the Derge's choice is that «སྡིག་པ» is taken by the scorpion (the eighth sign) and the crab's own «སྡིག་སྲིན» is close to it (the Śrīkośa's «sdig srin» in Negi is vṛścika), but that is an inference, no source says it. Whether «སྦལ་པ» is older than the period name of WB is not shown: no dated source found. Sources: Steinert's 64 dictionaries read on 2026-11-14 (headwords «sbal pa», «sbal chen», «sbal pa rkang mang», «rkang mang», «sdig srin», «sbal pa'i khyim»).
