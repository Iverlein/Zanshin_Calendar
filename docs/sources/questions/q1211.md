# KD vol. 3 p. 738: what is «thog 'don» in the trigram khen's bad list?

The digest of WB's fifth section lists «thog 'don» among the bad things of the trigram khen (KD vol. 3 p. 738); it is not glossed there. Wanted: a gloss in a dictionary or a manual.

## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-h)

Not settled. BDRC phrase search «ཐོག་འདོན» gives 126 hits; those in the almanac texts are the khen verse itself (WB vol. 2 I1KG12907 and the Gser thang I3CN5029, the Vaidurya dkar po MW2CZ8040: «འདི་ལ་ནད་པ་ཐོག་འདོན་དང་། ལྷ་ཁང་མི་ཉལ་རྒྱལ་པོ་བཅོས», with no gloss). The other hits use «thog 'don» as a verb of reciting or reading out: in Wylie, «dpe thog 'don dgos» (reading from a book), «seng ge'i khri thog 'don» (reciting on the lion throne) and «srog gud thog 'don bya ba» in prose and verse; none speaks of a sick person. So the word is a recitation word («'don», recite) and the khen line plausibly means that having a sick person recited over is bad on this trigram, but no dictionary or manual gives that sense and the pool's Steinert search (PLAN.md, inv-n 2026-11-14) found no headword. An inference without a printed gloss: open.
