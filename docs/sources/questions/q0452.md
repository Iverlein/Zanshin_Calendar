# Is the mansion verses' ransom noun «རྗེན་ཕྱེ» or «རྫེན» in print

In the mansion verses' ransom formula the noun is «རྗེན་ཕྱེ» on the 1996 scan but Yigdzin-1 reads «རྫེན» on the Gser thang reprint in several verses. Which spelling is printed? (RD79)

## Answered 2026-11-14

The noun is «རྗེན་ཕྱེ» (rjen phye), a real word and not a misreading of «རྫེན». It is the flour of «རྗེན», the dough of the ransom effigy: BDRC's full-text search (2026-11-14, inv-ggg) finds «རྗེན་ཕྱེ» in 148 places, among them the ransom («བཟློག་གླུད» zlog glud) rites «བཟློག་གླུད་ནི་རྗེན་ཕྱེ་བཙོས་ལ…» (Sa skya bka' 'bum; Tārānātha's collected works, dpe bsdur ma; Sgrub thabs rin po che kun las btus pa), «འབྲུ་ནག་གི་རྗེན་ཕྱེ་ཁྲག་གིས་སྦྲུས་པ» (Rin chen gter mdzod; Chos kyi grags pa), and the medicine of the Four Tantras («ཕབ་དང་རྗེན་ཕྱེ་ཞོ་བཙོས་ཞུན་བྱས་ལ», Gso rig rgyud bzhi; the Bri gung and Sangs rgyas phun tshogs copies), where it is a flour boiled in curds. The mansion verses' ransom formula itself, «རྗེན་ཕྱེའི་(ཁྱུང་/སྟག/མི/རྟ/གླང)གི་གཟུགས», has 25 hits for «རྗེན་ཕྱེའི་» in the Vaidurya dkar po's own etexts (1996, Gser thang; the Gser thang etext of I3CN5029 reads «རྗེན་ཕྱེའི་མི་ཡི་གཟུགས» too), the Kun 'dus chen mo, and the Chos rnam kun btus's chapter on the twenty-eight mansions (MW1KG2733_CE87A9, «ས་རྗེན་ཕྱེའི་ཁྱུང་གི་གཟུགས»); «རྫེན་ཕྱེ» and «རྫེན་ཕྱེའི་» have no hit at all. So the mansion verses mean an effigy of ransom dough in the shape of the animal, and the reprint's «རྫེན» in Yigdzin-1's reading is the reader's, as the 1996 etext's 23 «རྗེན་ཕྱེ» already said. The stack of the first letter on the black reprint was not made out by eye (the glyph stays scan-bound), but the word identifies it.
