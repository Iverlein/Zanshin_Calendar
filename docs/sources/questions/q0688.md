# «ཚད་ནད» and «མཇུག» in ch. 17's colophon

In the colophon of ch. 17 (p. 197), «ཚད་ནད་ཀྱི་མཇུག་ལག་དལ་གཉིད་བསྲུངས་སུ་བགྱིས་པ» (written with a slack hand to keep off sleep at the tail of «ཚད་ནད»): does «ཚད་ནད» mean a fever, an illness of the heat kind (the Tibetan medical sense), or something of the work, and what is meant by «མཇུག» (tail) here? (RD185, corpus/wb/ch17.md)

## Answered 2026-11-13

«ཚད་ནད» (*tshad nad*) is a fever: Rangjung Yeshe, Valby and Ives-Waldo give "fever, malaria; illness from heat, heat stroke" (Ives-Waldo: the disease that comes of being struck by heat or from heat grown), the Tibetan-medical sense, not a term of the work. «མཇུག» (*mjug*) is end, tail, "after, the rest of" (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives-Waldo). «ཚད་ནད་ཀྱི་མཇུག་» is thus "the tail of a fever", the time just after a fever: the author says he wrote the chapter, with a slack hand, to keep off sleep, in the aftermath of a fever. The dictionaries fix the words; that it is the author's own illness (and not a figure) is the plain reading of the sentence, not stated by any other source.
