# p. 187's fifth-place count: «བུ་གས» and the quarters' clearing

p. 187, the fourth place of the five-place count, «མཚོ(༤)བརྒྱན་བུ་གས་བགོ། ལྷག་མ་ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས་ལྷག་མེད་ན། སླར་ཡང་བུ་གས་སྦྱང་བར་བྱ།»: what is *bu gas* (nine?) and how is the remainder cleared by the quarters («ཕྱོགས») to leave the *sme ba*? (RD186, corpus/wb/ch16.md)

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

«བུ་ག» is nine: p. 30 writes «བུ་ག་༩» and «བུ་ག་མཁའ་མེ་༣༠༩», so «བུ་གས» is "by nine" and the fourth place is the day's number with 4 added, divided by nine. The clearing of the remainder «ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས» (by «ཕྱོགས» 10, as the word is 10 in the verses of pp. 29-32) and the repeated clearing of nine when nothing is left (10 − 0 = 10, then 9 away: 1) was not tested against a dated *sme ba*; the engines' *sme ba* is not tied to WB's elapsed-day count of this place. Left open.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-qqq)

«བུ་ག» as nine is confirmed by the dictionaries: «ལུས་ཀྱི་བུ་ག་ལ་དགུ་ཡོད་པས་གྲངས་དགུ་མཚོན» (Tshig mdzod), "9 [because the body has 9 holes]" (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, Valby). ## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-q)

A parallel manual's verse for the same count was found and read: Kun 'dus vol. 2, section 11 (etext VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4716, printed p. ~233; the verse form of the almanac's day-reckoning, with its own constants): «གསུམ་པར་ཀླུས་བརྒྱན་བུག་གས་བགོས། །ལྷག་མས་ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས་ལྷག་མེད་ན། །སླར་ཡང་བུ་གས་ཕྱོགས་ལ་སྦྱངས། །ལྷག་མ་ཉིན་དེའི་སྨེ་བ་ཡིན།» ("third: adorn with the nagas (8), divide by *bu gas* (nine); by the remainder clear the quarters; if nothing remains, clear again by nine against the quarters; the remainder is that day's sme ba"), followed by «བཞི་པའི་གནས་ལ་བསེ་རུ་བྱིན། །ཀླུས་བགོས་ལྷག་ནི་སྤར་ཁ་སྟེ།» (fourth place: add the rhinoceros; the remainder of the division by the nagas is the trigram). So «བུ་གས» is read twice as the divisor nine, the remainder of that division is "the day's sme ba" («ཉིན་དེའི་སྨེ་བ»; WB: «ལྷག་མ་ཉི་མའི་སྨེ་བ་ཡིན»), and the clearing «ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས» is a step on the remainder in both manuals; the constants differ (WB adds 4, this manual 8), so the one cannot check the other. The reading of the step that gives a falling cycle: sme ba = 10 − r for the remainder r of 1 to 8, and r = 0 giving 10, which is cleared by nine again to 1; then successive days run 1, 9, 8, ..., 2, 1, 9, one less a day as the daily sme ba does. The other reading (r = 0 giving 9) repeats a number and is no cycle. Not tested against a dated day (WB's count of elapsed days from the rab byung's start is not tied to the engine's day number). BDRC phrase search: «བུ་གས་བགོ» 9 hits, «བུ་གས་སྦྱང» 10, «ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས་ལྷག་མེད» 8, all copies of the same verse (WB's etexts I1KG12906, I3CN5028/5029; the Kun 'dus; Do shal vol. 1 I5875 and 'Brug pa Dpe bsdur ma I1PD107145 are OCR noise or search-only); no commentary.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z5, computed against Henning's almanac days)

What the printed almanac's number does, counted in `core/src/test/resources/vectors/henning-phugpa.tsv` (2245 days, 2000-2027, the vector the engine's `HenningPhugpaTest` passes; the rows with the number, 1996 consecutive pairs of days with the date up by one): the *sme ba* printed under the trigram goes up by one from date to date in every pair (1996 of 1996, mod 9), and the trigram goes up by one in the same pairs (mod 8); over a skipped date (the date up by two in one day) both go up by two (80 pairs, 2 more across a month end); over a doubled date (two days, one date) both go up by one, so the number follows the date and not the civil day (46 pairs); and at the end of each leap month the number drops by three against the continuous count (four cases: 2000-03-06, 2013-10-04, 2024-08-04 and 2027-04-06, the sme ba +7 mod 9 and the trigram +3 mod 8 where +1 is the rule), the thirty dates of the leap month not entering the count.

What follows for the reading of p. 187 (wb.1:p187) and for the clearing «ཕྱོགས་སྦྱངས»: (1) the three places before the fourth (weekday, animal, element) go up by one a civil day in the engine (`TibetanCalendar.of`: weekday (jd+2) mod 7, day animal and element by jd), as WB's first three places do with the elapsed days «ཞག»; (2) the sme ba and the trigram of the fourth and fifth place are then a civil-day cycle, which the printed almanac's number (date-based, drops at a leap month) cannot be, since WB's count of pure months «ཟླ་བ་རྣམ་པར་དག་པ» runs on through a leap month and the almanac's number does not; so p. 187's «ཉི་མའི་སྨེ་བ» is not the number printed under the trigram (wb.1:p178), whatever the reading of the clearing; (3) read as a falling cycle (sme ba = 10 - r, inv-q's reading) the fourth place goes down by one a day while the fifth place's trigram goes up by one, the reverse of the almanac's printed pair, in which both go up; read with the remainder as it stands (r, and 9 for r = 0) it goes up with the trigram like the almanac's. That is a point for the second reading only if the two counts are the same quantity, which (2) shows they are not; the direction of the day's own sixty-day count (p. 192, `DaySmeBa`) is forward after the winter solstice and backward after the summer one, so no perpetual cycle is that either. The clearing's direction is therefore still not decided by any dated day; WB's elapsed-day count of the third verse (the three places, 56, 160, 707) was not decoded far enough to fix its epoch (the divisor 707 is the engine's 11312 / 16, `Phugpa.M2` = 11135/11312), so the arithmetic against a dated day is not done.
