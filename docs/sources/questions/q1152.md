# Does the tradition take the Karmapa's bile of the midnight dream (rrd.7:p102) and his power of the spirits (rrd.5:p57-59) as one teaching said two ways or as two sayings

His commentary gives the midnight dream as bile's by the Mañjuśrī root tantra; his verses give Marpa's gdon dbang.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Two sayings, not one teaching said twice. In his own commentary on the Zab mo nang gi don the third Karmapa gives the dream's appearances by humour, evening phlegm, midnight bile, dawn wind, and names the root tantra of Mañjuśrī as its source (rrd.7:p102): the verse says what the dream shows (the objects' look), and nothing in it concerns whether to trust it. In the verses on dreams he gives Marpa's rule, a rule of trust: habit at dusk, power of spirits (གདོན་དབང, gdon dbang) at midnight, the dawn true (rrd.5:p57-59). They are in two different works, name two different sources, answer two different questions, and neither text refers to the other. The commentators who carry the humour verse, Dar mo sman rams pa and Zur mkhar ba (rrd.18:p285-286, rrd.17:p352-355), give it with the root tantra's four watches and without Marpa's saying; the song of Marpa (rrd.23:p102-104) has no humour. Bile is not named a spirit's work anywhere read. BDRC's exact searches «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་མཁྲིས་པ» and «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་གདོན» (2026-11-14, with RD405's) find no text that joins the two. So the tradition's texts keep them apart; whether the Karmapa himself held bile and spirits to describe one dream is not said, and a reader who joins them (the combined reading) does so on his own.
