# In the lama's remark in the dream verses (cbl:p236-238) the etext reads «འོ་ཡུག་པ་གཤིན་རྗེ་དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ་ཡིན་གསུང་སྐད» after the saying that the black girl's binding is the sign of the time of death's arrival: who or what is the «འོ་ཡུག་པ», and is the remark the old annotator's or Mkhyen brtse's?

RD331.

Looked at again (inv-y, 2026-11-16): the only BDRC hit of «འོ་ཡུག་པ་གཤིན་རྗེ» and of «གཤིན་རྗེ་དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ» is the CBL itself (MW3PD1002, Mkhyen brtse's works); nothing new. The remark stays in the CBL alone (see claim o-yug-pa-in-the-death-dream-remark and q1010).

**Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-h).** See q1010: «འོ་ཡུག་པ» is the name of a Sakya teacher, most likely BDRC's P3089 (died 1253); the saying has no Sakya carrier. «དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ་ཡིན» finds the CBL alone again; whether the remark is the old annotator's or Mkhyen brtse's is not told in any text reached.

**Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-i).** «དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ» (45 hits) and «གཤིན་རྗེ་དུས་མཚན» (21): the name is Yama's night goddess (gshin rje dus mtshan ma, Kālarātri, also dus mtshan nag mo: the Sakya protector rites of Ngor chen and Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams, Gu ge/Sa skya offerings, the Kālacakra year-sign lists), so the remark's «གཤིན་རྗེ་དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ» names a figure of Yama's retinue, and the black girl of the dream is read as her sign. The remark's own sentence occurs in the CBL only (MW3PD1002 etext, with «bla ma na re»); «འོ་ཡུག་པ་གསུང» and «འོ་ཡུག་པ་ཆེན་པོ» 0 hits. Whether the remark is the old annotator's or Mkhyen brtse's is still not told: «bla ma na re» attributes the first sentence to a lama, not to Mkhyen brtse. Open.

**Narrowed 2026-11-21 (sweep from inv-i).** «གཤིན་རྗེ་དུས་མཚན་ནག་མོ» names Yama's night goddess (Kālarātri), so the black girl is read as her sign; «འོ་ཡུག་པ» is the name of a Sakya teacher, most likely BDRC's P3089 (died 1253) (inv-h). The remark stands in the CBL alone, and whether it is the old annotator's or Mkhyen brtse's is not told.
