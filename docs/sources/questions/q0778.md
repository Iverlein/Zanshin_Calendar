# Ch. 6 p. 40 line 1: the stack ༡༡ over ༡༡

Ch. 6 p. 40 line 1 prints a stack ༡༡ over ༡༡ after «victor, hand, eye, two, two»; all four glyphs are the 1 with its tail, and the number-words give 222224. What does the stack mean? RD205 (2026-11-08).

## Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-u), narrowed

Arithmetic: the number-words «རྒྱལ་བ་ལག་མིག་གཉིས་གཉིས» read low word first give 24, 2, 2, 2, 2, so the number is 222224 (the same page's «མིག་དམར་སྟོང་པ་རྒྱལ་བ་༢༤༠» fixes «རྒྱལ་བ» as 24). The stack has four glyphs in two rows; it cannot carry six digits, so it can at most gloss the four small words «ལག་མིག་གཉིས་གཉིས» as ༢༢ over ༢༢ and leave «རྒྱལ་བ» (24) unglossed. Scan (I3CN5028 img. 47, line 2, 1.8x): the four glyphs have the curled head and tail of the print's ༡ (as in «(༡༨)» and «(༡༥)» on the same line), not the open hook of the print's ༢ (as in «ཟུང(༢)» of line 4), so the machine's ༡༡/༡༡ is the print's shape; the Gser thang etext has «(༢༣)» for the bracket and the 1996 etext «( )». What the print means is not decided: either a misprint or a figure not equal to the number-words. Open.
