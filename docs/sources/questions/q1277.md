# Which are the four roots of the one-day vow in Lo chen Dharma shrI's own list, and in what order? Only unchastity and false speech are named in the passages read.

Raised by the practice-days investigation (pd-c, 2026-11-16); see the cited passages.

## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-v)

Lo chen's list is not complete in the passage read: dpn:i70 gives only «མི་ཚངས་སྤྱོད་སོགས་རྩ་བའི་ལྟུང་བ་བཞི» (unchastity and the rest) and takes the eight from the Treasury, whose own commentary counts the four as the first four, down to giving up false speech (akb:i365-366), i.e. in the usual order killing, stealing, unchastity, false speech; Lo chen's own order is not written out. A later page of dpn naming the four would settle it; none is read yet. Stays open.

## Answered 2026-11-24

Answered from the same book (etext, read with the page image looked at): Lo chen's four roots are taking life (སྲོག་གཅོད་པ, srog gcod pa), taking what is not given (མ་བྱིན་པར་ལེན་པ, ma byin par len pa), unchastity (མི་ཚངས་སྤྱོད, mi tshangs spyod; for the one-day vow, dpn:i70) and false speech (རྫུན་དུ་སྨྲ་བ, rdzun du smra ba). Two witnesses of the order: the root text's verse of the novice's ten trainings on etext p. 9, «སྲོག་གཅོད་རྐུ་དང་མི་ཚངས་སྤྱོད་དང་རྫུན» (killing, stealing, unchastity, lying), and the commentary on the lay devotee's roots (dpn:i71), «སྲོག་གཅོད་པ། མ་བྱིན་པར་ལེན་པ། འདོད་པས་ལོག་པར་གཡེམ་པ། རྫུན་དུ་སྨྲ་བ» (killing, stealing, wrong sexual conduct, lying), where wrong conduct stands in the place of unchastity because the householder keeps it more easily. Lo chen does not write the list out at the one-day vow itself; it is the same four, with unchastity as the third, as the Treasury's commentary counts them (akb:i365-366). Rests on the etext; the page image of dpn:i71 was not read syllable by syllable.
