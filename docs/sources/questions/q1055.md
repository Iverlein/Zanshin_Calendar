# The measure of ch. 18 gives a watch as four chu tshod and as four yud tsam and the day-and-night as thirty yud tsam; is the tantra's chu tshod the same length as its yud tsam (one thirty-second of the day-and-night, as in the Hevajra and Kālacakra books' 32 water-hours), and is «four» a rounding of the 3¾ of thirty over eight?

RD378.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The Mchog dga' says in ch. 1 (printed p. 32, kd.3:p32) that «a day-and-night in thirty-two water-hours», «in sixteen or twenty-four junctions», «in four staffs» and «in a hundred staffs» are counts found in the inner scriptures, and keeps for its own place the Kālacakra's sixty. So a 32-count with chu tshod as the unit is a counted tradition in the books; it does not say that the tantra's chu tshod is the yud tsam. The Sanskrit has neither unit (mmk.2:p158-160 has only the eye's opening and closing and acchaṭa), so the Sanskrit gives no help. The arithmetic stands as in claim mmk-ch18-measure-chains-and-arithmetic: one chu tshod = one yud tsam if both chains give the same watch; «four» for 3¾ remains an inference.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes, in the Sanskrit as well: the tantra's chu tshod is the ghaṭī and its yud tsam is the muhūrta, and it gives four of each to the watch (prahara); «four» is not a rounding by the translators. The critical Sanskrit of the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa in the 84000 edition (Toh 543, ch. 24 there, verses 24.194-24.201; five manuscripts; read 2026-11-14 in its TEI source, licence non-commercial: facts only) has two chains side by side without reconciling them:

- «acchaṭā-śata-saṅghātaṃ nāḍikā ca prakīrtitā; catur-nāḍiyo ghaṭīty uktā, caturghaṭyāḥ praharaḥ smṛtaḥ»: 100 acchaṭā (finger-snaps; the Tibetan se gol) make a nāḍikā (the Tibetan rtsa rgyun), four nāḍikā a ghaṭī (the Tibetan chu tshod), four ghaṭī a prahara (the Tibetan thun).
- «catuḥpraharo divasas tu rātryā ebhiḥ; ebhir aṣṭais tathā yuktaḥ ahorātraṃ prakalpitam»: four praharas to the day, as many to the night, eight to the day-and-night.
- «daśonmeṣanimeṣaṃ tu kṣaṇa-mātram»: ten eye-blinks (unmeṣa-nimeṣa) make a kṣaṇa; «daśa kṣaṇā ... muhūrtaṃ parikalpitam, catur-muhūrttaḥ praharas tu mantrajñaiḥ»: ten kṣaṇas a muhūrta, four muhūrtas a prahara «according to mantra adepts».

So Tibetan «ཆུ་ཚོད་བཞི་ལ་ཐུན» = «caturghaṭyāḥ praharaḥ» and «ཡུད་ཙམ་བཞི་ལ་ཐུན་གཅིག» = «catur-muhūrttaḥ praharaḥ» render the Sanskrit word for word; the translators' glossary of 84000 equates yud tsam with muhūrta (1/30 of the day, 48 minutes), chu tshod with ghaṭikā (given there as about 24 minutes), rtsa rgyun with the nāḍī (half a muhūrta) and thun with prahara (three hours). With four to the prahara the text's own two chains make one ghaṭī equal to one muhūrta (the prahara divided by four both ways), which is the arithmetic of claim mmk-ch18-measure-chains-and-arithmetic; but the usual Indian ghaṭikā is half a muhūrta and 7.5 of them make a prahara, so the first chain does not give the ghaṭikā of 24 minutes; the tantra does not say that its two chains agree and the Sanskrit does not either The thirty muhūrtas of the day-and-night (the translation of 24.191 has «many of the muhūrtas, which are known to be thirty»; the Tibetan «ཉིན་ཞག་སུམ་ཅུ» is its counterpart, the Sanskrit line of that verse was not read here) against eight praharas of four muhūrtas, thirty-two, is therefore a disagreement inside the Sanskrit tantra's own text, not made by the Tibetan translators.

The earlier note (mmk.2:p179-181, and q1055's narrowing) that the Sanskrit has neither unit rested on the OCR of the Trivandrum edition and on its pages 158-160 and 175-176; the measure stands in the 84000 critical text at verses 24.194-24.201 (the page marks of that edition's V218 and S280). Not done: the Sanskrit was read in 84000's text, not in a printed edition nor in a manuscript.
