# «ནམ་མཇུག», the tail of the year, and the bird «འོལ་བ»

WB vol. 2, pp. 27–30 (img. 35–38): what span is «ནམ་མཇུག», "the tail of the year", which is long or short and is "cut" 21 days after the autumn equinox; which bird is «འོལ་བ»?

## Read further 2026-11-01

Answered in RD99: «ནམ་མཇུག» is the end of autumn; «འོལ་བ» the black kite (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Ives and Waldo); claims/ch28-nam-mjug.md, ch28-bird-days-forty.md.

## Answered 2026-11-14

«ནམ་མཇུག», the tail of the year, is the end of autumn, and «འོལ་བ» is the black kite. Read: WB's own clock on p. 28 (scan) puts the cutting of the tail 21 days after the autumn equinox, and p. 30 gives its length (15 days, or 5) by how long the autumn and winter hoarfrosts are dulled; Ives and Waldo and the Bod yig tshig gter give "end of autumn" for the word, and the dictionaries name «འོལ་བ» the bird that arrives with the days called she mo (claims/ch28-nam-mjug.md, ch28-bird-days-forty.md). Computed with the app's engine (2026-11-14): the day 21 days after the 2026 autumn equinox, 14 October, is in the Phugpa 9th month (tha skar), so the tail falls in the last month of autumn by the Kalacakra count; p. 31's "26th of the last autumn month, no snow-rain, the tail short" is then a sign given after the cut, a point that bears on q321.
