# In the Gtsang sman text the physician's dawn dream is the first of thirteen examinations of the sick (etext p. 263): does this arrangement, and the rule that the physician declines the case after a bad dream, rest on the Four Tantras' own chapter on examining the sick or on the Siddhasāra's chapter 5?

RD320; the relation of the three would need the Instruction Tantra's chapter on the messenger read against both.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered in part by RD330 (2026-11-10): the physician's own dawn dream as the first examination is in neither the Siddhasāra's chapter 5 nor the Four Tantras' chapter 7 (the messenger and the physician's setting-out signs are in chapter 7 of the Explanatory Tantra, the Instruction Tantra has no messenger chapter); in the Siddhasāra the dreams are a person's, healthy or sick, and the messenger follows; in the Four Tantras the messenger comes first and the dream verses are the sick person's; no physician's dream beside the messenger in Byang pa, Rin spungs pa or Khro ru; Gtsang sman's dawn likely rests on the Tantra's night rule (inference); 'the physician does not go' and 'do not treat that sick one' are Moonbeams verses quoted by Byang pa and Khro ru beside the Tantra. Which source Gtsang sman followed stays open.
