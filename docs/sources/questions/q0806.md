# Does the tradition fix LGD's dusk, midnight and early dawn by hours or by the watches of the Svapnohana?

LGD gives the three parts of the night no spans; to place its dawn against the other texts' watches.


Narrowed (RD284, 2026-11-10): LGD's srod, gung and tho rangs are the names of the three thirds of the night in RST's opening verses; neither text gives hours or watches, and LGD does not use the Svapnohana's four-watch scheme.

## Answered 2026-11-14

By neither. LGD's dusk (སྲོད, srod), midnight (གུང, gung) and early dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) are the names of the night's three thirds (ཐུན་གསུམ, thun gsum) that the tradition has from the older verses (the third Karmapa's verses on dreams quoting Marpa, RST's three watches, Klong rdol bla ma's verse, the Rin chen gter mdzod bardo instruction), and no text read gives them hours, water-hours or the four watches of the Svapnohana. The lexica define the unit the same way: the Tshig mdzod chen mo has «མཚན་ཐུན», any one of the night's three parts, and «when the night is cut in three watches, the dawn is let go in the last watch»; the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho the same. The Svapnohana's scheme, four watches and a fifth term of dawn (སྐྱ་རེངས, skya rengs), is a different one: the Mahāvyutpatti's list of the night is of five (srod, 'jing, lnga tshigs, nam gung, tho rangs), and the Hevajra and Mañjuśrī root tantra books count four watches to the night, each a quarter, so LGD's dawn third is not the Svapnohana's fourth watch by any statement. BDRC searches of 2026-11-10 for a span of the thirds found none, and none of the 2026-11-14 searches of the second part of the night's names (see q863) found one either. To place LGD's dawn against the other texts' watches the pool has only the shared name tho rangs and the tradition's rule that dawn is the dream that counts.
