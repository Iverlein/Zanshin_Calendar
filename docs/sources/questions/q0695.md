# «དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན» and «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» in p. 22's verse

p. 22, the verse «བསྟན་རྩིས་ཤོག་བུའི་དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན་འདིར། །འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས་ལ་དཔྱད་གསུམ་དག་པར་འཚལ།»: the sense of «དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན» (a dancer on the "corner" of the calendar leaf?) and of «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» is not clear. What do they mean? (RD187, corpus/wb/ch03.md)

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-ppp)

The words are in the dictionaries; the sense of the phrase is still the poet's figure. «དོ་ར» (do ra) is "a dancing ground, a stage" (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Tshig gter, Ives Waldo, Rangjung Yeshe), «གར་མཁན» (gar mkhan) "dancer, actor"; so «བསྟན་རྩིས་ཤོག་བུའི་དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན» is "the dancer on the stage of the leaf (sheet) of the doctrine's chronology" (bstan rtsis: "the reckoning that tells in which years the holders of the doctrine appeared", Tshig mdzod chen mo): the author speaks of himself as a performer on the leaf that carries the chronology of the teachings, which is the matter of the chapter (the Buddha's years). «འདྲེན་བྱེད» ('dren byed) is "the leader, he who leads" (Tshig mdzod chen mo), in the mngon-brjod language "the eye", and one of the names of the horse; «གཡོས» (g.yos) from g.yo ba "to move": «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» is then "the leader's movement" (RD187's rendering stands) or, if 'dren byed is the horse (the draught-horse that leads), the stirring of the horse; the dictionaries give no phrase «'dren byed g.yos». The bracketed gloss of the print, «གཟུགས་ཅན་འཕར་མ་དང་འབྲེལ་བའི་དངོས་པོ་ངེས་བསྟན» ("the matter bound with the addition by form is shown with certainty"), points to a chronological correction (the addition, འཕར་མ), so the "leader's movement" would be the stepping on of the reckoning that the three examinations (དཔྱད་གསུམ) are asked to purify. This is a reading of the figure, not a source; a BDRC phrase search «དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན་འདིར» and «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས་ལ་དཔྱད་གསུམ» finds only WB's own prints (four prints of the work: BDRC I1KG12906, I3CN5028, I4CZ74285, I1KG81102), no gloss. Source: tmz:box9, wb.1:p22.

## Narrowed 2026-11-17 (inv-a)

BDRC exact search «དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན» (5 hits) and «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» (14): WB's four prints give the whole verse with the line before it, which the pool's passage has too: «ཐོས་བརྩོན་རིག་བྱེད་སྨྲ་བར་འདོད་པ་གང། །རྣམ་དག་ལོ་ཡི་ངེས་པ་རྙེད་པའི་ཆེད། །བསྟན་རྩིས་ཤོག་བུའི་དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན་འདིར། །འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས་ལ་དཔྱད་གསུམ་དག་པར་འཚལ།»: 'whoever, diligent in hearing, wishes to speak as a learned man, in order to find the certainty of the pure year, in this dancer on the stage of the chronology-leaf, I ask that the leader's movement be examined thrice and made pure': the verse addresses the reader who would reckon the years, and «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» is what he is asked to examine. Two outside witnesses give the figure's wording: «དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན» is the stage dancer in a verse of another poem (the Rgyud sde kun btus etext, a reply of Karma chos dbang: «སྐོས་བྱེད་རྐང་ཐུང་དོ་རའི་གར་མཁན་མ»), and «འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས» occurs in a poetic example copied in six anthologies of ornate poetry (BDRC etexts *Snyan ngag gi snang brnyan gyi mdzes nyams brjod pa*, *Bod kyi snyan ngag gi mdzes nyams*, *Gzhon nu zla med kyi gtam rgyud*, *Brda dag snyan ngag sogs…gzhung gces btus*): «སྤྲིན་པོའི་འདྲེན་བྱེད་གཡོས་གྱུར་པས། །རྒྱུ་སྐར་ཚོགས་དབུས་ཟླ་བ་འམ།» ('when the cloud's leader had stirred, the moon among the host of stars…'), where the leader of the clouds is what moves them. So the phrase is a stock kāvya figure, the leader (of the dance, as of the clouds) set in motion, which WB turns to the reckoning: the one who leads the years' count is to be examined. The sense stays a reading of the figure; no commentary glosses it.
