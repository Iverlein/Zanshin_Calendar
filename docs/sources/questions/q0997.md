# Is the Brahmavaivarta's v. 2 of Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa 73 «tathaiva» or «tatraiva» in the manuscripts of the Kṛṣṇajanmakhaṇḍa?

RD357.

Narrowed (inv-rrr, 2026-11-14): no manuscript read, but three more witnesses read «tathaiva» (th, not tr). (1) The Bombay Venkateshwar Steam Press print of 1910 (Internet Archive acdg_brahmavaivarta-mahapurana-by-vyasa-sanskrit-purana-pothi-illustrated-bombay, page image n843 at 2000 px, chapter numbered 76 there, the Svapnādhyāya beginning after the colophon of 76): «sāmavedaś ca praśastaḥ sarvakarmasu | tathaiva kāṇvaśākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare || 2 ||», and on the same page v. 5: «dvitīye cāṣṭabhir māsaiḥ … caturthe cārddhamāsena» for the watches. (2) Aufrecht's Leipzig catalogue no. 1179 (a seven-leaf Svapnādhyāya «nach der Unterschrift aus dem Brahmavaivartapurāṇa», script of 18?? , the last digits blurred) gives the first lines in Aufrecht's own transcription, read on the page image n369: «… sāmavedaś ca praśastaḥ sarvakarmasu | tathaiva kāṇvaśākhāyāṃ puṇyakāṇḍe manohare» (th and a conjunct-free second syllable; he prints no verse numbers because the manuscript has none); that is a catalogue's quotation of a manuscript, not an image of it, and the manuscript is of the 19th century at the oldest. (3) The 1935 Banaras Svapnādhyāya with a Nepali commentary (iipt-swapnadhyaya-brahmavaivarta-purana-with-nepali-com) renders the half-line with «pani», also, which is «tathaiva»; its Sanskrit text layer is noise. A tatra-reading is found in nothing read; the older manuscripts remain unseen.
