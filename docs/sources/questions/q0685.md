# «སད» and «སད་སེར» on the crops (pp. 199, 204, 209)

What are «སད» (*sad*) and «སད་སེར» (*sad ser*) which come upon the crops (pp. 199, 204, 209)? The key (p. 209) says only that "from the north the herdsman rises" is the name of *sad*. (RD184, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Answered 2026-11-13

«སད» (*sad*) is frost that harms the crops: the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* has «རྩི་ཤིང་ལོ་ཏོག་རིགས་ལ་གྲང་ངད་ཕོག་པས་རྙིད་ཅིང་རོ་བཅུད་དང་འབྲས་བུ་མི་གྲུང་བར་འགྱུར་བའི་འཁྱགས་སྐྱོན» (the frost-harm by which cold strikes herbs, trees and the kinds of crop so that their taste and strength and fruit do not ripen), with «སད་སྐྱོན», «སད་རྒྱག་པ», «སད་ཉེན་ཆེ» (the example «མེ་ཏོག་ཚང་མ་སད་ཀྱིས་ཁྱེར་བ»); Rangjung Yeshe and Hackett: untimely frost. «སད་སེར» (*sad ser*) is "frost and hail" (Hackett, Ives-Waldo, Valby; *Sgom sde tshig mdzod chen mo*: "*sad* and *ser*; *sad* here is the illness in which the cold falls below the sky's measure, the crop is frozen stiff and cannot grow, much like English *frost*; *ser* is hail"). So «སད་འབྱུང» / «སད་འོང» is "frost comes" on the crops, and «སད་སེར» frost and hail, in the entries of pp. 199, 204 and 209.
