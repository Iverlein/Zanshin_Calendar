# What is nya stong brgyad gsum at bgs:i658 (the one-day fast of householders in 'Jigs med gling pa's own commentary Bden gnyis shing rta), and is sa chen bzhi a spelling of dus chen bzhi

From the 2026-11-21 practice-day readings (rd-f, rd-g).

## Answered 2026-11-21

Both halves are settled from other published texts; Jigs med gling pa's own page (bgs:i658) stays etext-only.

**nya stong brgyad gsum.** It is the stock phrase for "the three" fast days of a month, the full moon, the new moon and the eighth, with the nya stong («ཉ་སྟོང», nya stong) the full-moon and new-moon days and «བརྒྱད་གསུམ» (brgyad gsum) "the eighth, and the three". The question q1272 already settled this from the scan of Zhe chen rgyal tshab (zgt.1:p445-446: «ཟླ་བ་བྱུང་ངོ་ཅོག་གི་ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ», "the three of every month, the full moon, the new moon and the eighth") and from Zhabs dkar (zkt:p582), with Co ne's three good days, the eighth, the fifteenth and the new moon (dtn:i316). So in bgs:i658 «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ» is those three days; it numbers no day beyond the eighth. One caution: Mipham, in the mantra collection (Las sna tshogs pa'i sngags kyi be bum, Gsung 'bum vol. 33, etext p. 188, not read on the scan), says the Padma gling pa's Kha thun writes «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ» but "the three" are identified in various ways, and himself finds the eighth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth fitting for a dream rite; the identification is a custom, not a fixed reading.

**ས་ཆེན་བཞི (sa chen bzhi).** It is a misreading of «དུས་ཆེན་བཞི» (dus chen bzhi), the four great festivals. Two other BDRC etexts of commentaries on the same verses of the Yon tan mdzod carry the very sentence with «དུས་ཆེན་བཞི»: the Bden gnyis gsal byed zla ba'i sgron me (MW1PD100944_2B9780, "excellent quality" etext, which opens the sentence by quoting «མཛོད་འགྲེལ་ལས», "from the commentary on the Mdzod") reads «ཁྱིམ་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ་དང་། དུས་ཆེན་བཞི་གཙོ་བོར་གྱུར་པའི་དུས་ལ་བླང་ཞིང་བསྲུང་བའི་ཕྱིར་དུས་ཁྲིམས་…» (khyim pa rnams kyis nya stong brgyad gsum dang, dus chen bzhi gtso bor gyur pa'i dus la blang zhing bsrung ba'i phyir dus khrims), and the Sgo lcags 'byed byed bsdus 'grel (MW1PD100944_58F4C6; volume etext VEIE0OPI48DFFA46_I1PD106634, p. 1005) reads «ཁྱིམ་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ་དང་དུས་ཆེན་བཞི་སོགས་ལ་བསྲུང་རྒྱུའི་ཡན་ལག་བརྒྱད་པའི་དུས་ཁྲིམས་». The Gangtok print's own etext (MW1KG10193_E90C9C, "poor quality") has the same sentence word for word with «sachen bzhi» for «dus chen bzhi», so the «ས་ཆེན» is the OCR's (or the print's) spelling of «དུས་ཆེན»; Zhe chen rgyal tshab sets «དུས་ཆེན་བཞི» before «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ» for the same one-day vow (zgt.1:p445-446). The scan of the Gangtok print is too blurred to confirm the spelling syllable by syllable (bgs:i658), so the correction rests on the two commentaries that repeat the sentence.

Audit 2026-11-21 (aud-4): confirmed. Fresh BDRC phrase searches found both commentaries' sentences with «དུས་ཆེན་བཞི» (MW1PD100944_2B9780 at I1PD106632, MW1PD100944_58F4C6 at I1PD106634) and none with «ས་ཆེན་བཞི». Correction: Mipham's mantra collection is read on the scan in the pool (mgz.33:p180, vol. 33 image 188), not etext only; his note there takes the three nya as the 8th, 14th and 15th, while his notes on WB (wb.2:p509) gloss «ཉ་གསུམ» as «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ», so the identification of the three days is a custom, as the answer says.
