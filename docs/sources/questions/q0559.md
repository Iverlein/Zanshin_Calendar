# The gtong shes cited on p. 224 for the hours' avoidances

The *gtong shes* (གཏོང་ཤེས), the text WB p. 224 cites for the hours' avoidances, in which collection is it, and what does it say of the lone man with a spear?

RD169 (2026-11-07), final: dead end. BDRC's search finds the line «འཛེམ་པར་གཏོང་ཤེས་ལས་བཤད་དོ» only in WB's prints; no collection of a text named gtong shes, and nothing on the lone man with a spear.

**Searched again 2026-11-14 (inv-jjj).** «གཏོང་ཤེས་ཞེས» (4 hits): a folk-song collection («glu gtong shes zhes zer ba», "the saying that one who can speak can sing") and three studies of the Gesar epic («thang sgrung ... gtong shes zhes bkod yod pa red», the phrase used of an epic-teller who "knows how to" paint thang ga): the common verb phrase «གཏོང་ཤེས» ("knows how to send out"), no text of that name. Dead end confirmed.

## Searched again (2026-11-16, inv-e)

«གཏོང་ཤེས་ལས་བཤད» 5 hits and «གཏོང་ཤེས་ལས» 5: the 1996 print and the Gser thang reprint of WB, the Do shal copies MW1KG1617_A9DE66 and MW2PD17386_D19206 (the same line «'dzem par gtong shes las bshad do», followed there by «nyams na snang brgyad 'don pa dang / sgrib na ba thod 'phyar la sgrib»), and nothing else. No text named gtong shes. Dead end confirmed.

## Answered 2026-11-07

A dead end, stated as final by RD169 (2026-11-07, corpus/wb/ch32.md) and confirmed twice since (inv-jjj 2026-11-14, inv-e 2026-11-16): the line «འཛེམ་པར་གཏོང་ཤེས་ལས་བཤད་དོ» of WB p. 224 is found by BDRC's phrase search only in WB's two prints and the two *Do shal* copies (MW1KG1617_A9DE66, MW2PD17386_D19206), which repeat it with the lines that follow; «གཏོང་ཤེས་ཞེས» gives a folk-song collection and Gesar studies, where «གཏོང་ཤེས» is the common verb phrase "knows how to send out". No collection of a text named gtong shes was found, and nothing on the lone man with a spear.
