# Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal's «tho rengs gsum cha phyis 'byung gsal byed yin»: what do «gsum cha phyis» and «gsal byed» mean, and from what text does he take it?

Perhaps the last third of the night.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered by RD313 (2026-11-10), from the ACIP etext of Klong rdol bla ma Ngag dbang blo bzang's verse (p. 3): the night is in three thirds by the watches; dusk is the earlier habit-traces, midnight the demons' play with nothing fixed, the dawn third «phyis 'byung gsal byed», the clarifier of what arises later, and the next line says that at dawn good or bad fruit arises. The verse opens by saying it follows Ma gcig's Zab mo gcod kyi dmigs 'bum, so its source is a Chöd text. Claim st-night-midnight-to-dawn-unnamed-three-thirds-klong-rdol.
