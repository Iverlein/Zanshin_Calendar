# From which Edo or early Meiji book on does a 初夢 of 2 January stand in print

The 東都歳事記 and the 栞草 leave the 2 January night out; the 守貞謾稿, the 江戸府内絵本風俗往来 and the 諸国年中行事 are the books to ask.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14

Narrowed. The earliest print found that puts something on the night of 2 January is the 歳時記栞草: its entry 〔正月〕寶船敷 says that, although the dream from the Eve to New Year's Day is called the 初夢, "the custom of today is to lay the treasure ship on the night of the second" (今俗二日の夜に寶船をしくなり; mdz:i87533228, the 1892 reprint of the 1851 enlarged edition; the original print's leaf not compared). The first print found that calls the dream of that night the 初夢 outright is the 江戸府内絵本風俗往来 (1905, mdz:i76785615: "sleeping on the night of the second with the treasure ship under the pillow, one sees the lucky sign of the first dream"), a Meiji recollection of Edo. The 守貞謾稿 (restricted at the NDL, Iwanami reprints only) and the 諸国年中行事 (NDL OCR text for its 1939 railway-ministry edition only) were not read. No Edo book with 2 January as the 初夢 is yet in hand.
