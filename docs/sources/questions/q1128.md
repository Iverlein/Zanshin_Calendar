# From which Edo or early Meiji book on does a 初夢 of 2 January stand in print

The 東都歳事記 and the 栞草 leave the 2 January night out; the 守貞謾稿, the 江戸府内絵本風俗往来 and the 諸国年中行事 are the books to ask.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14

Narrowed. The earliest print found that puts something on the night of 2 January is the 歳時記栞草: its entry 〔正月〕寶船敷 says that, although the dream from the Eve to New Year's Day is called the 初夢, "the custom of today is to lay the treasure ship on the night of the second" (今俗二日の夜に寶船をしくなり; mdz:i87533228, the 1892 reprint of the 1851 enlarged edition; the original print's leaf not compared). The first print found that calls the dream of that night the 初夢 outright is the 江戸府内絵本風俗往来 (1905, mdz:i76785615: "sleeping on the night of the second with the treasure ship under the pillow, one sees the lucky sign of the first dream"), a Meiji recollection of Edo. The 守貞謾稿 (restricted at the NDL, Iwanami reprints only) and the 諸国年中行事 (NDL OCR text for its 1939 railway-ministry edition only) were not read. No Edo book with 2 January as the 初夢 is yet in hand.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-r)

No Edo print calls the 2 January night's dream the 初夢. Two things found: (1) the 山鹿素行先生精神訓 (pid 953845, the sayings of Yamaga Sokō, d. 1685, in a modern compilation; item 165, 石谷氏に示す, OCR image 70) records that a man's brother "on the night of 2 January dreamt" he sat in a seat above his rank, and Sokō's comment follows; the text does not call it 初夢, so it shows only that a dream of that night was noted, in an undated 17th-century saying printed in the 20th century; (2) the 松屋筆記's 「十二日の夜の夢」 (pid 2574511, image 100) is a 12th, not a 2nd (a false hit). The 守貞謾稿 and 諸国年中行事 remain unread. Unchanged: no Edo book with 2 January as the 初夢.

## Narrowed 2026-11-21 (inv-g)

New route: the NDL Lab full-text search (through the Japan Proton exit, `lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/search`) with the joined keywords 二日の夜初夢 (8 hits, all songs and folk-tale books of 1914-1943), 二日夜初夢 (5), 二日の夜初夢 (3), 二日の夜に宝船 (10), 二日の夜寶船 (6) and `fulltext-json` of the volume found. Three Edo-period witnesses that the earlier notes did not have, read in the OCR snippets and, for the first, the whole page (no image checked, so the wording is OCR):

1. The 貞丈雑記 (Ise Sadatake, 1717-84; NDL pid 2565150 manuscript copy, and the 故実叢書 reprint of 1906, pid 771945, p. 51): 「今時正月二日の夜宝船の絵を枕の下に敷事あり … 昔は節分の夜にこれを用ひし也。正月二日にはあらず」 ("nowadays one lays the treasure-ship picture under the pillow on the night of 2 January; formerly it was used on the night of the setsubun, not on 2 January"). An Edo author before 1784 who has the 2 January night's custom, but he does not call the dream the 初夢.
2. The 壺蘆圃漫筆 (anonymous manuscript miscellany, author unknown, the editor says; it holds a memorial inscription for the shogun Ienari, so it was compiled after 1841; printed in the 続随筆文学選集 第2, 1928, NDL pids 1224584 and 1883731, text page 436): 「二日の夜初夢迚宝船の繪を枕に敷も春の初の壽也、節分の夜獏の文字を枕に敷と同じ此話成べし」 ("the 'first dream' of the night of the 2nd, putting a treasure-ship picture under the pillow, is a New-Year blessing, the same story as the baku character under the pillow on the setsubun night"). This is the earliest text found that names the dream of 2 January the 初夢 outright, in a manuscript of the 1840s or later, but printed only in 1928.
3. The 馬琴's 俳諧歳時記 (1803), the parent of the 栞草: a 1943 book (雀色時, pid 1129832) quotes it under 宝船 as 「今は正月二日の夜この戯をなせり」; the 1803 print itself was not found in the NDL full text, so the line is second-hand.
Also the 世渡風俗圖會 (Shimizu Seifū's copy, Meiji, of a Tenpō-Ansei scroll; pid 2550815, p. 47) has under the treasure-ship seller 「毎年正月二日夜初夢」: a Meiji copy, not an Edo print. The 古事類苑 (1914) lists 二日夜初夢 as a heading under 初夢. Still open: a printed Edo book that says 初夢 for 2 January (the 守貞謾稿, 馬琴's 1803 print on the image, the 諸国年中行事 unread); the 貞丈雑記 and the 馬琴 are the earliest Edo witnesses of the custom, the 壺蘆圃漫筆 the earliest of the word.
