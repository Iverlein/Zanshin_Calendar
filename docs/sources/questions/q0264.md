# What the road omens of the joined hands say

The road omens that close cutting the joined hands (p. 140, ll. 27–28): meeting a person of another clan talking, or a man and woman whose line was cut, "takes hold" («ཐེབས»); a horse, donkey, widow or driven sheep is "the omen of turning back" («ལྡོག་པའི་ལ་ཉེ»); none, "wandering" («འབྱམས»). What takes hold, turns back or wanders (the cutting, the dead, the run of deaths), and is anything done after each?

**Narrowed 2026-11-05 (RD164, [reckonings.md](../reckonings.md)).** The road omen is a go-or-stop test (etext img. 249). What takes hold is likely the cutting, not stated.

**Searched 2026-11-16 (inv-x).** The whole verse lines «རབས་ཆད་ཕོ་མོ་ཕྲད་ན་ཐེབས། རྟ་བོང་ཡུགས་ས་ལུག་དེད་ཕྲད། དེ་ཚེ་ལྡོག་པའི་ལ་ཉེ་ཡིན། གང་ཡང་མ་ཕྲད་འབྱམས་པ་ཡིན» on BDRC: «རབས་ཆད་ཕོ་མོ་ཕྲད་ན་ཐེབས» 2 hits and «རྟ་བོང་ཡུགས་ས་ལུག་དེད་ཕྲད» 2 (the Vaiḍūrya dkar po, 1996 etext MW2CZ8040 and the Gser thang print MW3CN5026, nothing else), «ལྡོག་པའི་ལ་ཉེ» 3 (the same two and a Drukpa rite commentary, «phyir mi ldog pa'i la nye ba», of the irreversible stage, unrelated), «ལ་ཉེ་འབྱམས» 2 (unrelated Kālacakra and Jonang lines). No manual repeats or explains the omens. Still narrowed.
