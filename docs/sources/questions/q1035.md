# Why is the twelfth month of the Catuṣpīṭha and Vajradākatantra list called «རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་ཟླ་བ» (the Dream month)? Is it the Pleiades month (smin drug zla ba), as a modern dictionary's snippet says, and does the name say anything about dreams?

RD367.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The sources found give no reason, and the twelve names are not about dreams: they are code names. The fourth Zhwa dmar Chos kyi grags pa, in his treatise on omens (dds.26:p119-120, etext), reports the Vajraḍākatantra's list and says «their names are made in code» (དེ་ཡི་མིང་རྣམས་བརྡར་མཛད་པ): Increase, Delusion, the Good, the Bull, the Tortoise, the Crocodile, Yugs sa mo, Sme ba, 'Bugs pa, 'Phyo ba, the Dream and the Lord of the Earth, the first. The twelve names are laid on the days of the twelve months (each month's first day takes a different name, the third spring month's first day takes the Dream) and are named alike as the month, the lunar day and the instant («these are the month, the lunar day and the instant, three»). A list that holds a tortoise, a crocodile and a bull among its names is a cycle of labels; the Dream is the eleventh or twelfth of its places in the cycle, and no line in this treatise or in the Lde mig (dds.10:p38) gives the name a meaning of dreaming.

That the Dream month is the Pleiades month (སྨིན་དྲུག) rests on the dictionaries alone (svo:p395d, the Bod rgya skar rtsis rig pa'i tshig mdzod entry that names the Catuṣpīṭha as its source); this treatise does not set the Dream beside a lunar mansion. Not done: the Catuṣpīṭha itself (Derge Tanjur/Kanjur text) and its commentaries were not opened; BDRC's search for «འཕེལ་བ་གཏི་མུག་བཟང་པོ» finds the Zhwa dmar's treatise and the Dus 'khor Rtsis gter mdzod (MW30023_002912, search-only) only; a handbook that says why the Pleiades month takes the name may still exist in the tantra's commentaries.
