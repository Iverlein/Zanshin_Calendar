# «ཀླུམ་མནན» or «གྲུམ་མནན» on p. 169

On p. 169, «ཀླུམ་མནན» (Yigdzin-1) or «གྲུམ་མནན» (the etext): what is pressed down in "breath-taking, womb, body and bath, the four"?

RD165 (2026-11-06): the 1996 scan is too blurred for «ཀླུམ»/«གྲུམ»; stays a doubt.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-bbb).** BDRC's phrase search «གྲུམ་མནན» finds the 1996 etext and the Gser thang etext (both read «grum mnan»), «ཀླུམ་མནན» only the Zhol etext (I1KG1710): the split is between prints, not a misreading of one. Neither word is a headword (Steinert's 64 dictionaries have «grum» as lame, broken, crippled and «mnan» as pressed down, burial sorcery). The line sits in the beer reckoning (the strained beer finished or not; rise and decline of the body-power; «gud med», breath, womb, body and bath the four), which fits «mnan» as a pressing-down charm named with the four, but what «grum» or «klum» adds is not found. Open.

**Searched again 2026-11-16 (inv-h), nothing new.** The Steinert dictionaries (`locate.py dict`, «grum mnan», «klum mnan») have neither as a headword. The BDRC counts of inv-bbb stand.

**Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-n).** BDRC «ཀླུམ་མནན» gives one hit, which is the *Do shal* (MW1KG1617_A9DE66, poor OCR) reading «གུད་མེད་དབུགས་མངལ་ལུས་ཁྲུས་བཞི། ཀླུམ་མནན་ཐག་ཏུ་འངས་བ་ཡོད།» (in Wylie «gud med dbugs mngal lus khrus bzhi / klum mnan thag tu 'angs ba yod»): the line goes on «klum mnan» + «thag tu 'angs ba yod» (a hanging by a rope?), so «ཀླུམ» is read by two witnesses of the commentary line (Zhol and *Do shal*), «གྲུམ» by the 1996 print and the reprint; «དབུགས་ལེན་མངལ་ལུས་ཁྲུས» 0. Neither is a headword; unchanged.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (sweep from inv-bbb).** BDRC's phrase search shows the split is between prints: «གྲུམ་མནན» in the 1996 and Gser thang etexts, «ཀླུམ་མནན» in the Zhol etext and one *Do shal* copy; neither is a headword, and what is pressed down is not told.
