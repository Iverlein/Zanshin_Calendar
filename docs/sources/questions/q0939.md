# Do the commentaries on WB say that WB's eight watches of the day and night are the tantric ones of the Muktāvalī and Rang byung rdo rje?

RD314; no text says WB takes the division from the tantras.

## Answered 2026-11-13

No commentary read says so. Mipham's notes on WB (vol. 2, pp. 474-541), the regent's replies (G.ya' sel; OCR poor) and Dpal bsdus's manual hold no gloss of WB's eight watches (ཐུན་བརྒྱད, vol. 2 p. 403) or of its half-watches (ཐུན་ཕྱེད, vol. 1 p. 135) (svo:p271s); the Hevajra and Kālacakra commentaries (svo:p271o) name eight watches of the day-and-night, four by day and four by night, in their own tantric terms and nothing ties WB's wording to them. The wording WB uses (the day-and-night divided in eight, a planet and a nāga arising together at each) is the Dbyangs 'char verse's, whose half-watches are eight by day and eight by night, not the great watches of the tantras (dbyangs-char-half-watches-eight-by-day-and-eight-by-night; WB's own page p. 403 names the Mchog dga', the commentary on the Dbyangs 'char, for the fine reckoning: wb.2:p403c). Not read: the Muktāvalī itself and a commentary on Rang byung rdo rje's treatise on the watches, which would be the place for such a link.
