# WB p. 245 l. 1, the damaged line round «ཐལ་སྐྱ་བསེ་རགས་གནོད» (Yigdzin-1 ཐལ/བསེ, MITRA ཕ/པས): what does an undamaged print read?

## Answered 2026-11-14

The sense settles the line against MITRA's «པས»: «སྔོ་སྐྱ་ཐལ་སྐྱ་བསེ་རགས་གནོད» is a list of ground colours with their harm, "blue-grey, ash-grey: the bse rag harms". «བསེ་རགས» (bse rag) is a headword: "a spirit that eats the essence of food and wealth, the ghost of poverty" (Ives Waldo; Tshig mdzod chen mo), and the colour list before and after it is the same shape (the red rough ground: wounds and blood; the yellow-black dense: chronic ills). So Yigdzin-1's བསེ is right. ཐལ་སྐྱ is ash-grey (thal ba "ash", skya "grey"), a colour word beside སྔོ་སྐྱ; a word «ཕ་སྐྱ» has no headword. What an undamaged print reads at the damaged spot was not seen, but the line makes sense only with the spirit's name.

Source: tmz:box9 (read 2026-11-14), wb.2:p245. Decision by sense and headword; the print of another copy is still to be looked at for the single glyphs.
