# Is 日遊神's course outside the house reckoned in Japan

is 日遊神's course outside the house, on the forty-four days, reckoned in Japan at all? The 協紀辨方書 makes it 鶴神's, which is 天一's; the 暦林問答集 says only that it goes round the eight directions. Is there a Japanese reckoning, or is the day's 日遊神 only "in the house or not"? (RD94)

**Dead end 2026-11-05 (RD163).** No new reckoning: 和漢名數續編 joins 天一 to the 鶴神 but does not name 日遊; «日遊方» in the full-text search finds only the 方鑒精義大成, house only.

**Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z1).** The Edo handbook *Mandai dai zassho kokon taisei* (Itō Takayuki's transcription, *Media communication studies* 76, 2023-03-29, vol. 13, hdl.handle.net/2115/89040, section 天一神): 日遊神 is the 善神 under 天一神, who is Taishakuten's officer; "when 天一 goes up to heaven 日遊神 comes down to the world and takes people's houses for its own; it hates impurity, so during the sixteen days of 天一's stay in heaven (from 癸巳 to 戊申) everything must be kept pure, or the 日遊神 curses; avoid pulling down or building a house; hunting deer, hawking and fishing are avoided; when 天一 returns the 日遊神 goes up again." The same section gives 天一's forty-two days on earth by direction. So this Japanese handbook has the 日遊神 only "in the house" for 天一's sixteen days in heaven, and no course outside the house on forty-four days; its course by the eight directions is not given there. One handbook of the Edo period; the other volumes read (8, 9, 10, 12, 15 and no. 77) have no 日遊 passage.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z4)

The Japanese handbooks read give the god no outer course. The *Eireki zassho tenmon taisei kōmoku* (NDL 2543210, image 20, item 9) says that when Ten'ichi ascends, Nichiyū comes down and takes the house, and that when Ten'ichi has returned after the sixteen days "Nichiyū ascends to heaven" (日遊神天上する); the avoidances (hunting, pulling down, building) are for those sixteen days; the directions of the wheel on the facing page are Ten'ichi's. The 方位細鑑 (NDL 2541333, frames 66-67, OCR text only) has the same: a table of the god's places in the house by day, then Ten'ichi's directions. So in these books Nichiyū is in the house (in a named part of it) or in heaven, never in a named quarter outside; the second half of the question's guess holds for them. The 協紀辨方書's 鶴神 is in neither. Two books only, both undated in the NDL record; an older Japanese book with an outside course is not excluded. Passage mdz:i2543210. Dead ends: NDL Lab 日遊神八方, 日遊神四十四日, 日遊神外遊, 日遊神出遊, 日遊神東西 0 hits; 日遊神遊行 1 (the 方位細鑑); 日遊神天上 2 (that book's neighbour 永暦雑書 and the 広益秘事大全 of 1853, whose p. 27 repeats the sixteen days with no direction).

## Answered 2026-11-16

In the Japanese books read the god has no course outside the house: the *Eireki zassho tenmon taisei kōmoku* (NDL 2543210, image 20) and the 方位細鑑 (NDL 2541333) put Nichiyū in the house for the sixteen days of Ten'ichi's stay in heaven and in heaven when Ten'ichi returns, "never in a named quarter outside" (Narrowed 2026-11-16, inv-z4); the Edo handbook of inv-z1 says the same, and RD163 (2026-11-05) closed the search as a dead end. The day's 日遊神 is thus "in the house or not"; only two undated books and one Edo handbook were read, so an older Japanese book with an outside course is not excluded.
