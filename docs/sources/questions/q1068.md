# In Tilopa's Tika and the Ras chung collection the red-nosed monkey going east gives death «at the early morning of the next day» (nang par snga dro nyid du 'chi), and in 'Jig rten mgon po's questions «after three days»: which of the two is the older reading, and is «after three days» (zhag gsum nas) the Ras chung «next dawn» miscopied or a different count?

RD374.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

BDRC's exact-phrase search (2026-11-13) finds «spre'u dkar po sna dmar zhon nas shar du 'gro ba rmis na zhag gsum nas 'chi ba yin» as the word of Phag mo gru pa («dpal ldan phag mo gru pa'i zhal nas»), in the question put to him by Grags 'byung (Spyan snga Grags 'byung's fifteenth question), followed by «rtsa rlung gi gnad yin nam, spre'u dkar po sna dmar rmi ba'i rgyu mtshan ci lags zhus pas»: in the 'Jig rten mgon po gsung skor (Songtsen Library, 15 vols), the 'Bri lugs bang mdzod skor lnga and the collection of early Upper Khams masters «yul mdo khams stod kyi mkhas grub rnam pa'i gsung bcud bdud rtsi'i thigs phreng». So «after three days» is the answer the lineage reports in Phag mo gru pa's name, in four collections, and not a one-off slip for the Ras chung «next dawn». The other hits of «zhag gsum nas 'chi ba yin» (Tāranātha's Zab lam rdo rje rnal 'byor commentary and others) are a different statement, a child dying three days after birth. Which wording is older is not decided: the Tilopa Tika is dated by its attribution only, and no source read dates either reading.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-cc)

The Ras chung wording has one witness in BDRC's full-text search: «nang par snga dro nyid du 'chi» (ནང་པར་སྔ་དྲོ་ཉིད་དུ་འཆི) and «spre'u sna dmar zhon byas nas shar gyi phyogs su 'gro ba ni» occur only in the Ras chung snyan brgyud skor (MW1AC375, Mes po'i shul bzhag vols. 256-274, BDRC author Ras chung rdo rje grags pa), the text the pool already quotes (dds.15:p119-121); no other collection has either line, and the Tilopa Tika's verse form was not found copied. Persons' dates by BDRC: Ras chung pa 1085-1161, Phag mo gru pa 1110-1170, so the Ras chung collection's attribution (to Tilopa, with Ras chung's own teaching) is of the earlier man; but «after three days» is reported in Phag mo gru pa's name only through 'Jig rten mgon po (1143-1217) and the later collections, and a collection's compilation date does not date its text, so which wording is older is not decided. «Zla ba bzhi na 'chi bar 'gyur» of the donkey going south is in the same Ras chung line and in no text that has the monkey. Still open: a dated witness of either wording earlier than the collections.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-n)

New phrases. «སྤྲེའུ་དཀར་པོ་སྣ་དམར» 10 hits: besides the 'Jig rten mgon po collections (the question to Phag mo gru pa, «zhag gsum»), it is in the Ras chung snyan brgyud skor a second time, in prose, with a third wording of the time: «རྨི་ལམ་གྱི་འཆི་ལྟས་ལ། བོང་བུ་ཞོན་ནས་ཁ་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་སུ་ལྟས་ཏེ་འགྲོ་ན། ཟླ་བ་བཞི་པའི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཡིན། སྤྲེའུ་དཀར་པོ་སྣ་དམར་ཞོན་ནས་ཁ་ཤར་དུ་ལྟས་ཏེ་འགྲོ་ན་ཐོ་རངས་ཀྱི་འཆི་ལྟས་ཡིན» (the donkey going south is the omen of the fourth month, the white monkey with the red nose going east the omen of the dawn): so the Ras chung collection itself carries two wordings of the monkey's time, the verse «nang par snga dro nyid du 'chi» (next morning) and this prose «tho rangs» (dawn), which agree with each other and not with «three days». The Kun dga' rin chen collection and the commentary on Nāropa's six dharmas (Zab lam nA ro chos drug gi lhan thabs sbas don kun gsal) carry the monkey as the sign in the dream of the 'pho ba's drops («'pho ba'i thig le ni dkar cha haM nas mar 'phyug, dmar cha a nas yar 'phyug pas rmi lam du spre'u dkar po sna dmar bzhon nas…»; the snippet breaks off before any time). The Drigung mantra manual («'Bri gung pa'i man+t+ra bsnyen sgrub spyi yi spang blang») lists the monkey going east among the signs of a wish that will not be fulfilled, with no time. «སྤྲེའུ་སྣ་དམར་ཞོན་ཤར་དུ་འགྲོ» 0, «སྤྲེའུ་སྣ་དམར་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འགྲོ» 0, «ཕག་མོ་གྲུ་པ་སྤྲེའུ་དཀར་པོ» 0. Which of «dawn» (Ras chung) and «three days» (Phag mo gru pa through 'Jig rten mgon po) is older is still not decided; nothing says «three days» is a miscopy of «dawn», and the two are in the lineage's own records as different words of the two teachers.
