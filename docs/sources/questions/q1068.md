# In Tilopa's Tika and the Ras chung collection the red-nosed monkey going east gives death «at the early morning of the next day» (nang par snga dro nyid du 'chi), and in 'Jig rten mgon po's questions «after three days»: which of the two is the older reading, and is «after three days» (zhag gsum nas) the Ras chung «next dawn» miscopied or a different count?

RD374.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

BDRC's exact-phrase search (2026-11-13) finds «spre'u dkar po sna dmar zhon nas shar du 'gro ba rmis na zhag gsum nas 'chi ba yin» as the word of Phag mo gru pa («dpal ldan phag mo gru pa'i zhal nas»), in the question put to him by Grags 'byung (Spyan snga Grags 'byung's fifteenth question), followed by «rtsa rlung gi gnad yin nam, spre'u dkar po sna dmar rmi ba'i rgyu mtshan ci lags zhus pas»: in the 'Jig rten mgon po gsung skor (Songtsen Library, 15 vols), the 'Bri lugs bang mdzod skor lnga and the collection of early Upper Khams masters «yul mdo khams stod kyi mkhas grub rnam pa'i gsung bcud bdud rtsi'i thigs phreng». So «after three days» is the answer the lineage reports in Phag mo gru pa's name, in four collections, and not a one-off slip for the Ras chung «next dawn». The other hits of «zhag gsum nas 'chi ba yin» (Tāranātha's Zab lam rdo rje rnal 'byor commentary and others) are a different statement, a child dying three days after birth. Which wording is older is not decided: the Tilopa Tika is dated by its attribution only, and no source read dates either reading.
