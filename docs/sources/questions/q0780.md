# Ch. 3 p. 24 line 9: the year name «ཛ་ཆེན་ལོ»

On p. 24 line 9, after «(༡)» the print reads «ཕྲི་ཛ་ཆེན་ལོའི» («ཛ་ཆེན» as the text has it, but the name is not identified). Which year name is «ཛ་ཆེན་ལོ», and is the first syllable «ཕྲི» of the reduction? RD197c (2026-11-08).

## Answered 2026-11-14

«ཛ་ཆེན» is the print's form of the year name རྔ་ཆེན (rnga chen, "Great Drum"), the 56th year of the sixty (Dundubhi), the water male dog year. By WB's own chain the writing year is the Prabhava fire-hare year (the first of the twelfth rab byung, 1687); the passed year of line 9 is 786 − 1 = 785 before it, which is 902. 785 = 13 × 60 + 5, so it falls five places before Prabhava: the 56th place, and the stem and branch go five back from fire-hare to water-dog (the same step from the iron-bird year of 786 confirms the chain). The dictionaries give the 56th year as རྔ་ཆེན, water male dog (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives-Waldo, Tshig mdzod chen mo «(mngon) chu khyi lo»; q708), with no headword «ja chen» or «dza chen»; the White Beryl's ch. 18 list prints the same name as ཇ་ཆེན (claim ch18-printed-year-names). So ཛ and ཇ are the print's variant spellings of རྔ, and the year of Glang dar's killing in WB's count is the water-dog year. The first syllable «ཕྲི» is the reduction word, as read on the scan in RD197c. Which letter the scan itself shows (ཛ, ཇ or རྔ) is q1200's scan check.
