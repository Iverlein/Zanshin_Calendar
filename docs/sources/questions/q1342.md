# Which place and author does the list in KSR pp. 367-368 belong to (Tsa 'dra Rin chen brag or Rdzong shod), and is the Sangs gling gnas chog on the 8th a known rite

From the 2026-11-21 practice-day readings.

## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-s)

Pool: ksr:p367-368 itself says the list's place is not named in the pages read (p. 364 starts mid-list) and calls its «Sangs gling» gnas chog «the term not identified». BDRC phrase search «སངས་གླིང་གནས་མཆོག» 0 hits (a guessed spelling, not the question's «gnas chog»). The Rin chen gter mdzod catalogue lists «sangs gling bla ma dgongs 'dus» texts (MW19822_8A34D4, see q1150), so «Sangs gling» may be the Sangs rgyas gling pa's cycle, which fits the fifth month's Lama Gongdü and the tenth's guru accomplishment; no text in hand calls a gnas chog of it. Place and author unsettled. Still open.

## Answered 2026-11-21

The list belongs to Tsā 'dra Rin chen brag, Kong sprul's hermitage, not to Rdzong shod; the Sangs gling gnas chog is a known rite. Read in the BDRC etext of the volume (MW1KG14627, VEIE0OPI39993599_I1KG15643), pp. 363-368; the scans are not served, so state etext.

Place. The account of «བྱ་བ་ལས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ» (bya ba las kyi 'khor lo, the wheel of activity) begins on p. 363 with «ཡང་ཁྲོད་འདིར་ལྷ་ཁང་» (yang khrod 'dir lha khang), "here in this hermitage" a shrine, built after Mchog gyur gling pa prophesied that one was needed here, and names the eight halls: the Dpal chen lha khang, the Bla ma lha khang, the Gsung rab khang, the Mthong grol khang, the Sgrol ma khang, the Gnas brtan khang, the Thugs dam khang and the Mgon khang (p. 363). The shrines of each are listed on pp. 363-366, and p. 366 says their details stand in the catalogue, and cites the Tsā 'dra Rin chen brag catalogue's terma («ཙཱ་འདྲ་རིན་ཆེན་བྲག་གི་དཀར་ཆག་གཏེར་བྱོན་ལས»). The monthly list (p. 367) names the elders' hall and the protectors' hall (gnas brtan khang, mgon khang) of these eight, and on p. 368 the sentence on the Sangs gling gnas chog is about those «གནས་བརྟན་ཁང་དུ་སྡོད་མཁན» (gnas brtan khang du sdod mkhan), those who stay in the elders' hall. Rdzong shod is brought in only after it, as a second place: «རྫོང་ཤོད་དུའང་གུ་རུ་ལྷ་ཁང་དུ་…» (rdzong shod du'ang gu ru lha khang du …), "and at Rdzong shod, in the Guru lha khang …", with its own shrines. Elsewhere the volume names the two seats together as Tsā 'dra Rin chen brag and the Rdzong shod palace of the Bde gshegs 'dus pa (p. 106-107 of the etext's markers). Author: the pages are written about Kong sprul by a disciple who calls him «རྗེ་བླ་མ» (rje bla ma); the pages read do not name him (sources.yaml, ksr).

The rite. «སངས་གླིང་གནས་ཆོག» (sangs gling gnas chog) is a known text: BDRC lists «sangs gling gnas chog phan bde'i 'byung gnas» (MW21807_E36046), a gnas chog (the rite of the place or of abiding) of the treasure-revealer Sangs rgyas gling pa's cycle, by Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po (Gsung 'bum of Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po, PL480 collection; etext "poor quality"). That fits what the volume says: the elders' hall «ཚེས་བརྒྱད་བྱུང་ངོ་ཅོག་ལ» (tshes brgyad byung ngo cog la), on every eighth, do the Sangs gling gnas chog «ཚར་རེ་» (tshar re), once through, for the lasting of the teaching (bstan pa yun gnas). The BDRC search for the phrase «སངས་གླིང་གནས་ཆོག» finds it in this volume and as that title only.
