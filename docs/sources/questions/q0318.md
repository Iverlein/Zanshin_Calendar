# A speckled deciding pebble in the household round

In the household round a member brings four deciding pebbles (WB vol. 1, p. 385; the notes, p. 490), and a deciding pebble of the year of age may be speckled where a place's white and black are even (the notes, p. 490, «མཉམ་ན་ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག»). Does a speckled deciding pebble enter the round as a speckled pebble, and how is it read in a place whose readings (pp. 385–387) name white, black, blue, green, yellow and red only? The *Moonbeams* lays the member's pebbles "white or black as in general" (DZ f. 22b) and so has no speckled one.

**Narrowed 2026-10-25 ([claims/household-round-colours.md](../claims/household-round-colours.md)).** No source read lays or reads a speckled pebble in the household round: the regent's question and reply 123 (ys:i096, ys:i478-479, on the scan) only defend the round as Phugpa's, from the *Clear Lamp*; LT makes each of a member's four a single plain meeting, white or black, the trigram and mewa pebbles in their own colours (lt:p20-25); Zhechen Gyaltsab lays a speckled verdict for a tied year-of-age place and then names none in the round (zg:p285-286); the Bon *Mirror* (a witness only) and KD's digest none. Blue, green, yellow and red come only from the mewa (and LT's trigram) pebbles. Only the notes' own round can bring a speckled member pebble, and how it reads in a place is not said.

**Stated choice, 2026-11-05 (ROADMAP R9, SPEC §5.9.10):** a speckled deciding pebble enters the round speckled, and the screen says it has no line; it counts for neither side of the household's white and black. Open for a reader of the tradition.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-n, the notes' own line read in its sentence)

The speckled pebble of the notes is read in its sentence (Gser thang reprint etext VEIE0OPI3864355D_I3CN5029, [p496], printed p. 489-490, offset 1158087; BDRC exact phrase «མཉམ་ན་ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག» 1 hit, the reprint only; «ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག་པ» 16 hits, all other texts): «ཁ་དམར་ནི་མ་འཕྲད་དཀར་གསུམ་ལྟ་བུའང་གཅིག་ལ་བ། དགྲ་འཕྲད་ནག་གཉིས་ཀྱང་སོ་སོར་མི་བརྩི་བར་གནས་དེའི་དཀར་ནག་བསྐྲངས་ལ་དཀར་མར་དཀར་པོ། ནག་མར་ནག་པོ། མཉམ་ན་ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག བླ་རྡེལ་དྲུག་ནི་བླ་ཞེས་སྐབས་འདིར་བརྩི་བྱའི་སྐྱེས་སྦེ་རེས།» (the meeting-friend's three whites and the meeting-enemy's two blacks are not counted one by one: the place's whites and blacks are set against each other, white if there are more white, black if more black, and if even, speckled is laid; the six la pebbles are here the reckoned one's birth mewa each). So the speckled pebble is the verdict of one place whose whites and blacks cancel, not a colour added to the sums, and the sentence goes on at once to the six la pebbles and then, after a break, to «རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར་ནི་བཟའ་ཚང་སྤྱི་ལ་བརྩི» (the household round), which begins with the members' four deciding pebbles. How a place with a speckled verdict is read when a member's pebble is laid on it is not said; the sentence only says the tied place is marked, which agrees with the stated choice (neither side) and does not settle it. Still open for a reader of the tradition.
