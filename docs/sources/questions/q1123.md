# Is the dream of habit in Paṇḍa pa's question the dream of the previous night or the dream of the evening in the song's rule

The Mgur mtsho and the Life give the song as the answer to a question about a dream of bag chags; which dream is meant?

## Answered 2026-11-16

Answered 2026-11-16 (inv-w), from the Life's own page before the question.

The «dream of habit» of Paṇḍa pa's question is the dream Marpa had told the company a moment before, the one of Maitrīpa coming on a lion, and it is the dream of the night just past, not the dream of the evening of the song's rule. In the song that precedes the three watches (rrd.26:p87, Thrangu print of the Life, etext) Marpa says «མདང་བག་ཆགས་གཉིད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་ན། རྗེ་མངའ་བདག་རྒྱལ་སྲས་མཻ་ཏྲྀ་པ། ཁོང་སེང་གེ་ཆིབས་ནས་མཁའ་ལ་གཤེགས།» «last night (mdang), in the dream of the habit of sleep, the Lord Mnga' bdag Maitrīpa came on a lion through the sky», has the three symbols of the unborn shown to him, and then «ནམ་ནངས་གཉིད་དེ་སད་ཙ་ན» «at dawn, when I woke from sleep», remembers him; he closes the song with «ལར་རྨི་ལམ་བག་ཆགས་ཡིན་མོད་ཀྱང» «though in general a dream is habit, that the Lord came is a great wonder». Paṇḍa pa's question (rrd.26:p88, and the Mgur mtsho copy rrd.27:p102) takes Marpa's own words: «བུ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་བག་ཆགས་རྨི་ལམ་ན། རྗེ་མངའ་བདག་ཆེན་པོ་བྱོན་ནས་ཀྱང»: so «bag chags» there is Marpa's modest name for that one dream, said before any sorting of the watches, and not the evening dream of «ནམ་སྲོད་ལ་བག་ཆགས་རྨི་ལམ་འབྱུང».

What the song then does with the dream is a separate matter: it sorts the watches (evening habit, midnight Māra's display, dawn the gods' prophecy) and places the coming of Maitrīpa in «དགུང་ད་ནང་སྐྱ་རེངས», the deep dawn (rrd.26:p88-90), so by the song's own rule the dream is not a habit dream although Marpa called it so when he told it. The earlier dream of the same song, the woman in leaves, is the one set at «ནམ་ཐོ་རངས», dim dawn.
