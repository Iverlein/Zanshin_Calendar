# Is the boxed item of the Tokyo Gakugei Daizassho (1780, image 24, beginning with the character for dream) the charm for a bad dream, and does it say anything of a day on which the dream is not told

About ten columns, read in single glyphs only; by its placement and length it looks like a charm.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-vvv)

The page was fetched at full size (kokusho.nijl.ac.jp, IIIF manifest `/biblio/100408974/manifest`, image 24 = `TKGK-52334-00024.tif`, 4992 × 3328 px) and the box enlarged 2-3×. It is the top right of the right-hand page, under the picture of a man at a shrine gate among pines, and has five or six columns, not ten, with the small item number 百十七 above it. By eye: the heading column begins «夢あ…» (the character for dream, then a hiragana run that reads «あし» or «あま», not settled) and goes on «…大きに…よし…七へん…»; further columns carry «…けんそう…» and end «…のちひ…» (cursive hiragana). A «seven times» (七へん, 七遍) stands in the first column, so it is a recited charm or rule, as the placement suggested. Neither a day on which the dream is not told nor the word 語 or 告 was seen; the cursive columns are not read through, and the white scratch hides the second column's middle. The kana is the question for a reader of Edo cursive.
