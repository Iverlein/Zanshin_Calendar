# Is the serpent's place for the first stroke reckoned by the day's mansion or by the season?

*A General Account of Tibet* (BSB) says the serpent's body stretches, curls and turns "with the course of the planets and mansions" (pp. 435–436): is the place of the first stroke reckoned by the day's mansion, as Chos kyi grags pa's serpent walks one cell a day, or only by the season, as Longchenpa and Sādhuputra give it?

**Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-b).** The place of the first stroke is the serpent's armpit (མཆན་ཁུང, mchan khung), and the texts that give a rule for where the armpit lies give a calendar, not the mansion: Padma dkar po's ground rite walks the serpent one cell a house-day (claim ch32-serpent-walk-by-house-day, wb.2:p282's section). BDRC's phrase search for «ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་མཆན་ཁུང» gives 4 hits: the *Bod spyi bshad* itself (bsb:p435-436), the Sakya biography of Kun dga' rnam rgyal (MW15269, I1KG5126 pp. 125-126, read: the rite of the fourth examination, the Lto-'phye examination, «ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་མཆན་ཁུང་ནས་ས་རྐོ་བ», digging the ground from the serpent's armpit, at the founding of a temple, no rule of place), the Sa skya chos mdzod chen mo (the same sentence, search only) and the *Sngags rim blo sbyong* (MW1PD95727, search only: «ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་མཆན་ཁུང་ཐིགས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་དུ། ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་བརྟག་པའི་ཐིག་བཏབ་སྟེ», the armpit is fixed by drawing the serpent's examination line). «ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་རྒྱུ་སྐར» 0, «ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་ཁྱིམ་ཞག» 0, «ལྟོ་འཕྱེའི་དབུ་ཤར་ཕྱོགས» 0. So no text found ties the armpit to a mansion; the BSB's «with the planets and mansions» stays unconfirmed, and the answer found is by the season refined to the house-day, from Padma dkar po's etext alone, not read on a scan.
