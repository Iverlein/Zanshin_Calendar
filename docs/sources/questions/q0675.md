# p. 435's «ཉྭ་བཞིན» in WB vol. 1

What is the «ཉྭ་བཞིན» of p. 435 (every print has a ཉ-headed stack, with or without wa-zur): a hat-like thing, or a word a reader of the tradition knows? (RD175 third run, corpus/wb/ch24.md; replaces the earlier question on the Zhol print's ཉྭ/ཞྭ.)

INV-E (2026-11-14), narrowed: BDRC full-text search for «ཉྭ་བཞིན», «ཉ་བཞིན་ལ་ཁ་ནད» and «ཁ་ནད་ལྕེར་རླུང་འབྲུམ» finds nothing; «ཁ་ནད་ལྕེར» finds only WB itself (the 1996 etext I1KG12906 and the reprint I3CN5028), so no parallel text carries the line. The line's sense is of the fever of fire four and earth one («ཁ་ནད་ལྕེར», mouth illness at the tongue; «རླུང་འབྲུམ་དམར་ཙག», wind pox and red spots), and p. 436 has «ཉ་དང་ཚིགས་བཞི་ན་བ» with the same head and no wa-zur; so the word is a body part or sign of the fever rather than a hat, but no source glosses it. Steinert's dictionaries were not searched (a 4.5 GB clone; the web edition is a single-page app that a fetch cannot read).

## Answered 2026-11-14

The word is ཉྭ (nywa), "the calf of the leg, the muscle of a limb", and the line reads «ཉྭ་བཞི་ན་ལ», not «ཉྭ་བཞིན» with the particle ན fused to བཞི. The Gser thang reprint prints the syllables apart, «ཉ་བཞི་ན་ལ་ཁ་ནད་ལྕེར། རླུང་འབྲུམ་དམར་ཙག་ཅན་དུ་འོང་» (wb.1:p435b), and the Zhol and Taikhang prints, whose letters were read as ཉྭ་བཞིན, carry the same syllables with the wa-zur. The Great Tibetan-Chinese Dictionary has the headword nywa: "the calf of a person's leg" and, of beasts, the muscle from the knee to the hoof; «nywa bzhi» is "the four limb-muscles" (rkang lag gi nywa ril bzhi), glossed by Dan Martin "muscles of legs and arms"; the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs adds nywa log, an illness in which the calf's flesh swells. «ན» is the verb "to ache", as in the neighbouring fever lines on p. 436 (མགོ་ན, "the head aches"). So the sign of the fever of fire four and earth one is: the four limb-muscles ache, the mouth is sore at the tongue, wind pox and red spots come. It is no hat (ཞྭ) and the reading «ཉྭ་བཞིན» (like) is not needed.

Source: tmz:box6 (Steinert's public dictionary set, nos. 25, 37, 09, read 2026-11-14; a lexicon, not a commentary on WB). That the four muscles are those of the limbs is the dictionary's word; no commentary on WB was found that names the muscles in this fever.
