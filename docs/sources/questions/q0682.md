# «ཞོད» and «གཤོངས» in the sixty years' results

What do «ཞོད» (*zhod*) and «གཤོངས» (*gshongs*) mean in «ནམ་སྟོད་ཐན་ལ་ནམ་སྨད་ཞོད» ("the upper sky drought, the lower sky *zhod*") and «གཤོངས་ཞིང་འཕམ»? They recur in nearly every entry of pp. 198–212 and are left in Wylie in the English. (RD184, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Answered 2026-11-13

«ཞོད» (*zhod*) is heavy rain: the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* gives «ཆར་ཆུའི་རྒྱུན» (a stream of rain water; «ཆར་ཞོད་ཆེ་བའི་དུས», a time of heavy rain), the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* «ཆར་བ་ཆེན་པོའི་མིང» (name of a great rain; «ད་ལོ་ཆར་ཞོད་ཆེ»), Negi's Sanskrit dictionary «char zhod» = *varṣa*. So «ནམ་སྟོད་ཐན་ལ་ནམ་སྨད་ཞོད» is "the upper sky drought, the lower sky heavy rain" (early in the year dry, later wet), as the entries' own "early drought, later *zhod*" has it. «གཤོངས» (*gshongs*) in «གཤོངས་ཞིང» is the valley: «གཤོངས་ས», «ལྗོངས», «སྤང་གཤོངས» (*Tshig mdzod chen mo*); Ives-Waldo and Valby: valley, basin, low place, deep valley. «གཤོངས་ཞིང» is then the valley field, the pair of the mountain field «རི་ཞིང» (*ri zhing*, "the field on the face of a mountain", ibid.) with which it contrasts in p. 209 («རི་ཞིང་རྒྱལ་ལ་གཤོངས་ཞིང་འཕམ», the mountain field wins, the valley field is defeated); read thus, «གཤོངས་ཞིང་འཕམ» is "the valley field is defeated". Where it stands in other entries (the Conqueror year: «གཤོངས་ཞིང་ནས་མི་འབྱིན», the barley is not brought out of the valley field) the same sense fits; no dictionary gives it a separate meaning.
