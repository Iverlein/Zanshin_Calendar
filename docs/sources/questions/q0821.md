# ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ (Serthang) or ལྡེར་སོ་འགུལ (Drathang, machine) in ST ch. 81: which is printed, and what is ལྡེར?

Scan reads བཟོ; Drathang machine text has སོ; Drathang not resolved by eye at 2669 px.

Narrowed (RD264, 2026-11-09): the letters are settled by tradition. The Serthang scan (boxed བ and ཟ) and the 2005 Supplement's scan (typeset) read བཟོ; the Bhutan print's scan (img. 267, one glyph ས with ོ, no separate བ) reads སོ, as the Drathang print's machine reading does. What ལྡེར is stays open: no text or scan gives a sense for it alone; Khro ru's gloss is «a temple shaking» for the three words (claim st-lhas-tshor-lder-bzo).

## Answered 2026-11-09

ལྡེར་བཟོ (lder bzo) is a stock word for a clay statue (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, Dan Martin, Sgom sde), so ལྡེར་བཟོ་འགུལ་ is "the temple's clay statues move"; ལྡེར་སོ (lder so), the Bhutan and Drathang prints' spelling, is a known spelling of the same word (242 hits in BDRC's search; Kongtrul's treasury and others), not a different word. Khro ru's "a temple shaking" takes the three words as one. See the claim st-lder-bzo-is-the-temples-clay-statues.
