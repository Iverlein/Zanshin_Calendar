# The day's animal or the birth's in the twelve, pp. 428–429

Pp. 428–429's twelve animals are headed «ཉི་མའི་ནད་དང་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང», the day's illness: is the animal that of the day of falling ill, or the sick person's birth year (RD12's outline read it as the birth animal)?

**Narrowed 2026-10-25 (RD52, [claims/illness-day-animal-of-onset.md](../claims/illness-day-animal-of-onset.md)).** The animals of pp. 428–429 are those of the day of falling ill, by inference: WB p. 450 examines the time of onset first, the p. 431 mewa is the one under which one fell ill, reply 133 says the four times are those of the onset, and the notes' date, weekday and mansion are those of the day of falling ill; KD vol. 3 p. 701 (etext) copies WB's twelve animals under the same heading. No passage reads them by the birth year, so RD12's outline is unsupported.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The animals of pp. 428-429 are those of the day of falling ill ([claims/illness-day-animal-of-onset.md](../claims/illness-day-animal-of-onset.md): four converging readings, no passage reads them by the birth year). One more witness from the notes, read 2026-11-14 (inv-ccc) in wb.2:p496: «བབ་སྤར་ན་ཉིན་ཉི་མ་བབ་སྨེ་བཅས་ཀྱིས་ནད་གདོན་ངོས་བཟུང་ངོ་»: the illness and spirit are identified "by the descending trigram, the day (ཉིན་ཉི་མ), and the descending mewa", the same three of the time of falling ill that WB's section takes in turn (the trigram, pp. 427-428; the day, pp. 428-429; the mewa, p. 431), and the notes' p. 497 then names the year, month, day and hour of falling ill («ནམ་ན་བའི་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས») as the times that identify the illness. The "day" of the heading is the day, not the birth year. An inference from the structure and not a sentence that says "the day of falling ill"; the birth animal is read elsewhere (the ransoms by the sick person's year, pp. 429-431).
