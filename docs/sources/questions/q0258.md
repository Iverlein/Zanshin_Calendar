# The household round for a household other than five

The household round (རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར, rgya nag rdel skor; WB vol. 1, pp. 385–387) takes each member's four deciding pebbles and the mewa of the year, five to a member, and takes them up "the upper one's body with the lower one's element" in a zigzag, then folds the row. The notes lay it out only for five members (vol. 2, pp. 490–491, read cell by cell): the eldest man's body 1, the youngest's mewa 2, the outer pair first, then the next pair, the middle member last, with the vitality and body rows exchanged; twenty-five pebbles fold into four rows of six with one left over. For a household of four, six or more, how does the zigzag run where there is no middle member, and how do the fold and the refold fall when the count is not twenty-five (the notes' rule of dividing by three gives the leftovers, not the rows)? Does a member whose deciding pebble is speckled (a tie, open question 167) give a speckled pebble to the round? Wanted before the round can be built for any household (ROADMAP R4, the year of age's Together).

**Narrowed 2026-10-23 (RD43, [corpus/wb/notes-2.md](../corpus/wb/notes-2.md), [corpus/wb/ch24.md](../corpus/wb/ch24.md)).** The zigzag: all members' columns laid end to end (men eldest first, then women; each column body, life force, power, luck, mewa) and taken alternately from the two ends reproduces all 25 cells of the notes' five, the middle member's 21–25 included, and does not depend on the count: a rule worked out from the table, not stated ([claims/household-round-zigzag.md](../claims/household-round-zigzag.md)). The fold in half and the refold in half give four rows of ⌊5N/4⌋ with the remainder of 5N by four left over, three members too few, as the notes' "not fewer than four" ([claims/household-round-fold.md](../claims/household-round-fold.md)); the print's "divide by three" is question 317. A member brings the four deciding pebbles of its year of age and a pebble of its mewa's colour; the *Moonbeams*' plain meetings are the shortcut the notes reject (p. 491). Still open: a speckled member's pebble (question 318).


**Narrowed 2026-10-25 (question 317, [claims/household-round-fold.md](../claims/household-round-fold.md)).** The division is read as by four: «གསུམ» stands in every print of the notes, Mipham's own included, but every witness to the operation divides by four or counts by the folds (the *Good Vase*'s verse the notes correct, LT, Zhechen Gyaltsab). The manuals (LT, the *Good Vase*) lay the four rows as quarter-runs one after another, the notes fold in half twice: the two coincide when one pebble is left (25) and part when two or three are, which is the difference the notes name; by rank the notes' way stands. LT limits the round to four members at the least with five pebbles each, three with six (question 397). The *Spor thang* sheet of seven members (kd.3:p726, etext only; question 398) would show a count other than five laid out. Still open: the speckled pebble (question 318, narrowed: no source lays one).

**A count other than five laid out, 2026-10-25 (RD54, question 398).** The *Spor thang* sheet of seven members (KD vol. 3, p. 726, on the lent scan) runs its 35 pebbles in quarters of eight with three over and no speckled pebble: the quarter-runs and the division by four of the manuals, for seven. Which of the three is the fold's and which the refold's is not legible at the scan's size.
