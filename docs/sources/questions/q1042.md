## Searched 2026-11-14 (inv-yyy), narrowed by the root

The Derge etext of the Dbyangs 'char (VE23703_1521, fetched whole) was read around the dream verse and in the chapter before it. (1) The dream rule is the root's own: «རྨི་ལམ་རྨི་བའི་དུས་བསྟན་པ། ཏད་ཀཱལ་ཆ་ཤེས་སུམ་ཅུ་ཡི། རང་བཞིན་ཡིན་པས་དེ་ལ་བལྟ། ཆ་ཤས་བཅུ་ཤར་སུམ་ཆ་ན། བཅོ་ལྔ་ཤར་ན་ནམ་ཕྱེད་ན། སུམ་ཅུ་ཡོད་ན་ཐོ་རངས་ཏེ» ([p122]; the exact phrases «ཏད་ཀཱལ་ཆ་ཤེས་སུམ་ཅུ» and «ཆ་ཤས་བཅུ་ཤར་སུམ་ཆ» have one hit each in BDRC, this etext, offset 198106 of the search index). It names no start of the night and no night. (2) The root defines what the thirty parts are in the chapter on the junctions ([p110]-[p112]): a day holds twelve junctions («ཞག་གཅིག་ལ་ནི་བཅུ་གཉིས་ཏེ», [p110]), and the kinds of junction are the one, the half, the third, the ninth, the twelfth «and the particulars of the thirty parts» («སུམ་ཅུའི་ཆ་ཤས་བྱེ་བྲག་རྣམས། ཤེས་པར་གྱིས་ཤིག», [p110]); the part itself is computed from the remainder of the rising sign: «ཕྲི་བའི་ལྷག་མ་སུམ་ཅུས་བསྒྱུར། དེ་ལ་ཏད་ཀལ་ཆུ་སང་གིས་བགོས་པས་ཐོབ་པ་ཆ་ཤས་ཡིན» ([p112]), "the remainder of the subtraction is multiplied by thirty, divided by the tattāla's water-srang; what is obtained is the part", and the remainder multiplied by sixty gives in turn the staff, the water-srang and the breath. So in the root the thirty parts are thirtieths of the span of the sign that is rising at the moment (the same chain of units as the five places), not thirtieths of a night counted from some start; the dream verse lays that number beside the night («a third», «midnight», «dawn») without saying why. That is the answer to the second clause only as far as the root goes: the Mchog dga' reads a part of the junction as a part of the night because the root's verse does, and neither gives a ground. The start of the night and the night meant stay open; no commentator was reached.
