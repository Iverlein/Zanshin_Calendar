# Tsuda's Sanskrit of the Saṃvarodaya 19-23 ends «yamadarśanam» («to the sight of Yama») where the Derge Kanjur, the fourth Zhwa dmar's treatise and the Deceiving Death (cbs:p247-249) have «lto bar» («belly»): is «gshin rje'i lto bar» a misreading of an earlier «lta bar» in all the prints, or is the belly of Yama a reading a commentator of the tradition knows (and which Tibetan commentary on the Sdom 'byung explains the line)?

RD382.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-ds)

No Tibetan commentary explains the line. The Derge Padminī (Ratnarakṣita on the Saṃvarodaya, Toh 1420, etext VE23703_1337) comments chapter 19 only on deceiving death and the transference (pp. 142-145, no death-sign verse quoted; the Dpe bsdur ma copy answers 403). BDRC exact phrases «གཤིན་རྗེའི་ལྟོ་བར་འགྲོ་བར་འགྱུར» two hits (the Derge Kanjur, the fourth Zhwa dmar), «གཤིན་རྗེའི་ལྟ་བར» none. What bears on it: 'Deceiving Death' 1.128 has in Sanskrit «yāty asau yamamaṇḍalam» and in Tibetan «gshin rje'i gnas su 'gro» (place), Tsong kha pa's quotation «gshin rje ru ni 'gro bar bstan» (shown going to Yama's), the Sanskrit of the Saṃvarodaya «gacchate yamadarśanam»: neither witness reads a belly. «Bstan» there fits darśana, and «gshin rje lta bar 'gro bar 'gyur» («goes to see Yama») would be the word-for-word Tibetan; so «lto bar» in the Derge Kanjur and the Zhwa dmar's copy is most likely a corruption of «lta bar» (Tsuda's emendation), but no print or commentary was seen that reads «lta bar», and the Kanjur scan is too faint to read (the pool's earlier note).
