# WB p. 245, «གཅོང་» or «གཙོང་» (lines 2 and 18, «ནག་སྟུག་པ་གཅོང་ཅན་མང་», «ཅན་གཅོང་དང་དམུ་ཆུ»): the hook of ཅ/ཙ is lost under the vowel on the reprint; which word is meant?

## Answered 2026-11-14

It is གཅོང (gcong). The Tshig mdzod chen mo has the headword gcong: "(1) an illness hard to cure, a chronic one (consumption); (2) a cliff or ravine"; Ives Waldo the same; and the compounds «glo gcong» (a chronic lung illness, one of the eight lung illnesses) and «dmu chu» (dropsy, "one of the six chronic diseases", Valby: gcong chen nad drug). A word «གཙོང» has no headword. The two lines then read: «སེར་ནག་སྟུག་པ་གཅོང་ཅན་མང» (p. 245 l. 2) "yellow-black and dense [ground]: many chronic ills", and «སྐྱོན་ཅན་གཅོང་དང་དམུ་ཆུ་འོང» (p. 245, in the list of bad waters) "the fault: chronic illness and dropsy come": gcong beside dmu chu, the pair that WB writes again as «གློ་གཅོང་དམུ་ཆུ» (pp. 250 and 254), and «གཅོང་ནད» "chronic illness" stands in the list of bad colours ("yellow: the colour of bile; chronic and kidney illnesses are many", p. 244). The hook of ཅ against ཙ was not looked at again; the choice is by the headword and by the stock company of the word.
