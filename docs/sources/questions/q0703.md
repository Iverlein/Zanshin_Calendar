# «བསྐལ་ལི» (bskal li) of p. 70

What is the «བསྐལ་ལི» (bskal li) whose table opens the chapter on p. 70 and whose last half-circle is taken on p. 72: a reckoning of the Kali age, a person, or a name in the Phug lore? (RD189, corpus/wb/ch10.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

«བསྐལ་ལི» (bskal li) is a calendar word, not a person and not a reckoning of the Kali age: the Tshig mdzod chen mo and the Tshig gter rgya mtsho give «bskal li» as "a li tho (almanac) that serves for many years" (Ives Waldo: "li tho good for many years"). The table the chapter opens with is then the long-run table, and its own headword is there: «bskal li'i re'u mig» is "the name of the table of the enjoyment of the three hundred and one lunar days, to be added to the general wealth (spyi nor) of the six houses" (Tshig mdzod chen mo; the Tshig gter has "shown" for "added"; Ives Waldo: "table showing 371 coursing six houses, khyim drug spyi nor to be added"). That is exactly what pp. 70-72 build: the unit tables of hour, hole, arrow, water-weight, fire, breath, sea and part, the lunar-day circle of 301 (the verse's ༣༠༡), the six houses with wealth and debt, and on p. 72 «བསྐལ་ལིའི་ཕྱེད་དག་ཐ་མ» "the last of the pure halves of the bskal li". So the term names the basis table whose lunar-day circle is 301 days; the dictionaries do not say why it is called bskal (an age): the long span it serves is the reason they give.

Source: tmz:box9 (read 2026-11-14), wb.1:p70 and wb.1:p72 for the lines. The Ives Waldo figure 371 against WB's 301 is the dictionary's own and stays unexplained (WB prints 301).
