# In Negelein's manuscript i (Leipzig 1178) 'dvitīye cāṣṭame māse triyāme ca tripakṣake' has three fortnights for the third watch: does the Leipzig manuscript itself read tripakṣake, or is it Negelein's emendation of trimāsake?

No open copy read has it.


Narrowed (inv-i, 2026-11-13): Negelein's text of manuscript i prints «dvitīye cā'ṣṭame māse triyāme ca tripakṣake» with no «(!)» or «sic» on the word, where he marks other corruptions of the same lines with «(!)» (neg-p14-16; the Internet Archive text layer of the book read again, 2026-11-13); his parallel s (Adbhutasāgara) reads «tribhir māsais triyāmikāḥ». The Leipzig manuscript itself: a search of the Leipzig library's manuscript portal (mymssportal) for the Aufrecht 1901 number 1178 found no record; nothing shows whether «tripakṣake» is the manuscript's or the editor's.

Narrowed (inv-rrr, 2026-11-14): Aufrecht's 1901 catalogue of the Leipzig Sanskrit manuscripts (Internet Archive in.ernet.dli.2015.313499, printed pp. 368-370, read on the page image n369, 770 px wide; the text layer is noise) describes no. 1178: «Svapnādhyāya in 88 Ślokas», a bad manuscript of about 1830 ("Schlechte Handschrift von etwa 1830"), and quotes only its end (v. 87-88, «etatpuṇyaṃ ca pāpaghnaṃ … guruktaṃ» with Bṛhaspati as the teacher). It quotes nothing of the watch verse. So the manuscript is about 1830 and the catalogue cannot decide the word. The question stays open for the word itself: the manuscript is not online that the pool found (no image, no mymssportal record).
