# The year Aṅgiras: ཨདྒིར or ཨངྒིར (entry 9, p. 206)

ཨདྒིར or ཨངྒིར as the print's name of the year Aṅgiras (entry 9, p. 206, line 16)? The scan's second letter is a stack whose first member could be ད or ང; MITRA read ཨངྐིར; ch. 13 has ཨངྒི་ར. (RD190, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-13 (inv-x)

BDRC full-text search (the White Beryl etexts): «ཨངྒིར་ཞེས་ཆུ་ཕོ» 5 hits, among them the Gser thang etext (MW3CN5026, VEIE0OPI3864355D_I3CN5028, the volume of the print, char 335972 of the volume), where the line reads «ཨངྒིར་ཞེས་ཆུ་ཕོ་སྤྲེལ»; «ཨདྒིར་ཞེས་ཆུ་ཕོ» 0 hits. Other texts write the year's name as «ཨངྒིར» (ang+gir) or «ཨངྐི་ར» (3 hits, other works). So the etext of the same print has ང over ག, as ch. 13 does; an OCR reading, not a scan: the question stays open until the stack is seen on img. 205 at 6×.

## Answered 2026-11-14

## Answered 2026-11-14

The year's name is ཨངྒིར (*ang gir*), not ཨདྒིར: Rangjung Yeshe and Ives-Waldo have the headword «ang gir» "Aṅgiras, the sixth year of the sixty, the water male monkey year" (Ives-Waldo also «ang gi ra»), and the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* has «ang gi ra» «(legs) chu pho sprel lo»; no dictionary of Steinert's 64 has a headword «ad gir». The same print's etext (Gser thang, MW3CN5026) has ཨངྒིར, as ch. 13 does (the BDRC search of 2026-11-13). The pool's ཨདྒིར in entry 9 is therefore to be read ཨངྒིར. What the scan shows was not decided: on img. 205 (p. 206) line 16 at 8× the stack over the ར is a tall three-tier stack (a gigu over a head with a closed loop, a flat middle member, a lower member with a descender), which the 2040 px frame does not resolve into ད or ང over ག; the answer rests on the dictionaries and the etexts, not on that stack.

Source: Steinert's dictionaries (RangjungYeshe, IvesWaldo, tshig-mdzod-chen-mo-Tib; sparse clone of the public folder, read 2026-11-14), wb.1:p206.
