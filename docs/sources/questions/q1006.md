# In the rite of killing of the oath-bound great man (Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38) the god «who comes alongside» and the demon «born together» are the two attendants of the enemy; is this the use LGD p. 970 follows, so that «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» is the person's attendant demon and not Chöd's self-clinging?

RD355.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

Narrowed, not settled (etext, 2026-11-13). In the rite of killing of the oath-bound Great Man (Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38, etext p13, lgd.38:p13) the enemy aimed at is parted from «the god who comes alongside» and «the demon born together» turns the oath-bound's messenger; a second rite (Tshe sgrub gsang ba kun 'dus, snippet) hands the victim over «to the demon who comes alongside». So in the rites the pair are the victim's own attendants, and LGD p. 970 pairs «the god born together with oneself» with «the demon who comes alongside» in the same stock; the words are crossed between the texts. Chöd's gloss (the same demon as one's co-born ignorance) is the companion seen from within and does not exclude this. Whether LGD means the attendant of a person or the self-clinging stays open; claim lgd-companion-god-and-demon-in-the-oath-bound-rite-are-the-victims-attendants.
