# In the rite of killing of the oath-bound great man (Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38) the god «who comes alongside» and the demon «born together» are the two attendants of the enemy; is this the use LGD p. 970 follows, so that «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» is the person's attendant demon and not Chöd's self-clinging?

RD355.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

Narrowed, not settled (etext, 2026-11-13). In the rite of killing of the oath-bound Great Man (Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38, etext p13, lgd.38:p13) the enemy aimed at is parted from «the god who comes alongside» and «the demon born together» turns the oath-bound's messenger; a second rite (Tshe sgrub gsang ba kun 'dus, snippet) hands the victim over «to the demon who comes alongside». So in the rites the pair are the victim's own attendants, and LGD p. 970 pairs «the god born together with oneself» with «the demon who comes alongside» in the same stock; the words are crossed between the texts. Chöd's gloss (the same demon as one's co-born ignorance) is the companion seen from within and does not exclude this. Whether LGD means the attendant of a person or the self-clinging stays open; claim lgd-companion-god-and-demon-in-the-oath-bound-rite-are-the-victims-attendants.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-n)

Two more texts carry the pair. Karma Chags med (1613-1678), in the selected instructions of his collected works («nyer mkho phyogs bsgrigs»), in a rite against an enemy: «དེའི་སྟེང་དུ་ཁོའི་ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ་དང་། འོག་ཏུ་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ངོ་བོ་སྡིག་པ་དམར་ནག་གཉིས་…ཀྱིས་བཅིང་» (over it, his co-born god and, under it, the demon who comes alongside, the two being in essence the red and the black sin, are bound): the pair there is the enemy's («ཁོའི», his), again his attendants. And a volume of Kong sprul's Lce btsun Bi ma la'i zab tig (the Bla rung print, search-only snippet): «སྔར་ནས་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ་ལྟར་མཉམ་འགྲོགས་ཀྱི་ཚུལ་དུ་བྱུང་བ་ཞིག་ཡིན་པས་ན་བདག་ཉིད་གཅིག་པ་དང་ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་…» (as «the demon who comes alongside» is called, it arises in the manner of a companion, so it is one with oneself and co-born): there it is a companion in one's own stream, in the Great Perfection's sense, with no victim. BDRC phrases: «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» 68 hits (rites of «phyes» and «sprod» in the Rin chen gter mdzod, the Se ra mkha' 'gro, Karma Chags med, Kong sprul), «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་ལྷ» 1 (the Rin chen gter mdzod, with the demon as the co-born, the words crossed as in the oath-bound rite). So the rites use the pair as the object's own attendants and the Great Perfection use of Kong sprul reads the demon as the companion of the self; whether LGD p. 970's dream list means the one or the other is not said by LGD. Narrowed, not settled.
