# What does the Moonbeams' «མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད» mean: up to the half of the first part of the night, or up to the half (midnight) of the night?

RD313; Zur mkhar ba and the Blue Beryl read the second («ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད»), the Moonbeams' own words allow both.

Narrowed (inv-rrr, 2026-11-14): BDRC's exact search «མཚན་སྟོད་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད» has three hits, the Moonbeams (VE23703_1517), Byang pa's second edition (I4706) and a third etext (VEIE0OPIA3E40644_I1PD95958) that carries the same Moonbeams sentence; a line-broken copy of it stands in I1PD425 too (found by «ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད»). In Byang pa's first printing (MW15590, I1PD380) the gloss is in his own words: «མཚན་སྟོད་ཅེས་བྱ་བ་ནམ་ཕྱེད་པ་ཡན་ཆད་རྨི་ལམ་རྨིས་པ» (what is called the first part of the night: dreaming up to the half, nam phyed pa, of the night). So every writer who rewrites the Moonbeams' phrase (Zur mkhar ba, the Blue Beryl, Byang pa's first printing) reads «phyed pa yan chad» as up to midnight, none as up to the half of the first part; the Moonbeams' own phrase is found nowhere with a gloss by its author, and its grammar (mtshan stod + phyed pa) allows both. Open as to the author's intent; the tradition's reading is midnight (etext only, not read on a scan).
