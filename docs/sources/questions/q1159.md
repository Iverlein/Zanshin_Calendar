# Does the numeral under the trigram letter in box 5 of the model almanac (WB vol. 1 p. 156 reads 1 or 7; p. 165 differs) run by the day's place in a cycle that the tradition states, so that the cells need not be read one by one?

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-scan)

Looked at on the reprint (I3CN5028 img. 163, p. 156), the fifth box of each row (days 5, 10, 15, 20, 25), 2x. The small numeral under the trigram letter looks like: day 5 a hooked sign like «༤» or «༥»; day 10 a rounded blob («༨»?); day 15 a hook («༡» or «༧»); day 20 «༥»; day 25 «༧». No cycle is visible in these five and nothing in the tradition's text was found that states one, so the cells still have to be read one by one. Not answered; confidence low.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-scan3)

Not advanced. Location only: the Taikhang leaf of p. 154 is I5877 img. 190 and the Zhol I1KG1710 img. 173 (img. 174-175 the next leaves); p. 156 is two leaves on (Taikhang about img. 192-193, Zhol about 175-176, fetched but not looked at); the Zhol's boxes are drawn in ovals with the trigram letter inside and small hand-written lines beneath, so the numeral of box 5 has to be cut on the Taikhang first.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Answered, in the main, from KD vol. 1 (rd-kd1, 2026-11-14, etext): the numeral under a day's trigram letter is the day's mewa, the nine mewa turning one to each day (kd.1:p495a), so the cells run by a cycle the tradition states and the 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th and 25th cells are the day's mewa five turns apart each, not independent marks. What this does not settle: the direction of the turn and the month's first mewa, which are WB's chapter on the mewa and were not rechecked here; and the five numerals that inv-scan saw on the reprint (day 5 like 4 or 5, day 10 like 8, day 15 like 1 or 7, day 20 like 5, day 25 like 7) were not set against that rule, so they stay unread by rule. Next step: derive the nine-turn for the model almanac's 5th box from WB's mewa rule and compare the five readings with it. See kd-almanac-day-entry-numeral-under-trigram-is-the-mewa.
