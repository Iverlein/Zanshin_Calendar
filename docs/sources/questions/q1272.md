# Lo chen Dharma shrI says householders take the one-day vow at «ཉ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད»: is "the eighth" the eighth of the waxing half only, or of each half (the 8th and the 23rd)?

Raised by the practice-days investigation (pd-c, 2026-11-16); see the cited passages.

## Answered 2026-11-16

Answered from other authors, not from Lo chen's own words. Lo chen Dharma shrI writes «ཉ་སྟོང་ཚེས་བརྒྱད» and adds nothing (dpn:i55, dpn:i58). The stock phrase in full is «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ», "the three: full moon, new moon and eighth": so Zhe chen rgyal tshab, a Nyingma author, for the same one-day vow of householders, «ཟླ་བ་བྱུང་ངོ་ཅོག་གི་ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ» (zgt.1:p445-446, read on the scan), and Zhabs dkar (zkt:p582, etext). Three days in a month leave room for one eighth only. Co ne Grags pa bshad sgrub says which: the month has three good days, the eighth, the fifteenth and the new moon, by the moon's three clear marks, half grown on the eighth, full on the fifteenth, gone on the new moon, and "the half of the waning falls in the daytime and is not clear, so it is not counted" (dtn:i316, etext). The eighth is the eighth of the waxing half; the twenty-third is not one of the three.

What stays open: Lo chen himself does not say it, and Karma chags med's verse counts the twenty-third with the eighth as a day when deeds multiply (rcz:p286-288), though not as a fast day.
