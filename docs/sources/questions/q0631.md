# Bharaṇī's minor star on p. 314: «ཕུང་རྒྱབ་ཅན» or «ཕུང་རྒྱང་ཅན»

The minor star of Bharaṇī on p. 314 looks «ཕུང་རྒྱབ་ཅན» on the Zhol print (WBZ img. 1066) and «ཕུང་རྒྱང་ཅན» on the Gser thang reprint (img. 320): which is the star's name?

RD176 (2026-11-06), narrowed toward «ཕུང་རྒྱབ་ཅན»: on the Zhol (img. 1066, line 5) the boxy final matches the «པ» of «ཞེས་པ» in the same crop at 6×; the reprint's final is of the ང/ན/ད family, which that print does not keep apart; the 1996 etext and the Sakya etext (p. 452) both give «རྒྱབ». New question: is the reprint's final a ང printed for བ, or is there a Gser thang block reading «རྒྱང»?

RD176 second part (2026-11-06): SY has «སྐར་ཆུང་རྒྱུབ་ཅན» for Bharaṇī, a third witness to the final བ.

inv-rd-d (2026-11-16), three witnesses at 5-10×, each one crop of the syllable: the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 320, line 14 ends «ཕུང», line 15 begins «རྒྱང་ཅན་ཞེས་པ», 3× and 6×) prints the final as a ང, the same shape as the ང of «ཕུང» and of «སྐར་ཆུང» a line above, an open hook with a descending tail; the 1996 print (I1KG12907 img. 322, 10×) prints a closed, boxy བ, the same as the བ of «འབབ» in the line above it; the Zhol print (I1KG1710 img. 1066, line 5, x 1450-1700, 7×) shows a round closed bowl after the stack, with no ང hook, read as བ (the cursive hand leaves this one less certain than the 1996). So the two older witnesses of a different typesetting agree on «རྒྱབ» and the reprint's ང stands alone: the reprint was re-set, and a ང set for བ remains the likelier explanation. Not answered: the Zhol is cursive and the reprint is a reading of its own; no printed source giving the star's name was found. The question stays open, narrowed to «རྒྱབ» with the reprint's «རྒྱང» as a variant of that print.
