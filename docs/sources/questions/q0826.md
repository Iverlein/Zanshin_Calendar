# What are the goods of gossip called ram rug and, after it, spang g.yeng / sbang g.yer / sbang ma, in the Lama Gongdü's list (མི་ཁའི་རྫས)?

Five prints give «རམ་རུག» in three places and then «སྤང་གཡེང», «སྤང་གཡེར», «སྦང་གཡེར», «སྦང་མ», «སྦར་ཁྱེར»; the last pair is unsettled.


Narrowed (RD271, 2026-11-09): the «རམ་རུག་སྦང་གཡེར» is a short list of goods of a rite (མི་ཁའི་རྫས, mi kha'i rdzas, the substances of a rite against gossip), not a three-word name or a place or persons: «རམ་རུག» holds the slot of the plant ram bu or ram pa, «སྦང» is སྦང་མ (the beer's mash) and «གཡེར» is གཡེར་མ (the Sichuan pepper), each written short; Kongtrul's treasury's lists name «རམ་བུ་སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ» together as goods of gossip. What ram rug is exactly (the plant, by a spelling by ear) stays open; the spelling «རམ་ཏུག»/«རམ་བྲུག» occurs. Claims lgd-mi-kha-goods-of-gossip-are-rite-substances, lgd-ram-rug-is-the-ram-bu-spelling, lgd-sbang-g-yer-beer-mash-and-pepper.

Narrowed further (inv-j, 2026-11-13, BDRC search snippets): the phrase «རམ་རུག་སྦང་གཡེར་ལ་སོགས་མི་ཁའི་རྫས» stands word for word in the Rin chen gter mdzod chen mo (vol. 37, VE1KG14_037) in a list of misfortunes (being led away without control, wandering alone), so the Lama Gongdü's list is a stock phrase of the treasure texts. In Karma chags med's collected works the goods of the rite are «རྔར་མི་ཤ་དུམ་ཆང་བུ་དང་། །འབྲུ་ནག་རམ་རུག་སྦང་མ་སོགས», and in other treasure texts ram rug stands with the hot things: «ཚ་གཡེར་རམ་རུག་མཚུར་» (hot pepper, ram rug, the pungent), «ཚ་སྒོག་རམ་རུག་ཁྱི་སྤྱང་བྲུན», «རམ་རུག་སྒོག་བཙོང་». So ram rug is in these lists a pungent, hot substance beside pepper and garlic; its botanical identity is not given in the snippets, and no dictionary or materia medica entry was reached. The spelling «རམ་རུག» is the same in all ten hits.

Narrowed further (inv-r, 2026-11-13, Steinert's dictionaries by `locate.py dict`): no dictionary has a headword «ram rug». «ram pa» is a grass (Tshig mdzod chen mo: «rtswa zhig ste ran pa dang 'dra», a grass like ran pa; Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho adds that the Nyer mkho'i don gsal names ram pa among the names of dūrvā grass, dur ba; Jim Valby: quitch grass; Dan Martin: an edible root of the nomads); «ram bu» in the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs is a many-leaved fodder plant with a medicinal root (Valby: Polygonum viviparum). «sbang ma» is the residue of brewed beer (Tshig mdzod chen mo: «chang btsags zin pa'i ro ma»), «g.yer ma» the Sichuan pepper (Hopkins 2015, Tshig mdzod: «ro tsha», pungent, «kha gdangs, spor tsha, rtsa 'byed, shing a tsa ra»). So the dictionaries support «sbang» and «g.yer» of the earlier claim, and make «ram rug» a spelling of «ram pa»/«ram bu» (a grass or herb) the likelier reading; they do not confirm the pungent sense the BDRC snippets suggest. Still open.

Narrowed further (inv-f, 2026-11-14, BDRC phrase search «རམ་རུག་ལ», «ཞབས་རམ་རུག», «རམ་རྒྱུག»): a third place of the Lama Gongdü cycle itself has the word, in Sangs rgyas gling pa's practice texts (Bla rung 2006, MW1GS60392, the volume 'Bla ma dgongs pa 'dus pa las: sngon 'gro chos spyod sogs kyi skor'): «…བྲ་སྲན་རམ་རུག་ལ་སོགས་མི་ཁའི་རྫས་བསགས་པ་དང༌།» (snippet only), "gathering the goods of gossip, buckwheat-and-pea, ram rug and the like", set against the good store of grain, fruit and the rest laid up in the nest (tshang); so the cycle's own wording again names ram rug a good of gossip, in the company of the cheap field grains (the Karma chags med ransom has it beside 'bru nag, black grain, and «བྲ་སྲན་རམ་རུག»). That points to ram rug as a coarse food or crop of the same family as ram bu/ram pa, not a pungent spice; it does not settle which plant. The search «རམ་རྒྱུག» (15 hits) finds only «དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་རམ་རྒྱུག་རིས» (a mandala's drawn line, a different word), so that spelling is not this item. Still open: no source glosses the word; the three spellings of the earlier notes remain.
