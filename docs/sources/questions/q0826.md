# What are the goods of gossip called ram rug and, after it, spang g.yeng / sbang g.yer / sbang ma, in the Lama Gongdü's list (མི་ཁའི་རྫས)?

Five prints give «རམ་རུག» in three places and then «སྤང་གཡེང», «སྤང་གཡེར», «སྦང་གཡེར», «སྦང་མ», «སྦར་ཁྱེར»; the last pair is unsettled.


Narrowed (RD271, 2026-11-09): the «རམ་རུག་སྦང་གཡེར» is a short list of goods of a rite (མི་ཁའི་རྫས, mi kha'i rdzas, the substances of a rite against gossip), not a three-word name or a place or persons: «རམ་རུག» holds the slot of the plant ram bu or ram pa, «སྦང» is སྦང་མ (the beer's mash) and «གཡེར» is གཡེར་མ (the Sichuan pepper), each written short; Kongtrul's treasury's lists name «རམ་བུ་སྦང་མ་གཡེར་མ» together as goods of gossip. What ram rug is exactly (the plant, by a spelling by ear) stays open; the spelling «རམ་ཏུག»/«རམ་བྲུག» occurs. Claims lgd-mi-kha-goods-of-gossip-are-rite-substances, lgd-ram-rug-is-the-ram-bu-spelling, lgd-sbang-g-yer-beer-mash-and-pepper.

Narrowed further (inv-j, 2026-11-13, BDRC search snippets): the phrase «རམ་རུག་སྦང་གཡེར་ལ་སོགས་མི་ཁའི་རྫས» stands word for word in the Rin chen gter mdzod chen mo (vol. 37, VE1KG14_037) in a list of misfortunes (being led away without control, wandering alone), so the Lama Gongdü's list is a stock phrase of the treasure texts. In Karma chags med's collected works the goods of the rite are «རྔར་མི་ཤ་དུམ་ཆང་བུ་དང་། །འབྲུ་ནག་རམ་རུག་སྦང་མ་སོགས», and in other treasure texts ram rug stands with the hot things: «ཚ་གཡེར་རམ་རུག་མཚུར་» (hot pepper, ram rug, the pungent), «ཚ་སྒོག་རམ་རུག་ཁྱི་སྤྱང་བྲུན», «རམ་རུག་སྒོག་བཙོང་». So ram rug is in these lists a pungent, hot substance beside pepper and garlic; its botanical identity is not given in the snippets, and no dictionary or materia medica entry was reached. The spelling «རམ་རུག» is the same in all ten hits.
