# ཁྲེའུར and ཁོལ་བུར over the epidemics

ཁྲེའུར (*khre'ur*) in "epidemics come ཁྲེའུར" and ཁོལ་བུར (*khol bur*) in the same place: adverbs of what? Both stand over the notation's epidemics. (RD190, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-13 (inv-x)

Not answerable from the etexts: the Gser thang etext (VEIE0OPI3864355D_I3CN5028) has ཁྲེའུར in «ནད་རིམས་ཁྲེའུར་འོང་བ་ཡིན» and «ཁྲེའུར་འབྱུང» and ཁོལ་བུར in «ནད་རིམས་ཁོལ་བུར་འོང་», «བཀྲེས་འགྲངས་ཁོལ་བུར་འབྱུང་» as the pool has them; no BDRC phrase search was made for a gloss and none of the hits read is one. What the adverbs qualify is not stated. Open.

## Looked at 2026-11-14 (inv-i)

Narrowed, not settled. ཁོལ་བུར (khol bur) is the terminative of khol bu, "a part, a limb, a fragment" (Tshig mdzod chen mo 3: cha shas, yan lag, thor bu); the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs gives «lus khol bur na ba», "pain in the parts of the body". The adverb would then be "in parts, piecemeal, here and there": the epidemics come scattered, not all at once. The English of ch. 18 ("suddenly", ch18 entries 10, 12, 19, 21) is the gloss of glo bur (Tshig mdzod chen mo: "unforeseen, at once"), which is another word, and has nothing under it in the dictionaries. For ཁྲེའུར (khre'ur) no dictionary has a headword; the parts are khre'u, an old word for a striped rug (rnying; Tshig mdzod chen mo) and khre, the grain, and 'ur, which is a crowd's noise or uproar and, in the third sense, "suddenly" (= glo bur). The same verses stand in other almanacs on BDRC (the Bai dur dkar po's other prints and a sgang dril manual, MW3CN3480: «gzhon 'bring nad rims khre'ur 'ong», «'khon zhing rkun po khol bur 'ong»), with no gloss. In entry 60 of the Little Tiger-Ear list (p. 212) the same string stands where a grain is meant («ཁྲེའུར་བཙོན», "locked up", as of so and zar), so ཁྲེའུར may be the crop name with the particle, and the epidemic lines «ནད་རིམས་ཁྲེའུར་འོང» "epidemics come to the khre" would belong to the khre grain; nothing found settles it.
