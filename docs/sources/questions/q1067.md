# Why does the Gdams ngag mdzod's teaching name the midnight dream (ནམ་ཕྱེད) as the place of the death-sign images, when the Vajrāvalī rite says the middle watches give no dream, the Karmapa's saying (after Marpa) makes the midnight dream the power of spirits, and the Four Tantras name only the early night and dawn: is nam phyed there «the midnight dream» or a way of saying «the night's dream»?

RD374.

## Answered 2026-11-13

Narrowed 2026-11-13: the word cannot be read as "the night's dream" by the dictionaries. nam phyed is, in every keyed dictionary, midnight, one of the twelve double-hours of the day-and-night (the rat's in the Tshig mdzod chen mo, the ox's in the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho), or in Negi «half of the night», and none gives it the sense of the night as a whole (tmz:box5). So «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ» in the Gdams ngag mdzod (dds.9:p173-174) is the dream of midnight in the word's own sense, unless the teaching uses it loosely, which no entry shows; the question why it names the midnight dream when the Vajrāvalī rite says the middle watches give no dream, and the Karmapa's saying (after Marpa) gives the midnight dream to the spirits, is not answered: that the text takes it from the transference teaching of Marpa's snyan gyi shog dril bzhi, with the death signs of the sick yogin, is all the passage says, and no commentary on it has been found.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-cc)

BDRC's exact-phrase search for «nam phyed kyi rmi lam» (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ) gives ten hits. Six are the death-sign sentence itself («'chi ltas shes dgos te / de la phyi nang gsang ba gsum / dang po la gnyis te / nam phyed kyi rmi lam brtag pa dang / nyin par lus sems la brtag pa'o»), word for word in the Gdams ngag mdzod, the Bka' brgyud sngags mdzod nag 'gros, Marpa's collected works (MW1KG12222), the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod's bde mchog snyan rgyud part, and the history of the four sheets in the 'Bri lugs bang mdzod skor lnga and the Nechu Lhakhang copy: one text, no variant that says «dream of the night». The seventh is the 3rd Karmapa's line (Rang byung rdo rje's collected works: «spyir srod kyi rmi lam bag chags yin / nam phyed kyi rmi lam gdon dbang yin / tho rangs gang byung bden no zhes / bla ma mar pa'i zhal nas gsungs»), already used. The other three are narrative: in the Pad gling lo rgyus drang gtam («yig mkhan gcig 'bod dgos bsam pa'i nam phyed kyi rmi lam na bu mo lnga byung nas …») and in two Vairocana biographies («nam phyed kyi rmi lam la rgya gar mkhas pa nyi shu rtsa gcig gi snying byung nas zos pa rmis so», set before «gung yol gyi rmi lam», a later dream) it is a dream had at midnight, not the night's dreams in general. So the phrase is used elsewhere in its own sense; no commentary on the death-sign teaching explaining why it names midnight was reached. Why it differs from the Vajrāvalī rite stays unanswered; the Karmapa's line, which ranks the midnight dream by what causes it (spirits' power), is the nearest witness that the midnight dream was a recognised kind apart from the dawn dream.
