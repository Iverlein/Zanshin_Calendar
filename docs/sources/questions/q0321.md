# Ch. 28's seasons against the Phugpa months

WB vol. 2, p. 26 (img. 34), with the notes p. 537 (img. 545): the notes put the three dates in the first spring month "the eleventh Hor month", the *rgyal* month and the Miracle month, so that ch. 28's "spring" would be Hor months 11, 12 and 1; does the chapter's naming of the seasons (summer, autumn, winter months on pp. 30–31 and 38–39) follow the same count, shifted two months from the Phugpa calendar's?

## Read further 2026-11-01

Answered in RD99: the chapter counts by the Chinese reckoning; spring is Hor months 11, 12, 1 and the other seasons follow (claims/ch28-seasons-chinese-count.md; WB vol. 1's model almanac heads Hor 11 the first spring month, the tiger month).

## Read further 2026-11-14

Narrowed again, not closed; the answer of 2026-11-01 holds for p. 26 only. Read: the notes (p. 537, scan) name the three spring dates as Hor months 11, 12 and 1, so p. 26's spring is the Chinese reckoning's. For pp. 38-39 the table of the black small feet (scan) gives the months as printed: rat middle spring, ox last spring, tiger last spring, hare first spring, dragon middle summer, snake last summer, horse first autumn, sheep middle autumn, monkey last autumn, bird first winter, dog middle winter, pig last winter. Computed by hand from WB's own twelve month lines (tibetastromed.md): nine of the twelve (tiger and dragon to pig) are Hor month n for the animal that is n-th from the rat (dragon 5th, month 5 = Kalacakra middle summer; pig 12th, month 12 = late winter), and the three that are not (rat, ox, hare) are the slips already noted (the first summer month missing). Under the Chinese count the same months would stand two seasons off (month 5 would be early autumn), so the table's season words follow the Kalacakra count unless the author paired animal and month by another rule; no text found states one. Computed with the engine: 14 October 2026 is the Phugpa 9th month, Kalacakra late autumn, Chinese "mid winter"; WB p. 28 puts the tail of autumn 21 days after the equinox, i.e. on that date, which sits well with the Kalacakra count (last autumn, months 7-9) and badly with the Chinese (months 5-7 end in mid-September in 2026). So the claim ch28-seasons-chinese-count.md ("every season word of chapter 28 is read by this count") is shown only for pp. 26-27 and the notes; pp. 28-31 and 38-39 may count otherwise. Not settled: pp. 30-31's month words have no key of their own.
