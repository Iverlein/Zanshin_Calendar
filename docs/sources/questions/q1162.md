# What does «རྐྱང་ལྷགས» (rkyang lhags) mean in the closing note of the 7th month head of the model almanac (WB vol. 1 p. 167): the doing reckoning takes the ninth of the sun's mansion-places and seven chu tshod, and whatever stands there in this month and the next, a rkyang lhags is born?

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-x)

Not answered; the word is a calendar note, most likely a spell of cold wind, and no text glosses it. BDRC's exact phrase «རྐྱང་ལྷགས» gives 9 hits (the White Beryl's two prints; the *Bod kyi rtsis rig kun 'dus chen mo*; a Lhasa Men-Tsee-Khang almanac of 2013; a "Sde tshan brgya dang so gnyis pa" almanac; Padma rig 'dzin's collected medical writings; the *Skar rtsis 'byung rtsis zung sdeb kyi li tho sbyor tshul* with WB's very line; and two uses of another sense, «lam rkyang lhags tshe», "as they came straight along the road", in a Dalai Lama biography, and «phung nag rkyang lhags» in a memoir), snippets only. In the almanacs it stands among the season's weather notes: «ston zug tshad pa cung zad kha 'bri/ rkyang lhags skye/ bya skad 'chag» (autumn: the heat lessens a little, a «rkyang lhags» is born, birds' cries) in the 2013 almanac, and «char kha rkyang lhags skye/ bya skad 'chag 'bri/ spang rgyan me tog sgang» (the rain's mouth, a «rkyang lhags» born, birds' cries lessen, the meadow flowers fade) after «grang zug» (cold appears) in the other. The *Kun 'dus chen mo* (I4716, etext p. 338, a verse outline of the almanac's chapters) lists «རྐྱང་ལྷགས་སྐྱེ་བའི་དུས་ཚེས་རྣམས། ཚེས་གྲངས་སྔ་ཕྱིའི་གོ་རིམ་ལྟར» (the times and dates when a «rkyang lhags» is born, by the order of the lunar-day counts) after the «bstun pa'i 'brug dang bye'u slebs» and before the «སྐག་རིས་དཔྱིད་གསུམ» (the three spring months' Skag reckoning): a dated almanac item, like WB's. So WB's note says: whatever the sun's ninth mansion-place and seven chu tshod are in this month and the next, there a «rkyang lhags» is born. For the word: «lhags pa» is a cold wind in the dictionaries (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs «རླུང་གྲང་མོའི་མིང»; Rangjung Yeshe "a cold wind, chill"; Tshig mdzod chen mo «རྒྱུག་འགྲོ་བའི་རླུང་རྒྱུན»; q717), and «rkyang» as an intensifier means "only, completely" (Tshig mdzod chen mo «ཁོ་ན»), so "a plain, sheer cold wind" is the likely reading, unglossed: no headword «rkyang lhags» in the 64 dictionaries of Steinert's collection. Open: the almanacs' full text (the 2013 almanac and the Zung sdeb li tho are search-only, 403) for a rule saying which days.

## Narrowed again 2026-11-14 (inv-last)

The *Kun 'dus chen mo* etext (I4716) has «རྐྱང་ལྷགས» only in the verse outline of p. 338, which gives no date rule; the rule of its almanac chapter was not found under that word. The same book's almanac pages use «ལྷགས» alone as the season's cold wind: a sample almanac (p. 232) «རླུང་ལྷགས་སྐྱེ། བྱ་སྐད་འཆག» ("a wind-«lhags» is born, birds' cries") and its table of the breaths (p. 284) «བཅུ་གཉིས་རླུང་ལྷགས་གྲང་ངར་ཆེ» (the twelfth: wind-«lhags», cold and chill great), also «གྲང་ལྷགས» (p. 233, 392, 514, 515). So «ལྷགས» in «རྐྱང་ལྷགས་སྐྱེ» is the cold wind of the season notes. «རྐྱང» (Rangjung Yeshe: "only, solely, barely"; Hopkins: «རྐྱང་པ» "alone; only; mere") would make it the bare, lone wind; no dictionary has «རྐྱང་ལྷགས» as a headword (Steinert's 64 sets, 2026-11-14), and no text says what «རྐྱང» adds.
