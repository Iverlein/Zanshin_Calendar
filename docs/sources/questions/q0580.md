# The hand reckoning's «ལཱུ་བཞིན» with a long-vowel sign

On p. 538's «ལག་རྩིས་ལཱུ་བཞིན» the syllable is written with a long-vowel sign (Yigdzin-1 on the reprint and the 1996 print), not «ལུ»: is the word «ལཱུ» (or «ལླུ») a name of the method taken from another language, and is it so written on Mipham's own print (I2029 img. 743)?

## Answered 2026-11-03

**Answered 2026-11-03 (RD132, [verdicts.md](../verdicts.md), *The hand reckoning's «ལཱུ» on Mipham's own print and the notes' «ཕ་ཡི་དྭ་ཟླས»*).** Mipham's own print has the word on img. 744 (not img. 743, which ends before it), at the head of line 2, and again in «ལཱུ་བཞིན་རྩིས་འདི་ནི་སོག་པོ་དགེ་བཤེས་བསམ་གཏན་ལས་ལག་ཁྲིད་བྱས་པ་བཞིན་བྲིས་པའོ།» on img. 745: Yigdzin-1 reads «ལཱུ» both times, and the first on the bitmap carries a hooked sign beyond a plain ུ. Whether the sign is ཱུ or a subjoined ླུ the 1-bit scan does not settle. That the word is a foreign name of the method is the inference of [notes-hand-reckoning-three-results](../claims/notes-hand-reckoning-three-results.md), the colophon naming a Mongol geshe as its teacher; no gloss says it.
