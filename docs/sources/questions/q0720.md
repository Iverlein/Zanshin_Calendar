# «ཁ་དམར་མ་ལྡོག» on p. 92

p. 92's closing clause «ཁ་དམར་མ་ལྡོག་ལུང་སྟོན་བྱ» ("the red-mouthed [?] does not turn back, give the prophecy"): what are the *kha dmar* and the *ma ldog*, and who or what is the red-mouthed here? (RD191, corpus/wb/ch12.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

«ཁ་དམར་» (kha dmar) is a term of the astrologers: "a clear prophecy of the good or bad fruit of a reckoning, an oracle of what will come" (Tshig mdzod chen mo, sense 1; the Sangs rgyas chos gzhung dictionary has "formerly, to tell clearly the good or bad result of a reckoning"); Valby: "affirmative prediction (good or bad)", with «kha dmar phog» "the realization of an affirmative prediction" and «kha dmar tog» "its non-realization". The "red mouth" is the figure for a prediction spoken out; the red-mouthed is not a being here. «མ་ལྡོག» is the negated ལྡོག་པ "to turn back, reverse". So «ཁ་དམར་མ་ལྡོག་ལུང་སྟོན་བྱ» reads "give the prophecy so that the clear prediction does not turn back" (so that it does not fail): after working out the uncertain cases in detail, the astrologer gives the prediction as one that will hold. The same couplet «nges med la sogs zhib brtsis nas / kha dmar ma ldog lung ston bya» is quoted in six other BDRC etexts (the White Beryl's Gser thang and Peking prints, Bstan 'dzin chos kyi rgyal mtshan's collected works, the Vaidurya g.ya' sel MW8226, the Taikhang print), and the Vaidurya g.ya' sel, Sde srid's own correction of the White Beryl, comments on it («zhes bkod pa'i don ni…»), but that etext is poor OCR and was seen only in the search snippet, so its explanation is not read.
