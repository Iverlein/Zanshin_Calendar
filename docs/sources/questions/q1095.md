# Verse 2 of the Saṃvarodaya's chapter (Sanskrit trayadivasāt … pañcatvaṃ gacchate) reads in the Tibetan «lnga yi gong du 'gro bar 'gyur» («before five»): did a Tibetan commentator take «five» as five days, and do the commentaries explain the verse as «after three days, in dying», as the Deceiving Death puts it?

RD382.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The Sanskrit explains the line; no Tibetan commentary on it was found. The Saṃvarodaya's «pañcatvaṃ gacchate» is the idiom «goes to the state of five», to die, and the Tibetan «lnga yi gong du 'gro bar 'gyur» renders it word by word (pha rol = parāt, gong du = ūrdhvam, lnga yi = pañcatvam): «after three days, afterwards to fiveness he goes», so «five» is not a count of days. This is what 'Deceiving Death' says (after three days, dying; dds.25:p580-583 against the claim mvup-sanskrit-parallels-sut19), and the Tibetan of the Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇatantra's parallel has «zhag gsum nas ni 'chi bar 'gyur» without the five. Tsong kha pa quotes the Tibetan verse as it stands and glosses nothing (dds.16:p11-13); the Derge Padminī (Ratnarakṣita's commentary on the Saṃvarodaya, Toh 1420, etext VE23703_1337 pp. 142-145) passes over the death-sign verses to the transference. Whether any later Tibetan author read «five» as five days is not shown by any text read.
