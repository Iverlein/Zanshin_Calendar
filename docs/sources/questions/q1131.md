# Are the be'u bum's Śākyamuni and White Tārā rites taken from the tradition of the Sgrub thabs kun btus, and which text names their compiler

The mantra «oṃ buddha curu mācuru ke svāhā» and the four mirrors with the letters A, NU, TA, RA also stand in the Sgrub thabs kun btus, the Sgrub thabs rin po che kun las btus pa, a Snar thang tantra and a Lam 'bras notebook (by snippet; VE23681_001 timed out).

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The Sgrub thabs kun btus in 14 volumes (BDRC MW3KG767 Derge print, MW1KG17189 Kathmandu reprint, MW3KG633) has as creators in BDRC's record Ngor pa 'Jam dbyangs Blo gter dbang po (1847-1914) and 'Jam dbyangs Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po (1820-1892) (P560 and P258); the catalogue gives them as its authors. A compilation of the nineteenth century is later than the be'u bum's rites, so it can take them from the tradition but cannot be their source; the be'u bum's own compiler is not named in any text found. The Sgrub thabs rin po che kun las btus pa (MW3CN3381) is a separate record without a creator in BDRC. Not opened: the rites in the volumes themselves (the volume with the Śākyamuni dream rite).

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-s)

Both rites have an older source than the Sgrub thabs kun btus, and the White Tārā rite a named transmitter. (1) The Śākyamuni mantra «oṃ buddha curu mācuru ke svāhā» stands in two tantras of the Derge Kanjur (BDRC's exact phrase «ཙུ་རུ་མཱ་ཙུ་རུ་ཀེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ» 6 hits, etext windows read): the Tārā tantra *Phrin las thams cad 'byung ba'i sgrol ma 'dus pa don dam pa* (MW4CZ5369_0448, vol. 81 pp. 576-593, etext [p590]) and the *Dpa' bo gcig pu grub pa* (MW4CZ5369_0544, vol. 89 pp. 5-28, etext [p16]-[p17]; BDRC's colophon field «dpa' bo gcig pu grub pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po las le'u bzhi pa», translated by Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna and Dge ba'i blo gros). Its use there is the be'u bum's: «with this mantra, recited before a stupa with relics or before the Lord Buddha (sku gdung dang bcas pa'i mchod rten nam rje btsun sangs rgyas kyi spyan sngar), ten thousand times, then at the time of lying down 108 times, thinking "from where was I born, where shall I be born", one sees the births past and future, without doubt» (Toh 544; Toh 448 has «at a stupa, with cleanliness: one knows where one will be born, the end of life and long life»). The Sgrub thabs kun btus calls the section «Buddha curuka's examination of births» (བུདྡྷ་ཙུ་རུ་ཀའི་སྐྱེ་བ་བརྟག་པ) and its separate text «Bud+d+ha tsu ru ka'i skye ba brtag pa'i gdams ngag gzhung gsal byed dang bcas pa Dri bral nor bu'i me long» is by Mkhyen brtse'i dbang po himself, who draws, by that text's own line, on the Ekavīrasiddha tantra's piece, Grags pa's tantra commentary, the Snar thang abhisamaya hundred and eight, and Stag lung pa's instruction. So the be'u bum's Śākyamuni rite takes its mantra and purpose from the Kanjur tantras and the nineteenth-century collection takes the same: the collection is a later witness, not the source. (2) The White Tārā rite is the same text as the Sgrub thabs kun btus's item «Sgrol ma'i sgo nas rmi lam brtag pa'i man ngag» no. 6, which begins «rten 'brel mngon shes su 'char ba'i man ngag» (VE23681_001, read at the hit 9121086) and has the be'u bum's wording: one's own deity first, oneself as White Tārā, a white TAṂ in the heart-lotus, four silver mirrors with A, NU, TA, RA in the east, south, west and north, the invocation from Akaniṣṭha, the wish sent as a second TAṂ; and it adds the reading the be'u bum cuts short: what appears in the east mirror (a name-letter with A) means the aim is not accomplished, in the south (NU) accomplished, in the west (TA) a non-Dharma figure not accomplished and a Dharma figure accomplished, in the north (RA) a living being accomplished and a non-living form not. The Sakya Gorum library catalogue (MW3CN8601, snippet) lists two manuscripts «rten 'brel mngon shes su 'char ba'i man ngag» «Khro phu lo tsā ba Byams pa dpal gyi man ngag brgya rtsa las» (from Khro phu lo tsā ba Byams pa dpal's Hundred Instructions); the Sgrub thabs kun btus's neighbouring item ends «the lord translator said it is not to be spread to others». So the White Tārā rite is attributed to Khro phu lo tsā ba Byams pa dpal (the catalogue's naming; the collection's own text in the window read has no colophon naming him). The be'u bum's compiler remains unnamed. Not read: Khro phu lo tsā ba's Hundred Instructions in the volume, to see the rite's own lines and colophon.
