# The planets' and sun's mean-motion word on p. 32: dhruva, «རྒྱུ་བ» or «རླུ་བ»

In the note on p. 32 the word for the planets' and sun's mean motion: is it «དྷྲུ་བ» (the Sanskrit dhruva, as the machine text), «རྒྱུ་བ» (as MITRA, and as the verse of line 13 «རྒྱུ་བའི་བསྒྱུར་གཞི» seems to have it) or «རླུ་བ» (the etext), and is the first syllable of that verse line «ཟླ» or «རྒྱུ»? RD204 (2026-11-08): the stack cannot be told apart at the reprint's size; a sharper print (Zhol or Taikhang) is needed.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Read on the Taikhang reproduction of the Lhasa print (BDRC I5877, 8700 px, a bilevel image; img. 68 = the Zhol print I1KG1710 img. 51, folio 58, which ends the verse; img. 69 = Zhol img. 52, which has the prose note), 2026-11-14. The verse line 13 reads «ཟླ་བའི་བསྒྱུར་གཞི་སོ་སོ་ལ» ("for each one's multiplying base of the month"): the first stack, at 3× on the last line of img. 68, has the za head with la below it and no vowel sign below, while the stack of «བསྒྱུར» next to it carries the ya and the ུ; so «རྒྱུ་བའི» (MITRA, and the machine's «རྒྱུ་བ») is not what the print has, and the line does not speak of a mean motion at all but of the month's multiplying base. The same line was seen on the Zhol print (I1KG1710 img. 51, 3765 px) and looks the same, less sharply. In the prose note on img. 69 line 1 the word after «ལྔ་བསྡུས» («བཻ་ཌཱུར་དཀར་པོའི་དོ་ཤལ ... ལྔ་བསྡུས་དྷྲུ་བ་དང་བཅས་པའི་གྲུབ་རྩིས་») shows a stack with a subjoined ra and a curl below, the Sanskrit «དྷྲུ་བ» (dhruva) of the machine text, not «རྒྱུ་བ» and not «རླུ་བ» (no la below, no ra-head over a ga). So the word of the note for the planets' and sun's mean motion is the borrowed dhruva, as the pool's text has it (wb.1:p32), and «རླུ་བ» of the etext is a misreading. Not done: the other occurrences of «དྷྲུ» in the note (line 22 of the reprint, «ཉི་དྷྲུ་དང་མདའ་རོ་ལྷག») were not cropped on the Taikhang print, and the figure after «འཁོར་ཕྱེད» (q770's companion doubt) was not looked for; the 1996 print's stack was already settled as unreadable (RD204).
