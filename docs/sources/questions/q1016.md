# What is the work 紀事 that the 歳時記栞草 cites for the 初夢, and does the 栞草's nearby entry 鶏旦 (the dawn of the first day) fix anything about the night?

RD364.

## Answered 2026-11-13

Narrowed. The work 紀事 is named on the page by its boxed label only (mdz:i7333115b, read again at 6700 px); the 凡例 and the front matter give no list of sources (RD364), so its author and date stay unfound. The entry's own cross-reference sends the reader to the section け (ke) for 鶏旦, and the entry 初鶏 on the same leaf carries 鶏旦 as a second head, cited to 御傘 (Gosan): the cock that crows on New Year's morning (read in part). So 鶏旦 names the dawn of the first day, the end point of the 紀事 definition, and fixes nothing about the night beyond that: the night is the one before the dawn, "of the last day of the year", as the 紀事 sentence has it. The entry under け was not opened (449 canvases).

## Answered 2026-11-14

The work 紀事 is named only by its label in the 栞草 (mdz:i7333115b, and in the Meiji reprints mdz:i87533211); no title, author or date is given, and none of the pages read lists sources. The 栞草's nearby entry 鶏旦 fixes nothing about the night: the cross-reference of the 初鶏 entry ("○け部注す可見合") leads to an entry 〔正月〕雞旦 in the section け (the reprint's image 40, mdz:i87533240), which cites the 東方朔占書 (not the 紀事) for the New Year's Day being the day of the cock, whence New Year's Day is called keitan, and says nothing of a night or a dream. The 初鶏 entry, citing 御傘, adds that the bird crows at the tiger hour, so that "the night is of course meant" (夜分勿論なり); that is a statement about the cock, not about when the dream is dreamt.
