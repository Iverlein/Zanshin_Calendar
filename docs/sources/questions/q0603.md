# «ཡི་བམ» in reply 152: a spelling of «ལི་ཁམ» or another word

In reply 152 (I5875 img. 514, l. 3), is «ཡི་བམ» (ya with gigu, ba, ma) a copyist's spelling of «ལི་ཁམ», or a different word of the regent's hand?

RD151 (2026-11-06), by eye at 1.6–6×: the first syllable is a hook-and-loop stack under a gigu, and the hand writes the particle «ལ» two syllables on in the same shape; ya against la is undecidable in this hand, so «ལི་ཁམ» is not excluded on the first letter and the earlier «firm ya» is weakened. The second syllable has a ba-like cap; ba against kha not settled. Needs a certain ya and la from the same hand, or a second print.

## Answered 2026-11-14

It is a different hand's rendering of «ལི་ཁམ», not another word. «ཡི་བམ» is a headword in none of the 64 dictionaries and has no sense in the sentence; the sentence («དེས་ན་མོ་[ལི་ཁམ]་ལ་བབས་ནས་འབྱུང་ཆུང་བྱི་འབྲུག་སྤྲེའུ་ལས་མི་འདའ་བའི་དབང་གིས…») repeats the reply's own clause two sentences before, read plainly on the scan, «སྤར་ཁ་ལི་ཁམ་ལ་བབས་པའི་ཕོ», and WB's line it answers («བྱི་འབྲུག་སྤྲེལ་གསུམ་ལི་ཁམ་ཐོབ»): the woman whose trigram has fallen on li or kham. RD151 found ya against la undecidable in this hand, which fits. BDRC's phrase search «ལི་ཁམ་ལ་བབས» finds the words in other works only; the Dpe rnying kun btus etext of the replies (I1KG81103) is garbled at this clause («kyis spyi la dag gis», char. 626604) and gives no reading of it. Answered by sense and the parallel clause; the letter forms stay unread.
