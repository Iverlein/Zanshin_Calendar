# What are གནམ་ཐེ་དཀར་པོ and ཞེ་ཐག་པ་ནས་སྦྱིན in Marpa's inner-heat manual

In dds.19:p187-189: what is the gnam the dkar po that one holds prisoner with a white thread, and what is giving the offering 'from afar' (zhe thag pa nas sbyin)?

## Answered 2026-11-13

Narrowed 2026-11-13; the second half is answered, the first is not.

«ཞེ་ཐག་པ་ནས་སྦྱིན» is not "from afar": zhe thag pa is "the depth of the mind, the root of the thought" and «zhe thag pa nas» is "from the bottom of the heart, wholeheartedly" (Tshig mdzod chen mo, whose example is promising from the bottom of the heart; Ives Waldo, Valby, tmz:box3). The offering of the visualised body to the spirits that harm the drop is given sincerely. BDRC's exact-phrase search for the phrase finds two hits, both this very passage of Marpa's text (the Ser gtsug edition of Marpa's collected works, and the Nechu Lhakhang text Gegs sel yid bzhin nor bu gsal byed snying po, whose words are the same), so no commentary glosses it.

«གནམ་ཐེ་དཀར་པོ» (gnam the dkar po): the snippets of BDRC's exact-phrase search (92 hits; the first page's snippets read, no work opened, so not built on) name it in other contexts: the Gsung rab sgo mdzod has «the'u's son is gnam the dkar po and sa the'u nag po» (a descent list of the the'u spirits); the Vaidurya dkar po has «gnam the dkar po 'gong po, water-bog 'dre» among the harmers of a house; a Guru Drag dmar rite has «gnam the dkar po» in a line of crushing the harmful; and a lam rim lineage life, the lives of the 'Bras spungs abbots and Pan chen Bsod nams grags pa's collected works tell of a god «gnam the dkar po» appearing to a teacher in a dream, the Sa skya Lam 'bras and Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams' works of a Hor god of that name. The dictionaries give the'u rang as a sky-going spirit or hungry ghost (tmz:box3) and have no entry for gnam the dkar po. So the being held by the white thread is, in these sources, a named spirit of the the'u group, which fits the manual's spirits that take the drop; why that one is taken prisoner, and why by a white thread tied round the waist and the root of the male organ, no text read says. Open: a work on the the'u spirits that tells what gnam the dkar po does to a sleeper.

**Narrowed again 2026-11-14** (etext, not built on). The spelling is «གནམ་ཐེའུ་དཀར་པོ» (gnam the'u dkar po), the sky the'u: BDRC's exact-phrase search finds it in 87 hits against the 92 of the spelling without «འུ», and the Chos kyi blo gros volume (MW1KG12222, I1KG12251) holds Marpa's remedy twice, once as «མནམ་ཐེལ་དཀར་པོ» (etext p. 276, a copy of the text, with «ཞེ་ཐག་པ་ནས་སྦྱིན» too) and once in the writer's own words at p. 312: «གནམ་ཐེའུ་དཀར་པོ་བཙོན་དུ་བཟུང་བ་ནི་ཐིག་གུ་དཀར་པོ་གཅིག་རྐེད་པ་ལ་བསྐོར། དེའི་སྣ་གཅིག་རྡོ་རྗེ་ཡུ་བ་ལ་བཏགས་ལ་ཉལ། དེས་ངེས་པར་མི་སེལ་མི་སྲིད་གསུང་ངོ་» ("to hold the gnam the'u dkar po prisoner: wind one white cord round the waist, tie its one end to the vajra's shaft and lie down; it is not possible that this will not clear it, he said"): the cord's end is fastened to the penis, which the manual's «root of the male organ» says less plainly. There the fault is the dream of a dog, a donkey, an old woman, a bird-woman or a leprous woman, «lying with» her, and the drop lost. The Gsung rab sgo mdzod (a 2015 compilation, MW4PD502, I4PD4104 etext p. 484) says «ཡ་ཅོ་དཀར་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་ནི་འགོ་བའི་ལྷ་ཡིན་ཞིང་། གནམ་ཐེའུ་ལ་སོགས་གནོད་པའི་གདོན་ཡིན་ནོ» ("Ya co dkar po and the like are gods who lead beings; gnam the'u and the like are harmful spirits (gdon)"), and at p. 502 names «གཅིག་དཀར་གནམ་ཐེའུ་དཀར་པོ» as the first of a numbered row, white, blazing, sending the sky's white bird as messenger. So the being is a harmful sky-spirit of the the'u class (gdon), and the manual's measure is to tie it up by the cord at the root of the penis. Why a white cord and the waist, and what the spirit does to a sleeper, are still not said by any text read (q1187). The Bon master Nyamme Sherab Gyaltsen's collected works (MW8LS16918) gloss «gnam the'u dkar po gsum» as three that join the sky's and fall as gods and demons, with «sa the'u nag po gsum» that «snare the female line in the womb»: a Bon text, a witness only, not built on.
