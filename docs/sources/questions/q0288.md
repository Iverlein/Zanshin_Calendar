# «གནམ་གྱི་བྱི་ལམ» or «བྱ་ལམ»: the sky's road

«གནམ་གྱི་བྱི་ལམ» (p. 96) or «གནམ་གྱི་བྱ་ལམ» (p. 50): is the sky's road of the twelfth examination the "mouse road" as printed on p. 96, or the "bird road" of the sizes on p. 50? The 1996 print and the Gser thang reprint both carve «བྱི་ལམ» on p. 96.

**Narrowed 2026-11-05 (RD161, [word-hunts.md](../word-hunts.md)).** The digest has «གནམ་སའི་བྱི་ལམ»; both spellings stand.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Answered 2026-11-14 (inv-nn): the sky's road is the bird road, «གནམ་གྱི་བྱ་ལམ». The «བྱི» of p. 96 (both prints carve it) is read as the earth's road's spelling carried over, as the claim *dead-sky-road-bird-or-mouse* said; what is added is the sense and the headword.

- **Sense.** The pair is the sky's road and the earth's road: «བྱ་ལམ» is the bird's flight-path in the sky (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives Waldo: "the flight-path, imprint of a bird in the sky"; "sky, bird's flight"), and the earth's road, «ས་ཡི་བྱི་ལམ», is the mouse's run under the ground; each road is named for the creature that goes there. Steinert's 64 dictionaries (2026-11-14) have the headword «bya lam» and none for «byi lam». A mouse in the sky has no sense.
- **Other witnesses.** BDRC's phrase search «གནམ་གྱི་བྱ་ལམ» 4 hits, all the *Bai dur dkar po* (the 1996 etext, the Gser thang etext, the Do shal's etext and one more copy), p. 50, "gnam gyi bya lam gza' skar …"; «གནམ་གྱི་བྱི་ལམ» 1 hit, the p. 96 line itself in the 1996 etext ("gnam gyi byi lam zhes bya ba / gza' dang skar ma bzang la btang / sa yi byi lam bstan pa ni"). No work outside WB has either string. The digest's «གནམ་སའི་བྱི་ལམ» (word-hunts.md) shows that a reader of WB took the printed «བྱི» as it stands, so the spelling was current in the prints, whoever first wrote it.
