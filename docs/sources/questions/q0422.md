# How are the thirteen gates counted?

How are the thirteen gates counted (WB vol. 2, pp. 170–171)? Built (ROADMAP R2z) with these readings, each open: «ལྔ་ནས» after the start of gates 6, 10, 11 and 13 («གྲོགས་ཡོས་ལྔ་ནས», «རང་ཁམས་སྤྲེལ་ལྔ་ནས», «ཕག་ལྔ་ནས», «བྱི་ལྔ་ནས») read as no count, since gate 5 alone gives its five as one («ཕོ་ནི་ཐུར་ལ་མོ་ཡར་ལྔ»); gate 9's «ལུག་ཉིད་ཀྱི། །བདུན་ཟུར་རང་ཁམས་ས» as the own element's ox; "eight up", "three up" and "thirteen down" counted inclusively, the year reached the first, as the thirty count (D22); a man and a woman by the birth year's sex, as D22; the year reached fought against the moment's sign by element, cutting inward where its element overcomes the moment's. Two words of the harms are left out of the readings: «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ» (the steelyard's rope, or the line of births?) and «ལྷས་ངན་བབས» (by the gods, or «ལྟས་ངན», a bad omen? check on the scan, I3CN5029 img. 176).

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-fff, from RD145, [reckonings.md](../reckonings.md), *The thirteen gates' words*; claim moment-gate-balance-and-omen).** «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ» is the steelyard's rope, not a line of births: gate 1's two harms are the two parts of a balance (the rope cut, the gold-holder's leg broken). «ལྷས་ངན» is «ལྟས་ངན» (bad omen) on the scan, a subjoined ta under la. The «ལྔ» after the animal (gates 6, 10, 11, 12, 13; img. 177, ll. 6, 13, 14, 16, 17) is not explained in the text or in Mipham's note on the gates (p. 536); in gate 6 no count follows it, in the others 13 or 25. The counting of the gates as a whole (the years lived with the starting year first, male down, female up) is recounted in the notes' worked example (see q425). Open: the «ལྔ».

**Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-j).** BDRC phrase searches «བྱི་ལྔ་ནས» (4 hits), «རང་ཁམས་སྤྲེལ་ལྔ་ནས» (2), «གྲོགས་ཡོས་ལྔ་ནས» (2), «ཕག་ལྔ་ནས»: the hits are WB's own etexts (MW2CZ8040, MW3CN5026, I1KG12907, I3CN5029) and, for «བྱི་ལྔ་ནས», two unrelated texts (a rnam thar's «dgung lo thu byi lnga nas», the age of five, and MW25273). WB's etext shows gate 13 in context: «གནམ་ལོའི་རང་ཁམས་བྱི་ལྔ་ནས། མར་ལ་བཅུ་གསུམ་ཁྱེན་ས་ལས། ཕོ་སྦུབ་མོ་བཀེན་དུས་དང་འཐབ» (Wylie as BDRC prints it: gnam lo'i rang khams byi lnga nas / mar la bcu gsum khyen sa las / pho sbub mo bken dus dang 'thab), no commentary glossing the «lnga» was found. The «ལྔ» stays open.
