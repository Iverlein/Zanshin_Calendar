# «རུས་མཚན» in Viśākhā's verse, p. 322

What is «རུས་མཚན» in Viśākhā's verse on p. 322, where the other verses have «རུས་ནི» and a clan's name: is «མཚན་དབང་པོ» the clan, or is «དབང་པོ» the first of two deities with «མེ་ལྷ»?

RD176 second part (2026-11-06), narrowed: the Zhol (img. 1076 line 2) prints «རུས་མཚན་དབང་པོ་མེ་ལྷ» with a clear ཚ, so not a misprint of the reprint; «དབང་པོ་མེ་ལྷ» reads best as the two deities Indra and Agni (a query; no published list in the pool). Question: which published list names Viśākhā's deities as Indra and Agni?

RD176 third part (2026-11-07), the sense settled: the pool's verses use «དབང་པོ» alone for Indra (Jyeṣṭhā) and «མེ་ལྷ» for Agni (Kṛttikā, Hasta), so the two are Viśākhā's deities; the clan's name after «རུས་མཚན» is missing on both prints. See claims/wb-p322-visakha-deities-indra-agni.md.

## Answered 2026-11-07

The sense is settled (RD176 third part, 2026-11-07; corpus/wb/ch33.md, «དབང་པོ་མེ་ལྷ» (wb.2:p322, q648): the sense settled): «དབང་པོ» alone is Indra in the pool's verses (Jyeṣṭhā's «ལྷ་དབང་པོ་དང་རུས་ལེགས་བྱེད») and «མེ་ལྷ» Agni (Kṛttikā's «རྗེ་རིགས་མེ་ལྷ», Hasta's «རུས་ནི་འོད་སྲུངས་ལྷ་མེ་ལྷ»), so «དབང་པོ་མེ་ལྷ» are Viśākhā's two deities, not a clan named «མཚན་དབང་པོ»; «རུས་མཚན» is the Zhol's reading too (img. 1076, line 2, clear ཚ), so no misprint of the reprint. What stays open is only the clan: no name stands after «རུས་མཚན» on either print. Claim wb-p322-visakha-deities-indra-agni.
