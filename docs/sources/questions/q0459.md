# How is the la-knower's animal chosen in the lower table of WB vol. 1 p. 133

In the lower table of WB vol. 1, p. 133, each time of the day names its la-knower («བླ་མཁྱེན») with an animal (tiger at midnight and at sunrise; pig for the ox, snake and bird; monkey for the horse, sheep, monkey and pig), but how is that animal chosen, and does the day's own animal or the time's animal give the la-knower of a day? (RD78)

RD182 (2026-11-06), a lead only: WB's almanac boxes (vol. 1, etext pp. 150–157) pair «བླ་མཁྱེན» with «སྟག་གཟིག», tiger and leopard, so the la-knower's «སྟག» may be that company and not a day animal; the boxes not read on the scan.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-ggg).** BDRC's full-text search for «བླ་མཁྱེན་སྟག» (8 hits) finds the lower table's rows in other copies, in the order time, la-knower, animal: the Bhutan anthology *Dkar rtsis dang nag rtsis kyi gzhung* (MW26041, mediocre OCR) and a Bon students' textbook (a witness only, nothing built on it) give «བླ་མཁྱེན་སྟག» at the sunrise/«ཉི་དྲོས» row, «བླ་མཁྱེན་ཕག» at «ཉིན་ཕྱེད», «བླ་མཁྱེན་སྤྲེལ» at «ཕྱེད་ཡོལ» and «བླ་མཁྱེན་སྦྲུལ» at «ནམ་ལངས» (read from the search snippets in Wylie, not on a scan): the four animals are the la-knower's own and go by the time of the day, not by the day's animal, as the question's second half asked. Which of the twelve times takes which of the four is not read in full (the copies' OCR is broken). The «སྟག་གཟིག» lead of RD182 is the company of the la-knower in the almanac boxes, a second matter.
