# Which three watches does WB vol. 1 p. 17 mean by the night's name «ཐུན་གསུམ་ཀུ་མུད་བཞད་རྣམས», against four watches by night on p. 101 and vol. 2 p. 403?

Does the name rest on the Indian three-watch night?


Narrowed (RD287, 2026-11-10): on the reprint and the 1996 print «ཐུན་གསུམ་ཀུ་མུད་བཞད» is one epithet in a run of the night's names closed by «རྣམས་སོ», not a count of watches; the verse counts none (wb.1:p17c).

## Answered 2026-11-21

Yes, as a name and not as a count. WB's «ཐུན་གསུམ» (thun gsum) in the night's names (vol. 1 p. 17, a run of epithets closed by «རྣམས་སོ») is the Tibetan of Sanskrit triyāmā, "the one that has three watches" (trayo yāmā yasyāḥ sā triyāmā), a synonym of night in the Amarakośa's verse 1.4.5 (Negi's Tibetan-Sanskrit dictionary, Steinert no. 50; claim thun-gsum-is-triyama-in-negi-and-synonym-lexica; answered q941, q945). So the name rests on the Indian night of three watches, which the Bodhisattva-vow and Vinaya literature use as well (claim thun-gsum-night-is-the-three-watches-of-the-vows-and-vinaya): lying down in the middle one only. The name names no three watches by bounds and does not say which three; it is not WB's own count, which on p. 101 and vol. 2 p. 403 is four watches by night, eight in the day-and-night, as in the tantric books (claim wb-watches-five-places-set-against-dbyangs-char-and-tantric-eight). The two counts are not reconciled by WB and need not be: one is a word for the night, the other a division of the session's Rāhu.
