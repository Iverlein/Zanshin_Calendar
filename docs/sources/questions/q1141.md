# Does Tsong kha pa's bgegs mthar byed for Mar me mdzad bzang po's bad-dream remedy come from another work of his than the 450-verse rite

He quotes the rite's thabs sbyor in the vows part (tgb:p360-361) and names the Ender of Obstructors only in the dawn-dream part (snr:p370).

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

A second text gives the same remedy with Mar me mdzad bzang po's name: the Drukpa Vajrāvalī manual of the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod (dkb:p163-164) and, by BDRC's snippet, the Vajrāvalī empowerment manual in the collected works of Mkhas dbang Sangs rgyas rdo rje (vol. 8, VEIE0OPIB2682C2D_I2139, [p137], garbled etext, and MW3CN2232_75FA70 and MW23556_AC28CF). After the lines of the Mañjuśrī root tantra on the dream of the four watches it says «བཟློག་ཐབས་མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད་ཀྱི་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་བཟློག་པ་དང་། འདིར་ཡང་བདུད་རྩི་འཁྱིལ་བའི་སྔགས་བགྲང་བར་བཤད» («the remedy: Mar me mdzad bzang po turns it back by the mantra of the Ender of Obstructors, and here too the mantra Amṛtakuṇḍalin is said to be counted»). BDRC's exact-string search for «མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད» (2026-11-13) has three hits (I3CN2269, I2139, I4161), the first two read as this manual. So the attribution of the Ender to Mar me mdzad bzang po is the Tibetan Vajrāvalī tradition's too, and not only Tsong kha pa's; the manuals are later than the Sngags rim chen mo and may draw on it. No work of Mar me mdzad bzang po was found that has the Ender at a dream (the 450-verse rite does not: mmb). Open still: whether the manuals have it from Tsong kha pa or from a lost Dīpaṃkarabhadra text; the third hit (I4161) was not opened, and the Sangs rgyas rdo rje etext is too garbled to quote beyond the clause.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-vvv)

The third hit of BDRC's phrase search («མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད») is opened: etext VEIE0OPI7F0370A0_I4161, work MW19223, «Dpal 'brug pa'i lugs kyi dbang chog 'dod rgu'i bang mdzod» (a Drukpa empowerment compendium, by various masters, compiled by Slob dpon Yon tan 'od; BDRC person P5878), where it is the section «Dkyil chog rdo rje 'phreng ba'i dbang gzhung mtshams sbyor gyis brgyan pa smin bka'…»: the same sentence as the two others, with the printed page mark [496] inside it. So all three carriers are Drukpa Vajrāvalī empowerment manuals (the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod, Sangs rgyas rdo rje's works, this compendium), none older than the Sngags rim chen mo's tradition is shown to be, and none names a source for the clause. A fourth wording, with «ངན་པས» for the dream's remedy and no Mar me mdzad bzang po (the Hevajra summary «Dpal kye'i rdo rje'i spyi don…»), shows the Ender of Obstructors as the usual turning-back mantra for a falling-from-a-mountain dream in other manuals. Still open: whether Tsong kha pa or a Dīpaṃkarabhadra text is the source of the attribution.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-s)

The Drukpa sentence is Tsong kha pa's own, rearranged, so the three carriers are not independent witnesses of a Tibetan Vajrāvalī tradition. BDRC's exact-phrase search «རི་ལས་ལྷུང་བ་སོགས་རྨིས་ན» (9 hits, snippets read) finds, beside the Drukpa manuals, the Sngags rim chen mo itself (MW1ALS05281E, ACIP, and the Krung go edition of Tsong kha pa's works) and four modern works that copy it (Skal bzang chos kyi rgyal mtshan, Padma rdo rje's *Gnyid dang rmi lam bar gyi 'brel ba*, the Gangs ljongs rig bcu'i snying bcud chen mo). Compare snr:p370, where Tsong kha pa, after «if they dreamed falling from a mountain and the like, he does the guarding», gives three remedies in this order: the Garland's «amṛta whirling mantra», «Mar me mdzad bzang po says the Ender of Obstructors destroys it», «Klu byang says to think on selflessness: two remedies» (བཟློག་ཐབས་གཉིས་སོ), and the Common Tantra's pacifying fire offering. The Drukpa line in the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod (MW3CN2232_75FA70) reads, in the search snippet's Wylie, «dkon mchog gsum mthong ba sogs byung na dga' ba bsgom/ ri las lhung ba sogs rmi na bzlog thabs mar me mdzad bzang pos bgegs mthar byed kyi sngags kyis bzlog pa dang/ 'dir yang bdud rtsi 'khyil ba'i sngags bgrang bar bshad/ klu byang gis/ bdag med…» (the Hevajra summary, MW3CN2232_236751, has «bgegs mthar byed kyi sngags kyis bzlog pa dang/ sbyin sreg bya ba dang/ bdag med pa'i don bsgom pas zhi bar bya»): the same joy for the Three Jewels, the same fall from a mountain, the same Ender of Obstructors, the same amṛta whirling mantra, the same Klu byang and selflessness, and the same fire offering, in the same order as the Sngags rim chen mo's fifth part. Of the hits of the phrase searches, «Klu byang» beside «Mar me mdzad bzang po» stands only in these Drukpa lines and in Tsong kha pa, so the Drukpa authors had this passage in hand, or its source (a likeness of order, not a proof of borrowing). This turns the question: the attribution of the Ender to Mar me mdzad bzang po is Tsong kha pa's wording, repeated, and not a second witness. Whether Tsong kha pa had it from a work of Mar me mdzad bzang po other than the 450-verse rite (mmb) is not said by him or by any text read. BDRC's phrase searches «བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད་ཀྱི་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་བཟློག» 3 hits (the same three), «མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བཟློག», «བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད་ཀྱིས་བཟློག», «རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད», «བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད་ཀྱི་སྔགས་བཟླས་པས་རྨི་ལམ», «རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་བཟློག་པ་མར་མེ་མཛད» 0.
