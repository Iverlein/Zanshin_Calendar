# Does Tsong kha pa's bgegs mthar byed for Mar me mdzad bzang po's bad-dream remedy come from another work of his than the 450-verse rite

He quotes the rite's thabs sbyor in the vows part (tgb:p360-361) and names the Ender of Obstructors only in the dawn-dream part (snr:p370).

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

A second text gives the same remedy with Mar me mdzad bzang po's name: the Drukpa Vajrāvalī manual of the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod (dkb:p163-164) and, by BDRC's snippet, the Vajrāvalī empowerment manual in the collected works of Mkhas dbang Sangs rgyas rdo rje (vol. 8, VEIE0OPIB2682C2D_I2139, [p137], garbled etext, and MW3CN2232_75FA70 and MW23556_AC28CF). After the lines of the Mañjuśrī root tantra on the dream of the four watches it says «བཟློག་ཐབས་མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད་ཀྱི་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་བཟློག་པ་དང་། འདིར་ཡང་བདུད་རྩི་འཁྱིལ་བའི་སྔགས་བགྲང་བར་བཤད» («the remedy: Mar me mdzad bzang po turns it back by the mantra of the Ender of Obstructors, and here too the mantra Amṛtakuṇḍalin is said to be counted»). BDRC's exact-string search for «མར་མེ་མཛད་བཟང་པོས་བགེགས་མཐར་བྱེད» (2026-11-13) has three hits (I3CN2269, I2139, I4161), the first two read as this manual. So the attribution of the Ender to Mar me mdzad bzang po is the Tibetan Vajrāvalī tradition's too, and not only Tsong kha pa's; the manuals are later than the Sngags rim chen mo and may draw on it. No work of Mar me mdzad bzang po was found that has the Ender at a dream (the 450-verse rite does not: mmb). Open still: whether the manuals have it from Tsong kha pa or from a lost Dīpaṃkarabhadra text; the third hit (I4161) was not opened, and the Sangs rgyas rdo rje etext is too garbled to quote beyond the clause.
