# WB vol. 1 p. 65: the true sun and the nearer node differ by one mansion at most?

In WB vol. 1, p. 65, does «སྐར་མ་གཅིག་ལ་བབས་པ་འམ། གཅིག་ཁྱད་འདུག་ཀྱང་ཉུང་གིས་མང» say that the true sun and the nearer node differ by one mansion at most, and does the Mercury and Venus clause of p. 64 take the sun of that test? (RD179; the line is machine-read, the scan blurred.)

INV-1107 (2026-11-07), narrowed: on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5028 img. 72, sharp) lines 1–4 of p. 65 read by eye as the machine text had them, «སྐར་མ་གཅིག་ལ་བབས་པ་འམ། །གཅིག་ཁྱད་འདུག་ཀྱང་ཉུང་གིས་མང་། །སྦྱངས་པའི་ལྷག་མ་བརྟག་གཞི་སྟེ། །གདོང་གིས་ཉི་སྦྱངས་སྐར་གནས་སྟོངས།» (the last two read by Yigdzin-1 as well): «ཉུང་གིས་མང» is an instruction to subtract the fewer from the more and the remainder is the test, so 'one mansion, or with a difference of one' is supported but the clause is a subtraction, not a bare 'at most one'; that p. 64's Mercury and Venus clause takes this sun remains by context only. Claim [wb-p65-true-sun-node-difference-subtracted](../claims/wb-p65-true-sun-node-difference-subtracted.md).

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes for the first part, with the clause being a procedure. On the reprint (I3CN5028 img. 72, p. 65 lines 5-6, scan state in wb.1:p65) the sentence reads: of all three the true sun (ཉི་མ་དག་པ) and whichever of Rāhu's head and tail is nearer «སྐར་མ་གཅིག་ལ་བབས་པ་འམ། །གཅིག་ཁྱད་འདུག་ཀྱང་ཉུང་གིས་མང་། །སྤྱངས་པའི་ལྷག་མ་བརྟག་གཞི་སྟེ།»: either they fall on one mansion, or, even if a difference of one is present, the fewer is taken from the more, and the remainder is the ground of the test. So the two differ by one mansion at most for this test to be worked (the difference of one is the largest named), and what is tested is the remainder after the subtraction.

The second part is settled by the syntax of the page. p. 64's Mercury and Venus clause ends «འདི་ལ་གྲུབ་རྩིས་ལེ་ཚན་དང་། །ཅུང་ཟད་བསྟུན་ཙམ་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།» ("for this the true-reckoning chapter and some agreement are needed, because"), and p. 65's first line begins with «ཕྱིར།» and goes straight on to the true reckoning's northward travel and the sun of the three reckonings (the true reckoning's northward and southward travel, «བྱང་བགྲོད» and «ལྷོ་བགྲོད», and the Abridged Tantra). The clause therefore hands over to this test, and the sun it uses is the true sun of the three; the clause itself writes only "the sun" without a qualifier. The three are those two travels together with the Abridged Tantra of lines 1-3 of p. 65. Not computed with core/: the passage gives the rule in words, and the sixtieths it works on are not stated in the lines read.
