# The kung of «ཐུན་ཀུང» in the hand reckoning

What is the kung (ཀུང) of «ཐུན་ཀུང», which the hand reckoning counts after the year and the watch? No passage found explaining the watch or the kung (RD165).

RD177 (2026-11-06): «ཐུན་ཀུང» occurs in no other etext (the 1996 etext reads «ཐུན་ཀཱང»); lead: kung = «གུང» (noon), as in the notes' «གུང་གཉིས་ཟེར་གཉིས».

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-e)

The word «ཀུང» is the dictionaries' «གུང» (gung), a time-of-day word, and BDRC shows the pair in use. Steinert's public dictionaries: «gung» is the middle of the day or the night, noon or midnight (Rangjung Yeshe, Hackett, Valby, Ives Waldo); the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* lists «nyin gung» and «mtshan gung» (day-gung, night-gung) under «gung» 2; the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* says «gung» is "the name of a time of the twelve small parts of the day and the twenty-four of the night" (nyin gung, mtshan gung); Negi's Sanskrit dictionary gives «gung thun» as *madhyame yāme*, the middle watch (from the *Abhisamayālaṅkārāloka*). BDRC's exact search «ཐུན་གུང» (21 hits, snippets only, in Wylie: Kongtrul's *Rin chen gter mdzod*, Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams' collected works, Nam mkha' seng ge's, Nus ldan rdo rje's) shows «thun» and «gung» side by side in the practice texts' account of the sessions of the day («srod thun gung thun bsdus pa», «zhogs thun gung tshigs», «sa dro'i thun gung»), so the pair «thun gung» is a real phrase there, though a session-and-its-middle of a practice, not a reckoning. So the hand reckoning's «ཐུན་ཀུང» most likely counts the watch of the day and the middle (gung) of it, with ཀ written for ག as the 1996 etext's «ཀཱང» and the Gser thang's «ཀུང» suggest. No text says what the hand reckoning then does with them (which joints they fall on), and no commentary of the White Beryl glosses the phrase; the spelling ཀུང against གུང is not shown on the block print. Stays open for the joints.
