# Unidentified things of the funeral share and the corpse kinds

Several things in the funeral share and the corpse kinds are not identified: «གཡེར་མའི་ཆུ», «སྨན་ལེ་འབྲང་རྒྱས་བྱེ་ཐག», «བསེ་རགས་བྱུར་ཤིད», «འབྲང་རྒྱས» (pp. 68–69); «གསང་ཤིད་ཟུ», «བང་ས་དུ་རུ་ཅན» (p. 72); «ཕན་ངོན», «ཐལ་ཤལ», the third, sixth, eighth and tenth blades (p. 73). What are they?

**Narrowed 2026-11-05 (RD161, [word-hunts.md](../word-hunts.md)).** Answered: bse rags byur shid is the pauper's funeral, not a hide belt. Narrowed: sman le 'brang rgyas, gsang shid, bang sa, phan ngon, thal shal. Left: bye thag and the blades.

**Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-c).** BDRC phrase search: «བྱེ་ཐག་ཅན» 1 hit, MW3CN3228 (Go shul Grags pa 'byung gnas's grammar of Tibetan letters, a quotation from the Sba bzhed: «mchod rten bye thag can yang brtsigs», "a stupa with the *bye thag* was also built"; snippet only, a different context from the blades, but a place where *bye thag* is a thing of building); «ཐལ་ཤལ་ཅན» and «བཙལ་ཕན་ངོན» 0; «བང་ས་དུ་རུ» and «གསང་ཤིད་ཟུ» only WB's prints (I1KG12907, I3CN5029; the first also I1KG12740); «སྨན་ལེ་འབྲང་རྒྱས» five (WB prints, I1KG10634, I3690, I1KG81102; already known); «བྱེས་ཀྱི་གྲི» 2, WB's own etexts (MW2CZ8040, MW3CN5026), whose OCR puts the footnote marks as «rta gyi (5) thal shal byes kyi gri (6)», so *thal shal* stands in the sixth blade's name, not as a blade of its own (OCR, not read on a scan). The blades and *bye thag* stay open.
