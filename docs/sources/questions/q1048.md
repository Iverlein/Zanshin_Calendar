# In the lines «བྱ་བ་དུས་ཀྱི་རིམ་པ་ཉིད» (mmk.1:p400-401) is the Sanskrit's word «krama» (order) or «sama» (equal), and does «རྨི་ལམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ཡོད་པ་སྤངས» mean all dreams are set aside or only the «dream-named» kind?

RD372.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The first half is answered by the claim mmk-rim-pa-is-krama-not-sama (RD395): «རིམ་པ» is krama, order; «sama» is «མཉམ་པ». The second half stays open: «ཞེས་བྱ་ཡོད་པ» is «the so-called», which leans to the dream-named kind only being set aside, and no commentary on the lines was found. In the Derge volume the same twelve signs and the true-dream pages (mmk.1:p403) follow at once, which also speaks against all dreams being given up.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14

The first half is answered by q1136: the Sanskrit of Gaṇapati Śāstrī's edition (GRETIL keying, mmk.2:p149b) reads «kriyākālasamaś», sama, not krama. For the second half the same verse gives a new fact: it reads «svapnākhyaṃ sattvavarjitam», and the Tibetan renders it word for word: «ཞེས་བྱ» is ākhya ("called"), «ཡོད་པ» stands where the Sanskrit has sattva, «སྤངས» is varjita. So the pāda says "the so-called dream (is) devoid of being (sattva)", a statement about what the thing named dream is, and not a rule that all dreams are put aside; it does not set aside a dream-named kind among others either. The doubt: sattva is "being, existence" or "a living creature", and a commentary was not found to say which (the exact phrase «རྨི་ལམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ཡོད་པ་སྤངས» has the same two hits as before, BDRC search of 2026-11-14), so the reading rests on the Sanskrit's word order and not on a gloss. Etext of a modern edition, not a page image.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z4)

Second half, a translator's reading from the 84000 TEI of Toh 543 (github.com/84000/data-tei, translations/kangyur/translations/088-038_toh543-the_root_tantra_of_manjusri.xml, fetched 2026-11-16), which now carries chapter 15 with its Sanskrit and notes. The 84000 note on 15.53 says: "Possibly the Skt. sattva should be emended to tattva ('reality', 'truth'), as the meaning would then be that dreams are 'devoid of reality', which would tie in better with the next half-stanza which mentions 'those who show reality (tattvadarśibhiḥ)'", and the English renders the pāda «tasmāt sarvaprakāreṇa svapnākhyaṃ sattvavarjitam» as "Thus, that which is designated as a dream is devoid, in all its [three] types, of pure essence" and the Tibetan as "Since what is called a dream is based on mental images, it is devoid of existence". Both take the line as a statement about what a dream is, as the 2026-11-14 note did, and neither as a rule that all dreams are set aside; the "three types" are the three humours' dreams of the preceding verses (phlegm, bile, wind), and the verse follows the sentence that dreams always come from a mixture of the humours (15.52). That fits the reading that the line closes the account of humoral dreams, not a dream-named kind.
