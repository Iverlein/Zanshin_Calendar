# The byi sker and the sa khyi of the rat day's wool-thread remedy

What is the byi sker of SY's «བལ་སྐུད་བྱི་སྐེར་བཏགས་པས་ཐུབ» (printed p. 345): the rat's neck, a house-mouse's, or a name; and is the sa khyi of the Drigung verse work's «བལ་གྱི་སྐུད་པ་ས་ཁྱི་སྐོར་བཏགས» the earth-dog of the year or a figure of dough? (RD162, etext only)

RD173 (2026-11-06), narrowed: both lines are WB's own (vol. 2, p. 359: «བལ་སྐུད་ཀྱི་སྐེར་བཏགས་པས་ཐུབ», a wool thread tied at the neck, «སྐེར»); SY's «བྱི་སྐེར» and the verse work's «ས་ཁྱི་སྐོར» are copyists' variants of it, so no earth-dog and no dough figure; whose neck the line leaves unsaid. RD173 (2026-11-06), narrowed: WB's own line (p. 359) is «བལ་སྐུད་ཀྱི་སྐེར་བཏགས», a wool thread tied at the neck («སྐེར»); SY's «བྱི་སྐེར» and the verse work's «ས་ཁྱི་སྐོར» are copyists' variants of it, so the sa khyi is not the year's earth-dog nor a dough figure. Whose neck is open. The rat day's «ཆང་རྫས» is the versifier's wording (q655).

## Answered 2026-11-14

Neither: both are copyists' variants of WB's own «བལ་སྐུད་ཀྱི་སྐེར་བཏགས་པས་ཐུབ» (vol. 2, p. 359), a wool thread tied on the neck (ཀྱི་སྐེར, kyi sker; «སྐེར» is «སྐེ», neck, with the ར). The dictionaries of Steinert's set (read 2026-11-14) give «ཁྱི» nothing like an earth-dog here and «བྱི» only as to lose hair, to be bald, a mouse (Ives Waldo, Valby, Tshig mdzod); «སྐེར» only as "= ske ru" (Negi), «སྐེ» as neck, throat (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, Tshig mdzod), and «བལ་སྐུད» as wool thread (Rangjung Yeshe, Valby, Ives Waldo). So «བྱི་སྐེར» is no rat's neck and no name, and «ས་ཁྱི་སྐོར» is no earth-dog of the year and no dough figure: the sa khyi is the copyist's «ཀྱི་སྐེར». The same formula, «སྐེར་བཏགས་ཉེས་པ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན», stands in the lunar-date verses of lunar-dates.md (five-colour wool bound on the donkey's muzzle), where it likewise tells of a thing tied on at the neck and leaves the neck's owner unnamed; the rat day's line does the same.
