# The Brahmā lineage's count of the steelyard's rope

In the Brahmā lineage (MW2PD17386, «ཚངས་པ་ལྷ་རྒྱུད»), a manuscript etext in outline, how does the count of the steelyard's rope «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ» run (the snippet gives 'eight up' from the place where the landed year falls) and does it hold the other gates of WB's thirteen? RD168 (2026-11-06): «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ» has 0 hits; the work «ཚངས་པ་ལྷ་རྒྱུད» is item 3 of MW2PD17386, search-only.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-iii).** BDRC's phrase search «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ་ཆད་དམ་མ་ཆད» (1 hit), «ཡར་བརྒྱད་འགྱངས» and «རྒྱ་མའི་ཐུག་ས» show more of the Brahmā lineage's snippet (MW2PD17386_E5D8B1, BDRC's noisy Wylie, search-only): «sa tshod gang shar gyi la do gnyis phab ste rgya ma'i thag pa chad dam ma chad … 'bas pa'i lo yis dus tshod la bcad na rgya ma'i thug sa bsangs sa de nas yar brgyad 'gyangs sa'i mdo dang cha sa chod kyis ma bcad na du ma bcad de / de bzhin bya ba la …». So the count runs: the place the moment falls on gets the pebbles; whether the steelyard's rope is cut is judged by the landed year cutting the moment; from the place of the rope's cut eight up is counted; if the year does not cut, nothing is cut, and "so it is done" for the rest. That is the structure of WB's first gate (the landed year cutting the moment cuts the rope; the year eight up fights), but the snippet ends there and gives nothing on the other gates; the Utpala Garland (MW25167, etext p224) uses the rope only as a remedy rule («རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ་མ་ཆད་དགོས། རྒྱ་མའི་ཐག་པ་ཆད་པ་ན། ཤ་རྒྱས་ཁྱོག་ལ་ཀེག་སེལ…») and, in its list of the places counted up, «རྒྱ་མའི་མཐོ་དགོས་མ་བབས་དགོས» for the eighth, the mother-side uncle's place. Whether the Brahmā lineage holds the other twelve gates stays open: the etext answers 403.
