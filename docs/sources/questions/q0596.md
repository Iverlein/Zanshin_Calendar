# «སྤོས་གྲི་རྒོད» and «གླང་ཐབས» in the copper vessel's blue mewa line

What are «སྤོས་གྲི་རྒོད» and «གླང་ཐབས» in the blue mewa line of the copper vessel (WB vol. 2, p. 55, l. 2: «གླང་ཐབས་ལྷོག་པ་སྤོས་གྲི་རྒོད»), two harms beside the illness «ལྷོག་པ», or rites?

## Looked at 2026-11-16 (inv-u)

Narrowed: «གླང་ཐབས» and «ལྷོག་པ» are illnesses, not rites. The line stands in the verse on the corpses of the ancestors dead of epidemic, hidden by a rock («ཕ་མེས་རིམས་ཀྱི་རོ། ནའམ་ཕ་བོང་རྩར་སྦས་པས། གླང་ཐབས་ལྷོག་པ་སྤོས་གྲི་རྒོད»), which is what these harms come from. Dictionaries (Steinert's sets): «glang thabs» colic, stomach pain (Rangjung Yeshe; Valby "colic, gripes, spasms in the stomach, sudden"); Ives-Waldo "disease; sudden"; Dan Martin quoting the *Rgyud bzhi*'s commentary (SRZT 90) "colic" of hot, cold, worm and infectious kinds; «lhog pa» a large ulcer or sore (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives-Waldo; Valby "swelling in the throat", a hot disease; *Tshig mdzod chen mo*: a disease from epidemic falling on the flesh with swelling and pustules). Two medical works list them together: «གླང་ཐབས་ལྷོག་པ་གཟེར་ཐུང་འཇོམས། ཚད་པ་ཀུན་སེལ་ཁྱད་པར།» in the *Dpag bsam ljon shing*'s verse on the herb (quoted in Tsi bha's *Bod sman rtsi shing rig pa* MW1GS60399, 2007, and in Dga' ba'i rdo rje's *'Khrungs dpe dri med shel gyi me long* MW20069, 1995, search-only, snippets read; the herb is *Thalictrum*), so "colic and boils, stabbing pain" are the illnesses of the verse. «སྤོས་གྲི» stays unsettled: WB's other line «ནད་དུ་གཟེར་ཐབས་སྤོས་གྲི་དང་» (health.md) also sets it beside the stabbing («gzer thabs», vāta-pratoda, wind's goad, Negi) and shows no gloss; «རྒོད» stays unplaced (it may close the three: "wild", or open the next word). The Steinert sets have no headword «spos gri», «spo gri», «gri rgod». Dead ends: «གླང་ཐབས་ལྷོག་པ» 2 hits (both medical, above); «ཕ་བོང་རྩར་སྦས» 1 hit (WB's own copy, I1KG12907); «ཕ་མེས་རིམས་ཀྱི་རོ» 3 hits (WB's copies, I1KG81102 not opened); «གྲོན་གྲི་རྒོད» 0.
