# Does work 17's first syllable on p. 378 carry a subjoined ra, and what are «ཕྱར» and «ཕྱ་ཐེར»

Does the first syllable of work 17 on p. 378 carry a subjoined ra («སྦྲ»), and what are «ཕྱར» and «ཕྱ་ཐེར» of «སྦྲ་ཕྱར» and «ཕྱ་ཐེར་སྦྲ་འཚེམ»? The two clear prints show one stack at their size; the later verses and the Bhutan digest spell «སྦྲ»; neither word is glossed anywhere read.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-bbb).** The 1996 and Gser thang etexts of the verse lists read «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ» and «ཕྱ་ཐེར་སྦྲ་འཚེམ» with the subjoined ra; Steinert's dictionaries give «sbra» a yak-hair or felt tent (Mahāvyutpatti «paṭakuṭī») and «phyar ba» a covering sheet of wool, hair or hide (Mahāvyutpatti «kutapa»); no headword «sba» fits the slot. Two more manuals write the pair in neighbouring lines: «sbra dang phya ther 'tshem pa la» and «phur bu sbra phyar 'tshem pa bzang» (Rtsis rig shes bya kun gsal; Bod kyi skar rtsis rig pa'i rnam bzhag, snippets). The glyph on the p. 378 scans (1996 img. 386, reprint img. 384) is not told apart by eye; scan-bound, the Taikhang print's ra (RD68) stands.
