The colour word with the ལྗ head is therefore the one the texts use (spelt ལྗང་གུ there, ལྗང་ཁུ in the print), and the Delhi machine reading «ལྡང» is most likely the reader's slip for ལྗ; the scan remains the only proof. The mantra of the first verse: no hit again.

**Narrowed 2026-11-17 (sweep from inv-g).** BDRC searches show the colour word with the ལྗ head is the one the texts use («ལྷ་མིན་ལྗང་གུ» in the Ras chung snyan brgyud) and «ལྡང་ཁུ» occurs nowhere as a colour, so the Delhi machine reading «ལྡང» is most likely a slip for ལྗ; the scan and the first-verse mantra remain unread.

## Narrowed 2026-11-24 (inv-k)

The colour: in the four WB etexts (1996 and Gser thang, both volumes) «ལྗང་ཁུ» stands 2 times and «ལྗང་གུ» 40, «ལྡང་ཁུ» not once and «ལྡང་གུ» once: in the vol. 2 reprint etext (I3CN5029), «ཆས་དང་རུ་ཚོན་ལྡང་གུ་ཡིས», a dye-colour phrase where the sense is green and where the 1996 etext's parallel is not found: that is the same OCR slip of the reprint's ལྗ as the Delhi print's reading, which shows the print's ལྗ is read as ལྡ by machines, not that WB writes ལྡང་གུ. The pool's own corpus has «ལྗང་ཁུ» in two prints of this rite and in ch. 29's «བཞི་ལྗང་ཁུ». So the green of the six lights is «ལྗང་ཁུ» (ljang khu), and the Delhi print's machine «ལྡང» is a slip of the reader (as inv-g said; now with the count). Still open: the first verse's mantra and Bu ston's own text, which are not in WB.
