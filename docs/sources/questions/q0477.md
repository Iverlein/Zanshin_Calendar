# Is the feat of the body on p. 440 «ཟོར་ཚུགས» or «ཐོར་ཚུགས»

On p. 440 (img. 446) the list of feats of the body has «ཟོར་ཚུགས» (Yigdzin-1) or «ཐོར་ཚུགས» (MITRA) before «གོམ་སྟབས་»; which is the word, and what is the feat? (RD84)

**Answered in part 2026-10-31 (RD84, eye pass, [corpus/wb/ch35.md](../corpus/wb/ch35.md#wb-2-p440)).** The print has «ཐོར་ཚུགས» (MITRA's reading), not «ཟོར»: on the Gser thang scan (img. 446, line 21), enlarged three and four times beside the page's own «ཐོས» and «བཟོ», the letter has the open frame and inner stroke of ཐ. What feat the word names is still open.

RD178 (2026-11-07): not reached. The Zhol image of p. 440 was not located (cached scans lacked img. 1221–1228 until fetched; the Yigdzin-1 run for a page map was cut short by the time box). Taikhang and reprint not opened for it.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The print has «ཐོར་ཚུགས» (thor tshugs), as the eye pass of RD84 read it; «ཟོར» is not found. BDRC's search for «ཐོར་ཚུགས་གོམ་སྟབས» finds the run in six etexts: the White Beryl's 1996 etext (I1KG12907), the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029) and the Zhol print (I1KG1710) are among them, with the Do shal's etexts as snippets, besides two manuals (below), all with «ཐོར»; «ཟོར་ཚུགས» has four hits, none of them White Beryl (Nyingma sādhana texts of the Rin chen gter mdzod and a local history, where «zor tshugs» is a hair-knot tied to one side, «zor tshugs yon du bcings ba»: the same thing under another spelling, not a variant of WB's text).

What the feat is: the same run of the arts stands, in the same order, in two Tibetan lists of the arts that the same search finds: «yi ge, lag rtsis, grangs, rtsis chen, thor tshugs, gom stabs, lcags kyus sgyur thabs, ral gri'i thabs, zhags pa gdab pa, mda' bo che 'phen pa, mdun du bsnur …» (the Mi 'jigs seng ge'i nga ro, a commentary on a poetic treatise that lists the nine moods of the dance and then the crafts, I5719, and the chapter on crafts of the commentary on the "Mdo rgyud rin po che'i mdzod", I1ER1054; both etext snippets): WB's «ཡི་གེ་བགྲང་རྩིས་དང་། ཐོར་ཚུགས་གོམ་སྟབས་ལྕགས་ཀྱུས་བསྒྱུར། རལ་གྲི་ཞགས་པ་མདའ་ཆེན་འཕེན།» is that list in verse (the White Beryl's own list of the arts, before the sixty-four proper on p. 441). The word is the dictionaries' «thor tshugs»: a topknot, the hair bound up on the crown (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Jim Valby, Ives Waldo; Mahāvyutpatti «śikhābandha»), and Negi's dictionary quotes the Lalitavistara's list of arts with it as one of them: «mchongs pa dang … thor tshugs dang … spos sbyar ba la sogs pa'i sgyu rtsal» = «laṅghite … śikhābande … gandhayuktau» (la.vi. 80kha/108), a "skill of the arts", śikhābandha. So the feat of the body is the art of binding up the hair into a topknot (tying the hair), one of the skills of the art lists, named before the gait.
