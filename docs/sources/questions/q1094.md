# Rngog's exposition says the black girl «dus mtshan» is «Yama's mother (or consort), yum», and the etext prints «dus mtshams»: is this «dus mtshan» and does any teacher of the tradition say why her sign is seven days?

RD382.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-ds)

The first half is settled: «dus mtshan» is kālarātrī (Schneider's Sanskrit of 'Deceiving Death' 1.127 and the Saṃvarodaya 19.23; claim mvup-sanskrit-parallels-sut19), and the Tibetan gloss in Schneider's apparatus to that verse calls her «gshin rje'i chung ma», the wife of the death god, which agrees with Rngog's «yum» read as consort; Schneider adds that dictionaries give kālarātrī as a personification of the world's end, Durgā or Yamī. Why her sign is seven days: no text says; the Sanskrit gives only «saptāhān mṛtyukārikā». BDRC exact phrases «གཤིན་རྗེའི་ཡུམ» 48 hits (the Rin chen gter mdzod praises, Sa skya bka' 'bum, Tāranātha's volumes on Remati/Yamī, none on the death sign; the Tāranātha hit glosses «gshin rje mo» as Yama's consort), «དུས་མཚན་མོ་ནི་གཤིན་རྗེ» and «དུས་མཚན་ནི་གཤིན་རྗེའི» none.
