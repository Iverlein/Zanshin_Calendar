# Are WB's eight watches on p. 403 («ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད») eight of the day and night together, or eight of the day with a further eight of the night? Its division line reads «ཉིན་ཚད», its first line «ཉིན་མཚན».

RD333.

Narrowed (inv-y, 2026-11-16): BDRC's exact phrase «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» (8 hits) has the same wording in the verse of the Bai DUrya dkar po (VEIE0OPI51524892_I1KG12907, etext p. 402 f.; again in the I3CN5029 reprint and the do shal): «རང་དུས་ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད་ལ། ཕྱེ་བས་ཐུན་རེ་གཟའ་རེ་དང་། ཀླུ་རེ་ལྷན་ཅིག་འཆར་བ་ལས།» followed by the sun, moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn and Rāhu-with-Rigs ldan as the eight, so each of the eight watches of day-and-night as one has one weekday-planet and one klu; and a Phug-lugs table (I3CN8749, etext p. 75) sets «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད་ཀྱི་ཆ་གྲངས» beside «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག་གི་ཆ་གྲངས», eight watches against sixteen half-watches of the day-and-night together. So the phrase in the calendar tradition is eight for the day and night as one (sixteen half-watches), not eight of the day and eight of the night. The passage is not WB's own page; WB p. 403 is read by the same phrase, not shown to be the same verse. A terma text (Rtsa gsum gling pa) has «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད་བཅུ་དྲུག» in another, practice, sense.
