# WB ch. 2 p. 19 line 1: does «རྒུའི» or «ཀྲུའི» stand at the head, and which Sanskrit name stands behind that group of syllables?

The stacks are clotted on the 1-bit reprint past 3.5x.

## Answered 2026-11-21

«རྒུའི» stands at the head of p. 19, not «ཀྲུའི» and not MITRA's «རྒྱའི»: the line is the second half of the name «སྐྱེ་རྒུའི་བདག་པོ», "lord of beings", the Sanskrit Prajāpati, the presiding deity of Rohiṇī, whose mansion the line closes with «སྣར་མའོ» (Snar ma).

Two checks that do not rest on the scan. (1) The line is cut across the page turn: the last syllable of p. 18 (img. 25) is «སྐྱེ་», printed at the foot after «བི་རྫི་དལ་བའི་ལྷ་ལྡན་མ། །», so the first syllable of p. 19 must be a word that follows «སྐྱེ» to make «སྐྱེ་རྒུ», the common Tibetan word for beings, and «ཀྲུའི» or «རྒྱའི» make no word with it. The 1996 etext (I1KG12906) carries the page marker inside the phrase, «…ལྷ་ལྡན་མ། །སྐྱེ་ [p29] རྒུའི་བདག་པོ་སྣར་མའོ། །», and the typeset *Bod kyi rtsis rig kun 'dus chen mo* vol. 2 (VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4716, p. 41), which copies the verse independently of either print, has «སྐྱེ་རྒུའི་བདག་པོ་སྣར་མའོ». (2) The same word «སྐྱེ་རྒུ» stands in the yoga list of the same page of WB («དུལ་བ་སྐྱེ་རྒུ་གཞོན་བྱ་རོག»), spelt with ར.

So the Sanskrit name behind the group is Prajāpati (skye rgu'i bdag po), Rohiṇī's deity; the mansion is Rohiṇī, Tibetan Snar ma. The print's own stack was not separated from «ཀྲུ» at 2-3x, and it need not be: the sense and two text witnesses decide the spelling. Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-u).
