# Two rites of the day's twelve for the dead, p. 225

On p. 225 (the dead and the day's twelve), what do «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན» (rat day) and «སྤང་མ་ཕུད་གཏོར་ཡོལ་བས་སྒྲིབ» mean?

## Answered 2026-11-14

inv-fff (2026-11-14), recounted from the pool: the two lines are on p. 224, in the rat day's paragraph (reprint img. 230, l. 6), and the question is carried by its two halves. The first, «སྦང་མ་ཕུད་གཏོར་ཡོལ་བས་སྒྲིབ», is answered in q440: the first portion scattered is the beer's mash («སྦང་མ», the reprint has no subjoined ra; claim wb-sbang-ma-is-the-beer-mash), and the cloth is spread over the beer and the jar, not the threshold. In the second, «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན», the verb is «འཚག» "to strain" (claim wb-rat-day-yo-nas-wb-own-table; WB's own table, vol. 1 p. 133, has the short form «ཁ་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན»): the beer is not strained on the threshold, and «ཡོ་ནས་» is then drawn or served. What «ཡོ» is (the jar's mouth askew, "all", or a place) and whose «འདྲེན» it is (the beer's or the corpse's) no dictionary or manual gives (claim wb-yo-nas-in-the-dictionaries); that one word is kept in q439 and q446. This question is closed as superseded by q439, q440, q446 and q447.
