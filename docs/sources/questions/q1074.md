# Who are the «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག» (some of the Chinese and Nepalese) of the Mchog dga', whose lines give Saturn «bad company and climbing hills» in the dream (kd.3:p177-179)? No print of the root has the lines; BDRC's exact-phrase search finds them in the Kun 'dus chen mo only.

RD376.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The phrase is not peculiar to the dream lines: the Mchog dga' uses the same «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག» three more times in vol. 3, at pp. 71, 77 and 87 (kd.3:p71, kd.3:p77, kd.3:p87, read 2026-11-11), each time beside the Kālacakra commentators and as the holder of a calendar reckoning (the spelling «mon gre» of the mansion, the long and short vowels set in the directions and corners, the verse of Rāhu and the planets by direction). So «some of the Chinese and Nepalese» is the Mchog dga's regular label for a body of Kālacakra-style reckoning, of outside origin, and the Saturn lines of the dream are a fourth place where it quotes their verse. No name or title is given in any of them, and the lines are found in the Kun 'dus chen mo only (BDRC search 2026-11-10), so the book is not identified.

## Searched 2026-11-14 (inv-yyy), the meaning of «རྒྱ་བལ»

The Derge etext of the Dbyangs 'char (VE23703_1521) has no «རྒྱ་བལ» at all, so the label is the Mchog dga's. BDRC's exact phrase «རྒྱ་བལ་གྱི་ལུགས» (13 hits, 2026-11-14) is a ritual idiom: Tāranātha's Collected Works (MW1PD45495), the Mi 'khrugs pa texts of Khams sprul (MW1KG4191) and Kong sprul's collection (MW3CN2232) say of the torma's shape «རྒྱ་བལ་གྱི་ལུགས་ལ་ངེས་བཟུང་མེད་ཀྱང་བོད་འདིར་བླ་མ་གོང་མའི་ཕྱག་བཞེས་རང་རང་གི་ལུགས་སྲོལ་ལྟར་དུ་བྱས་པས་ཆོག» ("in the rgya bal way there is no fixed rule, but here in Tibet it is enough to do it as each earlier lama's own custom holds"), so «རྒྱ་བལ» there is the foreign side set against «བོད་འདིར», the lands from which Buddhist rites came. The dictionaries of Steinert's set (locate.py dict): Ives Waldo has «rgya bal: India/ CH and Nepal»; Rangjung Yeshe has «rgya bal: Chinese wool», which is another word; «bal po» is Nepal in every set. So «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག» is best read «some of the Indians (or Chinese) and Nepalese», the foreign authorities, in keeping with its three other places beside the Kālacakra and its commentators (kd.3:p71, p77, p87). Which book or school it names is still not shown: the label has no further hit («རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག་གི་རྩིས», «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག་གི་ལུགས», «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག་འདོད», «རྒྱ་བལ་གྱི་དུས་འཁོར» 0 hits; «རྒྱ་བལ་ཁ་ཅིག་གིས་» two, only the two prints of the Mchog dga').
