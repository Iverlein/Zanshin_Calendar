# WB vol. 2 p. 409: the narrow sixth line between «བྲ་ཉེ་ལག» and «སྨིན་དྲུག་དང་», its words and the human family's mansions

On WB vol. 2 p. 409 (reprint img. 415, y about 1250–1330) a narrow line between «བྲ་ཉེ་ལག» and the full-width «སྨིན་དྲུག་དང་།» line is missing from the machine text; it reads roughly «…མ་རྣམས་ནི་(མི?)་ཡི་རིགས། །མཆེ/མཁེ་དང་དག་པ·». Does it name the human family's mansions and end the list of the gods', the humans' and the rākṣasas' families, does another print of WB have it, and what are its words?

## Answered 2026-11-21

Yes: the narrow line closes the list of the human family's mansions and opens the rākṣasa family's, and another print has it.

The Zhol print's etext (BDRC IE0OPI7850CC9C, image 1175; OCR, many syllables garbled) runs through the same passage with the line in place, between «དབོ་དང་ཆུ་སྨད་བྲ་ཉེ་ལག» and «སྨིན་དྲུག་དང་»: «… ལྟར་མནམས་ནི་མི་ཡི་རིགས། །མཚུ་དང་ནག་པ་སྨིན་དྲུག་དང་། །མོན་དེ་མོན་གྲུ་ས་ག་དང་། །སྣོན་སྐག་སྣབས་རྣམས་སྲིན་པོའི་རིགས།». Read against the reprint's «…མ་རྣམས་ནི་མི་ཡི་རིགས། །མཆེ/མཁེ་དང་དག་པ·» this is «[སྣར]་མ་རྣམས་ནི་མི་ཡི་རིགས། །མཆུ་དང་ནག་པ་སྨིན་དྲུག་དང་།»: the reprint's fifth syllable is «མི» (the Zhol text agrees with the sixteenth pass), the blotted first stack, which the OCR gives as «ལྟར», fits «སྣར» (Snarma, སྣར་མ), the syllable before «དང» is «མཆུ» (Mchu), as the pass guessed, and the reprint's «དག་པ» is «ནག་པ» (Nag pa), whose ན the Zhol text has. The Sakya Centre etext (wb2, image 561) skips the line in its OCR and gives nothing against it.

What the line names: the human family's last mansion, Snarma, with «…རྣམས་ནི་མི་ཡི་རིགས» ("these are the human family"), then Mchu and Nag pa, the first of the rākṣasa family, which goes on with Smindrug, Mon dre, Mon gru, Saga, Snron, Skag and Snubs (that family is "the rākṣasa's", as the sixteenth pass read its last line). So the three families have nine mansions each, 27 in all: the gods' (Gyal, Tha skar, Nabs so, Mgo, Nam gru, Lha mtshams, Sa ri, Gro bzhin, Me bzhi), the humans' (Chu stod, Khrums stod, Khrums smad, Gre, Dbo, Chu smad, Bra nye, Lag, Snarma) and the rākṣasas' (Mchu, Nag pa, Smindrug, Mon dre, Mon gru, Saga, Snron, Skag, Snubs). The count and the Zhol text agree; the first syllable of the line stays a blotted stack on the reprint, read «སྣར» from the Zhol OCR and from the count of nine, not letter by letter.
