# What are «རྦད་མགོ» and «དྲྭ་ཕད» among the Drukpa realised ones' marks in the Blue Beryl's clause on those of like kind to the good messenger?

RD342; the exact phrase «རྦད་མགོ་དང་དྲྭ་ཕད» has one BDRC hit, in the Blue Beryl itself.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Two ordinary words of the dictionaries, not terms of a school: «རྦད་མགོ» (rbad mgo) is the fringe of hair that covers the forehead, a head with its hair cut in a fringe (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho, Tshig mdzod chen mo; Ives Waldo, "bangs"), and «དྲྭ་ཕད» (drwa phad, keyed in the dictionaries «dra phad») is a bag made of a net of cord, also named as the waistband (ske rags) in the Gold Mirror of Brda dkrol. The Drukpa realised ones' marks in the Blue Beryl's clause are therefore a fringe of hair instead of a shaved head, a net-bag or net-belt, the thighbone trumpet and bone ornaments: the dress of the wandering yogi, set beside the monks who follow Shakyamuni. The spelling «drwa phad» is found in the same sense in a Kāvyādarśa gloss (the verse that the dictionary has with «dra phad») and in a Sdom gsum commentary's list of ornaments (damaru, bells, net-bag, rosary); a modern copy of the clause has «dwa phan». The dictionaries' scans and the Blue Beryl's page were not seen (state etext); the Blue Beryl does not say whether the drwa phad is bag or belt.
