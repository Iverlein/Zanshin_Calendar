# In the entries of the hour's elements, do the lines on the asker's bed, pillow and burnt food, with «the dream is troubled; the father and the forefathers; of five or three brothers a death by the knife; the god a white man on a white horse» (kd.3:p588-589; WB vol. 2 p. 160), tell of the asker's own last night before coming, as the diviner's patter of a pebble asking, or of the sleep of a sick one?

## Searched 2026-11-14 (inv-yyy), no new text

BDRC's exact-phrase search for the lines' own wording («གདན་ནི་སྔོ་ནག་པོ་ཞིག», «ཤིང་གིས་སྔས་ཀྱང་བྱས») has no hit; «རྨི་ལམ་འཚུབ་ལ་ཕ་མེས» has three, the Kun 'dus chen mo (VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4717) and two other prints of the same Garland-of-Stars entries (VEIE0OPI51524892_I1KG12907, VEIE0OPI3864355D_I3CN5029), so no other genre quotes the lines and no gloss says whose night it is. The claim hour-entry-bed-lines-the-diviners-seeing-not-a-sick-bed stands: the verb is «འདུག», the diviner's seeing of the asker; the text has no word of illness, and none of «yesterday», «last night» or «before coming», so it cannot be told from the text whether the asker's last night is meant.
