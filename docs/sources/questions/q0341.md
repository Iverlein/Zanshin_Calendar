# «མཚོལ» or «མནོལ» on p. 165

On p. 165, is the scan's «མཚོལ» (Yigdzin-1 «མནོལ») a spelling of «མནོལ», defilement, in "struck by «མཚོལ» and widowhood"?

## Answered 2026-11-14

The word is «མནོལ» (mnol), defilement. BDRC's full-text search for the line's string finds «མནོལ་དང་ཡུགས» (mnol dang yugs) in nine etexts (the 1996 print I1KG12907 and the Gser thang reprint I3CN5029 both read it in the etext, which was opened; the Zhol print I1KG1710, the Sakya Centre copy I1KG81102 and further copies of the White Beryl among the hits) and «མཚོལ་དང་ཡུགས» in none. The pair «mnol» and «yugs» is also set side by side in a purification rite for defilement (the «Mnol bsang lha chab sngon mo» of the Rtsa gsum gling pa collection, etext: «thab dang gzhob kyis mnol dang yugs sas mnol», "defiled by hearth and by well, by «mnol» and «yugs», by earth"), so the line speaks of the two things that strike the mother's and the mother's sister's line first.

The dictionaries (Steinert's set, `locate.py dict`) have the headword «mnol»: defilement, impurity that a rite of lustration clears (Dan Martin, citing Karmay and Norbu), "mnol is grib or the unclean" (Brda dkrol gser gyi me long, citing the Ngag sgron commentary and the Bka' chems ka khol ma: «mnol grib phog»), a command form of «mnal ba» in the Tshig mdzod chen mo; no headword «mtshol». So the scan's letter, read by eye in RD110 as a ཚ with o, is the block's form of the ན of «མནོལ», or a cut of it; the print's own etext and every other witness give ན. Scan-bound only as a matter of the glyph (Gser thang img. 171, l. 13).
