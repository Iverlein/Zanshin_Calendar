# Is the Edo 初夢 night of 2 January counted by the lunar first month or by the Gregorian calendar?

The 日本大百科全書 says 正月2日 without saying which calendar.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The Edo 初夢 is counted by the old calendar, the lunisolar 旧暦, not the Gregorian. The Gregorian calendar came to Japan only with the reform announced for 明治5年12月3日, which became 明治6年1月1日 (1 January 1873) (ja.wikipedia 改暦弁: «来る明治5年12月3日をもって明治6年1月1日（1873年1月1日）とする新たな暦（グレゴリオ暦をもとにした新暦）»). Until then every Edo date, the New Year's Day and the 2nd of the first month with it, stood in the old calendar, so the Edo "night of 2 January" is the night of the 2nd of the first lunar month, which falls between late January and mid-February in the Gregorian count. The same page on 初夢 (ja.wikipedia) says that in the later Edo period the night of the 2nd to the 3rd was the usual one («江戸時代後期には「2日から3日」が主流となったが、明治の改暦後は、「元日から2日」とする人が多くなった»), and that the older rule of the 拾芥抄 age was the night from 節分 to 立春; the 日本大百科全書 gives no calendar because for its period only one existed. Which night an Edo book means (the 1st-2nd or the 2nd-3rd) is another matter and varies by source.
