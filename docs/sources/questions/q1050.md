# In ch. 18 of the Mañjuśrī root tantra a watch is «four chu tshod» and also «four yud tsam» while the day has «thirty yud tsam» (pp. 438-439); how are these counted, and is the dream verse's fourth watch the one between midnight and dawn, given that the omens count three watches, midnight, dawn and sunrise (pp. 436-437)?

RD372.

## Answered 2026-11-10

RD378: the arithmetic and the watch order: 100 se gol = 1 rtsa rgyun, 4 rtsa rgyun = 1 chu tshod, 4 chu tshod = 1 watch; 10 blinks = 1 skad cig, 10 skad cig = 1 yud tsam, 4 yud tsam = 1 watch; thirty yud tsam to the day-and-night against 32 for eight watches of four, so «four» is the yāma (30/8 = 3¾) rounded. The earthquake omens name the first watch, the second, midnight, the middle watch, the end of midnight, the third watch, dawn and sunrise and never a fourth: the dream verse's fourth watch is the night's last, before dawn (as mmk.1:p430 and p408 agree). The pages do not say it is the one between midnight and dawn alone, since the third watch lies there too. The Sanskrit side is not answered.
