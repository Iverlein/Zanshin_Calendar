# What does «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» (ch. 81, the last clause of the dream list) say: is ལྷས་ཚོར་ «the time of feeling the god (spirit)» or «when the time of feeling comes»?

It stands on every print and commentary read and has no gloss in the Drikung commentary or the dictionaries consulted (RD281).

## Answered 2026-11-16

Not «the god felt»: the commentaries read the clause as the time at which the sign (ལྟས, ltas, omen) is felt, and give «ལྷས» as the variant spelling of the same word (inv-y, 2026-11-16; BDRC exact-phrase search and etext windows, no scan opened).

BDRC's exact search finds «ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» in 4 hits and «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» in 7. (1) The glossary «sman gyi rgyud bzhi'i brda dang don 'ga' zhig bkrol ba» (BDRC title, ascribed to Rnam rgyal A ru ra's 'phreng ba; two copies, the Supplement volume I5609 and I00KG07616) says on the clause: «འདིར་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་དང་ནད་པ་རང་གིས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་བསྟན་པས་ཡིག་སྐྱོན་མེད་དོ། འགའ་ཞིག་ཟེར་ཀྱང་…» (read from the machine text of I00KG07616, partly garbled): here the two, the time at which the omen is felt and the time at which the patient himself feels, are taught by the clause, so there is no fault of the letters, though some say otherwise. (2) The comments on ch. 81 repeat the line as «དེ་རྣམས་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས» (etext I1PD412, the «gdug pa klu'i gdon nad gso ba (le'u 81)» copies) and «དེ་རྣམས་ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས་སོ» (the Four Tantras' great commentary, etext I5622, the other prints): these dreams are «the time of feeling the omen, the signs of being struck by a spirit». (3) A commentary on the diagnosis headings writes «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས་དང་» for the same heading, the dream examination as the time of this feeling. The clause is therefore the second reading of the question: the dreams are the time at which the sign is felt, and they are the sign of a spirit's seizing; ལྷས is a spelling of ལྟས (the glossary's defence of the letters shows the question was asked of the text before). The first reading (the god felt) is found in no commentary.

Answered (inv-z2, 2026-11-16, BDRC phrase search «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ», 14 hits; etext snippets): neither of the two offered readings as worded, but the second is nearer: «ལྷས་ཚོར» is "felt by the god" (ལྷས, lhas, the agent case of ལྷ, as in the stock idiom «མིས་མ་ཚོར་ཡང་ལྷས་ཚོར», "though men did not notice, the gods noticed"; claim st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god), and the clause «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས» is "the time the god feels it, the sign that a spirit strikes". The verse's own annotated copy says so in plain words: the Mchan brgyab lhan thabs (Sman pa Blo bzang dbang rgyal; MW1KG14616) glosses the preceding dreams of the possessed, «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས་དང༌» ("it is the time felt by the god, and the sign that a spirit strikes"), and the Mes po'i zhal lung (Rnam gling Paṇ chen, Beijing 2005) sets the dream examination on «ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» and the examination of body, speech and mind on «རང་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» ("the time of one's own feeling"), so the pair names two stages of the illness: the earlier one, felt only by the god (the dreams), and the later one, felt by the patient himself. The 14 hits are the verse's copies and commentaries (Gso rig rgyud bzhi, 'Bri gung, the Blue Beryl, the Mchan brgyab); none has another letter than ལྷས.

**Two answers on one day, set back 2026-11-16 (launcher).** inv-y reads the clause as the time at which the omen (ལྟས) is felt, ལྷས a spelling of ལྟས; inv-z2 reads it as the time the god feels it (ལྷས the agent case of ལྷ). The glosses quoted by both are genuine; they are not reconciled here, so the question is narrowed, not answered. Claims on both sides: ch81-last-clause-lhas-ltas-tshor-is-the-time-the-omen-is-felt and st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god.
