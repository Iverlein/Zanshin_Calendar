# What does «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» (ch. 81, the last clause of the dream list) say: is ལྷས་ཚོར་ «the time of feeling the god (spirit)» or «when the time of feeling comes»?

It stands on every print and commentary read and has no gloss in the Drikung commentary or the dictionaries consulted (RD281).

## Read 2026-11-16 (inv-y)

Not «the god felt»: the commentaries read the clause as the time at which the sign (ལྟས, ltas, omen) is felt, and give «ལྷས» as the variant spelling of the same word (inv-y, 2026-11-16; BDRC exact-phrase search and etext windows, no scan opened).

BDRC's exact search finds «ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» in 4 hits and «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» in 7. (1) The glossary «sman gyi rgyud bzhi'i brda dang don 'ga' zhig bkrol ba» (BDRC title, ascribed to Rnam rgyal A ru ra's 'phreng ba; two copies, the Supplement volume I5609 and I00KG07616) says on the clause: «འདིར་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་དང་ནད་པ་རང་གིས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་བསྟན་པས་ཡིག་སྐྱོན་མེད་དོ། འགའ་ཞིག་ཟེར་ཀྱང་…» (read from the machine text of I00KG07616, partly garbled): here the two, the time at which the omen is felt and the time at which the patient himself feels, are taught by the clause, so there is no fault of the letters, though some say otherwise. (2) The comments on ch. 81 repeat the line as «དེ་རྣམས་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས» (etext I1PD412, the «gdug pa klu'i gdon nad gso ba (le'u 81)» copies) and «དེ་རྣམས་ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས་སོ» (the Four Tantras' great commentary, etext I5622, the other prints): these dreams are «the time of feeling the omen, the signs of being struck by a spirit». (3) A commentary on the diagnosis headings writes «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས་དང་» for the same heading, the dream examination as the time of this feeling. The clause is therefore the second reading of the question: the dreams are the time at which the sign is felt, and they are the sign of a spirit's seizing; ལྷས is a spelling of ལྟས (the glossary's defence of the letters shows the question was asked of the text before). The first reading (the god felt) is found in no commentary.

## Read 2026-11-16 (inv-z2)

Read (inv-z2, 2026-11-16, BDRC phrase search «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ», 14 hits; etext snippets): neither of the two offered readings as worded, but the second is nearer: «ལྷས་ཚོར» is "felt by the god" (ལྷས, lhas, the agent case of ལྷ, as in the stock idiom «མིས་མ་ཚོར་ཡང་ལྷས་ཚོར», "though men did not notice, the gods noticed"; claim st-lhas-tshor-felt-by-the-god), and the clause «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས» is "the time the god feels it, the sign that a spirit strikes". The verse's own annotated copy says so in plain words: the Mchan brgyab lhan thabs (Sman pa Blo bzang dbang rgyal; MW1KG14616) glosses the preceding dreams of the possessed, «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས་དང༌» ("it is the time felt by the god, and the sign that a spirit strikes"), and the Mes po'i zhal lung (Rnam gling Paṇ chen, Beijing 2005) sets the dream examination on «ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» and the examination of body, speech and mind on «རང་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» ("the time of one's own feeling"), so the pair names two stages of the illness: the earlier one, felt only by the god (the dreams), and the later one, felt by the patient himself. The 14 hits are the verse's copies and commentaries (Gso rig rgyud bzhi, 'Bri gung, the Blue Beryl, the Mchan brgyab); none has another letter than ལྷས.

## Reconciled 2026-11-16 (inv-z6)

The glosses both quote were read again in the etexts (BDRC phrase search «ལྷས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» 14 hits, «ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» 4, «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས» 7, «ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» 7, «རང་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས» 6, «ལྟས་ཚོར་དུས་ཏེ» 1; windows of I1PD412 and of the glossary copies I5609, I00KG07616). Neither note's «answered» is forced by them; the question stays narrowed, with two readings of the one word and the same sense of the clause under both.

What both readings share. The clause names the first of two stages that the Blue Beryl's outline and the Mes po'i zhal lung, Skyem pa and Dar mo set side by side: «རྨི་ལམ་བརྟག་པ་ལྷས་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས་དང་། … ལུས་ངག་ཡིད་གསུམ་ལ་བརྟག་པ་རང་ཚོར་གྱི་དུས་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་བསྟན་པ» (the dream examination is the «lhas tshor» time, the examination of body, speech and mind the «rang tshor» time, the patient's own feeling). The Blue Beryl's gloss on the verse closes the dream list with «དེ་རྣམས་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན་ཏེ་གདོན་གྱིས་འདེབས་པའི་རྟགས» and, on the sister clause, «རང་གིས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་ཡིན» (one feels it oneself). So the dreams are the earlier stage, before the patient feels the harm himself; this does not depend on how ལྷས/ལྟས is read.

Reading A, ལྟས (ltas, the omen): «the time at which the omen is felt». Witnesses: the Blue Beryl's own gloss on the clause (I1PD412 and the two chapter etexts MW1PD3_ACDDC0 and MW1PD3_B43539), the Aru ra glossary (both copies; the sentence «འདིར་ལྟས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་དང་ནད་པ་རང་གིས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་བསྟན་པས་ཡིག་སྐྱོན་མེད་དོ། འགའ་ཞིག་ཟེར་ཀྱང་», where the omen is set against the patient himself), and the modern etext MW15925, whose editor corrects «ldas» to «ltas». No dictionary or commentary says why ལྷས would be written for ལྟས.

Reading B, ལྷས (lhas, the agent case of ལྷ): «the time felt by the god (the spirit)», against «felt by oneself». Witnesses: the root text on every print read (the scans), the headings of the Blue Beryl itself, Skyem pa, Dar mo, the Mes po'i zhal lung, and the glosses of the Mchan brgyab (Blo bzang dbang rgyal), Khro ru (a copy of the Blue Beryl's wording), Blo bzang bstan 'dzin and the Nyi zla collection, which write «ལྷས་ཚོར་བའི་དུས». The pair ལྷས/རང་ is grammatical as a pair of agents. But none of them says «by the god»: the Mchan brgyab line is the clause repeated, not a gloss, and the claim that it says so «in plain words» (inv-z2) is the translation of the repeated line.

Why not settled. The Blue Beryl, the highest-ranked witness read, writes both spellings in the one text, ལྷས in the heading and ལྟས in the gloss of the verse, so it treats them as one word or its etext mixes them (its printed page answers 401, q910); that is inv-y's evidence, and it shows only that ལྷས and ལྟས were not told apart, not that ལྷས is a mere spelling of ལྟས. The glossary's decisive sentence is garbled in both copies before «འགའ་ཞིག་ཟེར་ཀྱང་» («…ཡི་གེའི་དབྱིབས་དེ་འདྲ་བྱུང་ཡང་ … ཤེས་པའི་དོན་ཏེ་ལྟས་ཚོར་… ཡིན་ཞེས་»: that the letters are so, … it is the sense «known by the omen», so say some), and whether the glossarist keeps ལྷས or reads ལྟས stays what st-lhas-tshor-glossary-may-itself-read-ltas says. Count: ལྷས in the verse's prints and in the commentaries and later writings, ལྟས in the Blue Beryl's gloss, the glossary and one modern etext.
