# In the Tsong kha pa passage the first three parts of the night are called uncertain and the fourth taken, with a verse of the Mañjuśrī root tantra giving phlegm, bile and wind: is this the division by which the Tibetan medical dream texts (SDS, GSM) judge a dream, or a tantric one only?

RD329.

## Answered 2026-11-13

Tantric only, as far as the medical texts read go (claim tkg-four-parts-of-the-night-is-the-tantric-division-the-medical-rule-is-first-part-against-dawn, RD394). Tsong kha pa's four parts of the night, the first three uncertain and the fourth taken, with the Mañjuśrī root tantra's verse of phlegm, bile, wind and the fourth true (tkg:i385-388), is not the rule of the Tibetan medical dream texts: the Four Tantras (gt:i42) judge by the first part of the night against the dawn, Gtsang sman's physician's dream is the one at dawn (gsm:p263-264) and the Siddhasāra's dream signs give no part of the night (sds:p75-77). The four-watch verse does stand in medical authors' books, but as a quoted saying beside the three-part verse: Zur mkhar ba, the Blue Beryl (which copies his page) and Dar mo sman rams pa's Gser mchan give it under the Mañjuśrī root tantra's name (rrd.17:p352-355, rrd.18:p285-286; claim rrd-the-humour-verse-is-not-in-the-derge-manjushri-root-tantra), and none of them makes it the rule by which a patient's dream is judged. Not found: any medical text that judges a dream by the four watches.
