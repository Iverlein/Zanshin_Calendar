# The units «དབྱུ་གུ», «སྲང», «དབུགས», «ཆ» and the divisor «བགོད་བྱེད» (pp. 29–32)

pp. 29–32: in the procedures the terms «དབྱུ་གུ», «སྲང», «དབུགས», «ཆ» follow each other with the multipliers 60, 60, 6 and 707 (days, a unit of 60 to the day, breaths, parts). Which units does the text mean by «སྲང» and «དབྱུ་གུ», and is «བགོད་བྱེད» in these verses a single divisor (39592? 804?) written once and understood afterwards? (RD187, corpus/wb/ch03.md)

## Answered 2026-11-13

«དབྱུ་གུ» (*dbyu gu*) is the first sexagesimal place after the whole (a sixtieth of a day for the weekday, which is the hour *chu tshod*; a sixtieth of a mansion for the sun), «སྲང» (*chu srang*) a sixtieth of that, «དབུགས» (*dbugs*) the breath, a sixth of a *srang*, «ཆ» (*cha*) the part, 1/707 of a breath for the weekday and 1/67 for the sun. The multipliers 60, 60, 6, 707 (67 for the sun) are the divisions of one whole.

«བགོད་བྱེད» is not four divisors but one, written once and understood after: 39592 for the planet's reckoning (7 · 8657 / 39592 = 1;31;50;0;480/707 exactly, the month constant of p. 30) and 804 for the sun's (65 · 27 / 804 = 2;10;58;1;17/67 exactly, the sun's constant of p. 30). Both end with no remainder. Claim `wb-ch4-mean-month-and-sun-divisors`.
