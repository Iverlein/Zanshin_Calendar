# The Siddhasāra's chapter 5 ends «འཆི་ལྟས་ཀྱི་ལེའུ་སྟེ་དྲུག་ལྔ །པ་རྫོགས་སོ།» (etext p. 77, where both numerals stand), while the contents number the chapter fifth: is sixth the older count, a chapter having been split, or a slip of the print?

RD320.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered by RD330 (2026-11-10), on the etext: the Siddhasāra's colophon numbers of chapters 4–6 run one too high (fevers 'fifth', signs of death 'sixth', diarrhoea 'seventh', the blood illness again 'seventh'); the print's editors give the right number in square brackets at etext pp. 72 («ལྔ་[བཞི་པ») and 89 («བདུན་[དྲུག]པ»), and the «ལྔ» on p. 77 is the same correction with its brackets lost in the etext (inferred, no scan available). So 'sixth' is a miscount the editors corrected, not an older count or a split chapter; the contents (31 entries) and the body number the fevers 4 and the signs of death 5. The text's opening list of chapters (p. 27) has the signs of death fourth and the fevers fifth, the reverse; which order is older is not settled.
