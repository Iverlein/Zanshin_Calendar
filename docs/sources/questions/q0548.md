# Padma dkar po's «ཆ་དགུའི་ས» and WB's «དགུ་ཆའི་སར» on p. 282

Does Padma dkar po's «ཆ་དགུའི་ས» (the nine cells of the serpent's breadth) hold for WB's «ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་གནས་པའི་དགུ་ཆའི་སར» on p. 282 as well, or is WB's «དགུ་ཆ» the nine parts of p. 281's ten great parts? The commentator is a witness on the ground rite, not a gloss on WB.

RD169 (2026-11-06), narrowed: WB's own p. 281 gives a great part of nine small parts, and one *mtho* of the body equals one great part, so «དགུ་ཆའི་སར» can be that great part, not only Padma dkar po's nine cells. «རང་ཞལ་དོ» (img. 287) unsettled.

RD169 (2026-11-07), final: narrowed. WB's own p. 281 has a great part of nine small parts and «དོ» in «རང་ཞལ་དོ་ཡི་ཚད» is read clearly; whether p. 282's «དགུ་ཆའི་སར» is that great part or Padma dkar po's nine cells is said by no text.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes, it holds. See the answer to q482 (2026-11-14, inv-ggg): the Kālacakra's own Vimalaprabhā, which Padma dkar po follows, has the serpent «ས་ཡི་ཆ་དགུ་ཁྱབ་པར་ཉལ» on a plot of ninety cells a side, so «ཆ་དགུའི་ས» is the nine small parts of his breadth, one great part of p. 281's square, and WB's «དགུ་ཆའི་སར» is the same verse's term in other order. The commentator is a witness for the term, and the verse itself is the common source.
