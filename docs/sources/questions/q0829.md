# In the treatment of the unripe fever (st:p185-186) what is the medicine «བིག་པན» (ཐིག་པན in the etext), named with ash and beer, and which letter does the print have?

Yigdzin-1 reads བ, the etext ཐ; the scan's first letter is not settled.

## Answered 2026-11-14

An earth medicine (ས་སྨན, sa sman), and the print has བ: «བིག་པན» (big pan). On the Serthang scan (I3CN5038, img. 258, l. 14, at 4×, and again on img. 257 in the line «རྐྱེན་གྱིས་བྱེར་ན་བིག་པན…») the first letter is བ with the vowel sign i, not ཐ; the etext's ཐིག་པན is its OCR slip. Every commentary agrees: the Blue Beryl (བཻ་ཌཱུརྱ་སྔོན་པོ, bai ḍūrya sngon po; etext I1PD411) has «བིག་པན» 20 times and never «ཐིག་པན», and the Drikung dka' mchan (འབྲི་གུང་རྒྱུད་བཞིའི་དཀའ་མཆན, 'bri gung rgyud bzhi'i dka' mchan; etext I1KG24731, p. 331) prints «ཡུན་དུ་སྨིན་དཀའ་བིག་པན་ཐལ་བ་དང་» in the verse and «བིག་པན་སྒོག་ཐལ་ཆང་སྦྱར་བྱིན» (big pan, garlic ash, with beer) for the wind-unripe fever.

What it is: in the Blue Beryl's chapter on the potencies of medicines (etext p. 366, print p. 338), among the salts and earths, «བིག་པན་ལ་སྤྱི་དང་འདིར་མིང་གཅིག» («big pan has a general name, here one name»), it is classed with the earth medicines, blue (སྔོན་པོ) with a lighter kind, salty when tasted, of the same virtue as black salt (ནག་མཚུར, nag mtshur), cutting growths and tumours; its spoken Indian name is given there as «ནི་ལ་ཐོ་ཐ» (the letters as the etext has them). In the chapter on the pestilence the same commentary says «བིག་པན་ཏེ་ས་སྨན་ངོ་བོ» (etext p. 864, print p. 836): big pan, that is the earth medicine itself (ngo bo), burnt with three others (srad dkar, sna lo, kyi lce) without letting the smoke escape, the ash being mixed with the remedy for the sign at hand. In ch. 15 (the Blue Beryl, etext p. 792) it is ground to an ash: «ཡུན་དུ་སོང་ཡང་སྨིན་དཀའ་ན་བིག་པན་གྱི་ཐལ་བ», when a fever long gone is hard to ripen the ash of big pan is given, with the three good ones (ཛཱ་ཏི་སུག་སྨེལ་ཀ་ཀོ་ལ), and in the chapter's end, for a fever that has taken a turn from an outer cause, with beer.

Not settled: the plant or mineral the name stands for. The Blue Beryl names only the class (earth medicine, saltlike, burnt to ash) and quotes the Sakya physician's view (ས་སྐྱ་སྨན་གྲོང་པ, sa skya sman grong pa) that it is a «gser bre dngul bre, na bul tog», and disowns it. «ཐིག་པན» in the etext of st:p185-186 is to be written «བིག་པན».
