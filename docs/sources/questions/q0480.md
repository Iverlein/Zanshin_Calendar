# Is «ཚད་ཟླ» on p. 9 a word of its own or a slip for «ཚང་ཟླ»

The 1996 print and its Gser thang reprint print «ཚད་ཟླ» clearly, with a final da, in the black luck pebble's verse of the seventh circle (WB vol. 2, p. 9, l. 6), where Zhechen Gyaltsab's verse has «ཚང་ཟླ»: is «ཚད་ཟླ» a word of its own for a kind of kin, or a slip of the block for «ཚང་ཟླ»? (RD90)

RD178 (2026-11-06): Yigdzin-1 reads «ཚད་ཟླ་མག་སྐུད་བུ་ཚར་ངན» on Zhol img. 638 (p. 9, l. 6), with the da: a third print with the da, machine reading only; by eye at 3× ཚད against ཚང is not certain. Stays open.

RD178 (2026-11-07): RD178 second run: on the Zhol print (img. 638, line 2, 4×) the second letter of «ཚད» has the curl of da; the Taikhang is fused. Stays open.

RD178 (2026-11-13, seventh run): Zhol img. 638, line 2, 4×: the hand is cursive, da against nga not told apart; unchanged.

## Answered 2026-11-14

A slip for «ཚང་ཟླ», as claim ch27-tshad-zla-is-tshang-zla says; the further search does not find «ཚད་ཟླ» a word of its own in this sense. BDRC's phrase search: «ཚད་ཟླ་མག་སྐུད» two hits, the 1996 etext and the Gser thang etext (both with da, i.e. the White Beryl's two prints only); «ཚང་ཟླ་མག་སྐུད» one hit, Zhechen Gyaltsab's verse (I4PD2444). «ཚད་ཟླ» alone has 222 hits, but where it is not WB it is another word: «tshad zla» "hot month", a name of the spring or early summer in the lists of season names (Brda dag snyan ngag, with «so ga dpyid kyi ming»; Sa skya bka' 'bum: «rdul dus tshad zla nges sreg dus»), or «tshad zla ba N», "the measure of N months" in rites and biographies; none of these is a kind of kin, and the Jim Valby entry "brother" has no source. «ཚང་ཟླ» has 187 hits as a term of one house, and the White Beryl's own etexts use it in the kin reckonings (vol. 2 etext p. 531 «བུ་བག་གི་ཚང་ཟླའི་སྤུན་གྲངས», and a rite of those born in the bird and monkey years in the same work's vol. 1 snippet: «tshang zla yams skud sa bdag gnod»). So the verse reads "the kin of the house (ཚང་ཟླ), the son-in-law's line (མག་སྐུད), the sons' end: bad", and «ཚད» is the block's (and the Zhol print's machine-read) letter for «ཚང», or Sde srid's own slip; the glyph on the three prints (da or nga in the cursive) is not told apart by eye and stays scan-bound (Zhol img. 638, line 2).
