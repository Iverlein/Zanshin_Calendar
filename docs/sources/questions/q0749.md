# Is the «ཕྱ་ཐེར» of work 17 in ch. 34 (a sewn tent-like shelter in the manuals) the same word as «ཕྱར», spelled two ways, or a second article sewn beside the tent?

INV-1114 (2026-11-14), narrowed: work 17 is one work, and WB's own verses write its second word both ways in the same slot of the same list; whether «ཕྱ་ཐེར» and «ཕྱར» are one word is not shown. In the ch. 33 verses that run through the works in one order, vol. 2 p. 317 reads «དྲ་གྱོན་ཕྱ་ཐེར་སྦྲ་འཚེམ་སྐྱས» (1996 etext; on the Zhol print, img. 1060, line 5, read by eye at 3×: «ཕྱ་ཐེར་སྦྲ་འཚེམ», the machine etext's «ཕྱ་བར» is its error), while pp. 327, 334 and 382 give «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་འཚེམ» at the same place after «དྲ་གྱོན…» (1996 etext). BDRC's exact search finds «སྦྲ་ཕྱར» in 21 etexts, «ཕྱར་འཚེམ» in 10, «ཕྱ་ཐེར་འཚེམ» in 3 and «སྦྲ་ཕྱ་ཐེར» in 3, none with both words side by side («ཕྱ་ཐེར་དང་ཕྱར», «ཕྱར་དང་ཕྱ་ཐེར», «ཕྱ་ཐེར་ནི» 0 hits). «སྦྲ་ཕྱར» is also an ordinary prose compound for the nomad's tent and covers (the Ngag dbang blo bzang rgya mtsho's life, «སྦྲ་ཕྱར་སོགས་འབྲོག་རིགས»; the Jam mgon dgongs pa'i me long, «འབྲོག་པའི་སྦྲ་ཕྱར་ནག»), while «སྦྲ་ཕྱ་ཐེར» occurs only in the life of Dri med 'od zer, three prints of one passage (seventy of them made). Steinert's 64 dictionaries (2026-11-14): «ཕྱར་བ» is a covering sheet of hair, cloth or wool (Tshig mdzod chen mo; Ives Waldo: covering implement, tarpaulin, ground sheet; Mahāvyutpatti: kuṭapa), «སྦྲ» a cloth or felt tent (paṭakuṭī); no headword «ཕྱ་ཐེར», and the prefix search «phya th» finds only «phya thag». So: one slot, one work (a sewn tent-like cover beside the yak-hair tent); the dictionaries explain «ཕྱར» only and no source glosses «ཕྱ་ཐེར». Still open for a reader of the tradition.
