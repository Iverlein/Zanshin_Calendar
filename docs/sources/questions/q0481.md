# Who or what is «ཇི་ནོང» glossed beside Mañjuśrī on p. 3

Who or what is «ཇི་ནོང», glossed beside Mañjuśrī (འཇམ་དབྱངས, 'jam dbyangs) as the protector of the chapter's homage (WB vol. 2, p. 3, l. 4: «མགོན་པོ(འཇམ་དབྱངས་དང་ཇི་ནོང་།)»)? (RD90)

RD178 (2026-11-07): located the Zhol folio: WBZ I1KG1710 img. 629 holds the chapter's opening, the homage as an interlinear gloss under line 3 of the folio. Yigdzin-1 on that image reads the gloss «འཇམ་དབྱངས་དང་ཇི་སྐོང»; the gloss is about 25 px high there and at 8× the letters are a blur, so «སྐོང» against «ནོང» is not settled by eye. On the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 9, line 4, 3×) the parenthesis reads «འཇམ་དབྱངས་དང་ཇི་ནོང་།» plainly. So the reprint and the 1996 print agree on «ནོང»; the Zhol reading is a machine lead only. Not answered; who the figure is remains open.

## Answered 2026-11-14

One of the four Chinese "magically emanated men with eyes" of the White Beryl's own invocation of the reckoning, and by Thu'u bkwan's note the Tibetan form of the Chinese Shennong (神農). In WB vol. 2 p. 151 (the Gser thang etext's [p157], read on the scan in RD, wb.2:p150-151) the invocation names the four who hold "the great arrow of wisdom" on 360 crystal castles beyond the nine continents: «ཀོང་ཙེ་འཕྲུལ་རྒྱལ་དང་། །ཇི་ཀོང་ཇི་ནོང་ཝང་ཟེ་སོགས། །སྤྱན་ལྡན་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་མི་བཞིར་བདར། །རྣོ་མཐོང་རྩིས་ཀྱི་མངོན་ཤེས་སྟོན།» (Kong tse 'phrul rgyal, Ji kong, Ji nong, Wang ze and the others, the four emanated men with eyes, show the clairvoyance of the keen-sighted reckoning). The etext reads «ཇི་ནོང» there as at p. 3 (the scan of p. 151 gave «ཇི་ཉོང[?]», a wide letter ཉ more than ན, the p. 3 gloss «ཇི་ནོང» plainly); so the p. 3 gloss «འཇམ་དབྱངས་དང་ཇི་ནོང» names, with Mañjuśrī, the same master of the Chinese reckoning (ch. 27 is the Chinese reckoning of the thirteen ldings).

Who he is: Thu'u bkwan Blo bzang chos kyi nyi ma's *Grub mtha' shel gyi me long* (two etexts, snippets), on the Chinese authors of the divination books: «'di bzhi yin par 'dug rung / yi ge nor ram skad ma 'byor bas khung tsi la kong tse dang / ci'u kyung la ji gong dang / ji nong la she nong / wen wang la wang the zhes bris so» ("they seem to be these four, but through a wrong letter or a sound not matching, Khung tsi was written Kong tse, Ci'u kyung (Zhou gong) Ji gong, She nong Ji nong, Wen wang Wang the"). The four of that passage are Confucius, the Duke of Zhou, Shennong and King Wen, and the White Beryl's four names are the same four in the Tibetan spelling: Kong tse, Ji kong, Ji nong, Wang the (the p. 151 «ཝང་ཐེ» or «ཝང་ཟེ»). The Bai dur dkar po's own list of the "emanated kings" (MW2CZ8040 snippet: «kong tse 'phrul gyi rgyal po … ji nong 'phrul gyi rgyal po … ji kong 'phrul gyi rgyal po») and the Lha mo tshul khrims' manual (MW1KG21818: «ji kong ji nong wang the sogs 'phrul mi gzhan gsum») give the same trio beside Kong tse. The equation with Shennong rests on Thu'u bkwan's one sentence (a snippet, not seen on a scan); the passage before Thu'u bkwan's sentence was not seen, and that Mañjuśrī and Ji nong are named as the one protector of the chapter is the gloss's own wording.
