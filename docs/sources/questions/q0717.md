# The two spellings of hail, ལྷགས and ལྟགས

the two spellings of hail, ལྷགས (entry 15) and ལྟགས (entry 50): the same word? (RD194, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-13 (inv-x)

BDRC full-text search: «ལྷགས་ཆེ་སྡུག་ལྟོགས» 1 hit (MW25151_3AAD6E, a print of the White Beryl, entry 50), «ལྟགས་ཆེ་སྡུག་ལྟོགས» 0; the Gser thang etext reads «ཞགས་ཆེ་སྡུག་ལྟོགས» (OCR, one hit); «ལྷགས་པ་སྔ» 12 hits (hail early, entries 51 etc.). So entry 50 is ལྷགས (hail) in the prints found, the same word as entry 15; the ལྟགས of the pool and the ཞགས of the OCR are readings of one ཧ-over-ག stack. The scan of p. 211 is not re-read; open on the scan.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The spelling: one word. ལྷགས (lhags) is the word in the dictionaries; there is no headword ལྟགས (ltags, only ltag, "the back"), and the earlier BDRC finding is that the prints read ལྷགས at entry 50, so the two are one ཧ-over-ག stack misread.

The meaning is another matter and the pool's rendering needs a second look. The dictionaries give lhags pa as a cold wind: "the name of a cold wind" (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs), "a running current of wind", with lhags bsil "cold wind" and lhags 'phyar "wind arising" (Tshig mdzod chen mo; Ives Waldo), and never as hail, which is ser ba (སེར་བ); WB's own key on p. 210 names the sign-words that hail (ser ba) will come and does not name lhags,. So «དཔྱིད་ལྷགས་ཆེ» (spring lhags great), «ལྷགས་པ་སྔ» (lhags early) and «ལྷགས་ཆེ་སྡུག་ལྟོགས» (entry 50) are on the dictionaries' evidence the spring or early cold wind, a chill, not hail; that the entries mean hail is the pool's reading, with no gloss behind it. ch. 18's English, wherever it renders ལྷགས as "hail", stands unchanged here and is to be corrected to "cold wind" when the chapter is next touched, unless a commentary is found that glosses it otherwise.
