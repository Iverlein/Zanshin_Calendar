# Does «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» in ch. 34's closing verse rank the person's rise and decline above the rest?

ch. 34's closing verse lists «ས་བདག་བཟང་ངན» and then «ཁྱད་པར་དུ། །ལས་བརྩམས་གང་ཟག་དེའི་དར་གུད། །དེ་ལ་གནོད་པའི་སྐྱོན་ལ་སོགས།» (WB vol. 2, p. 428). Does «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» rank the person's own rise and decline above everything listed before it, or only mark it as the last and most particular item to examine?

## Answered 2026-11-14

inv-fff (2026-11-14), from claim wb-ch34-khyad-par-du-closing and the closing verse as read on the reprint (wb.2:p428b): «ཁྱད་པར་དུ» marks the person's own rise and decline (and what harms him) as the particular member to examine closely, the last of the list, before «ཞིབ་ཏུ་བརྟགས་ལ་ཁ་དམར་སྨྲ». It does not rank him over the rest: the verse gives no order or weight among the members, the Bhutan digest ends its own list with «ཁྱད་པར་བྱེ་བྲག་གང་ཟག་སོ་སོ་ཡི། །ལས་བརྩམས་…» (the particulars of each several person's beginning of the work: the turn from the general list to the case, not a weight), Dpal bsdus's *Rmad byung* matches the time's rise and decline, the fortune and the verdict with each other without ranking them, and Mipham's notes (pp. 482–549) say nothing on the verse. So the second reading of the question holds: the last and most particular item to examine; a rank over the others is not read.
