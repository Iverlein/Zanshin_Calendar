# What time of day is the «gro kha» in the third Karmapa's six sessions, and is «gung» in his session list the middle of the night or a longer part?

RD384.


Narrowed (inv-aa, 2026-11-14): gung is the middle of the night in this order of sessions; gro kha is not fixed. «གུང» in the dictionaries is the middle, «noon or midnight» (Ives Waldo, Rangjung Yeshe, Hackett, Valby), «nyin gung» noon and «mtshan gung» or «nam gung» midnight (Tshig mdzod chen mo: «nam gung = mtshan phyed»); the new-terms dictionary (Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs) gives it as the name of a time, the 12th small period of the day and the 24th of the night, as in «nyin gung, mtshan gung» (its numbering, twelfth and twenty-fourth, is not explained and not used here). It is a point of the night, not a longer part, and in the Karmapa's list (srod, gung, tho rangs) the unqualified word sits between dusk and dawn, so it is the night's middle, as «nam gung» in Marpa's song (rrd.23:p102-104) is. The same author's dream verses use «nam phyed» for it (rrd.5:p57-59); no text read says the one for the other, the order of the sessions does. «གྲོ་ཁ» (gro kha): the dictionaries give only «provisions for a journey, food supply» (Tshig mdzod chen mo: «lam rgyags», Ives Waldo: «food supply») and, as «'gro kha», the time of setting out; none gives a time of day. BDRC's exact search «གྲོ་ཁའི་དུས་སུ» and «གྲོ་ཁའི་དུས» finds this counsel only (rrd.5, offset 166608); «སྔ་དྲོ་དང་གྲོ་ཁ», «གྲོ་ཁ་དང་ཉི་མ་རྒས» 0. The one parallel scheme of day sessions found is the Sakya approach to Hevajra: four sessions from sunrise to midday, from midday to «nyi ma rgas», and from that to dusk (nine places, the Lam 'bras and the Kye rdo rje gzhi bsnyen copies): so «nyi ma rgas» is the afternoon and gro kha, which comes before it and after the early morning, lies in the late morning or about noon; if gro kha is the time of the food it is the forenoon meal, which is a reading from the dictionary sense and not a statement of any text.
