# Which work on the the'u spirits says what gnam the dkar po (གནམ་ཐེ་དཀར་པོ) does to a sleeper, and why is a white thread tied round the waist the means of holding it (Marpa's inner-heat manual, q1111)?

Raised by the 2026-11-13 investigation k.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14.** No work found that says what gnam the'u dkar po does to a sleeper. Searched: BDRC's exact phrases «གནམ་ཐེ་དཀར་པོ» (92 hits), «གནམ་ཐེའུ་དཀར་པོ» (87), «གནམ་ཐེའུ» (219), «ཐེའུ་རང་གི་གནོད» (41, the stock prayer against the harms of kings and the'u rang), «ཐེའུ་རང་རྨི་ལམ» and «ཐེའུ་རང་ཉལ» 0; the snippets read, not the works. What they say: a harmful spirit (gdon) of the the'u class and the first of a numbered row (one white, two black, three tall, four green, five yellow) (Gsung rab sgo mdzod, I4PD4104 pp. 484, 502); in Gter ma and Guru Drag dmar rites a figure to crush (Rin chen gter mdzod); in the lives of the 'Bras spungs abbots and of Pan chen Bsod nams grags pa a god so named who shows a teacher a monastery site in a dream; a Hor god in the Sa skya Lam 'bras and Ngag dbang kun dga' bsod nams; in the Bod kyi gna' rabs lo rgyus a fleeing assassin's false name «ང་ལྷ་གནམ་ཐེའུ་དཀར་པོ་བྱ་བ་ཡིན» ("I am the god gnam the'u dkar po"), and the Bai dur dkar po's harmer of the house. None says what he does to one who sleeps; the one text that bears on sleep is the Marpa manual itself and its restatement in the Chos kyi blo gros volume (q1111), where the cord ties the spirit through the penis. The Bon witness of q1111 (Nyamme Sherab Gyaltsen) is not a source. Unread: the Rnying ma rgyud 'bum's chapter 100 on the harms of the'u rang (listed in the catalogue, «The'urang gi gnod pa srung bar byed pa'i le'u»), the Dkar chag only seen.
