# «ཡར» and «མར» in the plank-tree: later or earlier years

In WB's plank-tree and the keg reckoning, do «ཡར» and «མར» count toward later or earlier years of the cycle, so that the sky year's fifth and sixth places below («མར་གྱི་ལྔ་མཐུན», «མར་གྱི་དྲུག་འབྲས») are named as years for a given sky year (the snake's trine partners for the harmony are bird and ox, but which of them is the one below)?

RD166 (2026-11-06), etext only: in the plank-tree verses a «མར» base counts «གྱེན་དུ» (up) and a «ཡར» base «སྦུབ» (down). In the Moonbeams' table (KD p. 517) the «མར» fifth-in-harmony is four years on in the cycle, but the table is by element pairs, so the snake example is not settled.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Counting from a «མར» base runs upward, to later years in the cycle, and from a «ཡར» base downward, as RD166 found in the plank-tree verses of the Moonbeams (KD) («མར་གྱི་ལྔ་མཐུན་ནས། དུ་ལོན་གྱེན་དུ་བགྲངས», «ཡར་གྱི་ལྔ་ནས་དུ་ལོན་སྦུབ»); the dictionaries say the same of the words (Hackett: mar lower, yar upper; Tshig mdzod chen mo: «yar zlog gong mar bcag pa»). The snake example is settled by the Moonbeams' table (etext p. 517): its rows are the year-animals sorted by their element: «ས་ཆུ་ལོ་དྲུག་བྱི་བ་ནས» (the six earth and water years, from the rat: dragon), «སྟག་ཡོས་ཡོས་ནས་ལུག» (the wood years, tiger and hare, from the hare: sheep), «རྟ་སྦྲུལ་རྟ་ནས་ཁྱི་ལའོ» (the fire years, horse and snake, from the horse: dog), «བྱ་སྤྲེལ་བྱ་ནས་གླང་ཐོག» (the metal years, bird and monkey, from the bird: ox), «གནམ་ལོ་གང་ཤར་དེའི། མར་གྱི་ལྔ་མཐུན་ལོ་པར་འབྱུང་» (whichever sky year comes up, its «མར» fifth-in-harmony year appears). The snake stands in the horse's row, so its «མར་གྱི་ལྔ་མཐུན» is the dog, not the bird or the ox: the five-harmony is not the trine partner of the snake. The «ཡར» fifth is the mirror only by the direction of the count; no line gives its table.
