## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-i)

An Indian parallel, not a source of the verse: Vāgbhaṭa's Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya, Sūtrasthāna 1.8, «vayo 'horātribhuktānāṃ te 'ntamadhyādigāḥ kramāt» (text as printed on satyori.com/source-texts/ashtanga-hridayam/sutrasthana/1/8/; no GRETIL file of the work could be fetched), says the three humours dominate the end, the middle and the beginning of life, of the day-and-night and of the meal, in turn; which humour is at which end depends on the order of the verse before it, which was not checked in an edition, so the match with the Tibetan verse (phlegm at dusk, bile at midnight, wind at dawn) is not asserted. No Tibetan tantra found. Unchanged.
