# In the Narthang catalogue entry on img. 77 does the Padmadra practice by Dpal ye shes (translated by Sumanaśrī and Rin chen grub) belong to the next work rather than to the Examination of Dreams

The Examination of Dreams carries only «Vibhūticandra's own translation»; the catalogue's habit is title, maker, translators.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes: the Padmadra practice is a separate work, the next entry, and not part of the Examination of Dreams. Three lines of evidence:

1. The Derge Tengyur's own volume. BDRC's work records for the Derge Tengyur run in order: MW23703_1749 is the Examination of Dreams, whose colophon ends «རྒྱ་གར་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་པཎྜི་ཏ་བི་བྷཱུ་ཏི་ཙནྡྲས་རང་འགྱུར་དུ་མཛད་པའོ» (Vibhūticandra's own translation) with no other name; MW23703_1750 is «པདྨ་དྲ་བ་འབྱུང་བའི་སྒྲུབ་ཐབས» (incipit title, Sanskrit title line «pad+ma dzA lod+b+ha ba sA d+ha ni»), whose own colophon reads «པདྨ་དྲ་བ་དཔལ་འབྱུང་བའི་སྒྲུབ་ཐབས་རྒྱལ་བ་དཔལ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱིས་མཛད་པ་རྫོགས་སོ། །ཁ་ཆེའི་པཎྜི་ཏ་སུ་མ་ན་ཤྲཱི་དང་། བོད་ཀྱི་ལོ་ཙཱ་བ་དགེ་སློང་རིན་ཆེན་གྲུབ་ཀྱིས་བསྒྱུར་ཅིང་ཞུས་ཏེ་གཏན་ལ་ཕབ་པ». So the practice is a work with its own maker (the Victor Dpal ye shes) and its own translators (the Kashmiri paṇḍita Sumanaśrī and the monk Rin chen grub), exactly the wording of the Narthang catalogue line. The next record, MW23703_1751, is a second Padma-net work (a mandala offering rite of Avalokiteśvara, also by Dpal ye shes under the name Padma rdo rje, translated by the Kashmiri paṇḍita «su ma na shrI bha dra» and Rin chen grub).
2. The prints of the Examination of Dreams end with its colophon and then carry the next work's title line (the Peking print, img. 318 line 6-7: svo:p271j).
3. The catalogues agree in order and wording: the Derge catalogue (svo:i736), Phur lcog's catalogue (svo:i472-473) and the Narthang catalogue (svo:i77) all give the Examination of Dreams as «Vibhūticandra's own translation» without a maker, then the Padma-net sādhana as a new entry, with maker and translators in the catalogue's habit (title, maker, translators).

The words after «རང་འགྱུར» on img. 77 therefore belong to the next work. Source state: BDRC's catalogue transcriptions of the Derge records (purl.bdrc.io, 2026-11-14), etext-level; the Narthang line itself stays a machine reading.
