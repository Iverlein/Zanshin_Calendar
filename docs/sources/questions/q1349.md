# WB ch. 16 p. 185 ma nu (18 or 14) and vol. 2 p. 334's three digits

From the number-word sweeps of 2026-11-21 (rd-ac, rd-ad). Ch. 16 p. 185 «ཚེས་གྲངས་མ་ནུ(༡༨)»: ma nu is 14 in the pool's other attestations (ch. 10 line 106, ch. 17) but the stored figure is 18, like the neighbouring «སྐར་མ་བཅོ་བརྒྱད(༡༨)». The line was not found on the reprint (img. 184: scans.py counts half-lines). If the print has 14, the solstice wheel claim "date 18" (wb-solstice-sixty-wheels) changes too.

Also open from the same sweeps, vol. 2 p. 334 (the Chinese-way verse): མཆུ(༦) for 9, དབོ(༤) for 11, ནམ་གྲུ(༢༤) for 26, against the Indian-way list two paragraphs earlier; the Zhol digits of that verse are unread.

## Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-aj)

The verse is on the Zhol (I1KG1710) img. 214, the second block line of the right-hand text (the left half of the image is the solstice wheel): «ཚད་དེ་ཚེས་གྲངས་མ་ནུ་དང་། །འདོད་ཆ་བདུན་དང་ལྔ་ཆ་བཞིར།» (the WBZ etext's p. 214 holds it as the second line). Over «མ་ནུ» one pair of figures stands, two glyphs of about 13 px, not legible at 3x (a hook and a curve; 14 or 18 is not told). The Taikhang I5877 counterpart is img. 231 (14,400 x 3,168 px, the wheel on the left); I did not find the word on it within this pass. Open.

## Answered 2026-11-24

Both places are settled by the words and the prints.

1. p. 185 «ཚེས་གྲངས་མ་ནུ»: 14. «མ་ནུ» is 14 in WB's key (`numwords.py spell «མ་ནུ»` gives 14; ch. 10 «མ་ནུ(༡༤)» on pp. 72 and 74 and the ch. 17 places), and the 1996 etext (I1KG12906, etext page 195 = printed p. 185) types «ཚེས་གྲངས་མ་ནུ(༡༤)དང༌། འདོད་ཆ་བདུན(༧)དང་ལྔ་ཆ་བཞིར(༤)»; the reprint's glyph is a hook and a curve (rd-aj), which is ༡༤ as well as ༡༨. The figure beside the neighbouring «བཅོ་བརྒྱད(༡༨)» was the source of the 18. So the black month's winter solstice at the author's place is the date 14 (the sage's date, not 18), the desired part 7, the fifth part 4. The table of p. 187 cannot confirm the date (a pig-day cell with desired part 7 has the dates 22, 16, 10, 4 down the table, 14 is none of them), so this rests on the word and the etext. The claim wb-solstice-sixty-wheels names 18 and is changed to 14.
2. vol. 2 p. 334, the Chinese-way list: the print (reprint and the 1996 etext, etext page 342) has «མཆུ(༦)», «དབོ(༤)» and «ནམ་གྲུ(༢༤)» in both; they are slips of the print, not variants: the same verse's Indian-way list two paragraphs earlier has «མཆུ(༩)», «དབོ(༡༡)», «ནམ་གྲུ(༢༦)», and the Chinese list itself runs «མཆུ་གྲེ་དབོ་མེ་བཞི་ནག་ས་རི» with གྲེ 10, མེ་བཞི 12, ནག 13, ས་རི 14, so མཆུ is 9 and དབོ 11 by the run, and ནམ་གྲུ follows «ཁྲུམས་སྨད(༢༥)» as 26. The printed 6, 4 and 24 repeat the figures of «ནབས་སོ(༦)», «མགོ(༤)» and «ཁྲུམས་སྟོད(༢༤)» a few words before. The mansions' own figures are 9, 11, 26.
