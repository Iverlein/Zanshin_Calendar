# Which fever is meant by འགྲམས་འཁྲུགས in ch. 15's «འགྲམས་འཁྲུགས་ཚ་བ་དགྲ་ཟིན་ལྟ་བུར་དབྱེ»?

It is set beside the pestilence's, ripened like beer: the border-troubled fever or the one gone astray.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Both: the fevers called 'grams (འགྲམས) and 'khrugs (འཁྲུགས), the two kinds that are not pestilence (རིམས), taken together. «འགྲམས་འཁྲུགས» is not one disease, nor a «border-troubled» or «gone astray» fever.

The Root Tantra's list of the kinds of fever (quoted in the Serthang etext's commentary and in the Blue Beryl, ch. 3 of the Explanatory Tantra) is: unripe, grown, emptied, hidden, old, turbid (མ་སྨིན་རྒྱས་སྟོངས་གབ་རྙིངས་རྙོགས), six; then «འགྲམས་པ་དང་འཁྲུགས་པ་དང་རིམས་ནད་དང་དུག་ཚད་རྣམ་པ་བཞི», four more kinds; 'grams has two (outer 'grams, ཕྱི་འགྲམས, and disease 'grams, ནད་འགྲམས), 'khrugs three (རྒྱས་འཁྲུགས, སྟོང་འཁྲུགས, འཇམ་འཁྲུགས), and the pestilence five (བལ་ནད, རྒྱུ་གཟེར, འབྲུམ་པ, གག་ལྷོག, ཆམ་པ) (Blue Beryl, etext I1PD411 p. 273 f.). The Instruction Tantra has a chapter of its own for each: ch. 21 «འགྲམས་ཀྱི་ཚ་བ་བཅོས་པ» (the treatment of the 'grams fever), ch. 22 «འཁྲུགས་ཀྱི་ཚ་བ་བཅོས་པ» (of the 'khrugs fever) and ch. 23 on the pestilence (རིམས་ཀྱི་ཚ་བ་བལ་ནད་བཅོས་པ); the Blue Beryl's chapter list and its remark that the sixteen stomach illnesses «འགྲམས་བབས་འཁྲུགས་བབས» are explained «in the 'grams-'khrugs chapters» (etext p. 972) say the same. So the sentence of ch. 15 sets the pestilence fever, ripened like beer, against the 'grams and 'khrugs fevers, which are not.

The Blue Beryl reads the sentence so (etext p. 792, print p. 764): «འགྲམས་དང་འཁྲུགས་ཀྱི་ཚ་བ་དགྲ་ནད་དེ་ཟིན་ནམ་གཉེན་ལྟ་བུ་ཟུངས་ཁྲག་དང་ངན་ཁྲག་སོ་སོར་དབྱེ་སྟེ་དགྲ་གཞོམ་གཉེན་གསོ་བ་ལྟ་བུར་དབྱེའོ»: the 'grams and 'khrugs fevers are to be sorted, like seizing the enemy or the friend, into the good blood (the body's essence) and the bad blood, destroying the enemy and nursing the friend. The reading of «དགྲ་ཟིན» as seizing (or holding) an enemy, as in st:p185-186, stands; the commentary adds the sorting of the two bloods as what the image means. What 'grams itself is, the Blue Beryl says in ch. 22 (etext p. 841): the 'khrugs heat that rests on 'grams is the heat 'grams (ཚ་འགྲམས), of two kinds, bursting and dull (ཚ་རྡོལ་, ཚ་རྨུགས་པ); 'khrugs (from the three faults being stirred, འཁྲུགས) is the fever of grown blood-bile heat, of emptiness and of softness.
