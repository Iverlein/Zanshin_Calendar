# The do gcig of the trigram repetition

What is the *do gcig* (དོ་གཅིག) of WB's trigram repetition (vol. 2, p. 89, «དོ་གཅིག་ལ་བབས་ས་དང་ནི།») and of the regent's reply 152 («དོ་གཅིག་དྭར་བབས་པ»): the dead's descended trigram in the year of death, as the notes' reading of the same line implies (wb.2:p504-512), or the descended trigram reaching the birth trigram, as the notes gloss «དོ་གཅིག» in the birth reckoning («སྐྱེས་སྤར་སྟེང་བབ་སྤར་ཕྱིན་པ», WB vol. 2, p. 480, etext only)?

**Narrowed 2026-11-01 (RD104, [claims/small-element-and-do-gcig-in-reply-152.md](../claims/small-element-and-do-gcig-in-reply-152.md)).** The regent's example puts the *do gcig* "fallen on dwa" and the *ma klungs* "born on dwa": the *do gcig* is what falls onto a trigram, and in the example it falls on the trigram of the birth. That fits the notes' reading (the descended trigram of the year of death, wb.2:p504-512) and is what the line asks (the four times of death stacked on a trigram that the descended and the birth trigram share); whether the word itself names the descended trigram or their coincidence the reply does not say.
