# The watch and the kung of the hand reckoning, and mung, krung, kyis

In the hand reckoning (pp. 538–539), what are the watch (ཐུན, thun) and the *kung* (the scan «ཐུན་ཀུང», Yigdzin-1 «ཐུན་ཀླུང») counted after the year, and what do the results *mung*, *krung* and *kyis* mean?

RD165 (2026-11-06), narrowed: the typeset notes (by eye, I1PD34647 img. 685–687) and the Gser thang etext read the three result names «མུང», «ཀྲུང», «ཀྱིས»; Mipham's block print, by the machine and RD132's eye, gives «ཕུང», «ཀླུང». The two editions part on the first two names; which Mipham wrote is open, and claims/notes-hand-reckoning-three-results.md (which calls the typeset readings misreadings) awaits review. The sense of the watch and of the kung is not found anywhere (q652).

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-ccc; Steinert's 64 dictionaries by `locate.py dict`, BDRC phrase search).** The kung. «གུང» is the middle of the day: Rangjung Yeshe "middle, centre; midnight; noon", Jim Valby "midday, midnight", the Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs "the name of the day's twelfth part and the night's twenty-fourth: nyin gung, mtshan gung", and Negi's Tibetan-Sanskrit dictionary has «གུང་ཐུན» for the middle watch (madhyame yāme, Abhisamayālaṅkārāloka). The Drukpa and Sakya session manuals divide the day into «ཐོར་ཐུན་གུང་ཐུན་ཕྱི་ཐུན», the dawn, noon and late sessions. So «ཐུན་ཀུང་སོགས» ("the watch, the kung, and so on") reads without strain as the day's divisions after the year, month and date; this supports claims/hand-reckoning-kung-a-lead.md, still a lead and not a reading, since no text uses «ཐུན་ཀུང» in a reckoning (BDRC «ཐུན་ཀུང» 1 hit, a meditation song's «thun kung 'bad»; «ཐུན་ཀླུང» 1, a Chinese place name; «ཐུན་གུང» 21, all «…ཐུན་གུང་ཐུན…» across the words of the three-session lists). The names. The two editions part on two words, and the dictionaries side with the block print: «ཕུང» is "ruin, disaster" (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, Tshig mdzod chen mo) and «ཀླུང» "fortune" (Jim Valby; WB's own «ཀླུང་རྟ», luck), while «མུང» has no headword in the 64 dictionaries (only an abbreviation of «མཇུག་བྱང») and «ཀྲུང» only as the prefix of «ཀྲུང་ཀྲུང» (the crane) and «ཀྲུང་གོ» (China): the typeset reading gives two syllables with no Tibetan sense, the block print two with one. That makes «ཕུང» and «ཀླུང» the likelier of Mipham's own hand, and the typeset notes' and the Gser thang etext's «མུང», «ཀྲུང» the editors' misreading; not proven, since the third name «ཀྱཱིས» has no sense in either (it is only the instrumental particle with a long vowel). The sense of the three results as a scale (ruin, fortune, the third) is not told by the page. Still open: the sense of the third name, what the watch and the kung are counted by (q652).
