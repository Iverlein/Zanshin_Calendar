# Does p. 349's verdict on the yogas count the 27 yogas or a shorter list

P. 349's verdict on the yogas, "six, nine, ten, thirteen and eighteen are bad; the self-born three, five, seven and two, eight, six are good", has the six in both lists and does not say whether it counts the twenty-seven yogas or a shorter list; which?

**Corrected 2026-11-05 (RD157, [claims/wb-p349-yoga-verdict-and-sesame-pounding.md](../claims/wb-p349-yoga-verdict-and-sesame-pounding.md)).** The eighteenth yoga is dpa' bo, as yogas.md has it, not yongs 'joms: the claim of 2026-11-01 was off by one. The good line stays open.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The bad line counts the twenty-seven yogas, not a shorter list: in the order of p. 347's verses the sixth is rab stongs (རབ་སྟོངས), the ninth gzer (གཟེར), the tenth 'bras (འབྲས), the thirteenth rma chen (རྨ་ཆེན) and the eighteenth dpa' bo (དཔའ་བོ), each with a result the verses give (claims/wb-p349-yoga-verdict-and-sesame-pounding.md; WB vol. 2, pp. 349-350, wb.2:p349-350, read on the scan). The good line "self-born three, five, seven and two, eight, six" counts the same list (q551, answered 2026-11-06 by list order): 2 mdza' bo, 3 tshe ldan, 5 bzang po, 6 rab stongs, 7 las bzang, 8 'dzin pa. The sixth stands in both lines because the verse itself puts it in both; WB's text is read twice on the scan and the numbers confirmed, so it is not a misreading. Why the sixth, whose own verse says wealth and cattle run out, is also called good is not explained anywhere read; the word «རང་སྐྱེས» (self-born) is unglossed (q551).
