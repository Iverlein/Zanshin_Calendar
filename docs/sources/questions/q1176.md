# Do Mipham's own print of the notes (I2029) and the Chengdu Collected Works print (I1PD34647) read «དུས་ཁམས» or «རུས་ཁམས» at the notes' first sentence (WB vol. 2 p. 474; img. 530-531 of I1PD34647 is the opening folio)?

Raised by the 2026-11-13 investigation f.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Both prints read «རུས་ཁམས་ནི་ངོས་…», with «རུས» (rus), the clan, not «དུས». Mipham's own print (I2029 img. 622, 3400 px, line 3) and the Chengdu Collected Works (I1PD34647 img. 530, line 4) show the same first glyph at 6-8×: a flat top bar with a spur and a leg, ུ below, then ས, without the rounded body that «ད» has in the same page («དེ་དང»); Yigdzin-1 on a crop of the sentence from each print reads «རུས་ཁམས་ནི་ངོས་ཟིན་ན» in Mipham's print and «རུས་ཁམས་ནི་ངོས་འཛིན་ན» in the Chengdu one (the Chengdu typeset has «འཛིན» where the woodblock and the Beijing etext have «ཟིན»). So the «དུས» of the two typeset editions of WB (Beijing and Gser thang, claim wb-notes-opening-dus-khams-in-two-printings) is a misreading or misprint of «རུས» that both inherited; the sentence's own next clause writes «རུས་ཆེན་ལྔ». Read by eye on crops and confirmed by the machine reader, so the letter ར against ད rests on a small glyph: state scan, with that doubt. Answers also q1025.
