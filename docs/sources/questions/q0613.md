# «ཕྱི་བསྐོར» in Glag bla's passage

Is ཕྱི་བསྐོར in Glag bla's passage (printed p. 442) the reverse of the Dharma circuit, or an outward turning of the roll? The Bon circuit it belongs to reads as the reverse by K of prayer-wheel.md.

RD171 (2026-11-06), narrowed: in Tshig mdzod chen mo «ཕྱི་སྐོར» first means circling with the object kept on the left (Ives Waldo: counter-clockwise); a Nyingma text ties «bon bskor» to the mantra head turned inward. The likeliest sense is the reverse of the Dharma circuit. No Bon text used for any sense.

## Answered 2026-11-21

BDRC's exact search «གཡོན་ཕྱོགས་སུ་ཕྱི་བསྐོར» (2026-11-21, inv-d) finds the word in use with its direction named, in the *Rin chen gter mdzod chen mo* (MW1KG14, Shechen print, etext VE1KG14_033, [p198], a rite of the earth-spirits' pit, a Nyingma treasure collection). The rite first has the water-source circled to the right: «ཨཱོྃ་ཕེ་ལཾ་ཧཱུྃ་ཕཊ༔ ཅེས་པས་སྦྲུལ་གྱི་གཡས་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འཁོར་བར་བསམ༔» ("by this, think that the snake's right side turns round") and then «དེ་ནས་གཡོན་ཕྱོགས་སུ་ཕྱི་བསྐོར་བྱ་བའི་དོན། བསྐོར་ཏེ་བྱང་ངོས་ནས༔» ("then, the meaning of doing the outer turning (ཕྱི་བསྐོར, phyi bskor) to the left side: circling, from the north side ..."). So «ཕྱི་བསྐོར» there is the turning to the left, set against the turning to the right: the reverse of the Dharma circuit. That is the sense the dictionaries' first entry (the object kept on the left) gave; Glag bla's «ཕྱི་བསྐོར་བྱ་དགོས» after the Bon circuit is read the same way: the reverse of the Dharma circuit, not an outward turning of the roll. Glag bla's own sentence still says nothing of the direction; the answer rests on the word's use in this rite. No Bon text is used.
