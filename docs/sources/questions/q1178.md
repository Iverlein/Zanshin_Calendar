# Who is the Mentsikhang head teacher Dbyangs can dgyes pa of the Precious Key's colophon (pk:i383-385), and is he a second man beside Prajñāmaṇi? The colophon's «འམ» (or) is unsettled.

Raised by the 2026-11-13 investigation g.

## Answered 2026-11-14

He is not a second man: Dbyangs can dgyes pa'i lang tsho is the name of Mkhyen rab nor bu (Prajñāmaṇi), the Mentsikhang head teacher himself, and the colophon's «འམ» ("or") joins two names of one man. The full phrase in the colophon is «བཤེས་གཉེན་དབྱངས་ཅན་དགྱེས་པའི་ལང་ཚོ་འམ། པྲཛྙཱ་མ་ཎིའི་ཞལ་སྔ་ནས», "the spiritual friend Dbyangs can dgyes pa'i lang tsho, or the presence of Prajñāmaṇi" (the pool's earlier rendering dropped «ལང་ཚོ»).
Evidence. (1) BDRC's person record P227 (Rtsed thang Mkhyen rab nor bu, 1883-1962) lists «pradz+nyA ma Ni» as one of his names. (2) The biography of him in the Lhasa Mentsikhang's «Bod kyi sman rtsis ched rtsom phyogs bsdus» (1986; BDRC MW1KG21754, etext VEIE0OPID02049E0_I1KG21849 [p62]-[p65]; born in the water-sheep year, 1883, at Rtsed thang) says, of his studies of Tibetan medicine and grammar at Lhasa: «བརྡ་སྤྲོད་ཀུན་བཤད་ཀྱི་མཚན་ལ་དབྱངས་ཅན་དགྱེས་པའི་ལང་ཚོ་ཞེས་སུ་གསོལ» — (the etext misprints the first word «བད་སྤྲོད») — "in (the matter of) the name of the Brda sprod kun bshad (his grammar studies), he was called Dbyangs can dgyes pa'i lang tsho"; the sentence stands also in the Bod lugs gso rig tshig mdzod chen mo (BDRC MW00EGS1017120). (3) The Mentsikhang's title: a memoir of the third Khri byang (MW14592) calls him «bod gzhung sman rtsis dbu 'doms mkhan chen Mkhyen rab nor bu», the head teacher of the Tibetan government's medicine and reckoning, the colophon's «gzhung sman rtsis dbu 'doms».
Date. The Mentsikhang was founded in 1916 (the 1986 volume's own foreword: seventy years before 1986), and Mkhyen rab nor bu was born in 1883, so the water bird year of the colophon cannot be 1873; it is the seventh year of the sixteenth Rab byung cycle, 1933. This settles the choice that pk:i383-385 left between 1873 and 1933.
Not found: a line in the Precious Key's colophon itself that equates the two; the identification rests on the epithet being his and on the same office.
