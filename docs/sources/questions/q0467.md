# Are the sky doors of pp. 367–368 fixed to dates or to days of the week, and who are the riders and skulls

Pp. 367–368 give each of the eight trigrams a rider and a skull and each of the nine mewa a rider, and the sky doors of guest, trade, child, war, kin, fortress and bride with a remedy: the machine's names of riders and skulls (the "rtsangs pa", Gin the jackal's skull) need the scan; are the sky doors fixed to dates or to days of the week?

RD183 (2026-11-06): the dates question is answered (the pool holds the rule: n-th date of each decade, not weekdays). The skulls are narrowed: Gin's is «ཤོ་རེ» with a peg driven in, Zin's a smith's skull, Zon's «སྨྱོན་མ» (a mad woman, not the etext's «སྨོན་མ») with rider «རྩངས་པ»; Li to Kham are etext only.

## Answered 2026-11-06

RD183b (2026-11-06), on the scan (Gser thang reprint I3CN5029 img. 373 for Li to Kham, img. 374 for Gin, Zin and Zon): the last text line of p. 367 reads «དྭ་ལ་དམེ་ཐོད་སྦྲུལ་ཞོན་སྒྲིབ། །ཁེན་ནི་མཛེ་ཐོད་ཕག་ཞོན་སྒྲིབ། །ཁམ་ནི་རབས་ཆད་ཐོད་པ་ལ། །གླང་ཆེན་ཞོན་པས…»; Dwa's skull is the kin-slayer's («དམེ»), not 'a pauper's' as the claim wb-p365-368-sky-doors-riders-skulls has it; the others are as the etext: Li a butcher's with a water-monster, Khon a carpenter's with a sinner, Khen a leper's with a boar, Kham the heirless one's with an elephant. With Gin's «ཤོ་རེ» skull with a peg, Zin's smith's and Zon's «སྨྱོན་མ» (RD183), the skulls are all read on the scan.
