# Whose year serves for the son and bride and the points the notes pass over

Which point does the notes' «བུ་བག» (the son's and the bride's year-horses, p. 532) belong to: the fourteenth (the bride's going), the thirteenth (husband and wife), or both? And whose year serves for the points the notes pass over (12–13, 16, 18–20, 22, 26–30)?

**In part 2026-10-23 (RD47, [claims/thirty-subjects-horses-whose-year.md](../claims/thirty-subjects-horses-whose-year.md)).** The regent's replies 137–138 (ys:i494-496, ys:i496): the tenth point, subjects, by the lord's year, or by the asker's own when one of his household asks for his own wish; the eleventh, horses, by whoever asks, chief owner, groom or buyer, each for his own matter: the reckoning is "in the power of whoever asks", stated. That the rule holds for the points the notes pass over is an inference; the son and bride are not named.
