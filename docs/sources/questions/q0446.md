# What is «ཡོ» («ཁ་ཡོ») in the rat day's «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན» and what is served or led from it

On p. 224 the rat day's «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན» (WB vol. 1, p. 133's table: «ཁ་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན»): the beer of the dead is not strained at the threshold (the verb is «to strain»), but what is «ཡོ» («ཁ་ཡོ»), the mouth of the jar askew, and what is served or led from it? Narrows q439 and q404 (RD74).

RD162 (2026-11-06): as q439; no new gloss of «ཡོ».

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-bbb).** As q439 (inv-bbb): no text beyond the three copies of the line; «yo» is "all" (an old word) or "crooked" in the dictionaries, and «ཁ་ཡོ» occurs only in Valby ("mouth crooked"). Open.

**Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z1).** BDRC phrases «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག» 3 hits and «མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས» 5: WB's Beijing and Gser thang prints, the Do shal, a Bhutan-anthology table (I1KG11905, vol. p. 560) and a work on the earth lords by month and day (MW2PD17386_85DBF4, I1KG81108, search-only, 403). The Bhutan text gives the line with what follows: «ཐེམ་བུར་མི་འཚག་ཡོ་ནས་འདྲེན། །དེས་འགྲེ་གནས་ཕྱོགས་ངན་པའི་ས། །ཉམས་ན་བཅོས་སོགས་རིམ་པ་བཞིན།» (the threshold ... not strained, led from the *yo*; by that the place of the 'gre-station is a bad-direction ground; if it is harmed, mend it by the usual order) and, in its table, «ཁ་ཡེ་ནས ཕྱོགས་བཞིར»; so «ཡོ» is the place from which the beer is led and the direction it harms is named after it, but no text names the thing. Open.

Nothing new on «ཡོ»; open.
