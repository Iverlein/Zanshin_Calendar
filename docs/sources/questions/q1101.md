# In the Cakrasaṃvara commentary of 'Jigs med dga' byed (VE23703_1333 p. 573) what is «ཆུ་ཚོད་ལྔ་པའི་ཐུན་མཚམས» (the session of the fifth chu tshod) after «མཚན་མོ་ཕྱེད» (midnight): the fifth of the six watches of the night, the fifth hour of the water-clock of the night, or the last session before the early morning; and is «རྨི་ལམ་དགེ་བ་བལྟ་བ» an instruction to dream or to watch for a dream?

RD386.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The session of the fifth chu tshod is not the fifth of six watches: the commentary has no six-part night. Its measures are the water-hour of sixty to the day and night, five of them to a junction, and eight watches, four sessions, sixteen half-watches (svo:i521-523, svo:i525). The sādhana of pp. 572-573 puts it after midnight and before the early-morning session (sleep, midnight, the fifth chu tshod, the early morning), which fits a count from midnight; the passage does not name the moment the water-hours are counted from (q1124, q1149 stay narrowed). In the Kālacakra commentary the eight watches likewise run four from midnight to noon and four from noon to midnight (svo:i538).

«རྨི་ལམ་དགེ་བ་བལྟ་བར་བྱའོ» is not glossed anywhere found; བལྟ is "to look at, watch", said after the practitioner has slept and woken to meditate at midnight, and the more natural sense is to look for a virtuous dream among the night's dreams than to induce one; no list of images, meaning or remedy follows.
