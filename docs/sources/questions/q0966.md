# In WB vol. 2 p. 403, are the eight watches (ཐུན་བརྒྱད) of the day-and-night the eight half-watches of the day together with the eight of the night (sixteen in all), as the later books count the Dbyangs 'char's half-watches, or eight in all?

RD326.

## Answered 2026-11-16

Eight in all (four by day, four by night); the sixteen are the half-watches (ཐུན་ཕྱེད), which p. 403 does not use.

Read 2026-11-16 (inv-k), etext (wb.2:p403d). In the Phug-pa books and in Mipham the words «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» name the day-and-night's eight (great) watches, and the half-watches are a separate count of sixteen: the Sgang dril (MW3CN3480, etext pp. 77, 78, 83, 121) has «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཆེན་བརྒྱད» and, halved, «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག», each with its own table; Mipham (etext p. 174) «ཉིན་ཞག་གཅིག་ལ་ཐུན་བརྒྱད། དེ་ཕྱེད་དུ་བྱས་པས་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག». Tsong kha pa (gsung 'bum, BDRC MW3PD188, volume I3PD276, etext p. 467) quotes the tantra's «ཐུན་ནི་བརྒྱད་ཆ» (the watch is an eighth part) and so reads «ཞག་གཅིག་ལ་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག་ཡོད་པའི་ལུགས», the way of sixteen half-watches to a day. So WB's «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད», with one planet-and-nāga pair to each watch and the eight pairs run once (wb.2:p403c), is the eight in all, not the sixteen of the Dbyangs 'char's half-watches.

What stays: the Dbyangs 'char's own Rāhu rule runs on the half-watches (eight by day, eight by night, Mipham's Kālacakra commentary and the Mchog dga'); whether WB's «own time» pairs are laid on the eight watches or on the day's eighths is said only by p. 403's closing lines (the day's measure divided by the nāga), and WB does not name «ཐུན་ཆེན» or «ཐུན་ཕྱེད» there.
