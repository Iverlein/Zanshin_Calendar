# Is the series year, eight months, three months, half a month, ten days at dawn, shared by the Svapnohana and the Brahmavaivartapurāṇa, older than the Purāṇa, and which older Indian dream text has it?

The Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya of Negelein's manuscript i, the Śaunaka-kārikā, the Śabdakalpadruma's Vāgbhaṭa Svapnādhyāya; could the Tibetan translator have known it?

Narrowed (inv-j, 2026-11-14): of the three texts named, none has the series. Negelein's Bṛhaspati Svapnādhyāya (his i) gives eight months, three fortnights, a month, ten days, and the four open copies (Penn, Srinagar, Ramtek) a month for the fourth watch; the Śaunaka Kārikā has no span in the pages cited; the Śabdakalpadruma's Vāgbhaṭa passage (his v) is Śārīrasthāna 6 and gives no series, the article's only series being the Brahmavaivarta's (claims neg-manuscripts-and-the-series, skd-svapna-article-parts-and-sources, indian-dream-spans-by-watch-nine-texts). The Atharva Pariśiṣṭa, Agni and Matsya have parts of the shape in other numbers. The Brahmavaivarta is dated from the 8th to the 16th century and a work of Bengal (Hazra, Rocher), and Vibhūticandra is called eastern Indian in the colophon: a Sanskrit text of that family could have reached him, but no text older than the Svapnohana (about 1200) carries the whole series, and whether the Purāṇa's chapter is older than the translation is not decided (claim brahmavaivarta-date-hazra-rocher).
