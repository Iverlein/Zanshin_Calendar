# Does «མཚན་སྟོད» mean the first part of the night up to midnight (Byang pa, 'Bum nag gsal sgron) or dusk with midnight alike (Khro ru), and is the part from midnight to dawn named anywhere in the Four Tantras' tradition?

Byang pa and the 'Bum nag gsal sgron take it as the first part up to midnight, Khro ru as dusk with midnight alike.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Both readings are in the tradition and the text itself fixes neither. The Four Tantras' ch. 7 rule has two parts of the night only: the first part (མཚན་སྟོད, mtshan stod), whose dream is forgotten and bears no fruit, and the dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs), whose clear dream does (gt:i42). The commentaries read (BDRC etexts, 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-14) divide three ways. The first part is the half of the night up to midnight (ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཡན་ཆད, nam phyed yan chad) in the Moonbeams (Zla ba'i 'od zer), Byang pa, the 'Bum nag gsal sgron and the Blue Beryl, and in Zur mkhar ba when he comments on the Moonbeams. It is dusk with midnight, both without fruit, in Khro ru, in Zur mkhar ba on the Four Tantras' own two lines (p. 356: the first part is dusk, midnight is of a kind with it) and in Skyem pa Tshe dbang (nam phyed mtshan stod together). Rin spungs pa and the Drikung give it no bound. As to the second half, no commentary gives the stretch from midnight to dawn a name or a rule: the searches of the terms for it (མཚན་བར, མཚན་ཕྱི, མཚན་མཇུག, མཚན་ཐ་མ, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཕྱི, ནམ་ཕྱེད་ནས, ནམ་གུང, མཚན་ཕྱེད, ནམ་ཕྱེད་འདས) found nothing, and the only time after midnight the commentaries name is the dawn. Searched again on 2026-11-14 in BDRC's full text without a hit: «མཚན་སྨད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ», «མཚན་སྟོད་ནམ་ཕྱེད་བར», «མཚན་སྨད་ནམ་ཕྱེད», «མཚན་སྟོད་དང་ནམ་ཕྱེད», «མཚན་སྨད་དང་ཐོ་རངས», «མཚན་སྟོད་མཚན་སྨད». The only paired use of the second part's name is one modern medical book (a manual of chronic diseases, 2016, MW3CN5510, search snippet only, the etext closed) that writes «མཚན་སྟོད་དང་མཚན་སྨད» (the upper and the lower part of the night) among the signs of an illness; it is not a commentary on the dream rule and gives no bound. The Vinaya and bodhisattva-vow literature pairs the same two words, «ནམ་གྱི་ཆ་སྟོད་དང་ཆ་སྨད» (Tsong kha pa, the Abhidharmasamuccaya commentary), as the first and last watches between which one sleeps in the middle one (svo:p271p), a scheme of its own. For the app: the Four Tantras' rule is dawn against everything earlier, and where the first part ends is a commentators' difference, midnight or dusk with midnight, that the text does not decide.
