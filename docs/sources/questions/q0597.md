# The la's wandering by the compass half, p. 16

For the la's wandering of p. 16 (the rat and pig year's la south, the snake and horse year's north), does a commentary or an oral tradition of the Phugpa state the rule that each la goes to the half of the compass opposite to its animal's own place, and why are only these four of the twelve years named?

## Searched 2026-11-21 (rd-z)

BDRC exact phrases from the verse (བྱི་བའི་ལོ་པ་བླ་ལྷོར་འཁྱམས, བླ་ནི་བྱང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སུ་འཁྱམས, ཕོ་མོའི་བླ་ཅིག་འཁྱམས་མ་འཁྱམས, ཤར་སྦྲུལ་ལོ་པའི): 2 to 3 hits each, the White Beryl's two prints (I1KG12907, I3CN5029) and one more large etext (I4CZ74285, offset 971421, vol. p. 659-660), a garbled OCR of the same verses with the rat, snake, horse and pig lines in the same order and with no gloss; no commentary states the rule. As to why four: the verse's own build names, for each of the four trines of sky years (rat-dragon-monkey, bird-ox-snake, tiger-horse-dog, pig-sheep-hare), one born year, so four is the number of the trines, not a choice among twelve; that part is the text's, the compass-half rule is still only the pool's own count (claim ch27-la-wandering-by-compass-half). Open for a commentary or an oral word.
