# Fire and iron clans' great and small cut-off: LT against the notes

LT's great and small cut-off for a fire clan (dog great, dragon small) and an iron clan (ox great, sheep small) (lt:p26-28) go against the notes' rule that dragon and sheep are great and dog and ox small (wb.2:p499-500): which holds on the la board for those clans?

## Answered 2026-11-14

On the la board the great cut-off is the clan's own grave stage and the small cut-off its seventh, which is LT's table; the notes' line is the corner rule worked on the wood clan. The White Beryl's verse on the graves, found in six BDRC copies (the Vaidurya dkar po's prints and handbooks, the Zlos gar, the Rdel 'grem 'bras bshad, Grags pa bshad sgrub), reads «ཤིང་དུར་ལུག་ལ་མེ་དུར་ཁྱི། ལྕགས་དུར་གླང་ལ་ས་ཆུ་འབྲུག །དུར་ཞུགས་ཆད་ཆེན་གཙོ་བོ་ཡིན། བདུན་ཟུར་ཆད་ཆུང་མནའ་མ་ཡིན།» (wood's grave the sheep, fire's the dog, iron's the ox, earth's and water's the dragon; entering the grave is the chief great cut-off, its seventh the small cut-off, the bride): so for a fire clan the great cut-off is the dog and the small the dragon, for an iron clan the ox and the sheep, as LT has them. The notes' sentence (wb.2:p499-500, «མཚམས་བཞི་འབྲུག་ལུག་ཆེ་ཁྱི་གླང་ཆུང་། … དེ་ཡང་རུས་ཤིང་ཁམས་པར་དཔེར་བྱས་ན་ཆད་ཆེན་ལུག་…», also in the White Beryl's etext of the notes) names the four corner animals, dragon and sheep great, dog and ox small, and goes on at once to the wood clan as its example (sheep great, ox small, the seventh); it agrees with the verse for wood, earth and water (dragon great, dog small) and swaps the pair for fire and iron. The verse is the White Beryl's own text and LT repeats it; the notes give no separate board for fire and iron and no word that the corner rule overrides the grave stage. So the la board for fire and iron follows the clan's grave: fire dog great and dragon small, iron ox great and sheep small. The notes' corner wording is a summary that fits the wood example, not a second rule; why Mipham's four corners are given with dragon and sheep great is not stated.
