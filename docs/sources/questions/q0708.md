# Printed year-name variants against ch. 13's list (entries 39, 43, 58–60)

ཐིལ་གནོན (printed, entry 43, p. 208) or ཟིལ་གནོན (ch. 13's list, Overpowering)? Also དགའ་བྱེད for དགེ་བྱེད (entry 39), གདང་ངན for བློ་ངན (58), ཇ་ཆེན for རྔ་ཆེན (59), ཁྲག་སྨྱུག་པ for ཁྲག་སྐྱུག (60): are these the print's own variants, and which does the tradition use? (RD190, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

## Answered 2026-11-14

The names the pool marks as variants, checked against the dictionaries' year names (Steinert's 64 sets):

- Entry 43: ཟིལ་གནོན (*zil gnon*) is the fire male horse year: Ives-Waldo «zil gnon … Parabhava, 40th year, {me pho rta}», the *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «zil gnon (mngon) me pho rta». No headword «thil gnon». The print's ཐིལ is a misreading or a slip for ཟིལ (the other White Beryl etexts have ཟིལ; BDRC search of 2026-11-13).
- Entry 58: the dictionaries give the iron female bird year (55th) as བློ་ངན (*blo ngan*, Durmati; Rangjung Yeshe, Ives-Waldo, *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «(mngon) lcags bya lo»). «gdong ngan» there is the fire male monkey year (30th, Durmukha); no headword «gdang ngan». So གདང་ངན is a misreading of གདོང་ངན (as the 2026-11-13 BDRC search had it), and གདོང་ངན for the bird year is this list's own name; the tradition's name is བློ་ངན.
- Entry 59: རྔ་ཆེན (*rnga chen*) is the water male dog year (56th, Dundubhi; Rangjung Yeshe, Ives-Waldo, *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «(mngon) chu khyi lo»); no headword «ja chen». ཇ་ཆེན is this print's variant.
- Entry 60: ཁྲག་སྐྱུག (*khrag skyug*) is the water female pig year (57th, Rudhirodgārin; the three dictionaries again, the *Sgra bye brag tu rtogs byed chen mo* too); no headword «khrag smyug pa». The pool's སྨྱུག is a misreading.
- Entry 39: the dictionaries give དགེ་བྱེད (*dge byed*) as the water tiger year (36th, Śubhakṛt); the *Tshig mdzod chen mo*'s «dga' byed» has no year sense (chang, the cock, the ogre). དགའ་བྱེད་དཔལ་ལྡན is thus not the calendar name; it is the *Sūtra of the Secret* list's own name for that year, as in every print found (2026-11-13).

So the tradition uses ཟིལ་གནོན, བློ་ངན, རྔ་ཆེན, ཁྲག་སྐྱུག and དགེ་བྱེད; the print's ཐིལ and its etext's ཇ་ཆེན, གདོང་ངན (for 58) and དགའ་བྱེད are, apart from the two misreadings in the pool, that list's or that print's own forms. The scan of img. 205–211 was not read for ཐིལ, གདང/གདོང and སྨྱུག; the dictionary headwords do not need it for the tradition's spelling.

Source: Steinert's dictionaries (read 2026-11-14), wb.1:p206, wb.1:p208, wb.1:p209.
