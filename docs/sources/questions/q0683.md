# The seven kinds of conduct of p. 201: the four lists not given

Of the seven kinds of conduct (སྤྱོད་པ་བདུན) of p. 201, which are the four whose lists are not given (the sharp, the one from the end of the combination, the fierce sinful one, and the seventh), and what do the numbers one to six, seven to twelve, thirteen to twenty-four and twenty-five to thirty count? (RD184, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

The seven kinds of conduct (སྤྱོད་པ་བདུན, spyod pa bdun) are the seven courses of Mercury (ལྷག་པ, lhag pa) that Varāhamihira's Bṛhat Saṃhitā, chapter 7 (Budha-cāra), verse 8, ascribes to the Parāśara tantra: Prākṛta, Vimiśra, Saṃkṣipta, Tīkṣṇa, Yogānta, Ghora, Pāpa. WB's list matches in order: the ordinary (ཐ་མལ, the Prākṛta, with Svātī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, Rohiṇī, as the Sanskrit gives it), the leading (རྣམ་པར་འདྲེན་པ, the Vimiśra, "mixed"; Sanskrit Mṛgaśiras, Ārdrā, Maghā, Āśleṣā, WB names Ārdrā, Maghā, Āśleṣā), the summarised (མདོར་བསྡུས་པ, the Saṃkṣipta: Puṣya, Punarvasu and the two Phalgunīs; WB names Punarvasu, Pūrvaphalgunī, Uttaraphalgunī), then the sharp (རྣོ་བ, the Tīkṣṇa), the one from the end of the combination (སྦྱོར་བའི་མཐའ, the Yogānta) and the fierce, sinful one (དྲག་པོ་སྡིག་པ, the Ghora and the Pāpa: the verse's «དྲག་པོ་སྡིག་པའི་སྤྱོད་པ་སོགས», "the fierce, sinful conduct and so on", folds two names into one and «སོགས» carries the seventh). So the four lists WB does not give are the Tīkṣṇa (Sanskrit: the two Bhādrapadās, Viśākhā, Aśvinī, Revatī), the Yogānta (Mūla and the two Āṣāḍhās), the Ghora (Śravaṇa, Citrā, Dhaniṣṭhā and the Vāruṇa/Tvāṣṭra mansions, the web edition's translation and Sanskrit differing) and the Pāpa (Hasta, Anurādhā, Jyeṣṭhā).

The numbers belong to a second division, of the four motions of Mercury "according to Devala" (Bṛhat Saṃhitā 7.15, «ऋज्वीअतिवक्रावक्रा विकला च मतेन देवलस्य एताः । पञ्चचतुर्द्व्येकाहा ऋज्व्यादीनां षडभ्यस्ताः»): straight (ṛjvī), very crooked (ativakra), crooked (vakra), irregular (vikala), whose effects last 5, 4, 2 and 1 times six days: 30, 24, 12 and 6 days. WB's "four conducts" are these: one to six is the vikala (the verse 7.16 gives fear and disease; WB: many kinds of bad disease), seven to twelve the vakra (war; WB: quarrel), thirteen to twenty-four the ativakra (loss of wealth; WB: famine), and twenty-five to thirty the straight course, good for all beings. So the numbers count days of the effect, counted up to the six, twelve, twenty-four and thirty days of each motion. WB's text does not say "days", and WB's results are its own wording (quarrel, famine), which only roughly follows the Sanskrit verse 7.16.

Source: Varāhamihira, Bṛhat Saṃhitā 7.8, 7.9-7.12, 7.15, 7.16, Sanskrit and N. Chidambaram Iyer's 1884 translation as set out at siva.sh/brihat-samhita/7 and wisdomlib.org (read 2026-11-14 as a web edition; the verses are quoted from it and not checked against a critical edition). The link of WB p. 201 to this chapter rests on the names, the mansion lists and the four numbers agreeing.
