# The mantra after «dus 'khor lo'r» on p. 14

The mantra after «gnyis med khyab bdag dus 'khor lo'r» on p. 14 reads ཧྲཱི་ཏྱཱ་ནྱཱ་ཀ་ཊུ་ཀ in the reprint and པྲཱི་ཎྱཱ་ཎྱཱ་ཀ་ཊཱ་ཀ in the 1996 scan; which is the word, and what does it name? (RD198, corpus/wb/ch02.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

The word is the Sanskrit of the next words, «dpal ldan 'bras spungs mchod rten chen po»: Śrī Dhānyakaṭaka, the great stūpa where the Kālacakra was taught. On the 1996 scan (BDRC I1KG12906, img. 24, p. 14, the line after «gnyis med khyab bdag dus 'khor lo'r», at 10×) the word is six syllables, each with its dot below (the print marks Sanskrit words with a dot under every syllable), which is the syllable count of śrī-dhā-nya-ka-ṭa-ka; the first syllable is ཤྲཱི (śrī: the machine reprint's ཧྲཱི and the scan reading's པྲཱི both stand for it), the last three are ka-ṭa-ka. Both machine readings, ཧྲཱི་ཏྱཱ་ནྱཱ་ཀ་ཊུ་ཀ and པྲཱི་ཎྱཱ་ཎྱཱ་ཀ་ཊཱ་ཀ, are therefore corruptions of one name, not two words. The Tibetan glosses it at once as «dpal ldan 'bras spungs mchod rten chen po». The middle syllables are small stacks that the scan does not give letter by letter at this size, so the exact printed spelling is not settled; the word, by its count, its dots and its gloss, names Śrī Dhānyakaṭaka.
