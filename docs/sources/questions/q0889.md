# Is the third syllable of the Gāyatrī's first word in the Svapnohana «ཏྲི» (Peking) or «ཏྲེ» (Yigdzin-1 on the Narthang strip), and is the word the Sanskrit gāyatrī rendered «ག་ཡ་ཏྲི»?

The vowel sign above the stack is a small curve; ི and ེ are not told apart on it (RD289).

## Answered 2026-11-16

Answered (inv-z2, 2026-11-16, BDRC phrase search; etext snippets): the word is «ག་ཡ་ཏྲི» (ga ya tri), the third syllable ཏྲི. The Peking print reads it so on a clear scan and the Derge and Dpe bsdur ma etexts agree (claim svo-gayatri-spelling). New: BDRC's exact search «ག་ཡ་ཏྲི» gives 17 etext hits and «ག་ཡ་ཏྲེ» 3. The 17 include the Svapnohana's own line «ག་ཡ་ཏྲི་ནི་སྟོང་ཕྲག་བཟླས» ("the Gayatri, a thousand rounds") in the Derge and Dpe bsdur ma copies, and the Sanskrit metre-list of the Tarkajvālā (Derge and Dpe bsdur ma) and of two Grub mtha' manuals, «ga ya tri dang / a sh+ni dang / u nu sh+thu ba dang / 'bri ha ti dang / sang ga ti dang / tish+thu pa dang / dza ga ti» (the seven metres of the «sdeb sbyor bdun»: gāyatrī, uṣṇih, anuṣṭubh, bṛhatī, paṅkti, triṣṭubh, jagatī), and the Kālacakra's goddess list, «ga ya tri dang / glog ma dang». The 3 hits with «ག་ཡ་ཏྲེ» are all inside mantra strings (a Ngor «anu raga ya tre lokya», a Rin chen grub dhāraṇī), where tre is the vocative ending of a mantra word and not the metre's name. So «ག་ཡ་ཏྲི» is the Tibetan spelling of Sanskrit gāyatrī and Yigdzin-1's «ཏྲེ» on the Narthang strip is its misreading of the small curve.
