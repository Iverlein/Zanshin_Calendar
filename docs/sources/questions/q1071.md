# Is the wording «the first three of the night made four parts are not certain, so the examination is in the fourth» of the Drukpa manuals (Yon tan 'od dam bstan 'dzin don kun grub; Mkhas dbang Sangs rgyas rdo rje, who names Padma dkar po and Lha rtse ba Ngag dbang bzang po as his teachers) taken from Tsong kha pa's Sngags rim chen mo or from a Drukpa manual older than both, for instance one of Padma dkar po's?

RD377.

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The wording was searched on 2026-11-13 in BDRC's full-text search as an exact phrase («མཚན་མོ་ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་ཐུན་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད»: one hit; the shorter «ཐུན་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད»: four). It stands in the Drukpa manuals: the Vajrāvalī rite in the 'Brug pa'i chos mdzod chen po bsam 'phel nor bu'i bang mdzod (VEIE0OPIDDB781DE_I3CN2269), Sangs rgyas rdo rje's «dkyil chog rdo rje 'phreng ba'i dbang gzhung mtshams sbyor» (MW3CN2232_236751, the same words) and the Gene Smith copy 'Brug pa'i lugs kyi dbang chog 'dod rgu'i bang mdzod; two more etexts (VEIE0OPIB2682C2D_I2139, VEIE0OPI7F0370A0_I4161) have the shorter phrase and were not opened. The sentence follows the root tantra's four lines as the manual's own gloss on them. No Tsong kha pa text and no older Drukpa manual with the wording was found, and no etext of the Sngags rim chen mo was searched, so its source is still not named.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Taken from Tsong kha pa's Sngags rim chen mo, in all probability; no older Drukpa manual with the wording was found. The Sngags rim chen mo's sentence is «རྨི་ལམ་ཡང་མཚན་ལ་ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད་པས་ཆ་བཞི་པ་ལ་བྱ་སྟེ» (snr:p370; the Drukpa manual has «མཚན་མོ་ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་ཐུན་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད»: «ཐུན» added, «མཚན» spelt «མཚན་མོ»). More than the sentence is shared: the Drukpa Vajrāvalī manual (dkb:p163-164) follows the Sngags rim chen mo's whole run of authorities in the same order after the verse of the four watches: joy at seeing the Three Jewels, a fall from a mountain a bad sign, the Nectar-swirling mantra (བདུད་རྩི་འཁྱིལ་བ), Mar me mdzad bzang po with the Ender of Obstructors, Klu byang's no-self meditation, the Common Tantra's pacifying fire offering, and Dārikapa's seven turnings-back. The attribution to Mar me mdzad bzang po, which the 450-verse rite itself does not support (mmb-remedy-all-doer-not-ender-of-obstructors), is found in these two and in no other text. BDRC's exact searches of 2026-11-14 for «ཆ་བཞིར་བྱས་པའི་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད» give six hits, all Tsong kha pa's Sngags rim chen mo or its quotations (the Beijing 2012 gsung 'bum VEIE0OPI9244B62A_I3PD268, the Asian Legacy etext VE1ALS05281E, VE1ER24, VE1ER48, VEIE0OPI409E1122_I1KG10372, VEIE0OPI51EA6051_I2PD16009); the Drukpa manuals' phrase «ཐུན་དང་པོ་གསུམ་ལ་ངེས་པ་མེད» four (VEIE0OPIDDB781DE_I3CN2269 and _I3CN2263, VEIE0OPIB2682C2D_I2139, VEIE0OPI7F0370A0_I4161), the same hits as before; no Padma dkar po text has it. Inference from wording and order, etext only. The Drukpa wording is therefore a copy, and gives no second witness of the Mar me mdzad bzang po attribution.
