# Is there a text the Kagyu call the Mañjuśrī root tantra, other than Toh 543, that contains the three-part verse «srod la bad kan shas che ste / nam phyed dus su mkhris par brjod / tho rangs rlung gi dus nyid de»?

RD384.


Narrowed (inv-aa, 2026-11-14): no second text. Every dictionary that has the headword «'jam dpal rtsa rgyud» (Tshig mdzod chen mo, Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho, the Buddhist-doctrine dictionary, Ives Waldo, Rangjung Yeshe) gives the one tantra, the 36-chapter Mañjuśrī root tantra translated by 'Gos lo tsā ba Śākya blo gros, i.e. Toh 543, and no other work of that name; its Derge etext has the four-watch verse only (mmk-dream-verse-four-watches-phlegm-bile-wind-true). BDRC's exact-string search for the three lines (2026-11-14): «སྲོད་ལ་བད་ཀན་ཤས་ཆེ་སྟེ» 9 hits, «ནམ་ཕྱེད་དུས་སུ་མཁྲིས་པར་བརྗོད» 8, «ཐོ་རངས་རླུང་གི་དུས་ཉིད་དེ» 4, «བད་ཀན་ཤས་ཆེ་སྟེ» 13: the works are the Karmapa's vol. 13 (the Zab mo nang don commentary), two copies of Kong sprul's commentary, Zur mkhar ba with its copies, Zhwa dmar IV's Sbas don, Padma rdo rje, Bya mdo Klu byams rgyal, a modern medical teacher's note and a verse text on death signs (ch. 7); in all of them the verse is introduced as «from the tantras» (རྒྱུད་རྣམས་ལས / རྒྱུད་ལས), and only Zhwa dmar IV (rrd.16:p220) writes «'jam dpal rtsa rgyud las». «འཇམ་དཔལ་རྩ་རྒྱུད་ལས་ཀྱང་» has 76 hits (opened: none beyond the commentaries already in the pool). So no text other than Toh 543 is called the Mañjuśrī root tantra by the Kagyu, and no text with the three-part verse has been found under that name; Zhwa dmar IV's attribution is not borne out by Toh 543 (q923). The Nyingma Tantra of the Black Smith has a shorter three-part rule without the humours (lgd.38:p7), no relation to Mañjuśrī. Stays open for Peking, Narthang and Co ne prints of Toh 543, not opened.
