# p. 381's «ངོ་ཐོག» or «ངོ་ཐེག» on the Zhol blocks

P. 381's «ངོ་ཐོག» or «ངོ་ཐེག» (Zhol img. 499, l. 2): the blocks' vowel sign over ཐ is not told at 4× and Yigdzin-1 reads ོ; does another impression of the blocks, or a reader of the tradition, carry «ཐོག», the top, or «ཐེག»? See year-of-life.md, RD126.

RD175 (2026-11-06), narrowed: Yigdzin-1 reads «ཐོག» on the Zhol (img. 499) and the Taikhang (img. 516 line 1); at 14× on the Zhol the sign over ཐ is a short stroke with a small head, nearer ེ than ོ, but the ink does not allow a verdict.

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (scan-b).** The Gser thang reprint (I3CN5028 img. 376, p. 381, line 3, typeset, 7×) reads «ངོ་ཐེག་དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པ་ཡི»: ཐེག, not ཐོག, with the sign over ཐ a single curved cap against the two-part «ོ» of the preceding ངོ in the same word. Of the prints read by eye the reprint and the 1996 etext have ཐེག; the Yigdzin-1 reading «ཐོག» of the Zhol and the Taikhang stands against them. On the Taikhang (I5877 img. 516, line 1, 2–6×) the phrase is at x about 5100–5300, y 440–590; the two marks over ངོ and ཐ there have the same cursive form (a thick wedge with a thin tail rising to the right) and so do not tell ེ from ོ in that hand. Not answered: the blocks' own reading.
