# How many finger-breadths is one mtho at the digging figure's navel (p. 387)

How many finger-breadths is one *mtho* of the digging figure's navel («མཐོ་གང་སོར་དྲུག», p. 387), if the *mtho* is a tenth of the plot? Ch. 32's count (p. 281) counts the stake as a whole *mtho* and ch. 34's example as half; which stands for a rite of digging?

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (rd-kd3, [claims/kd-digest-lto-phye-figure-measure.md](../claims/kd-digest-lto-phye-figure-measure.md)).** KD vol. 3 p. 744 (etext): the figure is ten *mtho* long (ལུས་ཀྱི་དཀྱུས་སུ་མཐོ་བཅུ་པ), its breadth the measure of his own face, and the hood, face, heart, navel and secret place are one *mtho* each, the snake's tail five; the square is ten great parts a side. So a *mtho* is one great part, a tenth of the figure's length, and the navel is one *mtho*; the digest gives no finger-breadths, so the count of «མཐོ་གང་སོར་དྲུག» stays open.

**Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z1).** The unit: *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, *Tshig gter rgya mtsho* and Ives Waldo: «མཐོ་གང» is «མཐེབ་རྩེ་ནས་གོང་མཛུབ་ཀྱི་རྩེའི་བར་ཚད» (thumb tip to middle finger tip), equal to twelve finger-breadths («སོར་བཅུ་གཉིས»); Negi's Sanskrit dictionary quotes the *Lalitavistara*: «སོར་བཅུ་གཉིས་ལ་ནི་མཐོ་གང་ངོ་» (dvādaśāṅguliparvāṇi vitastiḥ); Rangjung Yeshe the same. So «མཐོ་གང» as a measure is the twelve-finger span, which supports the claim wb-ch34-mtho-is-a-span: «མཐོ་གང་སོར་དྲུག» a span and six fingers, eighteen. It does not say whether the digging rite's *mtho* is that span or a tenth of the figure; open.
