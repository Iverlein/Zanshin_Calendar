# «གསུམ་གྱིས་བགོས» or «བཞི»: dividing the household's pebbles

The notes (vol. 2, p. 491, l. 9–10; «རྡེའུ་གྲངས་གསུམ་གྱིས་བགོས་པས», so on the Gser thang reprint, img. 497, and in Yigdzin-1 of the 1996 print) divide the household's count of pebbles "by three" and then name a remainder of three, "one of the fold and two of the refold"; a fold in half and a refold in half leave exactly the remainder of the count divided by four (1 the fold's, 2 the refold's, 3 both), as the notes' own ground, "the rows doubled and quadrupled", implies. Is «གསུམ» a slip for «བཞི», or does "dividing by three" mean another operation? Mipham's own print of the notes (I2029, img. 621–750) or the Zhol and Taikhang prints would show the word.

**Narrowed 2026-10-25 ([claims/household-round-fold.md](../claims/household-round-fold.md)).** Every print reads «གསུམ»: Mipham's own (I2029 img. 654, l. 3, on the scan at 4× with the contrast raised: ས over ུ, a final མ, no vowel sign above) and the typeset Chengdu edition of his works (MW2DB16631_DB4BAA, I1PD34647 img. 571, l. 3), so the word is not a later printer's. Every witness to the operation divides by four or counts by the folds: the *Good Vase*'s (བུམ་བཟང, bum bzang) verse, the rule this very passage corrects, "divided by four, three left" (carried by the Bon *Mirror*, sn:p70-71, a witness only); LT's four sets with no more than three left (lt:p20-25); Zhechen Gyaltsab's one, two or three by the folds alone (zg:p285-286). By three cannot leave three, and leaves two for four members and three for six where the notes' fold leaves none and two. Read as a slip of the author's text for «བཞི», kept as printed; the leftovers built from the fold. The *Spor thang* sheet of 35 pebbles (kd.3:p726, etext only) would confirm it on the scan (question 398).

**Confirmed for the division 2026-10-25 (RD54, question 398, [claims/kd3-p726-household-round-not-read.md](../claims/kd3-p726-household-round-not-read.md)).** Kun dga' rig 'dzin's worked round of seven members from the *Spor thang* (KD vol. 3, p. 726, leaf 745 at 3×) lays its 35 pebbles in four runs of eight, 1–8, 35–28, 20–27, 16–9, with 17, 18 and 19 over: 35 by four, three left, where by three would leave two. It lays the manuals' quarter-runs, not the notes' halves, so it confirms the division by four, not the notes' fold.
