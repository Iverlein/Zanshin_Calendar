# Who wrote the Sakya Vinaya treatise that places the dawn against the night's watches, and is its last clause on the nine parts the etext's error?

RD314.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered by RD322 (2026-11-10): the Sakya Vinaya treatise that sets the dawn against the night's watches is by Kong ston Dbang phyug grub pa (BDRC P987, work MW23205), a pupil of Go rams pa of the 15th century, not later than 1499: the 'Dul ba mdo rtsa ba'i spyi don legs bshad 'od kyi snang ba tshig don rab gsal; the colophon in the Go rams pa gsung 'bum etext, the editors' preface in the Sakya Gsung rab vol. 3 and the Darjeeling–Kathmandu vol. 35 contents agree; the Beijing collection's BDRC record names Go rams pa as creator, which the colophon decides against. The ninth-part clause is identical in all three etexts, so it is not an etext error; the passage cites 'the scripture' and the commentary on the bhikṣuṇī's Prātimokṣa, not naming the source of the nine parts.
