# «མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག» in the benefits texts

Does «མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག» in the Gangtok print and 'Jam dbyangs don yod mean a wheel set upside down, a roll wound the wrong way, or a wheel turned the wrong way?

RD171 (2026-11-06), narrowed: «མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག» is 'reversed, upside down' (Ives Waldo), elsewhere of an object or letter inverted, including a dhāraṇī put into a support head-and-tail reversed; the 1991 print has it too. Whether it means the roll or the manner of turning is not said; no text says the roll is wound that way.


## Searched again 2026-11-21 (inv-d)

BDRC exact phrase «མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག་པར» 18 hits (snippets read): head-and-tail reversed said of a transferred consciousness drawn out (Pad+ma dkar po, Maitrīpa's phowa), an obstructing spirit turned (Karma chags med), a ཧཱུྃ་ seed meditated reversed (a *Rlung sems dbyer med* khrid yig and its pith: a fault), a pig-fat drink bound (Zin thig), and the Do shal's list of the ways of laying out a corpse («མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག་པར་བྱ་བ»). None is a wheel or roll, none says whether the wheel, the roll or the manner of turning is meant. Not settled; the benefit line's «མགོ་མཇུག་ལོག་ནས་བསྐོར་བ» (reversed, turning) still reads as something done to the thing turned.
