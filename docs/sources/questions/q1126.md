# Is ཚིག་པ་ཟ in the hour entries «eats burnt food» or «is scorched, pained»

In «སྐབས་སུ་ཚིག་པ་ཟ་བར་འདུག» (kd.3:p588-589): the same words are used of a person's heart in Tāranātha's life and Pu to ba's long instruction.

## Answered 2026-11-13

It is the idiom «ཚིག་པ་ཟ་བ» (tshig pa za ba), "to be irritated, angry", not "eats burnt food". The Bod rgya tshig mdzod chen mo and the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho gloss it «ཁོང་ཁྲོ་སྐྱེ་བ» ("anger arises"), with the examples «རྒྱུ་མེད་རྐྱེན་མེད་དུ་ཚིག་པ་ཟ་ལོང་ལོང་ཞིག་བྱུང་» ("for no cause or condition, a fit of irritation came") and «རྣ་བར་མི་འགྲོ་བའི་སྐད་ཅ་ཐོས་དུས་ཚིག་པ་མ་ཟ» ("on hearing words unpleasant to the ear, he did not get irritated"); Rangjung Yeshe "to be angry, aggravated", Valby "irritated, angry", Ives Waldo "irritated, get angry"; Negi's Sanskrit dictionary renders it abhiṣajyate, kupyati ("is vexed, is angry") of a monk who sees newcomers (Negi's source siglum a.śa. 137ka). So «སྐབས་སུ་ཚིག་པ་ཟ་བར་འདུག» is "at times he is (seen) irritated", which is also how Pu to ba's «སྙིང་ནས་རང་ཚིག་པ་ཟ་བར་འདུག» and Tāranātha's beggars read: a state of temper, not food. Searched in Steinert's dictionaries (2026-11-13, locate.py dict).
