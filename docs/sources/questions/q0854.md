# In the Kālaprakāśikā ch. XLII v. 66 does the Sanskrit give a half-month for the fourth watch besides the twelve days at dawn that Iyer's English has?

The line is garbled in the OCR; the page image of p. 189 shows it.

## Answered 2026-11-10

Answered by RD291: no. The Kālaprakāśikā's verse (p. 189 on the page image) gives a year, half a year, three months, a month and twelve days at dawn; there is no half-month, and Iyer's English drops the third span (claim kalaprakasika-xlii-67-fourth-span-is-a-month).
