# The Lord Lama's replies and the iron bird and monkey

Reply 137 cites "the precious Lord Lama's replies on the reckoning of stars and the Chinese reckoning" («རྗེ་བླ་མའི་རྩིས་སྐར་ནག་གི་དྲི་ལན»), which say the thirty are "turned from upon the iron bird and monkey": whose replies are these, where are they found, and is the iron bird and monkey a fixed starting pair or an example?

**Narrowed 2026-11-01 (RD104, [claims/lord-lama-replies-on-star-and-chinese-reckonings.md](../claims/lord-lama-replies-on-star-and-chinese-reckonings.md)).** The work is cited a second time, by reply 156 (ys.2:i016, machine), under the same title and the same mark of honour but another epithet, «༧རྣམ་འདེན་མཆོག» ("the Supreme Guide"): no author's name stands in either place, so whose replies they are is not read from the YS. On the second half: reply 137 itself gives the pair as the stated point of turning for the thirty of China and Tibet («ལྕགས་བྱ་སྤྲེལ་སྟེང་ནས་བསྐོར»), takes it as the pointer to the source of the treatise's way of reckoning, and adds the male or female of the reckoned person's year as the treatise's own division; it gives no other pair and does not call the iron bird and monkey an example. Still open: the author.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Read from BDRC's full-text search snippets (search-only etexts), 2026-11-14 (inv-eee). The replies are the Fifth Dalai Lama's, Ngag dbang blo bzang rgya mtsho: his *Replies on the Star and Black Reckonings*, the «ཉིན་བྱེད་དབང་པོ» (*Nyin byed dbang po*, "the Sun") written in the fire monkey year, 1656.

Phrase search «རྩིས་སྐར་ནག་གི་དྲི་ལན» has five hits, all snippets, quoted here in the search's own Wylie. (1) The Taikhang *Bod kyi bstan rtsis kun gsal bai DUr+ya'i mgul rgyan* (Bsod nams dpal ldan, MW1PD137891), in the entry of the fire monkey year, 1656: «gong sa rgyal dbang lnga pa rin po ches rtsis skar nag gi dri lan nyin byed dbang po brtsams», "the Fifth, the Victorious Lord of the Gong sa, composed the replies on the star and black reckonings, *Nyin byed dbang po*". It agrees with the entry already cited at q266 (KD's history, MW28845_60D816). (2) WB's own *Bai dur g.ya' sel* (MW8226, etext I5876, offset 13840): «rnam 'dren mchog gi rtsis skar nag gi dri lan du», the Supreme Guide's, the epithet of reply 156 (ys.2:i016). (3) Another etext of the *Khrul snang g.ya' sel* (MW2PD17386, in the Tibetan medicine collection *Bod lugs gso rig rtsa che'i dpe rnying kun btus*): «rje bla ma phyag na pad+ma'i rtsis skar nag gi dri lan sogs las», "the Lord Lama Phyag na Padma's replies on the star and black reckonings", and «rtsis skar nag gi dri lan du gnang ba», with the honorific «gnang» ("gave") as in reply 137. «Phyag na padma» (Padmapāṇi) is Avalokiteśvara's name, the Dalai Lamas' emanation; so the Lord Lama of reply 137, the Supreme Guide of reply 156 and Phyag na padma are, by the Regent's own wording in these places, one author, and the Kun gsal names him: the Fifth Dalai Lama. (4) Two bzo rig compilations (MW8LS20245, MW1ER166) quote the Regent's «rje bla ma'i rtsis skar nag gi dri lan du'ang…» line.

The second half stands as narrowed in [claims/lord-lama-replies-on-star-and-chinese-reckonings.md](../claims/lord-lama-replies-on-star-and-chinese-reckonings.md): the iron bird and monkey is the stated point of turning for the thirty of China and Tibet in reply 137's quotation, not an example, and the other etext of the *G.ya' sel* (MW2PD17386) gives the same sentence («lcags bya sprel steng nas bskor»); the work's own text is not in the etexts in hand, so what it says of other pairs is not known.

## Audit 2026-11-14 (inv-audit): stands, with the evidence chain stated

Rechecked with bdrc_search.py: «རྩིས་སྐར་ནག་གི་དྲི་ལན» gives the same five hits. The attribution to the Fifth Dalai Lama is made by one snippet only: the Kun gsal (MW1PD137891, entry of the fire monkey year 1656) says «gong sa rgyal dbang lnga pa rin po ches rtsis skar nag gi dri lan nyin byed dbang po brtsams»; that snippet does attribute the work and gives its name. The three places in the Regent's own text (the Supreme Guide, the Lord Lama, the Lord Lama Phyag na padma) carry no author's name; that they are one work with the Kun gsal's rests on the identical title, not on the Regent's wording, and «phyag na padma» is an epithet that names an emanation, not a person. The identity is probable (one work of this title is known from the snippets) and is held as answered; the claim file should say "by the title's identity".
