# Does the tradition read «the emanation of Māra» in Marpa's song as a dream sent by a demon, a deception of the sleeper's mind, or as spirits, and is the Karmapa's gdon dbang a gloss of the line

The lines «ནམ་གུང་ལ་བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན། དེ་ལ་ཡིད་བརྟན་བྱར་མི་རུང་།» are about the dream of midnight; how is the emanation of Māra read, and is gdon dbang a gloss?

Narrowed (inv-hh, 2026-11-14): no Kagyu text read glosses the line, so neither a demon sending the dream nor the sleeper's mind deceiving itself is the tradition's stated reading. BDRC's exact search «ནམ་གུང་ལ་བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན» finds three places, all the song itself (the Nechu Lhakhang copies of Marpa's Life, MW1KG14303, two and the Rnam thar ngo mtshar sa ma ya); «བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན་པ», «བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན་ཞེས», «ནམ་གུང་གི་རྨི་ལམ་བདུད», «ནམ་གུང་གི་རྨི་ལམ་གདོན», «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་བདུད», «ནམ་ཕྱེད་ཀྱི་རྨི་ལམ་ལྷ་འདྲེ» 0. What the texts do show: the line's three witnesses read it as Māra's work and not to be trusted (rrd.23:p102-104; Gtsug lag phreng ba «cho 'phrul»); the Karmapa recasts it as the spirits' power (rrd.5:p57-59); Karma chags med as the display of gods and spirits (rrd.19:p271); the Nyingma Black Smith tantra as «cho 'phrul» (lgd.38:p7). So the readings go from Māra to spirits to gods and spirits, and none adds a cause. Open for a commentary on Marpa's song or a Kagyu dream-yoga manual that explains «bdud kyi sprul pa».
