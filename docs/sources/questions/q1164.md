# What do the cells of the strip charts of WB vol. 1 pp. 142–147 stand for (the nine sme ba, the earth lords of ch. 14, the sun's course named in the heading row), and by what rule is one cell filled and its neighbour left empty?

## Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-y)

Narrowed. What the cells stand for is not shown by the printed text of the cells (names of the earth lords of the year and date such as bla mkhyen, dur len, bshan pa, ba dan, snang sel, gza' thog, nang bdud, with parts and directions); the heading row of p. 142 (wb.1:p142e) names the stretches of the day (ནམ་ལངས dawn, ཉི་ཤར sun's rising, ཉི་ཕྱེད noon, and further cells not settled), the same stretches by which ch. 23's sky-dog rule turns the mouth round the compass through the seasons (see q734, 2026-11-21): so the sun's course in the heading row is probably the time axis of the strips, the numerals under each heading its measure, and a filled cell the place and time where that lord stands. The rule by which one cell is filled and its neighbour empty is not in any text read; the empty cells are as the printed pattern shows (p. 147: the fullest row is the bottom). Not answered without the charts' key or a commentary.

## Narrowed 2026-11-24 (inv-l)

What the cells stand for: the strip charts are the twelve months (wb1-ch13-strip-charts-are-the-twelve-months), and their cells hold the scattered lords of vol. 2 pp. 216-219 that the verse puts on that month, each at the animal's place the verse names (ba dan, bya khyung, the grave-taker: 12 of 12, 12 of 12 and 8 of 8 placeable charts; the light-clearer in a grave animal's corner, q1364) or with that month's own date and time (the nine-corpses cell, 12 of 12: wb1-ch13-strip-charts-ro-dgu-by-month). The sun's course of the heading row of pp. 142-143 is the black destroyer's table of the date by the time of day (wb1-ch13-heading-rows-black-destroyer), not an axis of the strips; so the guess of 2026-11-21 that the strips' time axis is the heading row is dropped. Why one cell is filled and its neighbour empty: a place is filled in a month when the verse sets some lord there in that month, and is empty when it sets none; shown for the four lords above, not for nang bdud, bshan pa, gza' thog, bla mkhyen, gshed po or the multi-line cells, which were not placed. Open for those lords' verses of pp. 216-221 and a block match of their words over the twelve charts.
