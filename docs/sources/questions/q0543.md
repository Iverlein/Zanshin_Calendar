# Does the colophon's «དམར་ནག་སོགས་ཆེས་ཟབ» mean the red-black verdicts of the reckonings

Does the colophon's «དམར་ནག་སོགས་ཆེས་ཟབ» mean the red-black verdicts of the reckonings (ཁ་དམར་ནག), since the term occurs nowhere else in ch. 34's text?

RD169 (2026-11-06), narrowed on etext only: WB uses «དམར་ནག» elsewhere for wheels (1996 etext vol. 1 pp. 81, 112–113); ch. 12's colophon has the shape of ch. 34's, so ch. 34's «དམར་ནག» is as likely the red-black wheels as the verdicts. The ch. 12 colophon was not found on a scan.

RD169b (2026-11-06), close to answered: ch. 12's colophon (printed p. 103, reprint I3CN5028 img. 110) reads «…རཱ་ཧུ་བརྒྱད་ སོགས་དམར་ནག་འཁོར་ལོའི་མེད་དུ་མི་རུང་བའི་མན་ངག་དང་ཚེ་རྩིས་སོགས་ལ…ཡིག་ཆ་ཟུར་པ་ལོགས་སུ་བཀོད་པ་དང་», ch. 34's (printed p. 428, I3CN5029 img. 434) «…ཀྱང་དམར་ནག་སོགས་ཆེས་ཟབ་རྣམས་གུད་སྦས་ལས་སོ།»; both put «དམར་ནག» with «སོགས» in a list of material set down apart; ch. 12's ties it to the wheels; nothing in ch. 34's line ties it to the verdicts' «ཁ་དམར་ནག», so it very likely means the red-black wheels.

## Answered 2026-11-07

Answered as far as the texts go (RD169): in ch. 12's colophon (printed p. 103, reprint img. 110) «དམར་ནག» is tied by its own words to the wheels («འཁོར་ལོའི») and the life reckoning; ch. 34's colophon (printed p. 428, img. 434) uses it in the same kind of list of material set down apart, and nothing there names the verdicts' «ཁ་དམར་ནག». It means the red-black wheels; a reader of the tradition could still say what the hidden treasure holds.
