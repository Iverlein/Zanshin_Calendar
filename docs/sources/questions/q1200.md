## Answered 2026-11-24

By the etexts of the very print and by counts, the four names read as the question has them, with one caveat for the third.

- Entry 43: the Gser thang etext (I3CN5028) reads «ཟིལ་གནོན་ཞེས་བྱ་མེ་ཕོ་རྟ» at the entry itself; ཟིལ་གནོན stands 4 times in it and 3 times in the 1996 vol. 1 etext, «ཐིལ་གནོན» only once, in ch. 13's list verse («ཐིལ་གནོན་སྤྲེའུ་ཕུར་བུ»), where the 1996 etext has «ཟིལ་གནོན»; 0 for «ཐིལ་གནོན» in vol. 2. So ཟིལ.
- Entry 58: «གདོང་ངན་ལྕགས་བྱ་ལོ» in both vol. 1 etexts (Gser thang 6 «གདོང་ངན», 1996 5); «གདང་ངན» 0 in all four. So གདོང.
- Entry 59: the 1996 etext reads «རྔ་ཆེན» (5 in vol. 1, 1 in vol. 2), including the same verse's «ནག་ན་རྔ་ཆེན་གཞུང་རལ་ཏེ»; the Gser thang etext reads «རྔ་ཆེན» 6 times but «ཇ་ཆེན» in exactly three places (the entry's heading «ཇ་ཆེན་ཆུ་ཁྱི་དཀར་མོ་གྲོ», its verse's «ཇ་ཆེན་ཞེས་བྱའི་ལོ་ལ་ནི» and that same «ནག་ན་ཇ་ཆེན་གཞུང་རལ་ཏེ»), so the print's stack is one the OCR takes for ཇ where the other print has རྔ; «ཇ་ཆེན» has 0 hits in the 1996 etexts and no dictionary headword. So རྔ་ཆེན (the print's glyph itself is not told apart).
- Entry 60: «ཁྲག་སྐྱུག་པ་ཞེས་ཆུ་མོ་ཕག» in the Gser thang etext, «ཁྲག་སྐྱུག» 4 times there and 8 in the 1996 etexts; «ཁྲག་སྨྱུག» once, in the 1996 etext's sixty-year table (a table cell, clearly noisy). So སྐྱུག.
The scan itself was not cut again (rd-y's limits stand); this is the answer by usage and the OCR of the print.
