# How does the night's name ཐུན་གསུམ་པ relate to the Mahāvyutpatti's third watch of five?

RD322.

## Answered 2026-11-13

They are two different words that share the syllables. In the Mahāvyutpatti's list of the day's times the ordinals run first watch (thun dang po, or srod), second watch (thun gnyis pa, or 'jing), third watch (thun gsum pa, or lnga tshigs), fourth (thun bzhi pa, or nam gung), fifth (thun lnga pa, or tho rangs): there thun gsum pa is the ordinal "third", tṛtīyapraharaḥ, the third of five (svo:p271q; Negi, ma.vyu. 8242). The night's name thun gsum pa (also thun gsum) is the sense "having three watches", the Sanskrit triyāmā of the Amarakośa's synonyms for night ("trayo yāmā yasyāḥ sā triyāmā", a.vi. 1.4.5), which Negi lists as the dictionary's second sense of thun gsum pa beside the ordinal; Tshig mdzod chen mo has it as a name of the night (mtshan mo) and as the night's three parts, and WB's p. 17 list takes it as one epithet of the night among others (night-watches, wb.1:p17c). So the name does not mean the Mahāvyutpatti's third watch, and the Mahāvyutpatti's five watches of the night are not the night's three; the Mahāvyutpatti's list is the one place where the same word is an ordinal.
