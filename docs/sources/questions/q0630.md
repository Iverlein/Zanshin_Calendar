# «རྗོན» on pp. 317 and 323: a spelling of «རྔོན» or another word

«རྗོན» stands on both prints in p. 317's avoid list, «རྗོན་རྐུ», and as Mūla's deity on p. 323, «རྗོན་པ་ཅན», where the sense has been taken as hunting and hunters (རྔོན, rngon): is «རྗོན» a spelling of «རྔོན» in this text, or another word, and is the deity a name that can be identified?

RD176 second part (2026-11-06), dead end: no BDRC hit for six phrases around «རྗོན»; SY names no deity for Mūla.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-e)

Steinert's 64 dictionaries have no headword «rjon», «rjon pa», «rjon pa can», «rjon rku» or «rjon shing» (and the prefix «rjon» gives nothing), while «rngon» is the hunt (Rangjung Yeshe, Ives Waldo, *Tshig mdzod chen mo*: «ri dwags rgyag pa'i ming»), «rngon pa» the hunter (also the opening performer of the Tibetan opera), and Valby has «rngon cha» payment gifts; so nothing in the dictionaries supports «རྗོན» as a word of its own, and the reading as a spelling of «རྔོན» stays the only one with a headword. BDRC phrase search «ལྷ་ནི་རྗེན་པ་ཅན» and «ལྷ་ནི་རྗེན་པ» (rjen for rjon) 0 hits. The deity of Mūla is not named by any list in the pool; the Kālacakra commentary passage svo:i538 gives Nirṛti only to the south-west quarter. Stays open.
