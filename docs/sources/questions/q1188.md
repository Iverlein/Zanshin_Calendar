# The Tshig mdzod chen mo makes nam phyed the rat's double-hour, and the Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho the ox's: which is meant in the teaching on the death-sign images, and does it change what the midnight dream covers (q1067)?

Raised by the 2026-11-13 investigation k.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Nam phyed there is the rat's double-hour, midnight, 23 to 1 h; the ox's in the Tshig gter rgya mtsho's entry looks like a slip. The Tshig mdzod chen mo's own list of the twelve «dus tshod» runs «srod 'khor, nam phyed, phyed yol, tho rangs», the pig, then nam phyed, then phyed yol, then dawn, and the Tshig gter rgya mtsho's list under «dus tshod bcu gnyis» is the same words in the same order, so by the list nam phyed is the rat and «phyed yol» the ox. Valby's entry for «dus tshod» pairs «nam phyed» with the rat (byi ba) and «phyed yol» with the ox (glang); the Dung dkar tshig mdzod chen mo lists «nam gung» (a synonym of «mtshan phyed», midnight) as the rat, 23-1 h, and «nam phyed yol» as the ox, 1-3 h. The Tshig gter's entry «nam phyed» = the ox's time agrees with none of these (tmz:box8). So the teaching on the death-sign images (dds.9:p173-174) names midnight, the double-hour centred on the middle of the night, 23-1 h; it does not move the dream to the small hours after one. Whether that fits what the midnight dream covers in the other sources is the question q1067 left open, and the choice between rat and ox does not touch it: both entries are the double-hour of the middle of the night, the rat's centred on it, the ox's after it.
