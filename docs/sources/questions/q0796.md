# Is Ngag dbang at the end of the Svapnohana's remedy Vāgīśvarakīrti, whose Deceiving Death stands before it on p. 268?

«འདི་ནི་ངག་དབང་དགོངས་པ་སྟེ»; the colophon of the preceding work on p. 268 line 3 says chapter four.

Narrowed (inv-j, 2026-11-14): BDRC's phrase search finds the remedy line «འདི་ནི་ངག་དབང་དགོངས་པ་སྟེ» in four places only, all copies of the Svapnohana itself (the Derge etext MW23703_1749 and the next work's etext MW23703_1750, which runs on from it; the Dpe bsdur ma etext MW1PD95844_0652; Padma rdo rje's 2002 book on sleep and dreams, MW25126, which quotes the whole text and glosses nothing in the snippet). «ངག་དབང་དགོངས་པ» gives the same four; «ངག་དབང་གི་དགོངས་པ» and «ངག་དབང་དགོངས་པ་ཡིན» none. The full form «ངག་གི་དབང་ཕྱུག་གི་དགོངས་པ» occurs in five etexts, all commentaries on Daṇḍin's Kāvyādarśa ("the teacher Ngag gi dbang phyug's intention", snippets only): the phrase is thus an ordinary way of citing a teacher's mind, which shows the idiom and nothing about the Svapnohana's man. No text names the remedy's Ngag dbang; Scheuermann's chapter (Brill 2020, pp. 161–180) has no open copy (its DOI page, the FAU and LMU records give metadata only). The identification with Vāgīśvarakīrti stays probable by neighbourhood and date (claim svo-ngag-dbang-bdrc-records), not shown.
