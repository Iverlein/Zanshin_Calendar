# «མཛེས་ཅན་མིན་པའི་མི» on p. 278

On WB vol. 2 p. 278 (img. 284) is the thumb-sized bone in the earth-deposit vase that of «a person not of beauty» or of «a person not a leper» («མཛེས་ཅན་མིན་པའི་མི»)?

RD183 (2026-11-06), narrowed: the print (p. 278) reads «མཛེས་ཅན» with the ས, literally 'not beautiful'; a leper reading would need «མཛེ་ཅན»; «mdze can» appears only in a search snippet of the Bzo rtsis zlos gar, perhaps OCR.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The leper reading is the lexical one: «མཛེས་ཅན» is a recorded spelling of «མཛེ་ཅན», leper. Rangjung Yeshe has «mdzes can: a leper», and the 84000 dictionary links «mdzes» and «mdze» as synonym spellings under «mdze can» (leper, Skt kuṣṭha; Synonyms {mdzes}, {mdze}), while the "beautiful" sense of «mdzes can» (Negi: = mdzes pa can; an attendant of the Buddha in the 84000 glossary) is a proper name or an adjective of persons, not what the vase needs. In the line (p. 278, img. 284, l. 3) the vase is filled with the five scents, medicines, grains, essences, jewels and sugars and «མཛེས་ཅན་མིན་པའི་མིའི་རུས་པ། མཐེ་བོང་ཙམ་པ»: a thumb-sized bone of a person who is not a leper, a clean bone among clean fillings; "a person not beautiful" would put an ugliness requirement in a list of pure things. So the print's ས (read on the scan, RD183) is the spelling, not a misprint, and the bone is of a person who is not leprous. The Bzo rtsis zlos gar's «mdze can» is the usual spelling of the same word.

## Audit 2026-11-14 (inv-audit): stands, wording to be read with care

Rechecked: Rangjung Yeshe has «mdzes can: a leper» and «mdze can: leprosy»; the 84000 Dict has «mdze can: leper, leprous» and «mdzes can: Endowed with Beauty»; the 84000 Synonyms set lists «mdzes» and «mdze» under «mdze can»; BDRC phrase search «མཛེས་ཅན་མིན་པའི་མི» finds nothing. The leper reading therefore rests on one dictionary line, a synonym record and the sense of the list, not on a second print of the verse; "not a misprint" above is a claim about the print's letter (read on the scan), the equation with «mdze can» is the dictionary's. Held as answered, as the better-supported of two readings.
