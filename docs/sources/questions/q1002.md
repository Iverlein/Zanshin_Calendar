# Do the 東都歳事記's and the 歳時記栞草's wordings of the 初夢 entry, and the 江戸歳事記 reprints of the 1890s, all keep the eve-to-dawn night, and from which year does a 2 January night appear in print?

RD358.

## Answered 2026-11-13

Narrowed, not answered. The 栞草 (1851, mdz:i7333115b) keeps the eve-to-dawn night (大晦日の夜より元日の暁), and its 紀事 label goes no further back; the 東都歳事記 has no 初夢 entry on its second-day leaf (RD364); the 江戸歳事記 reprints of the 1890s were not reached (no open copy found in Kokubunken: the search lists 83 records for the 東都歳事記, 21 with images). No print read gives a 2 January night, so the year from which a 2 January night appears in print stays unfound.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14

Narrowed. The three wordings keep the eve-to-dawn night where they have the 初夢 at all: the 栞草 (1851, read in the Meiji reprints of 1892 and 1894, NDL pids 875332 and 875338) in its 初夢 entry (mdz:i87533211), the 東都歳事記 has no 初夢 entry, in the 1838 leaf (mdz:i70618) or in the reprints of 1893-94 (NDL pids 764254 and following, mdz:i76425418). The 栞草 does carry the 2 January night in another entry: under 〔正月〕寶船敷 it says that the dream from the Eve to New Year's Day is called the 初夢, "but the custom of today (今俗) is to lay the treasure ship on the night of the second" (mdz:i87533228), so a night of 2 January is in print for the treasure ship in the 栞草 (1851 edition, seen in the 1892 reprint; the Edo print's own leaf not compared) and, in so many words as the night of the first dream, in the 江戸府内絵本風俗往来 of 1905 (mdz:i76785615), a reminiscence of Edo, not an Edo print. The 江戸歳事記 print of 1838 (NDL pids 8369317, 8369318) has an OCR text but was not read. The earliest print that names the 2 January night as the first dream's remains unfound.
