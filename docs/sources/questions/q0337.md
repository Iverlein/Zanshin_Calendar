# «སྤྲ་སྒོ» and «རྨེ་བདུན» in the corpse's facing

WB vol. 2, pp. 99, ll. 25–26 (wb.2:p97-100): the corpse's facing is headed «སྤྲ་སྒོ་རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས» and avoids «རྨེ[?]་བདུན་ཤར་བའི་གདོང»; what are spra sgo and rme bdun (is rme for sme, the mewa)?

**Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-mmm).** «རྨེ་བདུན» is the Great Bear, as the claim says; the dictionaries (Ives Waldo, *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, *sme bdun*) name it a lunar-path star group, "the stars near Arundhati", so the first half stands. For the heading: the three etexts of the line do not agree on the first syllable, and the earlier note had the 1996 etext with «སྤྲ». Read in the etexts (bdrc.py etext, 2026-11-14): the 1996 print (MW2CZ8040, I1KG12907) «སྒྲ་སྒོ་རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས་ནི», the Gser thang reprint (MW3CN5026, I3CN5029) «ཟླ་སྒོ་རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས་ནི», the Zhol print «སྨྲ་སྒོ». BDRC phrase search «རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས» finds only these two WB prints and an unreadable OCR page of another volume (I1PD95859, p. 1630); «སྤྲ་སྒོ་རོ» and «སྨྲ་སྒོ་རོ» 0 hits. Of the four words (སྒྲ, ཟླ, སྨྲ, སྤྲ) only «སྨྲ་སྒོ» is a headword: *smra sgo*, "gate of speech", the mouth (Ives Waldo; *Tshig mdzod chen mo* «ངག་གམ་གཏམ་གྱི་འབྱུང་ཁུངས་ཁ»; Negi), which suits «རོ་ཡི་ཁ་ཕྱོགས» ("the corpse's mouth's direction") and the Zhol etext's reading; the stack on the blocks (s over a lower letter and ra) is compatible with it. So the heading is most likely «སྨྲ་སྒོ», "the mouth", and the line "the direction of the corpse's mouth (face)". Not settled on the scan: Zhol img. 771 and the reprint's img. 105 are blotted.
