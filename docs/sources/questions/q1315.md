# WB vol. 1 p. 19 line 8: the syllables naming the mansion of Jyeṣṭhā between «ཞེས་རྣམས» and «འདོད»

On WB vol. 1 p. 19 (reprint img. 26, line 8) the line that names the mansion of Jyeṣṭhā, between «ཞེས་རྣམས» and «འདོད», has a tall clogged stack with ོ above and a dot pair below, not a plain མ, followed by a syllable like དུ (or ནུ). What are its syllables? This narrows the 2026-11-12 question about the same line (then placed on line 7 or 8).

## Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-u), narrowed

Checks. (1) The second syllable is «དུ», not «ནུ»: at 3x on I3CN5028 img. 26, line 8, set beside the final ད of «འདོད» on the same line, it has the flat top bar and the lower hook of ུ, and no ན leg. (2) The stack is not «མོ»: the 1996 etext (I1KG12906) reads the pair «སྒོ་དུ་འདོད» and the Gser thang etext «སྒྲོ་དུ་འདོད» (both OCR, both with a tall stack whose vowel is ོ), the typeset *Bod kyi rtsis rig kun 'dus chen mo* vol. 2 (VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4716, p. 42) restates the line as «ལྡེའུ་གདུ་བུ་ལྷ་དབང་ལྡན། སྣོན་གྱི་མིང་དུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ།», and the 1996 etext has, in a later mansion run (a verse listing the mansions by their short names), «སྣོ ན» for the same mansion; the pool's own WB lists give Jyeṣṭhā as «སྣྲོན» (personal-mansions.md, 17). So the line names the mansion's short name Snron/Snon, and the machine's «མོ» is a misreading. Which of «སྣོ» and «སྣྲོ» the stack is (ན with or without ྲ below ས) the clotted ink does not decide; the etexts' ག is not the print's ན either. Not settled letter by letter; the two witnesses' «སྒ» is the OCR's guess at an ས-over-ན stack. Open.

## Answered 2026-11-24

The stack before «དུ་འདོད» is «སྣོན» (Snon), Jyeṣṭhā's short name: WB spells it so in about 20 places and «སྣྲོན» in none; the p. 16 month name reads «སྣོན་ཟླ» likewise. By usage, not by the letters (the final ན is not told by eye). Claim wb-1-p19-jyestha-short-name-snon (RD203).
