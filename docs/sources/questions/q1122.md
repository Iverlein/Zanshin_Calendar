# Is Paṇḍa pa (Pen ta pa, Piṇḍa pa) of Gtsug lag phreng ba's Life the Nepalese teacher known elsewhere, and why is he seated at the head of the assembly

The Life calls Paṇḍa pa the lama; the prints spell his name Piṇḍa pa and Pen ta pa. Is he the Nepalese teacher of Marpa known elsewhere (the Pham mthing pa brothers' circle, or Piṇḍo)?

## Narrowed 2026-11-13 (inv-s)

The Life of Marpa by Gtsang smyon (rrd.23:p262, p292), already in the pool, makes Bla ma Pen ta pa Marpa's teacher in the Rin chen tshul temple in Nepal (the Ral gcig ma abhisamaya, the Bdud rtsi 'od ldan sādhana and scattered completion-stage teaching) and the lama who goes with him to India: this is the same figure as Gtsug lag phreng ba's Paṇḍa pa/Pen ṇḍa pa, whom his song addresses as «lord who keeps the ascetic's conduct» (བརྟུལ་ཞུགས་སྤྱོད་པ, brtul zhugs spyod pa) and who is seated first because he is the teacher of the gathering. Secondary English literature (Wikipedia, uncited) names Marpa's Nepalese teachers as Paindapa and Chitherpa, students of Nāropa, and Paindapa as the one who went with him to Pullahari; no Tibetan source for that spelling or for a link to the Pham mthing pa brothers or Piṇḍo was found. BDRC's autocomplete index has no person under «pen ta pa» or «pe ndha pa» (the only hit near «piṇḍa» is P3237, Piṇḍapātika, a text-title entry). So: the Nepalese teacher of Marpa, yes, by the Life's own frame; his Sanskrit name (Paiṇḍapa, Piṇḍapa) and his place in Nāropa's circle rest on the English tradition only.
Still open: a Tibetan source that spells and places him (the Blue Annals' or the Mgur mtsho's own mention, the Pham mthing pa pages).

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes: the Nepalese teacher of Marpa, and the 2010 Thrangu edition's own summary of Gtsug lag phreng ba's Life says so in terms. Its list of the thirteen lamas who were most kind to Marpa (dds.44:p15) runs through Nāropa, Maitrīpa, Zhi ba bzang po, Ye shes snying po, the yoginī Rus pa'i rgyan can, Ka so ri ba and Ri ri ba (the brothers Seng ge gling pa), Jetāri, Kusulu che ba, Prajñārakṣita, «Spyi ther ba and Pen ta ba, the Nepalese brothers» (སྤྱི་ཐེར་བ་དང་པེན་ཏ་བ་སྟེ་བལ་པོ་སྐུ་མཆེད), and Jo bo rje Atiśa; and says of Marpa in Nepal that he heard the Dpal gdan bzhi and some tantras from Spyi ther ba. So Pen ta ba (the Life's Paṇḍa pa, Pen ṇḍa pa) is a Nepalese, one of two brothers named together, which matches the Life of Gtsang smyon (Rin chen tshul temple, Nepal; Pen ta pa beside the Nepalese Spyi ther ba, rrd.23:p202, p303) already in the pool. That he leads the gathering in which Marpa sings (rrd.26:p88-90) is the place of a teacher at the head of the gathering, whom the song addresses as «lord who keeps the ascetic's conduct» (the claim rrd-pen-ta-pa-leads-the-fellow-students-marpa-sings).
Not supported: any link to the Pham mthing pa brothers or to Piṇḍo. BDRC's Pham mthing pa pair (P3086 'Jigs med grags pa, P3087 Ngag gi dbang phyug, 10XX, Kālacakra transmitters) have no note joining them to Marpa, and BDRC has no person under «pen ta ba», «pen+Da pa», «spyi ther ba» (autocomplete, 2026-11-14); P3237 «Piṇḍo» is the Piṇḍapātika entry with Indian students. The Sanskrit form (Paiṇḍapa, Piṇḍapa) rests on the English literature only. The summary is the 2010 editors', drawing on the Life; the Life's own list of the thirteen lamas was not found in the pages read of rrd.26.
