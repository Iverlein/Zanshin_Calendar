# «རྒྱ་ཁང» or «གྱོ་ཁང» of the gold reckoning

On p. 538, is the gold reckoning's grid «རྒྱ་ཁང» (Yigdzin-1) or «གྱོ་ཁང» (the etext, question 125)? The Gser thang reprint is not clear.

## Answered 2026-11-14

**«གྱོ་ཁང».** Read by eye on three prints (Mipham's own print I2029 img. 744, the Gser thang reprint I3CN5029 img. 544, the 1996 print I1KG12907 img. 546): a ག with ya-btags and the ོ sign above, no ར-mgo, so «གྱོ», and Yigdzin-1's «རྒྱ» on the 1996 image is the model's slip (claims notes-gold-grid-gyo-khang, notes-gold-grid-gyo-khang-reprint-and-1996).

New on 2026-11-14 (inv-ccc; BDRC exact-phrase search, etext snippets only): the word is the same in other works, never «རྒྱ་ཁང»: «རྒྱ་ཁང་རེའུ་མིག» has 0 hits, «གྱོ་ཁང» 23, among them the Bhutan anthology of white and black reckonings (the line «skar mnag rtsis sam gyo khang» (BDRC's Wylie), "the mansion-black reckoning or gyo khang"), a *sgang dril* manual of the mansion reckoning's practice («gyo khang re'u mig byar grangs bzung»), a miscellany on the Phug lugs and the grub rtsis («gyo khang re'u mig tshes khongs su bkod pa»), and the Sakya *'Bras rtsis rab gsal nor bu'i me long* (I5721), whose verse on the "new gold reckoning" ends «gyo khang sogs 'bras don bsdus bcas». So the spelling is «གྱོ་ཁང», and the word is the grid of the new gold reckoning (གསར་འགྱུར་གསེར་རྩིས་ལུགས, gsar 'gyur gser rtsis lugs) with its little squares (རེའུ་མིག, re'u mig), here, a table the year of the day is read in. Its etymology no text found gives: Steinert's 64 dictionaries have no headword «gyo khang», «rgyo khang» or «gyo kha» (prefix search); «gyo» alone is a clay vessel or potsherd (Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho) or deceit, neither a grid.
