# In which Edo source is the 初夢 night fixed at 2 January, and is it counted by the lunar months of the Edo calendar, the only one in use until 1872?

RD308; the Japanese Wikipedia's 初夢 (read 2026-11-10, a lead and not a source) names three nights in the Edo period, New Year's Eve to 1 January, 1 to 2 January and 2 to 3 January, the last the commonest in the late Edo period.

Narrowed (inv-r, 2026-11-13): the NDL's reference service (crd.ndl.go.jp, entry 1000013905, «初夢とはいつみる夢の事か», answering with the Edo-Tokyo Museum's reply) says that in Edo the dream of New Year's Eve was the old first dream, that from about the Tenmei years (1781-89) the night of 1 to 2 January was meant, and that the night of 2 January later became usual, with the 事始め of the second day and the sale of treasure-ship pictures on it as proposed reasons; it quotes no Edo source, only modern reference books (渡辺信一郎 江戸の庶民生活・行事事典 2000; 竹内誠 春夏秋冬江戸っ子の知恵 2013 p. 90). A secondary answer, not a source for the night; the 守貞謾稿, 東都歳事記 and 歳時記栞草 were not reached in a text (Wikisource ja has none; NDL restricted). The question of the primary text and of the lunar-month counting stays open. Not recorded as a claim.

Searched again (inv-y, 2026-11-16): a web search for the 守貞謾稿's text on the 宝船 and 初夢 finds only modern summaries (the 宝船 pictures sold in Edo on the evening of 1 and 2 January, Morisada buying one in 1851); the original is not in an open text, so the night of the 初夢 in an Edo source and the lunar-month count are still open.

## Narrowed 2026-11-16 (inv-z3)

The Morisada mankō is reached through a modern article that quotes it (nippon.com's series on 守貞漫稿, part 15, read 2026-11-16, https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/g02003/): «江戸ハ、今モ専ラ元日二日ノ宵ニ、小民コレヲ売リ巡ル» (in Edo, still now, the common people go round selling it [the treasure-ship picture] on the evenings of the first and second days). That fixes what Morisada says of the sale (the evenings of 1 and 2 January, sale at dusk); the dream's night is the article author's inference («the night of the 2nd, the picture bought after dusk and laid under the pillow»), not a quoted line, and nothing in the quotation counts months. A web search for the passage's own text (守貞謾稿 初夢 二日, 2026-11-16) found no transcription; the NDL's digital copy was not opened. So the Edo source for the second night is still a source for the picture's sale, not the dream; open.

**Searched again 2026-11-17 (inv-d).** A web search for the 東都歳事記's entry for the second day and for the 初夢 gave only modern summaries (the 宝船 sold and laid under the pillow on the night of 2 January; the 事始め of the second day offered as the reason the dream moved there); no text of the 東都歳事記 itself, and no source that counts the date by the Edo lunar months. Open.
