# In which Edo source is the 初夢 night fixed at 2 January, and is it counted by the lunar months of the Edo calendar, the only one in use until 1872?

RD308; the Japanese Wikipedia's 初夢 (read 2026-11-10, a lead and not a source) names three nights in the Edo period, New Year's Eve to 1 January, 1 to 2 January and 2 to 3 January, the last the commonest in the late Edo period.

Narrowed (inv-r, 2026-11-13): the NDL's reference service (crd.ndl.go.jp, entry 1000013905, «初夢とはいつみる夢の事か», answering with the Edo-Tokyo Museum's reply) says that in Edo the dream of New Year's Eve was the old first dream, that from about the Tenmei years (1781-89) the night of 1 to 2 January was meant, and that the night of 2 January later became usual, with the 事始め of the second day and the sale of treasure-ship pictures on it as proposed reasons; it quotes no Edo source, only modern reference books (渡辺信一郎 江戸の庶民生活・行事事典 2000; 竹内誠 春夏秋冬江戸っ子の知恵 2013 p. 90). A secondary answer, not a source for the night; the 守貞謾稿, 東都歳事記 and 歳時記栞草 were not reached in a text (Wikisource ja has none; NDL restricted). The question of the primary text and of the lunar-month counting stays open. Not recorded as a claim.
