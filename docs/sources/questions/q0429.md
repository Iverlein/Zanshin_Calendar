# Whose are the year and the three of the group «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་གསུམ་གྱི་གསུམ» in p. 503's twenty?

In p. 503's twenty, whose are «ལོ་» and the three of the group «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་གསུམ་གྱི་གསུམ»: the year, month and day of death with the twelve times of death's tests, or the dead's own year; and is the group's «བླ་བར» (in the notes' sentence the la, up to which the five are counted) as it is read here?

**Narrowed 2026-10-27 (RD58, second reading, [verdicts.md](../verdicts.md), section A).** p. 503 read whole on the scan and the reprint: l. 1 names the three the six numbered tests are laid on as the month, day and time («ཟླ་ཞག་དུས»), l. 5 names the last group as «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་གསུམ་གྱི་གསུམ» (year in place of time) and p. 502 names the four of the first examination as «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས». Which three the last group counts, and whose year its year is, the note does not say. The group's «བླ་བར» is not settled by the letters: the 1996 print and the Gser thang reprint show «བླ» and «ཟླ» in one shape; the reading «བླ་བར» "up to the la" stays by the sense.

**Narrowed 2026-10-27 (RD58, third reading, [verdicts.md](../verdicts.md), section B).** The stacked letter of «བླ་བར» is not decided on Mipham's own print (I2029 img. 676) but is «བླ» on the typeset Collected Works (I1PD34647 img. 600), whose «བླ» and «ཟླ» differ by the bars of the head; «ཡོས» and «ལོག་མུ་ཅོར» read the same on all three witnesses, unexplained. The year and the three of the last group: still open.

**Searched 2026-10-27 (RD58, fifth reading, [verdicts.md](../verdicts.md), section B).** «ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་གསུམ་གྱི་གསུམ» occurs only in WB's vol. 2 prints; «ཤི་བའི་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས» (the dead's year, month, day and time) is in WB's ch. 29 and in Mipham's *Deathless Elixir* (OCR too poor to quote at length) but no other text says whose year the last group counts. Open.

**Narrowed 2026-10-27 (RD58, sixth reading, [verdicts.md](../verdicts.md), section B).** The lent scan of MW23671 (a gazetteer of Driru county, Lhasa 2002, not a Bon work; modern, rank 3, facts only), p. 115 (leaf 172, by eye): «ཤི་བའི་ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་བཞི་དང་སྤར་མེ་རོ་འདོན་ལ་སོགས་རྩིས་འབྲས་ལ་བརྟག་པར་བྱ།» the four of the dying's year, month, day and time, with the trigram and mansion of taking the body out, are examined in the reckoning's results; the next sentence names the dead's year-sign («འདས་པོའི་ལོ་རྟགས») and the household's as a separate matter (a mouse-year dead: dragon, horse and monkey households may not lead or touch the body). So this manual reads «ཤི་བའི་ལོ» as the year of dying, apart from the dead's own year-sign, which favours p. 503's group counting the year of death; it does not name the group's three and is a county custom, not WB's twenty. Still open for WB; the reading by the year of death gains a witness.
