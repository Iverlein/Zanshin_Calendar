# «སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ» in the five doors of bad luck

In the notes on the five doors of bad luck (wb.2:p483), «སྲོག་དགྲ་དར་ཀྱང་སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ་དང་སྤར་སྨེ་ངན་པ་མ་འཛོམ་ན»: is «སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ» "the life force's mother in decline" or "the life force's mother not in decline", and which condition must come together with the enemy's rise (དགྲ་དར, dgra dar) to make the aspect bad?

**In part 2026-11-01 (RD104, [claims/regent-dgra-dar-black-and-white-in-reply-148.md](../claims/regent-dgra-dar-black-and-white-in-reply-148.md)).** The reply on the dead's reckoning (not on the karma reckoning) puts the mother and the friend on the white side and the enemy on the black; read so, «སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ» is the life force's mother not in decline, a condition for the enemy's rise not to make the aspect bad. A reading only: the notes' line is not touched.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Read from the spelling on three prints and the sentence's grammar, 2026-11-14 (inv-eee). «སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ» is «སྲོག་མ» + «མ་གུད་པ», "the life force's mother, not in decline". The three prints all carry the doubled «མ་མ» (Gser thang reprint I3CN5029 img. 489, the 1996 print I1KG12907 img. 491 and Mipham's own print I2029 img. 641, [claims/notes-sroq-ma-ma-gud-pa-three-prints.md](../claims/notes-sroq-ma-ma-gud-pa-three-prints.md)), so no single-«མ» «སྲོག་མ་གུད་པ» exists to read as "in decline" (BDRC finds «སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ» in one etext and «སྲོག་མ་གུད་པ» in none); a dittography on three independent prints, one the author's, is not credible. In the sentence «སྲོག་དགྲ་དར་ཀྱང་སྲོག་མ་མ་གུད་པ་དང་སྤར་སྨེ་ངན་པ་མ་འཛོམ་ན་དེ་ཙམ་མི་སྐྱོན། འཛོམ་ན་ངན་ནོ།» the two conditions then stand alike as negatives, «མ་གུད་པ» and «མ་འཛོམ», and the second line «འཛོམ་ན་ངན་ནོ» reverses them: even if the life force's enemy rises it is not so faulty when the mother is not in decline and a bad trigram and mewa do not meet; when (these) meet, it is bad. The same notes' p. 484 writes «མ་སྲོག་ལ་མ་བཅད་པ» for "the mother not cutting the life", the same negative «མ» before a verb, and the page before the sentence (p. 483, ll. 26-27) names the mother among the ones "in decline" when their verdict is black, which the clause then denies of the mother. So the enemy's rise alone is not a bar; it makes the aspect bad when the mother is in decline or a bad trigram and mewa meet it. The grammar is read from the text: no gloss on the clause exists in the notes or the replies.
