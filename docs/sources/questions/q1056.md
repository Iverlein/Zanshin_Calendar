# What are the Sanskrit names behind the twelve yud tsam of the day on p. 438 (དཀར, བྱམས་ལྡན, མིག་དམར, དྲག་བུ, དབང་ཆེན, དག་པ, བྱི་བཞིན, ལེགས་མཛེས, འཁོར, འཁོར་བར་བྱེད་པ, དགེ་བ་སྦྱིན, ཟླ་བ་མཆོག་སྦྱིན་པ), and do they belong to a list of muhūrta names?

RD378.

## Searched 2026-11-13, no answer

The twelve names occur in the Derge volume (VE4CZ5369_I1KG9214) only on p. 438 (དྲག་བུ nowhere else; བྱམས་ལྡན also on p. 245 in another list; ལེགས་མཛེས and དགེ་བ་སྦྱིན on other pages as plain words), and not at all in the Kun 'dus vol. 3 etext. The Sanskrit edition's text layer (Trivandrum Sanskrit Series 70, part 1) has no «muhūrta», and no list of the day's divisions near mmk.2:p158-160 or p. 175-176. No Tibetan or Sanskrit list of muhūrta names was found in the pool. The resemblance of the first two names to Śveta and Maitra remains a doubt; a published list of the muhūrta names (a Kālacakra or Jyotiṣa text) has to be found first.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The Sanskrit is found, and the twelve are a list of muhūrta names, in the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa's own Sanskrit: the critical edition used by 84000's translation (Toh 543, ch. 24 there, verses 24.189-24.191, five manuscripts; read 2026-11-14 in its TEI source, the 84000 data-tei repository on GitHub, licence non-commercial: facts only, nothing copied) has «dvādaśaiva muhūrtāni tasmin kāle prayojayet» («only twelve muhūrtas are to be joined to that time») and then the names in the order of the Derge:

- དཀར = Śveta (the 84000 glossary gives དཀར་པོ).
- བྱམས་ལྡན = Maitra.
- མིག་དམར = Raktākṣa, «red-eyed»: the Sanskrit has «raktākṣāḥ prakīrtitāḥ», an epithet of the first two, and the Tibetan made it a third name; so the Derge's twelve is eleven names of the Sanskrit plus this epithet.
- དྲག་བུ = Raudra (the glossary gives «drag bu»; the Tibetan renders the sense «fierce», the second syllable is not explained).
- དབང་ཆེན = Mahendra.
- དག་པ = Śuddha.
- ལེགས་མཛེས = Suśobhana, «beautiful»: the Sanskrit's «caiva suśobhanaḥ»; the Tibetan took the word as a name of another muhūrta (the translators' note says so).
- བྱི་བཞིན (the 84000 glossary spells བྱི་བཞིན་ཉིད) = Abhijit, the noon muhūrta; the same Tibetan word stands for the nakṣatra Abhijit.
- འཁོར = Bhramaṇa and འཁོར་བར་བྱེད་པ = Bhrāmaṇa.
- དགེ་བ་སྦྱིན = the Sanskrit's refrain «śubhapradaḥ», «giver of good fortune» (an inference from the word; the glossary has no entry for it).
- ཟླ་བ་མཆོག་སྦྱིན་པ = Saumya or Soma with Varada (the glossary has «zla ba mchog» = Saumya and «sbyin pa nyid» = Varada); the Sanskrit lists Saumya, Varada, Soma, Varada, and the translators say the repetition looks like a redactor's way to reach twelve, and that the Tibetan leaves out the verse's second and third lines.

So བྱི་བཞིན (Abhijit), Śveta, Maitra and Raudra are also names in the Indian list of the day's muhūrtas (Raudra, Śveta, Maitra, Sārabhaṭa, Sāvitra, Vairāja, Viśvāvasu, Abhijit; the Atharvaṇa jyotiṣa's list as a web summary gives it: hindu-blog.com/2024/06/atharvana-jyotisha.html, fifteen names, only the eight up to noon named there, with a gnomon shadow in angulas for each); the tantra's list is not that list: it has Mahendra, Śuddha, Suśobhana, Bhramaṇa, Bhrāmaṇa, Saumya, Varada and Soma besides, and only twelve. The earlier doubt («བྱི་བཞིན» and «དྲག་བུ» not understood) is settled: Abhijit and Raudra. The earlier note that the Sanskrit edition (Trivandrum Sanskrit Series 70) shows no muhūrta list stands for its OCR text layer, which is garbled and was searched for the wrong words; the critical Sanskrit in the 84000 appendix has the list and a line on the muhūrta in verse 24.199 (see q1055).

Not done: the names were not read in a printed edition of the Sanskrit; the Tibetan of the Derge was not compared with the 84000 translators' Tibetan beyond their glossary and notes; no source for the meaning of the Tibetan «དྲག་བུ» (probably «fierce one», from «raudra») was found.
