# «ཤི་སྐབ» or «ཤི་སྒབ» throughout the thirteenth

In WB vol. 2, pp. 104–108 the term for the run of deaths is printed with one stack everywhere (p. 104, ll. 27–28; p. 105, l. 15; p. 106, ll. 2, 15; p. 108, ll. 11, 24, 26): is it «ཤི་སྐབ» or «ཤི་སྒབ»? The grave chapter's claim has «ན་སྒབ་ཤི་སྒབ».

## Read further 2026-10-23

In part 2026-10-23 (RD46): the blocks carve «སྒབ» at pp. 105 and 108, two of its seven places.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Answered 2026-11-14 (inv-nn): the word is «ཤི་སྒབ» (shi sgab), with ག, at all seven places and in the heading «ཤི་འགྲམས་སྒབ»; the 1996 setting's «སྐབ» is a spelling slip of the typesetting, not a second word. The carved glyph itself was not told apart on any scan (the reprint's seven stacks are one shape; question 594 keeps that), so this rests on sense and on the other places the word stands.

- **Headword.** Steinert's public dictionaries (64 sets, 2026-11-14) have «sgab» (= «'gab», the hind part, the end, the tail: Ives Waldo "afterwards, end"; Dan Martin, from Btsan lha, "the tail or lower part"; the *Dag tshig gsar bsgrigs* "the hem and the lower part, as the hem of a garment") and no «skab» in any sense that fits the run of deaths (Valby has «skab» "delay" only). «Sgab» as the stretch that runs on to its end is the sense a "run of deaths" needs.
- **Other authors, the same phrase** (BDRC's phrase search «ཤི་སྒབ», 61 hits, 2026-11-14; typed etexts in Wylie, so a typist's reading, not a carve): «shi sgab gcod/bcad/chod» "to cut the run of deaths" in the *Rin chen gter mdzod chen mo* ("shi sgab gcod na mgul du gdags"), in Mipham's collected works ("phyugs kyi shi sgab bcad pa'i gto dang god mnan", the rite that cuts a run of deaths among livestock), in Karma chags med's collected works, in Chos kyi grags pa's, in a Drikung medical anthology ("shi sgab chod par zhus pas … lo nyer gcig bar shi sgab chad"), in the *Sgo mdzod rin po che'i glegs bam* and in the *Bai dur dkar po* itself (the heading "sa dpyad bcu gsum shi 'grams sgab" and "shi sgab gcod gto"). All of them spell it with ga.
- **The other spelling.** «ཤི་སྐབ» has three hits (2026-11-14): the Gser thang etext of the *Bai dur dkar po* (MW3CN5026), the Kangyur etext MW29468_0044 (poor OCR: "khaka shi skab[m]na zheg gses") and a medical collection's OCR (MW2PD17386_9933DC, "yang kha'i shi skab rtsi ba ni"); the first is the same setting as the question's, the other two are OCR noise. «ཤི་འགྲམས་སྐབ» has two hits, both OCR of the *Bai dur dkar po* (one in the Gene Smith collection, "shi 'grams skab").

For «སྤར་ཁའི་སྐབ» of p. 104 (question 221) the same holds: «སྒབ».
