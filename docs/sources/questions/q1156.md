# What stands in the line of WB vol. 1 p. 19 (img. 26, line 7 or 8) that names the mansion of Jyeṣṭhā, where the machine reads «མོ་དུ་འདོད»?

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-scan)

Looked at on the reprint (I3CN5028 img. 026, lines 7 and 8 by the line finder, each in halves at 1.8x, then line 8's right end at 3.5x). Line 8 ends «… ཛྱེ་ཥྚ་ཞེས་རྩམས་[stack]་དུ་འདོད།» (the mansion's name is written as a Sanskrit stack and «ཞེས», «called»); the stack before «དུ་འདོད» is a tall stack with a hook below, not a plain «མོ». The stack's top and middle letters were not told apart at 3.5x of the 1-bit print. Not answered; confidence low. Next: the Zhol print (I1KG1710) at the same line.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-scan3)

Not advanced: the time went to finding the 11th month's leaf for q1161; the Zhol and Taikhang images of WB vol. 1 p. 19 were not located (the cached Zhol images are 29, 41-48 and 126-165, the Taikhang 150-190; p. 19 on the Zhol is below img. 29 or near it, to be found with `locate.py map` before cropping).

## Read again 2026-11-21 (rd-u), narrowed

Same line as q1315 (img. 26, line 8): the syllable after the stack is «དུ» (hook of ུ against the final ད of «འདོད», 3x), the stack carries ོ and is the mansion's own short name, which the pool's lists and the typeset Kun 'dus (VEIE0OPI2F246A26_I4716 p. 42: «སྣོན་གྱི་མིང་དུ») give as Snron/Snon; the two WB etext OCRs read the stack as «སྒྲོ» (Gser thang) and «སྒོ» (1996), not «མོ». Whether the print has ྲ under the ན is not told apart. See q1315.

## Answered 2026-11-24

The stack before «དུ་འདོད» is «སྣོན» (Snon), Jyeṣṭhā's short name, so the line ends «…ཞེས་རྣམས་སྣོན་དུ་འདོད»; the machine's «མོ» is a misreading. This is q1315's answer (RD203: WB spells the short name «སྣོན» in about twenty places and «སྣྲོན» in none); the 1996 etext's «སྒོ» and the reprint etext's «སྒྲོ» are OCR guesses at the same clotted stack. By usage, not by the letters.
