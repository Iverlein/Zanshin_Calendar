# ད་བོར among the nine sme ba (the eight, white)

ད་བོར in "the eight, white" of the nine *sme ba* (children's danger of body): what is it? (RD194, corpus/wb/ch18.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-14 (inv-p)

The 1996 scan (BDRC I1KG12906, img. 222, p. 212) prints «བྱིས་པ་ད་བོར་ལུས་ཉེན་ཆེ» at the foot of the nine *sme ba* list; at 10× the syllable before བོར has a hooked foot under the ད (དུ or དྲ), so the print is not plain ད, and བོར, ལུས་ཉེན་ཆེ are clear. A second WB print is searchable on BDRC (MW2PD17386_D19206, in the collection *Bod lugs gso rig rtsa che'i dpe rnying kun btus*; the etext answers 403, the snippet is a Wylie conversion): «brgyad dkar rlung dmar chen po'ang / than pa dang bza ba mi 'ong / byas pa dwakhor lus rten che», that is a different wording again («dwa khor … lus rten che»); the phrase searches «བྱིས་པ་ད་བོར», «བྱིས་པ་དུར་བོར», «བྱིས་པ་ད་བོར་ལུས», «བྱིས་པ་དོར་ལུས» found nothing. The word is not identified; the clause means that the eighth patch bears on the child's body and its danger, which is all the row uses. Stays open.

## Narrowed 2026-11-14 (inv-e)

A lead for the second print's «dwa khor» (BDRC MW2PD17386_D19206): «དྭ» (dwa) is a headword in the dictionaries with two senses, the trigram of the eight spar kha (Valby: the metal one; *Tshig mdzod chen mo*, *Bod yig tshig gter rgya mtsho*: «rgya» spar kha brgyad kyi ya gyal zhig, a Chinese word) and an orphan, «dwa phrug», a child whose father has died (*Sgom sde tshig mdzod chen mo*, citing the *'Dul ba rnam 'byed*; the *Brda dkrol gser gyi me long*). Neither is shown to be the word of the print's row: «ད་བོར» has no headword («da bor», «du bor», «dur bor» absent from Steinert's 64 dictionaries), and BDRC phrase search «བྱིས་པ་དྭ་འཁོར», «དྭ་འཁོར་ལུས་རྟེན་ཆེ» 0 hits. If the print's hooked syllable is a wa-zur (དྭ), the word would be the second print's «dwa khor» and the row would speak of the dwa trigram's circle, but that rests on the Wylie of a snippet only. Stays open.

## Narrowed 2026-11-17 (inv-b)

Two more witnesses to the line, both text. (1) WB's own etexts of the nine *sme ba* verse (Beijing 1996, I1KG12906 [p222]; Gser thang, I3CN5028 [p211]; the BDRC search finds the line only in these two) read the syllable «རྭ», not «ད»: «བརྒྱུད་དཀར་རླུང་དམར་ཆེན་པོ་ལྡང༌། །ཐན་པ་དང་ནི་འཐབ་མོ་འོང༌། །བྱིས་པ་རྭ་བོར་ལུས་ཉེན་ཆེ/ཚེ»: so the two OCRs of the two prints agree that the syllable carries a wa-zur (a subjoined ཝ), which is the hooked foot seen on the scan (ར with it in the etexts; the scan's syllable may equally be ད with it). (2) The *Sgo bzhi mdzod lnga'i yang bcud* (BDRC I4PD3283, open etext; the verses on the nine *sme ba*, offset 218157) has a different recension of the same lines: «བརྒྱད་དཀར་བྱིས་པ་དྭ་བུ་མང༌། །ཐན་པ་འཐབ་མོ་རླུང་དམར་ལྡང༌།» (the eight, white: many children «dwa bu»; drought, quarrels, the red wind rises). Its «དྭ» is the word of the second WB print's «dwa khor» (q719, 2026-11-14), the «dwa» of the dictionaries (a child whose father has died, *dwa phrug*; WB's own line «ཕ་ཡི་དྭ་ཟླས་མ་འཆི», q236, uses «དྭ» of the father's death). So the syllable of the print is most likely «དྭ» (dwa), the clause is about orphaned or fatherless children and the child's body at risk, and «བོར» (the print) against «བུ» (Sgo bzhi) and «ཁོར» (second print) is the word still not settled («bor» «to be lost, cast»; «dwa bor» would then be «fatherless-lost»). Not settled; no dictionary gives «dwa bor». The reading of the hooked syllable as «དྭ» rests on the three recensions and on the scan's shape, not on a headword. BDRC phrase search «བྱས་པ་དྭ་འཁོར», «དྭ་ཁོར་ལུས་རྟེན་ཆེ», «བྱིས་པ་དྭ་བོར» 0 hits. Stays open for the print's own letters.
