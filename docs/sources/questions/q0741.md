# The 2nd head's last two lines (p. 159): «byed pa'i nyi skar» and the thunder phrase

In the 2nd head's last two lines (p. 159) the phrases "the sun's mansion of the doing (byed pa'i nyi skar)", "of this and the next, whichever kind, in the east at the time the dragon [thunder?] comes" and "the chick arrives; time not to sow the late fields" were read but their sense is not settled; what do "byed pa'i nyi skar" and "ga[ng] rigs la shar dus 'brug 'ong" mean? (RD201, corpus/wb/ch13.md)

## Looked at 2026-11-16 (inv-u)

Not settled; the field narrowed to four copies. BDRC search: «བྱེད་པའི་ཉི་སྐར» 4 hits, «ཤར་དུས་འབྲུག་འོང» 5 hits, «ཞིང་ཐག་མ་དུས་མ་འདེབས» 2 hits: all are this very sentence in the Beijing 1996 etext (I1KG12906, MW2CZ8040, p. 158), the Gser thang etext (I3CN5028, MW3CN5026), the Do shal (MW2PD17386_D19206) and the *Bum bzang* (MW1GS88389, poor OCR «byed pa'i nyi skar drag bod …»), with the Beijing etext's wording «dbang phyug 'god / byed pa'i nyi skar / 3 'di dang phyi ma gang rigs la shar dus 'brug 'ong / bye'u slebs / zhing thag ma dus ma 'debs ran»; the Gser thang etext has «26» where Beijing has «3» before «'di dang phyi ma» (a stray mark of the print, not read). No copy glosses it, no other text repeats it («བྱེའུ་སླེབས་ཞིང་» 0, «ཉི་སྐར་བྱེད་པ» 0). The plain parse: after the lord is set («dbang phyug 'god»), "the sun's «byed pa» mansion" is a day-mark of the almanac, and "of this and the next, whichever kind, in the east, at the time, thunder comes; the chick arrives; do not sow the late fields in time" is the weather omen for that day. «byed pa'i nyi skar» is not in Steinert's sets. Dead ends as above.


## Narrowed 2026-11-21 (inv-k)

«བྱེད་པའི་ཉི་སྐར» is glossed by WB's own wording elsewhere, the first half is settled from the printed text. (1) WB's ch. 4 calls the abridged reckoning «བྱེད་པའི་རྩིས» (*byed pa'i rtsis*, p. 31: «བསྡུས་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རྗེས་འབྲངས་པའི། །བྱེད་པའི་རྩིས་ནི་བསྟན་པ་ལ།», month-entries.md), and (2) the 7th head (p. 167), which stands in the same model almanac, spells the same item out: «དག་རྩིས་ལ་སེང་གེའི་ཁྱིམ་སླེབས་ལ་བྱེད། བྱེད་རྩིས་ལ་ཉི་སྐར་གྱི་སྐར་གནས་དགུ་དང་ཆུ་ཚོད་བདུན། ཟླ་བ་འདི་དང་ཕྱི་མ་གང་འདུག་ལ་རྐྱང་ལྷགས་སྐྱེ།» (in the true reckoning the doing is at the sun's arrival in the Lion's house; in the *byed rtsis*, the sun's mansion-place nine and seven water-hours; whichever of this month and the next stands there, a single surplus arises). So «བྱེད་པའི་ཉི་སྐར» of the 2nd head is the sun's mansion by the *byed* (abridged) reckoning, set beside the true reckoning's sun-entry, the next entry the one the figure gives; the 7th head's «ཟླ་བ་འདི་དང་ཕྱི་མ་གང་འདུག» is the same «this and the next, whichever» as the 2nd head's «འདི་དང་ཕྱི་མ་གང་རིགས», so «གང་རིགས» is "whichever of the two [months]", not a kind of thunder. Not settled: «ཤར་དུས་འབྲུག་འོང» ("in the east, at the time, thunder comes") stays a plain parse, no text glosses it. BDRC exact «ཟླ་བ་འདི་དང་ཕྱི་མ་གང་འདུག» 2 hits, both search-only copies of the same 7th-head clause (Blo bzang sbyin pa's collected works, MW25151_3AAD6E, a *skar rtsis 'byung rtsis zung sdeb* handbook, and the Do shal, MW2PD17386_D19206, whose Wylie snippet has «rlung lhags skye» for «rkyang lhags skye»); the 2nd head's clause occurs in no handbook with a gloss. Open for the thunder phrase.
