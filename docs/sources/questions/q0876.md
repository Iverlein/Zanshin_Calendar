# Is «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» the demon of self-clinging that Chöd teaches as the co-born demon (ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད), or an attendant adversary of the person set beside «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ»?

The pair god and demon is stock in the Rin chen gter mdzod; no snippet glosses the phrase (RD284).

## Read 2026-11-16 (inv-y)

It is the first: «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» (mnyam por 'ong ba'i bdud, the demon who comes as an equal) is, in the Chöd texts that gloss it, a name of one's own self-clinging, not an outside adversary (inv-y, 2026-11-16; BDRC exact-phrase search, 68 hits for the phrase, snippets and one etext window read).

The Chöd life and instruction texts of Ma cig Lab sgron (nine copies by «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཅེས», among them the biography by Khams smyon Dharma seng+ge, MW3CN4777, and the Chöd instruction «ma cig lab sgron gyi gcod kyi 'khrid yig»; the window read in the volume I1KG24707, «dus gsum rgyal ba kun gyi yum gcig 'phags ma lab kyi sgrol ma'i rnam par thar pa») say of the karmic spirit (ལས་གདོན): «ལས་གདོན་ཞེས་པ་ནི། ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་མ་རིག་པའམ། ངའོ་སྙམ་པའི་བདག་འཛིན་ནམ། མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཅེས་སམ། མིང་གང་ལྟར་བྱས་ཀྱང་ཐར་པ་ཐོབ་ཏུ་མི་སྟེར་ཞིང་ཉེས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱེད་པར་བྱེད་པའི་རྩ་བ…»: whether it is called the co-born ignorance, the clinging to «I», or the demon who comes as an equal, it is the root that gives no liberation and begets every fault. So the Chöd name equals the self-clinging, and «co-born ignorance» is a second name beside it. Kong sprul's text in the Bi ma la'i zab tig volume (MW1KG12985, snippet) uses it as a known phrase: «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ་ལྟར་མཉམ་འགྲོགས་ཀྱི་ཚུལ་དུ་བྱུང་བ» (as the so-called demon who comes as an equal, it arises in the manner of a companion). In the rites' lists it stands as the pair of the god: «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ་དང་ཕྱེས། མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་དང་སྤྲོད» (Rin chen gter mdzod, MW1KG14, Ngag dbang dpal bzang's works, Se ra mkha' 'gro's treasures, Kar ma chags med's «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ» … «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་གཉིས»): the co-born god and the demon who comes as an equal are the two that dwell with a person from birth, and are the first dispelled; here the second is the one that the Chöd texts call self-clinging, so the lists and the glosses agree. No text read says «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད» for it. What the author of the Lama Gongdü (q908) meant on p. 970 stays his own: the pair stands there among the causes of a confused dream and is not glossed.

## Read 2026-11-16 (inv-z2)

Read (inv-z2, 2026-11-16; claims lgd-mnyam-por-ong-ba-bdud-is-the-companion-demon-of-self-clinging and lgd-co-born-god-and-companion-demon-keep-white-and-black-pebbles, written from RD307, and a fresh BDRC search): it is both, one figure taught in two registers, and it is not the Chöd's «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད». Machik's Chöd texts gloss «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» as the demon of self-clinging, one's own co-born ignorance, "the demon who comes alongside". The exact phrase has 68 hits (Rin chen gter mdzod, the Kongtrul and Karma chags med volumes, Chöd lives), «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད་ལ» only 3, so the Chöd's own name for it is the «mnyam por 'ong ba» one. As an attendant it stands beside the co-born god: Karma chags med's black rite (A rA ga'i bka' 'bum, pp. 255–256, etext) sets on the enemy, "above him his co-born god, below the demon who comes alongside, the essence of the two being the red and black sins", and the rites of the Rin chen gter mdzod and of Ngag dbang dpal bzang say «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ་དང་ཕྱེས། མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་དང་སྤྲོད།» ("part from the co-born god, hand over to the demon who comes alongside", Ngag dbang dpal bzang's spelling, snippet). The demon is therefore the person's own companion-adversary in the god-and-demon pair; the Chöd explains what it is (self-clinging), the rites use it as the keeper of one's sins.

## Reconciled 2026-11-16 (inv-z6)

The two notes above do not differ in what they found; they differ in whether the treasury rites' demon and the Chöd's are one. The sources say they are, and the pool's claims of 2026-11-10 (lgd-mnyam-por-ong-ba-bdud-is-the-companion-demon-of-self-clinging, lgd-co-born-god-and-companion-demon-keep-white-and-black-pebbles) already said it before either note. Read again in the etexts, four witnesses meet on the one name (BDRC phrase search «མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ» 5 hits, «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ལྷ་དང་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད» 2 hits; windows of I1KG13062 and I2DB57577):

1. The Chöd of Ma cig Lab sgron glosses it as the self-clinging, the co-born ignorance (inv-y; nine copies).
2. Kong sprul (Bi ma la'i zab tig, I1KG13062, etext p. 527; the same sentence in Mkhyen brtse's collected works) says of the stain that is no other than awareness: «སྔར་ནས་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ་ལྟར་མཉམ་འགྲོགས་ཀྱི་ཚུལ་དུ་བྱུང་བ་ཞིག་ཡིན་པས་ན་བདག་ཉིད་གཅིག་པ་དང་ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་མ་རིག་པ་སོགས་སུ་གྲགས་»: it arises in the manner of a companion, like the so-called demon who comes as an equal, and so it is known as the «one with oneself» and the co-born ignorance. This is the bridge: the companion demon of the rites is named for the same thing as the Chöd's co-born ignorance.
3. The sermon on death (Zhabs dkar, Chos bshad gzhan phan nyi ma; the same text in the Mkhyen brtse volume I2DB57577, etext p. 462): beside the co-born god who keeps the white pebbles on the right shoulder stands «ཡང་མཉམ་པོར་འོང་བའི་བདུད་ཟེར་བ་ཞིག» who keeps the black pebbles on the left and chases the dead from behind; the rites' pair (inv-z2) is this pair.
4. The rites' lists (Rin chen gter mdzod, Ngag dbang dpal bzang, Kar ma chags med) set the god and this demon as the two that go with a person from birth.

So the demon who comes as an equal is one figure: one's own self-clinging in the Chöd's register (and Kong sprul's), and the attendant who keeps the black count in the narrative's and the rites'. Not shown: that the author of the Lama Gongdü (p. 970) meant the one rather than the other, and a text that says «ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་བདུད» of it. The older claim lgd-demon-who-comes-alongside-is-the-co-born-gods-pair ends «not decided by the snippets»; it is overtaken by the two claims named above, from the same day.
