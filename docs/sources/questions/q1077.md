# The Edo 六甲の夢うらなひ gives each of the sixty days a cell of one quarter, one person and one outcome, and no Chinese table read has that shape; does a Japanese 大雑書, 夢合 or 暦注 text before the 夢合長寿鑑絵抄 carry the same sixty cells?

RD380.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Narrowed, no older text with the sixty cells found. New places looked at on sheets (kokusho contact sheets, all pages, 2026-11-14): the 秘伝ゆめうらなひ (ORMK-02755, 18 images), the 神明夢うらなひ / 夢相金鏡録 (ORMK-00156, 寛政2, 17 text images) and the 神明夢うらなひ (ORMK-02432, 文政4, 21 images, sheets of its images not read beyond the first). The 秘伝ゆめうらなひ is illustrated articles by groups (神道, 仏法, 草木, 山水 ...); the 夢相金鏡録 is dense running text with a table at its head (人躰学術之相推: day stems 甲乙丙丁戊 / 己庚辛壬癸 against trades and month numbers, a table of a person's trade, not of sixty days) and the charm table at the end (mdz:i15617); on the sheets neither shows a table of sixty day cells. NDL Lab full-text searches for 六甲の夢, 夢うらなひ 甲子, 今日夢をみれば, 夢合 六十日, 夢占 甲子の日 return nothing relevant (the index is poor on cursive). The 夢合判断大成 (1830) is the Waseda copy bunko30_e0278, unreachable (503). The 夢合長寿鑑絵抄's 六甲 table has no older printed counterpart found.
