# P.3908's rule of silence (ch. 23) is for a bad dream seen in the night; does any Chinese or Japanese text read as a rule for dreams «good and bad alike» on listed days, as the Edo table's closing line does (善悪ともに語らず), or is the Edo rule a Japanese widening of the bad-dream rule?

RD375.

## Answered 2026-11-14

Yes, in Japan, and earlier than Edo. The 拾芥抄 (Shūgaishō, a Kamakura court encyclopedia; Kojitsu sōsho print of 1906, mdz:i772010, image 73) has the entry 不語夢日 («days of not telling a dream») with a table by month and day branch and the reason 「件日語吉夢、滅成惡夢凶也」 (jiàn rì yǔ jí mèng, miè chéng è mèng xiōng yě: "on those days, telling a good dream destroys it; a bad dream becomes an evil one"). So the rule for listed days covers good and bad dreams alike, as the Edo table's closing line 善悪ともに語らず does; the Edo line is inherited from the 拾芥抄, not a Japanese widening made in Edo.

P.3908 (cdb:i13-16) has two silence rules, both about bad dreams and neither tied to listed days: chapter 22 ends 「夫人惡夢三日不說化為珠寶」 (fū rén è mèng sān rì bù shuō huà wéi zhū bǎo: "if a person does not tell a bad dream for three days, it turns into jewels"), a rule of waiting three days after the dream, and chapter 23 begins 「凡人夜得惡夢　早起且莫向人說」 (fán rén yè dé è mèng, zǎo qǐ qiě mò xiàng rén shuō: "when a person has a bad dream in the night, on rising do not tell anyone"). The preface's «宜說惡夢» (to be told: bad dreams) is too faint on the scan to read further, so it is not used.

No Chinese text with a table of days on which dreams are not told was found (the Wikisource searches of RD375 and inv-vv; the Yongle dadian lines of cdb:box1 give omens by day and no silence). Whether the 拾芥抄 table has a Chinese ancestor, and whether its two-sided reason came from a bad-dream rule like P.3908's, stays unknown.
