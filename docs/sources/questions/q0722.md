# The eight near planets of p. 99

p. 99 lists eight "near" planets (*nye ba'i gza' brgyad*): lightning, *shu la*, dragon, tail, *btso*, thunder, earthquake, pestle (with their numbers 1 to 8), each placed by counting from the sun's mansion (5th, 8th, 14th, 18th, 20th, 22nd, 23rd, 24th as the text lists them). What are *shu la*, *btso* and *'brug* here, and is the series a known one? (RD191, corpus/wb/ch12.md)

## Narrowed 2026-11-13

The numbers are counts of feet (*rkang pa*, four to a mansion) from the sun's foot, with the sun in the Pleiades' first foot: the 5th is the first foot of Snar ma, the 8th its last, the 14th a foot of Lag, the 18th and 20th of Nabs so, the 22nd to 24th of Rgyal (feet 1-4 Smin drug, 5-8 Snar ma, 9-12 Mgo, 13-16 Lag, 17-20 Nabs so, 21-24 Rgyal); the text itself says the 5th is the beginning of Snar ma. The names: «ཤུ་ལ» is the Sanskrit *śūla* ('spear, pike', as the same page's spear unions), the rest are lightning, dragon, tail, thunder, earthquake, pestle; «བཙོ» (read «བཅོ» in RD191's translation) and «འབྲུག» were not identified. Searched without a result: the pool (the two pages of WB only) and a web search for the series in Tibetan and for the Sanskrit upagraha lists (Dhūma, Vyatīpāta, Parivesha, Indracāpa, Upaketu; Gulika, Māndi), which are a different series. Dead end for the series' source.

## Answered 2026-11-14

The series is a known one, and the Kun 'dus chen mo gives it with the same eight names and the same counts. The Mchog dga' commentary on the Dbyangs 'char (Kun 'dus vol. 3, printed pp. 242-243) says: "There are eight near planets, said to rise depending on the sun: glog, shu la, 'brug, du mjug, btso, thog, sa g.yos and gtun shing." It writes the fifth as «བཙོ» (btso), as WB does, so «བཅོ» of RD191's translation is a misreading. The counts are those of WB: with the sun at the first foot of the Pleiades, the lightning stands at the fifth foot (the first of Snar ma), shu la at the eighth (the last of Snar ma), 'brug at the fourteenth (the second of Lag), du mjug at the eighteenth (the second of Nabs so), btso at the twentieth (the last of Nabs so), thog at the twenty-second, sa g.yos at the twenty-third (the third foot of Rgyal), gtun shing at the twenty-fourth (the last of Rgyal). The ills of the commentary are WB's (stabbing pain for glog, liver illness and swellings for shu la, a fall into a gorge for 'brug, unwelcome food for du mjug, diarrhoea for btso, trembling for thog, a blow or a fall for gtun shing).
The names: «ཤུ་ལ» (shu la) is śūla, "spear" as before; «གློག» lightning; «འབྲུག» ('brug) the dragon of the year-signs and its roar, thunder (Tshig mdzod chen mo; Ives Waldo and Rangjung Yeshe: "thunder, clap, crack"); «ས་གཡོས» earthquake; «གཏུན་ཤིང་» (gtun shing) a wooden pestle, in the 84000 glossary the club (musala). «བཙོ» (btso) has no sense in the dictionaries that fits a sky body ("boiling, refining, dye" in Ives Waldo; "residue in a cow's stomach" in the Tshig mdzod): a name taken as given. «དུ་མཇུག» (du mjug, "smoke tail") has no headword in the 64 sets. The Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa (Jam dpal rtsa rgyud) and the root rite of Tārā (Ral pa gyen brdzes) use the term «nye ba'i gza' brgyad» for eight near planets to be drawn with the planets and the twenty-seven mansions, going over the earth's disc (BDRC search snippets: «gza' brgyad dang rgyu skar nyi shu rtsa bdun dang / gang yang nye ba'i gza' brgyad sa steng gi dkyil 'khor na rab tu rgyu ba yang bri bar bya'o»): the snippets do not list the eight, so whether they are WB's eight is not shown. The Sanskrit upagrahas (Dhūma and the rest) are another series, as the narrowed note said.
