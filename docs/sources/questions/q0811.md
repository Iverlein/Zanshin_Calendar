# Is «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» one division of the day and night into eight watches, or the day's and night's lengths each divided by eight?

As on p. 101 (four by day, four by night), or as the notes p. 535 divide for the trigrams.


Narrowed (RD287, 2026-11-10): p. 135 reads «ཉིན་མཚན་ཚད་ནི་བརྒྱད་ཀྱིས་བགོས» (བརྒྱད, the 1996 etext's བརྒྱུད is OCR error); vol. 2 p. 403 cuts the day and night into eight watches, notes p. 535 cut the day's length in eight and the night likewise; whether p. 403's eight is four and four stays open.

Narrowed further (inv-g, 2026-11-14): on p. 403 itself the first line divides «ཉིན་མཚན» (day and night together) into eight watches, each with one planet and one nāga, and the eight pairs run from sun with Mtha' yas to Rāhu with Rigs ldan once (wb.2:p403b, p403c), so the page counts eight in all, with no place for eight by day and eight by night; the closing lines then divide «ཉིན་ཚད» (the day's length) by the nāga without saying whether day alone or day-and-night. Whether the eight are four and four is not said on the page; p. 101's numbering (the sun from the fourth place, Jupiter from the eighth) fits the tantric books' eight in a run (svo:p271o), while the Dbyangs 'char's half-watches are eight by day and eight by night (claim dbyangs-char-half-watches-eight-by-day-and-eight-by-night). No commentary on WB was found that settles it.
