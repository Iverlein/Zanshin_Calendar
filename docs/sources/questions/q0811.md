# Is «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» one division of the day and night into eight watches, or the day's and night's lengths each divided by eight?

As on p. 101 (four by day, four by night), or as the notes p. 535 divide for the trigrams.


Narrowed (RD287, 2026-11-10): p. 135 reads «ཉིན་མཚན་ཚད་ནི་བརྒྱད་ཀྱིས་བགོས» (བརྒྱད, the 1996 etext's བརྒྱུད is OCR error); vol. 2 p. 403 cuts the day and night into eight watches, notes p. 535 cut the day's length in eight and the night likewise; whether p. 403's eight is four and four stays open.

Narrowed further (inv-g, 2026-11-14): on p. 403 itself the first line divides «ཉིན་མཚན» (day and night together) into eight watches, each with one planet and one nāga, and the eight pairs run from sun with Mtha' yas to Rāhu with Rigs ldan once (wb.2:p403b, p403c), so the page counts eight in all, with no place for eight by day and eight by night; the closing lines then divide «ཉིན་ཚད» (the day's length) by the nāga without saying whether day alone or day-and-night. Whether the eight are four and four is not said on the page; p. 101's numbering (the sun from the fourth place, Jupiter from the eighth) fits the tantric books' eight in a run (svo:p271o), while the Dbyangs 'char's half-watches are eight by day and eight by night (claim dbyangs-char-half-watches-eight-by-day-and-eight-by-night). No commentary on WB was found that settles it.

## Answered 2026-11-16

Four and four: «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» is the one day-and-night cut into eight great watches (ཐུན་ཆེན, thun chen), four of the day and four of the night, as p. 101 numbers them.

Read 2026-11-16 (inv-k), etext (wb.2:p403d). The Sgang dril's verse (a Phug-pa working book, BDRC MW3CN3480), citing the great commentary Dri med 'od, says that the day-and-night's twelve time-unions (དུས་སྦྱོར) made «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཆེན་བརྒྱད» and, halved, «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག» (sixteen half-watches), and prints two tables, «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད་ཀྱི་ཆ་གྲངས» and «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག་གི་ཆ་གྲངས»; its p. 121 has the same verse with the words «ཉིན་མཚན་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» exactly as WB writes them. Mipham (*'Grel chung zung 'jug nor bu'i sgron me*, etext p. 174) says it outright: «ཉིན་ཐུན་ཆེན་བཞི་མཚན་བཞི་སྟེ་ཉིན་ཞག་གཅིག་ལ་ཐུན་བརྒྱད། དེ་ཕྱེད་དུ་བྱས་པས་ཐུན་ཕྱེད་བཅུ་དྲུག»: four great watches by day and four by night, eight to one day-and-night, sixteen half-watches when halved; with day and night equal each great watch is eight chu tshod and a half-watch four. In his Kālacakra commentary (etext p. 281) the Nyingma texts' «ཉིན་བཞི་མཚན་བཞིའི་ཐུན་བརྒྱད» is the same eight. So the eight are not each of the day and of the night (that is the sixteen half-watches' eight and eight, the Dbyangs 'char's, claim dbyangs-char-half-watches-eight-by-day-and-eight-by-night): the watches are four and four, and the half-watches are what the day's and the night's measure divided by eight give (p. 135, p. 403's closing lines).

What stays: the sources are books after WB's own, and WB's p. 403 does not say «ཐུན་ཆེན» or «བཞི་བཞི»; the reading is of the term, not of a sentence of WB. No commentary on WB was found.
