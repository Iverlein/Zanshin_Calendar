# WB vol. 1 p. 62 multiplies virtue a hundredfold on «དུས་བཟང་དཀྱུས་རྣམས» and does not list them: which days are the ordinary good occasions, the full moon, new moon and eighth, the festivals written at the month heads (p. 176), or both?

Raised by the practice-days investigation (pd-e, 2026-11-16); see the cited passages.

## Searched again 2026-11-24 (inv-a)

Two published texts use «དུས་བཟང» (dus bzang, 'good time') for named days, and both name the full moon, new moon and eighth, or the 8th, 14th and 15th, not the month-head festivals. (1) The Kālacakra verse manual *Skar rtsis kyi lag len byed grub sgang dril ltar bkod pa Dri med 'od kyi snang ba* (BDRC MW3CN3480, I3CN8749, p. 346, etext): «གཉིས་པ་ཚེས་ཀྱི་བླང་དོར་འབྲས། སྤྱི་ནི་ཡར་ངོ་མར་ངོ་ཡི། ཚེས་བརྒྱད་བཅུ་བཞི་བཅོ་ལྔ་དང་། ལ་ལར་ཚེས་གཅིག་རྣམ་པ་གཉིས། དུས་བཟང་ཡིན་པས་དགེ་ལས་བཟང་། ཁྱད་པར་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མའི་རྒྱུད་དུ། ཚེས་བཅུ་རྣམ་གཉིས་མཆོག་ཏུ་བཤད།» (in general the 8th, 14th and 15th of the bright and dark halves, in some the 1st, in two kinds, are good times, so virtue is better; in the Yoginī tantra the two tenths are the best). (2) The 'Bri gung text of q1260: «ཉ་སྟོང་བརྒྱད་གསུམ» are «དུས་བཟང་འདི་གསུམ», virtue on them a hundredfold. WB's line is not glossed by either, and neither uses «དཀྱུས» (dkyus); but the reading 'the ordinary good occasions, full moon, new moon and eighth (with the 14th)' has two witnesses and the 'festivals at the month heads' none. Narrowed.
