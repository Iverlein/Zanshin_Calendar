# The seven-star enjoyment's rule on p. 102

p. 102's rule of the seven-star enjoyment (*smin bdun longs spyod*): the arithmetic as the machine gives it (sixty, seven, six, seven and the divisions) is not settled, and the sentence that follows the twelve results («ཞེས་དཔལ་དབྱངས་འཆར་རྒྱུད…», "thus directly spoken in the Glorious Rising-of-the-vowels") may close the rule or open the next paragraph. (RD191, corpus/wb/ch12.md)

## Answered 2026-11-14

The Kun 'dus chen mo gives the rule as a verse and says whose it is. The Mchog dga' commentary on the Dbyangs 'char (Kun 'dus vol. 3, printed pp. 476-477) quotes "the oral instruction of Nam mkha' bzang po" on the rising of the seven (smin bdun): «nyi skar dag par 'khor phyed bsres / logs su tshes grangs bdun gyis bsgyur / de la res gza'i grangs de byin / skar gnas drug cus bsgyur ba la / bsgyur ba byin la drug gis bsnun / bdun gyis phye lhag gza' dag la / smin bdun longs spyod rdzogs par mthong». In steps: take the sun's pure mansion and mix in the half-wheel; set it aside multiplied by sixty (the mansion's place times sixty); separately the lunar-day count times seven; add to that the number of the day's planet; add the two and strike the sum by six; divide by seven: the remainder, for the planets, shows the seven-star enjoyment complete. WB's lines are the same rule in its own words (the sun and half-wheel times sixty, the date times seven, the day's planet added, "the junctures" six, the rock seven), so the machine arithmetic of RD191 (sixty, seven, six, seven) is right and the verse settles the order of the steps. The verse goes on: "what rises together with the day's planet is not virtuous, but supremely good for burning, accomplishing power and expelling; consecration, empowerment and dances are avoided; as the great Dbyangs 'char says, this reckoning is wonderful and without error" and ends with «skad gnyis smra ba nam bzang gis / rgyud las ma 'khrugs gsal bar phye». That closes the rule in the Kun 'dus. The commentary adds, after it, that some call the same reckoning the secret "nectar session" and gives a variant verse of Grags pa seng ge.
The sentence in WB, «ཞེས་དཔལ་དབྱངས་འཆར་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལས་དངོས་སུ་གསུངས་པའི་དེང་སང་འགྲེལ་པར་ཡོངས་སུ་ཁྱབ་པར་གྲགས་ཆེ་བའི་འཁོར་ལོ་བརྒྱ་དང་ཉེར་གསུམ་…», opens the next paragraph. The relative clause «dpal dbyangs 'char rgyud kyi rgyal po las dngos su gsungs pa'i» qualifies the following «འཁོར་ལོ་བརྒྱ་དང་ཉེར་གསུམ» (the hundred and twenty-three wheels "spoken directly in the King of tantras, the Dbyangs 'char, and widely known in the present commentaries"), as the author's account of what he leaves out; the «ཞེས» before it closes the twelve results. The twelve results are not in the Kun 'dus passage (nor in this etext volume), so their source is not shown; the «ཞེས» only closes them as a quotation, the Dbyangs 'char is named for the wheels that follow.
