# The age of the nine-eye and the old kinds, and the rounds to the executioner

The nine-eye strike (pp. 87–88) strikes at 9, 18 … 81, and the old kinds (pp. 77–79) are named by the age at death: is the age the dead's year of age (lo) counted from the birth year, or full years? And how are "the male round and the female round reaching the executioner" (p. 87) counted, from which start?

## Answered 2026-11-01

The age is the year of age counted with the starting sign as the first; the male round starts at the rat of the same power element as the person and the female round at the bird, each counted upward (backward through the signs), and each ninth place lands on one of the four executioner signs (dragon, sheep, dog, ox), as LT pp. 93–94 set out, trigram by trigram; WB's trigrams for each age agree with LT's two rounds, with one difference at 63 (claim dead-nine-eye-rounds-by-age). A modern witness beside WB; WB itself does not state the start.
