# Is Marpa's saying (dusk dream habit, midnight the power of spirits, dawn true) in a work or oral teaching of Marpa, Mi la ras pa or Sgam po pa, and is «gdon dbang» there the spirits' power?

RD384.

## Answered 2026-11-14

In a work of Marpa, and the wording differs. The saying is the dream of the three parts of the night in a song of the translator on his dream of Maitrīpa: at the evening (ནམ་སྲོད, nam srod) the dream arises from habit (བག་ཆགས, bag chags) and there is nothing to put weight on in it; at midnight (ནམ་གུང, nam gung) it shows the emanations of Māra (བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ, bdud kyi sprul pa) and is not to be trusted; at dawn (ཐོ་རངས, tho rangs) the gods' prophecy, if great, is a wonder (rrd.23:p102-104, rrd.23:p296-298; the same song in the twenty-five great songs of Marpa's collected works, vol. 5, etext p. 31). It stands in the Lives of Marpa as the singer's own song, and in the songs of his collection; no oral teaching of Marpa is recorded behind it, and whether the song is his own composition or the biographers' cannot be tested from the texts.

«གདོན་དབང» (gdon dbang) is not there. The third Karmapa's line «the dream of midnight is the power of spirits» (rrd.5:p57-59) is his paraphrase of Marpa's «Māra's emanations»: the figure at midnight in Marpa is Māra's work (bdud), not the spirit class (gdon). So «gdon dbang» is the Karmapa's own wording, and in it «the power of spirits», read as the spirits' hold on the sleeper, which fits the use of the phrase in the rites (the 103 hits of «གདོན་དབང་» are spirits' power as a hindrance to be subdued), not a gloss found in a commentary.

Mi la ras pa, Ras chung pa and Sgam po pa: nothing. BDRC's exact-string search «ནམ་གུང་བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྲུལ་པ་སྟོན» finds eleven places, all prints and copies of the one song (Marpa's collected works, the Mthong ba don yod in three prints, the Bka' brgyud mgur mtsho, the Lo pan collection); the etexts of Marpa's seven volumes, Ras chung pa's whispered lineage and the volumes of the Karmapa searched by words have no other place; Mi la ras pa's songs and Sgam po pa's works have no etext that was found for the search (RD387, RD390, RD405).

New witness (inv-aa, 2026-11-14): the same three-part rule is in a treasure text of the Nyingma side, the Tantra of the Oath-bound Black Smith (Dam can mgar ba nag po'i rgyud), Rin chen gter mdzod vol. 38, ch. 5 on the omens (lgd.38:p7): «dusk's dream is of habit; at midnight the dream of magical display (ཆོ་འཕྲུལ, cho 'phrul) comes; at dawn examine the mark of the sign». Its midnight word is «cho 'phrul», a third wording beside Marpa's «Māra's emanation» and the Karmapa's «power of spirits»: the three parts of the night with habit, a display that is not to be trusted, and the dawn that is examined are a common rule of the tradition, and the midnight line is the one that is told in three ways. Claim rrd-marpa-song-has-the-three-watch-saying-midnight-mara stands; rrd-marpa-saying-only-in-the-karmapa-no-second-witness is the one corrected by RD390.
