# Festivals and monthly observances

Sigla and the rules for quoting are in [README.md](README.md). What the
festivals' and the monthly observances' readings rest on (SPEC §5.7,
§5.8; ROADMAP X5), read 2026-10-16. Rabten's and Henning's wording is
theirs: the readings state its facts in the project's own words.

## Edition Rabten, *Tibetan Calendar 2026*

The front matter (rabten.eu/downloads/calendarEN.pdf, 23 pages; read in
its text layer), in substance:

- The 8th, the full moon, the new moon and eclipse days are special days
  for any wholesome action; lay practitioners are especially recommended
  to take the Eight Vows on them (the readings: "the eight precepts").
- The 10th and the 25th are days for the *ganacakra* or tsok rites of
  tantra (ཚོགས, tshogs), especially recommended for guru pūjās.
- Sojong (Upoṣadha) days, twice a month, are for the confession of
  monastic communities (the full and the new moon in the calendar's
  pages).
- *Dharma festivals*: Chötrul Düchen, the day of the great miracles; Saga
  Dawa, the fourth month, on whose full moon the Buddha took birth,
  attained enlightenment and passed into parinirvāṇa; Chökhor Düchen, the
  first turning of the wheel; Lhabab Düchen, the return from the realm of
  the gods; Gaden Ngamchö, Tsongkhapa's parinirvāṇa; the masters'
  parinirvāṇa days and birthdays (not kept by the app). Of them: very
  special days for practice, on which any positive or negative action has
  a strongly multiplied effect.
- *Tibetan festivals*: Losar, the first day of the year, celebrated in
  every auspicious and joyous way; Zamling Chisang, the smoke offering
  (བསང, bsang) to all the protectors; Sangpo Chuzom, the ten good omens
  (not kept, SPEC §5.7).
- In the month pages: "Thanksgiving to Dharma-Protectors" on the 29th of
  the twelfth month (16 February 2026), the day before Losar.

## Henning, the Phugpa calendar for 2026

Henning's computed calendar (kalacakra.org/calendar/tdata/pl_2026.txt,
read in the browser) gives each festival a line under its date and no
reading: 1/1 "From 1st to 15th, Demonstration of Miracles"; 3/15
"Revelation of the Kalacakra Tantra"; 4/7 "Birth of the Buddha"; 4/15
"Enlightenment and Parinirvana of the Buddha"; 6/4 "Turning of the Wheel
of the Dharma"; 6/15 "The Buddha's entry into the womb of his mother";
9/22 "Descent of the Buddha from the realm of the gods". His list page
(tiblist.htm) only links the years.

## The White Beryl's month heading

WB vol. 1, p. 176 ([almanac-page.md](almanac-page.md), *The month's
heading*): «ཐུབ་དབང་ལྷུམས་ཞུགས་བལྟམས་པ་དང་། །ཆོས་འཁོར་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་མྱ་ངན་འདས། །ལ་སོགས་དུས་ཆེན་བཅས་པ་རྣམས། །རང་ལུགས་གང་བཤད་བཞིན་འདྲི», the Sage's
entry into the womb, birth, turning of the wheel, miracles and passing,
and the other great occasions (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen), are written at the head
of their month as one's own tradition gives them. The festivals' family
note says this. WB's ch. 9 merit, «དུས་བཟང་དཀྱུས་རྣམས་བརྒྱ་འགྱུར» (p. 62), a
hundredfold on the ordinary good occasions, does not name the festivals,
so no reading uses it for them.

### What Nyingma communities publish, read in the browser on 2026-11-16

Facts only; the pages' wording is theirs.

- Rigpa Wiki, *Four major Buddhist holidays*
  (rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Four_major_Buddhist_holidays): the four
  great festivals (དུས་ཆེན, dus chen) of the Buddha's life are the
  miracles, the Saga Dawa festival, the turning of the wheel and the
  descent from heaven; on them the effect of good and bad actions is
  multiplied ten million times. No source is named. Its page *Bumjur
  Dawa*: the first month of the Tibetan year, in which the festival of
  miracles falls and whose first fifteen days are the days of the
  miracles, multiplies actions a hundred thousand times throughout. No
  source is named. Its search finds no page on eclipses and multiplied
  merit, and no page listing the buddhas of the month's days.
- The Padmasambhava Buddhist Center (padmasambhava.org): the site's menu
  and its *Events* page carry no lunar practice calendar and no notes on
  the days; the events listed are dated by the Western calendar (a
  ganacakra of Guru Padmasambhava on 19 November 2026; the anniversary of
  Khenchen Palden Sherab's passing kept with a feast offering). The
  addresses /calendar and /search-results answer "page not found".
- Lotsawa House (lotsawahouse.org/topics/): the topics index has a topic
  for the feast offering and none for the days of the month, the
  festivals or eclipses; no page was opened beyond the index.

Rigpa Wiki's ten million for the festivals is neither RCZ's hundred koṭi
nor DTN's hundred million, and its hundred thousand for the whole first
month stands in none of the Tibetan passages read here.

### What Nyingma houses' calendars mark, read in the browser on 2026-11-16 (second wave)

Facts only; the pages' wording and lists are theirs.

- Rigpa, *Tibetan Buddhist Calendar* (rigpa.org/tibetan-buddhist-calendar/):
  Rigpa's printed calendar follows the Phugpa reckoning as the
  Men-Tsee-Khang computes it and marks anniversaries, the monks'
  confession days, Tārā days, Guru Rinpoche days and the great festivals;
  the page gives no dates. It links the calendar of a group of Rigpa
  students, tibetanbuddhistcalendar.org, which says it is based on the
  printed one without being bound to it.
- That calendar's key (/legend) has eight marks: Tārā day, Medicine Buddha
  day, Buddha day, Amitābha Buddha day, Guru Rinpoche day, Ḍākinī day, the
  Dharma protectors' day and "wheel day", besides anniversaries and other
  holidays. In its months 9, 10 and 11 of the fire horse year (/month) the
  marks stand on the same dates each month: the 8th, Medicine Buddha and
  Tārā; the 10th, Guru Rinpoche; the 15th, Amitābha; the 25th, Ḍākinī; the
  29th, the protectors; the 30th, Śākyamuni Buddha; the wheel on the 22nd
  of month 9, the descent from the gods. In month 11, where the 15th is
  skipped, Amitābha's mark stands on the 16th. Its *About* page: a skipped
  date's occasions move to the next day, a doubled date's stand on the
  first, after the Men-Tsee-Khang. The comparison with RCZ and Rabten:
  [claims/monthly-days-in-the-rigpa-calendar-against-rabten.md](claims/monthly-days-in-the-rigpa-calendar-against-rabten.md).
- Its yearly list (/yearly-events-overview), the special days: Losar on
  1/1; the festival of miracles on 1/15; the Buddha's birth on 4/7; the
  Saga Dawa festival on 4/15; the local deities' day on 5/15; the turning
  of the wheel on 6/4; the descent from the gods on 9/22; Gutor on 12/29.
  No tenth of the monkey month is in that list. Among its anniversaries
  by the Tibetan date, those of Nyingma masters (month/day): the first
  Dzogchen Rinpoche 1/5; Tertön Sogyal 1/10; the first Dodrupchen 1/13;
  Garab Dorje 1/15; Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo 1/21; Terdak Lingpa 2/2; Do
  Khyentse 2/20; Tertön Mingyur Dorje 3/16; Sangye Lingpa 3/30; Patrul
  Rinpoche 4/18; Mipham 4/29; Chokgyur Lingpa 5/1; Jamyang Khyentse
  Chökyi Lodrö 5/6; Shechen Gyaltsab 5/18; Jomo Menmo 7/10; Dilgo
  Khyentse 8/19; Rigdzin Kumaradza and Katok Tsewang Norbu 8/25; Jigme
  Lingpa 9/3; Adzom Drukpa 9/25; Dudjom Rinpoche 11/18; Jamgön Kongtrul
  11/26; Longchen Rabjam 12/18. The list holds masters of all four
  schools (Milarepa 1/14, Sakya Paṇḍita 11/14, Tsongkhapa 10/25, Atiśa
  9/18) and gives no practice for any of them.
- Namdroling Monastery, *Regular events* (namdroling.net/Portal/Page/Regular-Events;
  the page's year is 2018–2019), with Tibetan dates: Losar 1/1; the great
  accomplishment rite from 1/8 to 1/16, with the great scroll of Guru
  Rinpoche shown and the dance of his eight manifestations on 1/10; a
  month's retreat from 1/18; the great accomplishment rite of the Kama
  from 4/9 to 4/15, with the scroll of Śākyamuni shown on 4/10 and a fire
  offering on 4/15; Mipham's anniversary on 4/30; the summer retreat from
  6/15 to 7/29; the Nyingma prayer festival at Bodh Gaya from 12/1 to
  12/10; Longchen Rabjam's anniversary on 12/18; Gutor from 12/25 to
  12/29. The page lists no monthly days.
- Mindrolling Monastery, *Calendar* for the fire horse year
  (mindrollinginternational.org/mindrolling-monastery-calendar/), Western
  dates only: Losar, the festival of miracles, Saga Dawa, the smoke
  offering day, the turning of the wheel and the descent from the gods;
  the anniversaries of Mindrolling Trichen, Terdak Lingpa (20 March
  2026), Mipham (14 June 2026), Khenchen Ngawang Khyentse Norbu, Minling
  Chung Rinpoche and Longchen Rabjam (25 January 2027); a great
  tenth-day ceremony with dances (28 March 2026) and a three-day one from
  22 June 2026; accomplishment rites through the year (Avalokiteśvara,
  the Eight Commands, Vajrasattva, the protectors, the Kama, Vajrakīla);
  the summer retreat; the "nine bad omens" day. No monthly days.
- palyul.org answered 403 Forbidden; rigpa.org/calendar and
  /tibetan-calendar do not exist.

Mipham's anniversary is the 29th of month 4 in the Rigpa students' list
and the 30th in Namdroling's page for 2018; the two were not reconciled.
None of the three houses publishes a list of what is practised on the
month's days: the monthly marks come from the Rigpa calendar alone, and
they are RCZ's. The Men-Tsee-Khang's own full almanac prints none of
