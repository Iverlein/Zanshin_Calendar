# A Russian calendar after the White Beryl, compared

Sigla and the rules for quoting are in [README.md](README.md). The
Institute of Tibetan Astrology and Medicine's daily calendar
(tibetastromed.ru/calo.php, "version 2.00", Russian, English and
Bulgarian) says it follows the White Beryl and two other texts. It was
compared with the app on 2026-10-08: every day of 2026 in its "new
style", September to November 2026 in its "old style", and its election
by activity for October 2026, each set against the app's engine and
Henning's computed calendar (`henning-phugpa.tsv`).

**The rule for what follows (the owner, 2026-10-08): WB counts above all.**
The site is a witness to what a WB calendar can show, not a source; where
it and WB part, WB decides. The WB passages below were found in BDRC's
etext of the 1996 edition (etext page − 8 = printed page); **none is yet
read on the scan**, so each is a reading task in ROADMAP T2.10–T2.18.

## How the site works

One page per day (`docom.php?tdat=M/D/YYYY&tipv=0&type=old|new&lang=ru`)
gives every reading in what it calls descending order of importance:
festivals, the element pair ("main combination"), the named combination
("great"), the special days, the mansion, the weekday, the lunar date
(with hair washing, haircut, setting out and the house god), the date's
animal, trigram, sme ba, the soul's place, and the courses of the
"protectors of the teaching", the nāgas, the eight classes, Rāhu
("the dragon") and "the black demoness of the earth, Rāhu's sister". It
gives no yoga and no karaṇa. Years 2005–2026 only.

- **"New style"** (the site: Gelug, Nyingma, Sakya) is Phugpa: lunar
  date, weekday, the date's animal, trigram and sme ba agree with the app
  on all 365 days of 2026, the festivals too.
- **"Old style"** (the site: Kagyu, Yungdrung Bon, Indian) keeps the same
  lunar dates, counts the month animals from the tiger (Hor month 1 the
  tiger month: its trigram and sme ba then agree with the app's formula on
  91 of 91 days), and puts the mansion about one further on.

## Where it agrees with the app

- Lunar dates, doubled and skipped days, weekday, the date's animal,
  trigram, sme ba, the twelve festivals of SPEC §5.7.
- Rāhu by date: WB's general course (pp. 236–237), times included. The app
  shows the detailed course on the dates it names (the 12th and the 18th
  differ), which WB calls the more exact.
- The soul's place by date: WB's Phugpa list (pp. 303–304), except the
  29th (site: ankle; WB ངར་གདོང, the shin, as the app has it).
- Election (October 2026): haircuts on all 7 days the site names, weddings
  on 11 of 12, setting out on 11 of 14. The site ranks two Mondays best
  for setting out; WB's Monday verse avoids it (weekdays.md).
- Special days of weekday and mansion: *'grub sbyor*, *zung sbyor*, *bdud
  rgyal*, *chub nyi*, *mthun nyi*, *'chi sbyor* and *gtan spang* as WB's
  table (p. 341).

## Where the site errs or departs from WB

- **The mansion is wrong on 38 of 365 days** (in runs of up to six days);
  Henning agrees with the app on all 38, and with it the named combination
  and the element pair. It is neither an astronomical moon (best fit 240
  of 365) nor Henning's a₂, nor the moon at the end of the lunar day.
- **One column per kind of special day**: where WB's table has two, the
  site keeps the second for *grub nyi*, *sbyor nyi* and *mi 'phrod*, the
  first for *bkra shis*, *'phel*, *mi mthun* and *'jig pa'i nyi ma*; the
  app counts both, as WB gives both. Its Tuesday *bdud nyi* is Maghā (9),
  WB's table Āśleṣā (8). The *Rdo rje gtsug lag* days are not shown.
- **The order of importance** puts the element pair first and Rāhu near
  the end; KP (weighing.md) puts Rāhu first and the planet over the
  mansion.
- **The "old style"**: WB's model almanac gives Hor month 1 the dragon
  (below), so counting from the tiger is not WB's.

## WB's months and seasons

WB's model almanac (vol. 1, pp. 156–173, one line per month) gives each
Hor month the Chinese reckoning's season and animal: «ཧོར་ཟླ་བཅུ་གཅིག་པ་རྒྱ་རྩིས་དཔྱིད་ར … སྟག»,
the 11th month the first spring month, the tiger; «ཧོར་ཟླ་དང་པོ་རྒྱ་རྩིས་དཔྱིད་ཐ … འབྲུག»,
the 1st the last spring month, the dragon; then the 2nd early summer
(snake) … the 10th late winter (ox) (etext). Beside it the Kālacakra
seasons (the 4th month «དུས་འཁོར་བའི་དབྱར་ར», early summer).

- The app's month animals (`monthAnimal`, Janson) are WB's: the 1st month
  the dragon. The site's "old style" is not.
- The app's season names (the catalog's `TibetanMonth.N.season`: the 1st
  early spring) follow the Kālacakra seasons. WB's Chinese-reckoning
  seasons put spring in the 11th, 12th and 1st months and autumn in the
  5th, 6th and 7th.
- Several WB passages are keyed by season-month («དཔྱིད་ར», «སྟོན་ར»): Rāhu
  by month (p. 238, built as months 1, 2 and 7–9), the eight classes
  (p. 232), the nāgas (p. 234), the bad days by month (p. 226). Which
  season system each uses decides its Hor months.

## WB's almanac page

WB vol. 1, pp. 173–180 (etext) sets out what a Phugpa almanac writes for
each day, in order: the date with its Kālacakra and *dbyangs 'char*
correspondences, the five-fold cycle and the dates' stages (child, youth,
adult, old, ripe); weekday, mansion and their combinations; the yoga; the
times of Viṣṭi; the special days of weekday and mansion (given there in
verse: «ཉི་མ་མེ་བཞི་ … འགྲུབ་སྦྱོར་འཆི་སྦྱོར་སྲེག་སྦྱོར་དང་། །བདུད་ཉི་བདུད་རྒྱལ་འཇིག་པའི་ཉི། །མི་མཐུན་ཉི་མ»);
the burning dates; the twelve links; the date's animal, trigram and sme ba
(«སྟག་རྟ་ཁྱི་གསུམ་ལི་ཡིས་ཚེས», «སྨེ་བ་ར་བཞིར་གཅིག་དཀར»: the first of a month
by its animal, which the app's formula gives); the strikes and turnings of
the nāgas and the eight classes; the sky doors, *gnyan pa*, *snag tsha*
and the earth lords of the Chinese reckoning. Its verse on the special
days is a second witness to the table of p. 341.
