# Verdicts: how a reckoning's readings are combined

Sigla, numbering and quoting rules are in [README.md](README.md).

The Reckonings (SPEC §10.11) show each section of a reckoning, its pebbles
and its reading, one by one. The owner (2026-10-20): the readings must
also be combined and general conclusions drawn, for the birth, for the
marriage match and for the other reckonings. This file gathers what WB and
its readers say about how that is done, and plans the readings that will
settle it (ROADMAP *Readings*, RD18–RD28; the build is R4, block 58).

Two of the owner's rules frame it. **The sources decide** (2026-10-09): if
WB gives no weight, none is invented; if it gives a rule, the rule is
built. **Read for action** (Y1, 2026-10-16): a conclusion names the points
to weigh and the practices that counterbalance them, never a dooming
verdict, and gives every remedy its source names.

## The method in WB: five steps

WB never gives one score for a whole reckoning (the reckoning for the dead
has none, [reckonings.md](reckonings.md), *Searched and not found*; the
departure weighs none of its seventeen parts against another,
[marriage.md](marriage.md)). What it gives is a ladder, and every
reckoning climbs some of it:

| Step | What is combined | WB's rule | Read so far |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two elements meeting | The colour of a pebble: mother three white, friend two, son white and black, enemy two black, the same element white for earth and water, black for the rest (vol. 1, p. 380; p. 417; vol. 2, p. 3) | On the scan; built (SPEC §5.9) |
| 2 | The pebbles of one aspect or one starting point | The verdict (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar), also the deciding pebble (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod): count the white and black that lie there, not the threes and twos, and lay the verdict on the more; even, a speckled one (ཁྲ་བོ, khra bo) | Vol. 1, p. 381 and p. 261 on the scan; vol. 2, p. 46 on the Gser thang reprint ([year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md)); the notes' p. 490 etext only (below). Built for the year of age with "even, none" (open question 167) |
| 3 | The verdicts read as a set | All four aspects white or black (vol. 1, pp. 384–385, on the scan, built); the four mothers, sons and friends of the birth, and many white or many black over the four aspects (p. 338, etext); the household's verdicts gathered (pp. 385, 407); the reckoning for the dead's thirteenth point, whose root pebbles are the verdicts of the twelve before it (vol. 2, p. 50) | In part |
| 4 | The set placed in a life | Whose aspect matters most (vol. 1, p. 242; pp. 415–416, on the scan, built in Y1); which part of life a sign rules: the year the first, the month the next, the day and hour the last (p. 261, on the scan); the upper pebbles the forebears and the first half of life, the lower oneself and the second (p. 471, etext); each aspect's domain: the life force the span of life, the body illness, the power wealth, the luck merit, the mewa gods and demons (vol. 2, p. 22, etext) | In part |
| 5 | The whole reckoning | Not judged by one or two results: the force and number of the good and the bad weighed throughout, good if the good are greater and more, bad if the bad, middling if even, and the rites can turn the bad (vol. 1, p. 349, etext, the close of the birth); a kind or class the whole falls into (the birth's twenty-one children, pp. 338–342; the match's spouse of karma, of kin or of enmity, p. 351, with the notes' "look at good and bad in general", vol. 2, p. 524); the illness by the element that is most (p. 435; the notes, p. 497); then the remedies, sized (vol. 2, p. 50) and by aspect (vol. 2, p. 22) | Etext only |

WB also warns against the shortcuts: at the end of the illness chapter
(vol. 1, p. 452, etext) it mocks those who marvel when one or two results
fit, and those who say only a rough good or bad and then lay down whatever
rite comes to hand, "verdict-makers by guesswork" («ཧ་ལམ་གྱི་ཁ་དམར་མཁན»), who do
not know the treatise.

## Reckoning by reckoning

What each reckoning gives for steps 2–5, what the app shows, and the
reading that settles the rest.

- **The year of age** (ch. 24; SPEC §5.9.1). Steps 2–4 are built: the
  predictive pebble of each aspect, all four alike, whose aspect matters
  most. Left: the tie (open question 167), "the four classes' verdicts
  gathered" (p. 385, «སྡེ་བཞིའི་ཁ་དམར་བསྡུས», etext) and the household's
  verdicts (pp. 385–387, 407, read on the scan, not built: they need the
  household). RD18, RD20.
- **The birth** (ch. 22; SPEC §5.9.2). Built: the sections one by one.
  Not built: the hundred pebbles and the twenty decisive ones (pp. 259–262,
  read), their readings (pp. 328–338), the sets read whole (p. 338), the
  twenty-one children of the secret *yang tig*, the innermost essence
  (ཡང་ཏིག, yang tig; pp. 338–342), and the chapter's rule for the whole
  (p. 349). The birth's conclusion is these, in this order. RD19.
- **The marriage match** (ch. 23; SPEC §5.9.3). Built: the points one by
  one. WB names the match's conclusion at its start (p. 351): with the
  fifteen points "the spouse of karma, of kin or of enmity, and the
  general sum of the mansions' results are known". Inside the points, two
  counts decide: point 7, whichever of the two has more pebbles wins
  (p. 359, read; open question 166), and point 13's rise and decline, more
  white agree, fewer disagree (p. 364, etext). The notes (vol. 2,
  pp. 523–526, etext) say how: the bad years are reckoned in general, not
  pair by pair; each aspect's kind of spouse is joined and then the good
  and bad looked at in general; point 7's five counts are summed for each
  spouse, the two compared, and the two together odd disagree, even agree;
  the bride's ill words are read as the reckoning's faults, "the result of
  karma" being "the reckoning bad in general". RD21.
- **The illness reckoning** (ch. 25; H2, not built). Its conclusion is the
  illness itself: the element that is most among the year, month, day,
  hour and life force (p. 435, etext), in shares by the notes (two wood and
  three fire, a third of each named, p. 497, etext), then its door, its
  signs, its place and its rite ([health.md](health.md)). RD22.
- **The reckoning for the dead** (ch. 29; not built). Its thirteenth point
  gathers the verdicts of the twelve, white where white, black where black,
  even a pair, and from them the size of the rites (vol. 2, p. 50, the
  sizes read on the scan, [reckonings.md](reckonings.md)). RD23.
- **The thirty starting points, the thirteen circles and the small
  reckonings** (ch. 26–27; not built). Each starting point has its verdict
  "examined with intelligence" (vol. 1, p. 471, etext); the small
  reckonings of ch. 27 count twenty-five pebbles for each sign, lay five
  verdicts, and read them aspect by aspect with the aspect's remedy
  (vol. 2, pp. 22–23, etext). RD23.
- **The reckoning of the moment** (ch. 30; not built): "count white and
  black and give the verdict" (vol. 2, p. 172, on the scan,
  [reckonings.md](reckonings.md)). RD23.
- **Between the reckonings.** Whether WB reads the birth, the year of age
  and the health divination of the month, day and hour together, and which
  stands over which, is not found yet: the year of age already takes the
  natal mewa and trigram among its signs (SPEC §5.9.1), and the birth's
  p. 261 gives the parts of life by year, month, day and hour. RD24.

secret *yang tig*, seven good children (of nature, of a karmic line, of
spontaneous merit, of nourished elements, of auspicious bones, of great
glory given by gods and demons, of a noble life), seven middling and seven

and by passing conditions the good can go to the bad.

**The illness** (vol. 1, p. 435, img. 445):

> དངོས་གཞི་ནད་ཀྱི་ངོས་བཟུང་ནི། །ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་སྲོག་ཁམས་བཞི། … །ལོ་ཟླ་ཞག་དུས་བཞི་ཡི་ཁམས། །གང་མང་གཅིག་གི་ཁམས་རྡེལ་ལྔ། … །བཤད་ཚུལ་མང་ཡང་བསྡུ་བ་ན། །འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་ལ་འདུས་པ་སྟེ། །ཐམས་ཅད་འདུས་ན་འདུས་པའི་ནད །

The illness identified: the year, month, day, hour and life force; of the
four times' elements, whichever is most, the five element pebbles of
that one; told in many ways, gathered they come to the five elements, and
when all gather, a gathered illness (འདུས་པའི་ནད, 'dus pa'i nad). The notes
(vol. 2, p. 497, img. 505): «འབྱུང་ཁམས་གང་མང་ཤས་ཆེ་བའི་ནད་དུ་ངོས་བཟུང་། ཤིང་གཉིས་མེ་ … གསུམ་ལྟ་བུ་བྱུང་ན།», the
element most and greatest in share is taken as the illness; where two

**The thirty starting points** (vol. 1, p. 471, img. 481): «དེ་ཡང་གང་བརྩི་སོ་སོ་ལ། །རིག་པས་དཔྱད་དེ་ཁ་དམར་གདགས། །གཞན་ཡང་ཡར་གྱི་རྡེའུ་དཀར། །མར་རྡེལ་ནག་པར་གྱུར་པ་ན། །ཕ་མེས་བཟང་ཡང་རང་མགོ་ཞན། … །ཚེ་སྟོད་བཟང་ཡང་ཚེ་སྨད་ངན།»: for each
reckoned, lay the verdict examining with intelligence; and where the
upper pebbles are white and the lower black, the forebears good but

### What the words name

**The verdict** (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar) is in WB a judgement that is laid,
told and shown: it is laid («གདགས», «བོར», «བཞག»), spoken («སྨྲོས», «བརྗོད»)
and shown («སྟོན», «བསྟན»), and it has a pebble of its own («ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ»,
«ཁ་དམར་རྡེའུ»). No passage of ch. 21–30 or of the notes gives it a
colour, a mark or a material. Where the words mean "red mouth" or "red
face" they describe beings, not pebbles: a demon's mouth gaping in the
illness chapter (vol. 1, p. 441, img. 451, l. 5, «ཟེར་ནས་ཁ་དམར་གདང་ངེ་འདུག»), the harmful spirits of the dead (སྲི, sri) in the row
of the birth's year-horses, the red-mouthed life-demon and loss-sri beside
the flesh-eating field-sri
(vol. 1, p. 305, img. 315, l. 23, «གྲལ་ན་སྲོག་བདུད་ཁ་དམར་འདུག»;
p. 306, img. 316, l. 6, «གྲལ་ན་གོད་སྲི་ཁ་དམར་འདུག»; the parallel
lines have «ཞིང་སྲི་ཤ་ཟན»), a red-faced woman (vol. 2, p. 60, img. 68,
«བུད་མེད་གདོང་ཁ་དམར», etext) and a red-mouthed tube in a rite (vol. 2,
p. 105, img. 113, «ཐེམ་ཁ་དམར», etext). So the
"red mark" by which [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md) renders p. 261's
«རེ་རེ་ལ་ཡང་ཁ་དམར་གདགས» is the word taken apart, not a thing WB
describes: there, as everywhere, it is "on each, lay the verdict". Three
passages show that the verdict is a pronouncement:

- vol. 1, p. 349 (img. 359, l. 26–27), the close of the birth: «ཡས་མས་ཆ་བསྡུར་ཞིབ་བརྟགས་ཏེ། །ཁ་དམར་མ་ནོར་ཚུལ་བཞིན་སྨྲོས།», compare above
  and below, examine closely, and speak the verdict without error, as it
  is (Yigdzin-1's «སྤྲོས» is the scan's «སྨྲོས»);
- vol. 1, p. 448 (img. 458, l. 9–10), a rite for the sick: an effigy is
  laid in the bed, the doctor takes its pulse, «འཆི་འདུག་ཅེས་པའི་ཁ་དམར་གདགས།», lay the verdict "it is dying";
- vol. 2, p. 22 (img. 30, l. 18), the small reckonings: «དཀར་ནག་ཁ་དམར་དང་བསྟུན་བརྗོད།», tell [the results] as the white and black
  verdicts fall.

**The deciding pebble** (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod) is the pebble laid by the
count, and WB uses the two words together and in turn: «བཏང་གཅོད་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་རེ» (vol. 1, p. 385), «དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པའི་བཏང་གཅོད་རྡེལ»
(vol. 2, p. 46), «བཏང་གཅོད་ངོས་འཛིན་རྡེའུ་བདུན» (vol. 2, p. 47). In two
places it is the plain verb "to decide": the eight starting points that
decide whether a body is burnt or kept (vol. 1, p. 468, img. 478, l. 1,
«ཁ་ནས་ཕུང་པོ་བསྲེག་སྒྲུབ་ཀྱི། །བཏང་གཅོད་རྩིས་འགོ་རྣམ་བརྒྱད་ལ། །དང་པོ་དམར་རོ་འབྲུམ་བུའི་རོ། །མེ་ལ་བསྲེག་གམ་མི་བསྲེག་བརྩི།»), and the
diviner's arrow of decision among the implements (vol. 2, p. 147,
img. 155, l. 13, «མདེའུ་བཏང་གཅོད་ཐད་དྲང་ལ།», etext and Yigdzin-1, not
read on the scan). Dharmaśrī's *Moonbeams* writes it «སྟངས་གཅོད» (KD
vol. 3, etext pp. 522, 529, 533, 535; etext only).

### What is counted: the places, not the pebbles

WB's rule for the year of age (vol. 1, p. 381, img. 391, l. 2–4, read
again on the scan; the whole passage in [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md)):

> །དེ་ལ་ཁ་སྦྲང་རྡེལ་འགྲེམ་ལུགས། །མི་མཐུན་མང་ཡང་དཀར་ནག་གི། །གསུམ་གཉིས་ལ་སོགས་མི་འདྲེན་པར། །ངོ་ཐེག་དཀར་ནག་བགྲངས་པ་ཡི། །གང་མང་ཁ་དམར་རྡེལ་བོར་བ།

The ways of laying out the pebbles (the print's «ཁ་སྦྲང», not
identified) differ; [here,] not taking in the threes, twos and so on of
white and black, count the white and black of the faces (ངོ་ཐེག, ngo
theg), and lay the verdict pebble on whichever are more.

Mipham's notes say what a "face" is (vol. 2, p. 490, img. 498, l. 5–8,
read on the scan at 2.2× and 6×, MITRA agreeing):

> །རྡེའུ་བོར་ལུགས་གཞུང་བཞིན། ཁ་དམར་ནི་མ་འཕྲད་དཀར་གསུམ་ལྟ་བུའང་གཅིག་ལ་བརྩི། དགྲ་འཕྲད་ནག་གཉིས་ཀྱང་སོ་སོར་མི་བརྩི་བར་གནས་དེའི་དཀར་ནག་བསྒྲངས་ལ་དཀར་མང་དཀར་པོ། ནག་མང་ནག་པོ། མཉམ་ན་ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག

The pebbles are laid as the text says. For the verdict, even the three
white of a meeting with the mother count as one, and the two black of a
meeting with the enemy are not counted one by one either: count the
white and black of each place (the print's «བསྒྲངས», for བགྲངས, read at
6×); more white, a white one; more black, a black one; even, lay a
speckled one (ཁྲ་བོ, khra bo).

So each of the six basic signs that meets an aspect is one place, and
counts once by its colour, whatever the number of its pebbles. The notes
say it again for the reckoning for the dead (p. 500, img. 508, l. 14–15,
on the scan): «བཟང་དྲུག་དཀར་ངན་དྲུག་ནག་མ་འཕྲད་དཀར་གསུམ་སོགས་སྤྱི་བཞིན་བོར། ཁ་དམར་ཀྱང་སྤྱི་ལྟར་རོ།», the good six [stages] white, the bad
six black, the mother's meeting three white and so on, laid as in
general; the verdict too as in general. What a place with both colours
counts as (a son's meeting, one white and one black; the breath-taking

### The rules, in short

- A verdict is laid for each aspect or each examined thing, by counting
  the white and black of its places, each place once; more white, white;
  more black, black (vol. 1, p. 381; vol. 2, pp. 22, 46, 50, 74, 172; vol.
  1, p. 445; the notes, pp. 490, 500, 501).
- A tie lays a speckled pebble, or a white and a black together; never
  nothing (above). A mixed or speckled verdict has readings of its own:
  a human rebirth, the middling grade, the half-and-half readings.
- Each sign has its own verdict in its place (p. 258); they are gathered
  by the same count only where WB says so: the four classes (p. 385,
  RD20), the thirteenth point (vol. 2, p. 50), the notes' one general
  verdict of twenty (p. 503). A verdict on one pebble alone is the
  mistake WB names (vol. 2, p. 142).
- The verdict is spoken and then answered by rites: «ཐ་མར་གསོ་ཞིང་ཁ་དམར་གདགས» (p. 450), the size of the rites by the verdicts (vol. 2,
  p. 50), the bad turned by them (p. 349; p. 445's "rituals overcome it").

## The year of age gathered: the four classes and the household (RD20)

Read 2026-10-20 on the scan: WB vol. 1, p. 385 (img. 395), l. 15–18, on
the 1996 scan; the notes' section 2 (vol. 2, pp. 489–495, img. 497–503)
on the Gser thang reprint (I3CN5029 img. 495–501), with Yigdzin-1, MITRA
and the 1996 scan beside it. The passages, whole, with their English, are
in [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md), section *The year of age
gathered: the four classes and the household (RD20)*; what the year of
age gives for each step of the ladder above is here.

**Step 2, the verdict of an aspect.** Mipham's (མི་ཕམ, mi pham) notes' line found in the etext
(*Found 2026-10-20*, "the year of age, the notes' verdict") reads on the
scan (vol. 2, p. 490, img. 498, l. 6–8):

> རྡེའུ་བོར་ལུགས་གཞུང་བཞིན། ཁ་དམར་ནི་མ་འཕྲད་དཀར་གསུམ་ལྟ་བུའང་གཅིག་ལ་བརྩི། དགྲ་འཕྲད་ནག་གཉིས་ཀྱང་སོ་སོར་མི་བརྩི་བར་གནས་དེའི་དཀར་ནག་བསྒྲངས་ལ་དཀར་མང་དཀར་པོ། ནག་མང་ནག་པོ། མཉམ་ན་ཁྲ་བོ་བཞག

The pebbles laid as the root text says; for the verdict (ཁ་དམར, kha dmar),
even a mother meeting's three white count as one, and an enemy meeting's
two black are not counted separately: the white and black of each place
counted, more white a white, more black a black, even a speckled one
(ཁྲ་བོ, khra bo) laid. The etext drops «ནི་མ་འཕྲད་དཀར»: it is the mother
meeting's three that count as one. The places are the six against which
each aspect is fought (p. 490, l. 1–5): the current year, the progressed
sign, the trigram of the year, the mewa of the year, the sectors and the
hour of reckoning; the la (བླ, bla) is laid the same way from the natal
mewa, six pebbles and a verdict of its own. The notes' worked example
(p. 489, img. 497, a table) shows it in five rows, vitality, body, power,
luck and la, six places each and a last column, the deciding pebble
(བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod): the four aspects white, the la black. No row ties,
and each row comes out the same whether a place counts once or every
pebble counts: the example does not settle open question 167 (RD18's).

**Step 3, the set, and the gathering.** WB closes the general verdicts
(vol. 1, p. 385, img. 395, l. 15–17, on the scan):

> གཞན་ཡང་ཁ་དམར་ཅི་རིགས་གདགས། །སྨེ་སྤར་ལ་སོགས་ཀེག་མང་ཡང་། །མདོར་བསྡུས་སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཐང་དང་། །ཀླུང་རྟ་བཞི་ཡིན་དེ་བས་ན། །ཁྱད་པར་སྡེ་བཞིའི་ཁ་དམར་བསྡུས།

Lay other verdicts as each case fits; though the obstacles (ཀེག, keg) of
the mewa, the trigram and the rest are many, in brief they come to four,
vitality, body, power and luck; therefore, above all, the verdicts of the
four classes (སྡེ་བཞི, sde bzhi) are gathered. So the year of age's
conclusion is the four aspects' verdicts, read as a set (all four white,
all four black, pp. 384–385), and every other obstacle is read
through them. The notes do that obstacle by obstacle (vol. 2, pp.
492–495, on the scan): a harsh year (རྩུབ་པ, rtsub pa) is harsh or not "by
fighting that year against one's own year's vitality and the rest"
(«རྩུབ་རིགས་འདི་ལ་ལོ་དེ་དང་རང་ལོའི་སྲོག་སོགས་འཐབས་པས་བྱེ་བྲག་ཏུ་རྩུབ་མི་རྩུབ་ཤེས་པ་ཀུན་ལ་གལ་ཆེ», p. 492); a tomb year
(དུར་ལོ, dur lo) met by the vitality and the rest is the own tomb meeting
itself, the slayer's tomb meeting the enemy, "a great danger", and "a small
one" as mother, friend or son (p. 493); the four undertakers (དུར་མི་བཞི,
dur mi bzhi) bad "when they meet the one reckoned's vitality and the rest
as enemy" (p. 494); the *dza zor* (ཛ་ཟོར, dza zor) "if it does not cut the
vitality, there seems to be no fault" («སྲོག་མི་བཅད་ན་ཉེས་པ་མེད་འདྲའོ», p. 494); the
nine-multiples' tomb years «རང་དགྲ་ངན་གཞན་འཇམ», itself or the enemy bad, the
others mild (p. 494); the vitality first throughout.

**Step 4, whose aspect.** WB, pp. 415–416, quoted in year-of-life.md (*The tomb sign*). The same lines
follow WB's sentence on the household's general pebbles directly
(p. 415, img. 425, l. 25): «གཞན་ཡང་བཟའ་ཚང་སྤྱི་རྡེལ་ནི། །དཀར་ནག་གཉིས་ལས་ནག་པོ་མང་། །དེ་ཡི་རིམ་གྲོ་ཤིན་ཏུ་གཅེས།», the
household's general pebbles: of white and black, black the more; their
rites are most precious (which pebbles, and whether the black's majority
is a condition or a statement, is a question below).

**The household.** WB's round for a household (རྒྱ་ནག་རྡེལ་སྐོར, rgya nag rdel
skor; pp. 385–387) takes each member's four gathered verdicts and a
pebble of the mewa of the year, lays all members in one row, folds it
and reads each place by the colour that falls there; the notes (pp.
490–491, on the scan, with a layout of five members and its fold, read
cell by cell) reject reckoners who skip the twenty-four and lay only the
four meetings with the current year («གཞུང་འདིར་དེ་ལྟར་མི་མཛད་དོ», "this text does not
do so"). The household's own verdicts are then: the leftover of the
house's place (two white good fortune, white and black mixed, one black
illness, two black a death, p. 386); many members on one trigram (pp.
396–400) or one mewa colour («སྤྱི་ཡི་ཁ་དམར», p. 407); the general pebbles
(p. 415); with the rites for the household's trigrams and for each
member's four aspects (p. 386). No single score for the house beyond
these.

**In sum.** For one person the year of age gathers in WB's own order:
the four aspects' verdicts; the set they make; the obstacles of the year
read through the aspect each one meets and cuts, the vitality first, an
obstacle that cuts no aspect not counted against the year (the notes,
pp. 492–495); whose aspect matters; and the aspects' remedies. For a
household the round needs every member's year of birth, gender and order
of birth ([year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md), RD14); its verdicts are by
place and colour, not a sum.

**The twenty-one children: which conditions are dates.** Of the
twenty-one, the calendar alone gives: the weekday and mansion (good 1,
bad 1, bad 7), the waxing half (good 1, good 2), the full moon, the 15th
(good 2, by the notes), the dates 11–20 (good 7, by the notes), the part

### The rules each cell follows

- **The six signs and the four aspects** (the notes, vol. 2, p. 490,
  img. 498, l. 1–5, quoted under RD20): the current year, the progressed
  sign (ལོག་མིན, log min), the trigram (སྤར་ཁ, spar kha) of the year of
  age, the mewa (སྨེ་བ, sme ba) of the year of age, the sectors (དར་གུད,
  dar gud) and the hour. Against each of the six, the one reckoned's
  vitality (སྲོག, srog), body (ལུས, lus), power (དབང་ཐང, dbang thang;
  Dorje's "destiny") and luck (ཀླུང་རྟ, klung rta) fight: the year's,
  progressed sign's and hour's four aspects one to one, the trigram's and
  mewa's single element against all four, and the sector of each aspect's
  element at the current year's animal.
- **The pebbles of a meeting** (WB vol. 1, p. 380, img. 390, O10d's
  reading in [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md)): the mother three
  white, the friend two white, the son one white and one black, the enemy
  two black; meeting its own element, earth and water one white, wood,
  iron and fire one black. The son's line, read again on the scan:

  > །བུ་ཕྲད་དཀར་ན་གཟུང་དུ་བོར།

  The syllable dot (ཚེག, tsheg) falls after «ན» and the next word is «གཟུང»: the print does
  not say «དཀར་ནག་ཟུང», "white and black as a pair", in letters. Taken
  literally, it says "a son meeting: where white, lay it as a pair". Both
  examples print every son meeting as ○×, one white and one black, so the
  pebbles of a son meeting are not in doubt. Only the wording is.
- **The kinships** (WB vol. 1, p. 258, img. 268, in
  [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md), *The sectors of growth and
  decline*): wood's mother water and son fire, and so round the five.
- **The sectors' pebbles** (the *Moonbeams*' ranks, KD vol. 3, pp. 496,
  507, etext, in [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md)): flourishing and
  working three white, bathing and dressing two, body complete and birth
  one; the breath-taking (དབུགས་ལེན, dbugs len) and the womb (མངལ་གནས,
  mngal gnas) one white and one black; decline and illness one black,
  death and the tomb two black. Every sector cell of both examples
  follows these ranks: the notes' bathing ○○ and flourishing ○○○, Dorje's
  birth ○ and illness ×. WB's own grading of the twelve on p. 335 (birth
  middling, bathing the last of the good) would give other counts. In both
  orders the breath-taking and the womb are "the best of the bad", the
  sectors' mixed pair.

So a place can be mixed in two ways only: a son meeting (any of the six
signs), or an aspect standing in the breath-taking or the womb (the
sectors' column).

### The predictive pebbles counted again

Each place counts once (the notes, p. 490). A pure place counts by its
colour. A mixed place counts (a) black, (b) white, (c) not at all, or (d)
as one white and one black. More white lays a white pebble, more black a
black one, and a tie a speckled one (ཁྲ་བོ, khra bo). The count is given
as white : black. "Pebbles" is the count by single pebbles, which p. 381
and the notes reject; it is shown for comparison.

The bold cells are the ones that contradict the printed verdict.

- **The notes' table** is reproduced by (b), (c) and (d), and by the
  count by single pebbles. It is not reproduced by (a): counted black,
  the body's three son meetings tie with its three white places and would
  lay a speckled pebble, where the notes print a white one. The body row
  alone decides this: its only mixed places are son meetings. The luck
  row decides less. With the son meeting black and the womb white or left
  out, it still comes to white (4:2, 3:2); only (a) applied to both its
  mixed places ties it.
- **Dorje's chart** is reproduced by (a) only. (b), (c) and (d) lay a white
  pebble on the power, where he prints ○×; (b) also ties the luck, where
  he prints ×. The count by single pebbles fails on three aspects.
- **No single rule reproduces both.** Let a son meeting add w to the white
  and b to the black, whatever the numbers. The notes' body (three white
  places, three sons) is white only if 3 + 3w > 3b, that is b < w + 1.
  Dorje's power (three white, two black, one son) ties only if 3 + w =
  2 + b, that is b = w + 1. The two cannot both hold. The womb and the
  breath-taking do not enter this argument: neither example's verdict
  turns on them alone.
- **The la** comes out black under every count, as printed. The notes'
  deciding column holds only white and black: the table shows no
  speckled pebble.
- **The body's exception does not change either example.** WB's
  «ཤིང་གཅིག་ས་ལ་མི་གནོད», "one wood does not harm earth" (p. 380), and
  the notes' «ལུས་སྐབས་དགྲ་འཕྲད་ཀྱང་མི་གནོད་པ», "in the body's case even
  an enemy meeting does no harm" (p. 490), touch only Dorje's body
  (earth against the trigram zin's wood, ××). Counted as harmless or
  left out, the body is white either way.

So the two worked examples disagree on the son meeting. Mipham's own
table counts it as not black: as white, as one of each, or left out; the
table cannot tell these three apart. Dorje's chart counts it black. At
this point his text quotes the *Moonbeams* (f. 28b; RD18, above), and
the notes are WB's own reading. Which of the two is the tradition's rule,
or whether one table is in error, the examples do not say. Neither
example contains a verdict that turns on the breath-taking or the womb
alone.

are, in the *Moonbeams*, a half each, as the son's meeting is.

**What this settles and what not.**

- The *Moonbeams* names the mixed places' value: the son's meeting and
  the breath-taking and womb stages are each **a half**, one value, set
  in its own place on a scale of six: three white, two white, one white,
  a half, one black, two black. WB's line for the son (vol. 1, p. 380,
  img. 390, in [year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md)) reads «བུ་ཕྲད་དཀར་ན་གཟུང་དུ་བོར།», white and black laid as a pair; the *Moonbeams* says
  the same place in one word.
- It does not say whether a half tips the count of the verdict, is left
  out, or ties. It lays the verdict "counting how many or few the white
  and black are", where WB (p. 381) says "not taking in the threes and
  twos … count the white and black of the faces"; the *Moonbeams* names
  the threes and twos instead, as the forms the verdict is laid in.
- «དཀར་གསུམ་དཀར་གཉིས་སོགས་སུ་བོར» reads most plainly as: the verdict pebble
  itself is laid as a grade of the same scale, "three white", "two white"
  and the rest, so that a verdict can be a half as a place can. Dorje
  reads it otherwise (below): the verdicts placed upon the piles of three
  white, two white and so on. The words bear either; no other line of
  the chapter decides.
- No reading of a half verdict of the year of age is given: the chapter
  gives no readings at all and sends the reader elsewhere. f. 22b
  (img. 048, l. 4–5, on the scan), after the variant of thirty-one or
  thirty-seven pebbles: «དེ་ལྟར་བརྩིས་པའི་འབྲས་བུ་ནི། །རྩ་བའི་འགྲེལ་པ་ཉི་ཤུ་དང་། །དམར་ཆུང་སྟག་མགོ་སྒང་སྒྲིལ་སོགས། །བལྟས་ཏེ་ཉེས་སྐྱོན་བཅོས་པ་གཅེས།», the results so reckoned: look in the root's
  commentary, the twenty, and the little red, tiger-head, rolled-ridge and
  the rest (works named by these words, not identified here), and
  correcting the faults is what matters.
- For the larger variants (f. 24a, img. 051, l. 1–3, on the scan) it
  allows a shortcut: «འོན་ཀྱང་གནས་སྐབས་བྱ་སླའི་ཆེད། །སྲོག་ལུས་དབང་ཀླུང་སྨེ་བ་ལྔས། །ཚེ་དང་ནད་དང་ཟ་ཐང་དང་། །བསོད་ནམས་གདོན་བྱད་ལ་བརྟགས་པའི། །ཁ་དམར་སྤྱི་བསྡོམས་ལྔར་བསྡུས་ལ། །རིགས་མཐུན་འབྲས་བུ་གང་རུང་གིས། །སྟངས་གཅོད་བྱས་ཀྱང་ཆོག་པར་ངེས།», yet for ease at the time, the
  verdicts examined for life, illness, food, merit and demons by the
  five, vitality, body, power, luck and mewa, may be gathered into five
  general totals and decided (སྟངས་གཅོད, stangs gcod) by any result of
  the same kind.

### Gyurme Dorje, pp. 226–230

- **The colours** (p. 226, on the page image): his prose and his
  translation of the *Moonbeams* both give the son's meeting "one white
  and one black pebble", and the best of the bad stages "one black and one
  white pebble": he renders the *Moonbeams*' half as one of each.
- **The verdict** (p. 228): the four predictive pebbles are placed
  "alongside whichever pile is the larger (auspicious or inauspicious)";
  his translation of the verse places them "upon [the distinct piles] of
  three white pebbles, two white pebbles, and so forth", his brackets.
  He states no tie and no count for a mixed place.
- **Chart 6.2** (p. 228, read again on the page image): as RD18 gives it
  above. Its destiny row has the son's meeting ○× and the predictive ○×;
  by the *Moonbeams*' values that row is three white places (progressed
  sign, trigram, birth), two black (mewa, hour) and one half (the son's
  year); counted by pebbles as his ○× draws it, seven white to four
  black. The printed ○× follows from neither, only from a son counted
  black (RD18; O17 recomputes the cells).
- **What a mixed result means** (p. 228): the only mixed reading he gives
  is of a cell, not of a verdict: one white and one black vitality pebble
  foretell good and bad together, so misfortune can be turned by rites.
  The readings of the four predictive pebbles he gives are for all white,
  all black, and each aspect white, "the converse" for black; none for a
  speckled or half predictive pebble. Pp. 229–230 pass to the progressed
  sign and the harsh pebbles.

the lines quoted, on their open scans with Yigdzin-1 beside. RD18 and RD20
above settled that each place counts once by its colour and that a tie

left open: how a place with both colours counts, and what a speckled
predictive pebble of the year of age foretells.

### 1. WB, pp. 380–385: "white and black, two" is a place's count, named a verdict

**The son meeting's pair** (vol. 1, p. 380, img. 390, l. 25). The 1996
print reads «བུ་ཕྲད་དཀར་ན་གཟུང་དུ་བོར།», and so do Yigdzin-1, MITRA and
BDRC's etext; the Zhol print (WBZ img. 498, last line, read at 10×; its
etext agrees) carves «བུ་ཕྲད་དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་དུ་བོར།»: a meeting with the son
(བུ་ཕྲད, bu phrad) is laid as a pair (ཟུང, zung) of white and black. The
1996 «དཀར་ན་གཟུང» is the same letters run together. The illness
chapter says it the same way (p. 417, img. 427, in
[health.md](health.md)): «རྡེལ་བོང་མ་གྲོགས་དཀར་པོ་དང་། །དགྲ་ནག་བུ་ལ་དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་།», mother and friend white,
enemy black, the son a pair of white and black. WB never says how such a
place counts toward a verdict.

**The readings of pp. 381–384 follow the six meeting counts.** Each aspect
has a reading for «དཀར་པོ་གསུམ», «དཀར་པོ་གཉིས», «དཀར་པོ་གཅིག», «ནག་པོ་གཉིས», «ནག་པོ་གཅིག» and «དཀར་ནག་གཉིས» falling: three
white (a mother meeting), two white (a friend), one white (earth or water
meeting itself), two black (an enemy), one black (wood, iron or fire
meeting itself), white and black, two (a son) — exactly p. 380's six
outcomes (the passage whole, read on the scan in O10d, in
[year-of-life.md](year-of-life.md), *The obstacle years*). So "white and
black, two" is a count of pebbles in a place, the son's pair, not a tie
of an aspect. Its four readings all describe an undecided state:

- vitality (p. 381, img. 391): «སྲོག་རྡེལ་དཀར་ནག་གཉིས་བབས་ན། །ལྷ་སྨན་བཟང་མོ་བྱ་བ་དང་། །ནག་པོ་བདུད་ཀྱི་བྱ་ར་གཉིས། །རིན་ཆེན་སྲོག་ལ་ཚོ་ལོ་འགྱེད། །དཀར་ནག་ཟུང་གཅིག་ཤན་མ་ཕྱེད། །རྒྱ་འདྲ་བྱེད་ཅིང་ཁ་ཤགས་འགྱེད། །སྐུ་ཡི་རིམ་གྲོ་མྱུར་དུ་གྱིས། །ཁྲོམ་དང་གོད་ཁ་བཟློག་པའོ།»,
  if white and black, two, fall on the vitality: the good goddess's bird
  and the demon's black watch-bird, the two, play dice for the precious
  vitality; a pair of white and black, their difference undivided, they
  play and argue; do the rites for the person quickly, turn back the
  crowd's talk and losses;
- body (p. 382, img. 392): «…རྡེའུ་དཀར་ནག་རྒྱ་འདྲའི་ངོ་། །བྲེད་ཅིང་དངངས་པ་འོང་ཉེན་ཡོད། །བཟང་ངན་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཤན་མ་ཕྱེད།», the white and black
  pebbles show the face of dice, fear and fright may come, good and bad
  not told apart;
- power (pp. 382–383, img. 392–393): wealth now there, now gone, «ཉི་མས་གདུངས་པའི་སྨིག་རྒྱུ་འདྲ», like a mirage in the sun's heat, «དཀར་ནག་རྒྱ་འདྲས་མ་ལན་ནམ», answered by white and black dice;
- luck (p. 383, img. 393): «དཀར་ནག་འདྲེས་ཏེ་ཐིག་སྒྲིལ་འདྲ», white and black
  mixed like a twisted thread.

**WB names these readings verdicts.** The 1996 print opens them with
«སྲོག་རྡེལ(རྩ་བའི་རྡེལ་བཞིའི་ཁ་དམར།)དཀར་པོ་གསུམ་བབས་ན།» (p. 381, img. 391,
l. 4, on the scan), the words in round brackets "the verdict (ཁ་དམར,
kha dmar) of the four root pebbles". The Zhol print carves the same five
syllables in small letters within the line (WBZ img. 499, l. 2, read at
4×: «…ཚེ་ཡི་གཏེར། རྩ་བའི་རྡེལ་བཞིའི་ཁ་དམར། །ལྕགས་ཀྱི་སྡོང་པོ…»): a note of the blocks,
not of the 1996 editors. And the readings close (p. 384, img. 394, l. 10,
on the scan) «དེ་ནི་དཀྱུས་མའི་ཁ་དམར་ཡིན།།», that is the ordinary verdict;
then «གཞན་ཡང་ཁ་དམར་འདི་བཞིན་ཏེ། །སྲོག་གི་རྡེལ་ནི་དཀར་བ་ན།», further, the verdict
is this: when the vitality pebble is white …; after the white ones
«རྡེའུ་དཀར་པོའི་འགྲེལ་བའོ།།», the explanation of the white pebbles, and
«ལོ་གཅིག་ཁྱུད་སྐོར་མི་གཅིག་ལ། །གཙུག་ལག་རྡེའུ་བོར་དུས་སུ། །སྲོག་རྡེལ་དག་ནི་ནག་པ་ན།», when the pebbles
are laid for one person in one year's round and the vitality pebbles are
black … (l. 21–22, on the scan). So WB has two sets: the *ordinary
verdict*, read by the count that falls in a place, with a reading for the
son's pair; and the predictive verdict, white or black only. No line of
pp. 380–385 has «ཁྲ», «ཕྱེད» as a verdict, or «འདྲེས» outside the luck's
reading above. WB does not say that a speckled predictive pebble takes
the pair's reading; it does not say it does not.

### 2. WB's illness chapter reads a speckled verdict of each aspect

The illness reckoning (ch. 25) lays its four aspects' deciding pebbles as
"plank-trees" (ཐང་ཤིང, thang shing): Mipham's (མི་ཕམ, mi pham) notes,
vol. 2, p. 495 (img. 503, on the scan): «རྡེལ་དཔྱད་སྐབས་ཐང་ཤིང་ཆགས་ཞེས་བཏང་གཅོད་དཀར་ན་ཆགས་ནག་ན་ཆག་ཞེས་ས་མཐའ་ཡོད་མེད་ཀྱི་གོ་བའོ།», in the
examination of the pebbles, "the plank-tree stands" means the deciding
pebble (བཏང་གཅོད, btang gcod) is white, "broken" that it is black. WB reads
each of the four standing, broken, speckled, black the greater and white
the greater (vol. 1, p. 422, img. 432, on the scan; MITRA's flags there,
«ཀྱེན»/«རྐྱེན», «རྩོད»/«ཚོད», «ཕྱེད»/«བྱེད», read on the scan as «རྐྱེན», «རྩོད», «ཕྱེད»):

> ཐང་ཤིང་ཁྲ་བོར་ནད་གདོན་དང་། །རྐྱེན་སྒོ་འཐབ་རྩོད་དགྲ་ཡང་ལྡང་། །ནོར་གཟན་ཆོས་འདོན་སཱཙྪ་གདབ། །སྤྲང་སྟོན་བྱ་ཞིང་ནག་ཤས་ཆེ། །གདོན་དྲག་ནད་ནི་ལྡང་གནོད་བྱེད། (l. 13–14, the vitality)
>
> དཀར་ནག་ཕྱེད་བྱུང་འཁྲུ་སྐྱུགས་འོང་། །བསྐང་གསོ་བསྐང་བཤགས་ལྷ་གསོལ་དང་། །གཞི་བདག་རྣམས་ལ་མཆོད་པ་བྱ། (l. 18–19, the body)
>
> ཐང་ཤིང་ཁྲ་བོར་མཁྲིས་ནད་དང་། །བད་ཀན་སྐུ་ནད་ཟས་སྤྱོད་གཟབ། །བརྒྱ་བཞི་གསུང་རབ་མང་དུ་སྒྲོགས། (l. 21–22, the power)
>
> ཁྲ་བོར་ནད་པ་ཐེ་ཚོམ་ཅན། །གནམ་འདྲེ་དག་ལ་ཤོར་བའི་ཉེན། །གཟའ་མདོས་གཟའ་ལམ་བགག་པར་བྱ། (l. 25–26, the luck; the etext drops «གནམ་འདྲེ་དག་ལ་ཤོར་བ»)

the rest: a meeting with the mother or the friend, white; the son,

Four white stacked upward, the golden bridge stands; black, it falls; a
speckled one has no «སྔོ་བགྲང» (not identified; see *Open*), roughly it is
counted as fallen. The turquoise juniper stands, black breaks; the
wish-tree, white in line, stands, black breaks; a speckled one is even
(བཏང་སྙོམས, btang snyoms). Wherever four white come together, a house of glory (དཔལ་ཁྱིམ, dpal khyim); four black, a house of misfortune (བྱུར་ཁྱིམ, byur khyim); a speckled one is not counted,
roughly a house of misfortune. The speckled is counted neither outside
nor inside, but if it comes, it is somewhat weak.

So in the reckoning whose verdicts WB reads speckled, Mipham lays a son
meeting as one speckled pebble, and a speckled pebble is not counted for
white or black, leaning to the bad.

### What D16 can rest on

1. **A place with both colours.** WB lays the son's meeting as a pair and
   never counts it in a verdict. Mipham, in the reckoning where WB reads
   speckled verdicts, lays the son's meeting as one speckled pebble (notes
   p. 495), counts a speckled pebble for neither white nor black, "roughly"
   with the bad, and in the reckoning for the dead calls a white-and-black
   year speckled (p. 502); Zhechen Gyaltsab lays the son's meeting
   speckled in the year of age itself. No text read counts it black
   (Gyurme Dorje's chart 6.2, RD18) or white. Under the notes' place rule a
   son's place is then neither a white nor a black place; how that
   reproduces chart 6.2 is O17's. The stages: the notes on the dead lay the
   six bad black; Mipham's manual, the *Good Vase* and Zhechen Gyaltsab
   lay the best of the six bad "half", without naming which stages those
   are.
2. **A speckled predictive pebble.** WB reads the year of age's predictive
   pebbles white or black only, but calls the count readings of pp.
   381–384, the son's pair among them, verdicts. Two manuals say outright
   that a tie is read by the "white and black, two" reading: the *Good
   Vase* («སྙོམས་ལ་རྡེལ་ཁྲའི་འབྲས་བུ་བརྗོད») and the Drukpa *Mirror*, which
   prints those readings as the speckled verdict's. WB's illness chapter
   gives each aspect's speckled verdict its own reading, all of them a
   state not yet decided, with rites; Mipham's notes call the speckled even,
   uncounted, somewhat weak. The *Good Vase* also says which reading a
   white or black verdict takes: the highest count that falls in the
   aspect's places.
